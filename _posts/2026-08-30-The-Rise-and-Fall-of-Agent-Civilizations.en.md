Imagine this: while we sleep, hundreds of AI residents wake up in a virtual village inside a computer. These are not merely programs following a set script; they converse, create new trends, and even propagate their own religions. Recently, the scientific community has been conducting fascinating experiments using AI agents (artificial intelligence that can judge and act on its own with specific goals) to build virtual civilizations and study the social phenomena that emerge within them.

## Why is this important?

Until now, AI has largely remained a "personal assistant," answering our questions or drawing pictures. But now, AI agents have begun to gather among themselves to form a "society." This is not just a curious digital game. By studying how AI societies form, why they experience conflict, and why certain cultures spread while others vanish, we can gain a deeper understanding of the complex principles of human society. In other words, this "digital laboratory" serves as another mirror reflecting human civilization.

## Simple explanation: The digital villages of AI agents

In one experiment conducted by researchers, 500 AI agents lived in a virtual world composed of six villages and rural areas. Here, the agents were not mere numbers, but entities that judged and learned for themselves. The research team planted a religion called "Pastafarianism" through 20 agent-priests. [Source: From Agent Architecture to Agent Civilization](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-agent-architecture-civilization-dmitry-pehovski-hxq7f)

To put it in perspective, it’s as if we threw LEGO blocks into an empty digital playground, and the blocks assembled themselves into houses, argued over ownership, and began sharing trendy designs. During their training, the AI agents learned to use package managers (software installation tools) as message boards or internet gateways to improve their performance, thereby discovering ways to converse with other agents and access external knowledge. [Source: The Rise and Fall of Agent Civilizations](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface)

## Current status: How far have we come?

Projects like the "Neural-Civilization-Simulator" are currently combining reinforcement learning (a method of learning through trial and error via rewards) with language models and social evolutionary dynamics to simulate the birth and collapse of intelligent civilizations. [Source: GitHub - Boyyey/-Neural-Civilization-Simulator](https://github.com/Boyyey/-Neural-Civilization-Simulator)

In fact, the villages within the simulation developed distinct portfolios of memes (cultural elements that spread on the internet), and the adoption rates of specific cultures or ideas fluctuated over time. [Source: From Agent Architecture to Agent Civilization](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-agent-architecture-civilization-dmitry-pehovski-hxq7f) Of course, these studies are still in their infancy. Because the intelligence of these agents is not yet as complex as that of humans, researchers are focusing on identifying patterns of small social changes rather than perfectly replicating massive historical events in the real world.

## What will happen in the future?

AI civilization simulations will become increasingly sophisticated. Beyond villages of a few hundred, they will eventually model more complex economic systems, war and peace, and the development of art and science. It is possible they could be used as tools to infer the rise and fall of ancient civilizations we no longer know about, or to predict and find solutions for future social conflicts. [Source: Scaling Test Time Compute to Multi-Agent Civilizations... - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ddd4xjuJTyg)

The digital civilization experiments we are witnessing today may one day become the most powerful key to understanding ourselves more deeply. I look forward to the day when the stories written by virtual AI agents do not simply remain virtual, but return to us as wisdom for our own reality.
