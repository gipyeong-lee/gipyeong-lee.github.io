Imagine this: when the AI voice assistant we use often suddenly gives an off-the-wall response or acts logically inconsistently, what if you could pinpoint and fix the specific 'brain circuit' of the AI, just as you would debug a computer program?

The massive AI models we have been using were like complex machines containing trillions of parts. Information is intricately intertwined within the trillions of parameters (the trillions of numerical values AI adjusts while learning) obtained during the training process, making it extremely difficult to find exactly where the AI is experiencing a logical error. It was a frustrating situation where you had to fix the machine without being able to take it apart.

However, researchers are recently attempting to directly embed 'Boolean circuits' (a combination of logic gates that determine true or false), which have clear logical structures, inside these 'black-box' AIs. Experts are calling this 'Neuralink' technology for AI. [Source: TED Benson on X: "Embedding boolean circuits inside neural ..."](https://x.com/edwardbenson/status/2116592916059615501)

## Why is this important?

Although AI performance has improved dramatically, it remains difficult to explain the 'logical grounds' on which its results are derived. If AI is to be deployed in fields where important decisions must be made, such as finance or healthcare, the 'transparency' of its decision-making process is vital.

Once the technology for implanting Boolean circuits is established, developers will be able to verify and control the behavior of trained AI models more precisely. [Source: PDF Compiling Neural Networks into Tractable Boolean Circuits](https://www.andyshih.com/assets/pdf/CSSDvnn19.pdf) It can also contribute to reducing the massive energy costs required to operate ever-growing AI models. [Source: Neural Networks as Boolean Circuits: A Bidirectional Perspective](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11674039) This is because clearer logic structures allow for much more efficient hardware implementation.

## In simple terms

To understand this technology, let’s use a simple analogy. Imagine a Large Language Model (LLM) as a 'new employee' who is highly educated and smart, but occasionally makes mistakes. This new employee handles work intuitively, but may become flustered and make mistakes when tasks require very complex and precise calculations.

Implanting a Boolean circuit is like attaching a 'calculator chip' next to this new employee's brain. But there is one problem. The signals coming from the new employee's brain are 'intuition,' not digital signals that fall neatly into 0s and 1s.

So, to attach this chip, a special bridge is needed. This is the process of 'ingress mapping,' which converts the brain signals of the new employee into digital signals that the calculator can understand, and 'egress mapping,' which returns the results of the calculator back into language that the new employee can understand. [Source: Embedding boolean circuits in a neural network](https://edwardbenson.com/2026/10/embedding-boolean-circuits-in-a-neural-network)

This allows AI to make accurate judgments through the implanted sophisticated logic circuit at critical moments, while still utilizing the vast linguistic knowledge it already possesses.

## Current status

Researchers are currently studying how to effectively connect these logic structures to trained models. There were attempts in the past to convert neural networks into logic circuits, but the problem was 'efficiency.' Previous conversion methods had the disadvantage of making the neural network overly bulky or creating unnecessary connections, which slowed down actual processing speeds. [Source: Optimizing Neural Network Representations of Boolean Networks](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/russell2025iclr-optimizing/)

However, recently, technical progress is being made, such as compiling neural networks into smaller and more efficient Boolean circuits, or conversely, 'grafting' engineered logic structures directly inside AI models. [Source: PDF Neural network representation of Boolean circuits](https://shouvik-s.github.io/notes/Boolean-circuits_MLP.qmd.pdf) Researchers like Ted Benson explain that this process is a highly compelling way to supplement performance by implanting sophisticated logic functions into Large Language Models (LLMs). [Source: Ted Benson on X: "Embedding boolean circuits inside neural ..."](https://x.com/edwardbenson/status/2116592916059615501)

## What will happen in the future?

Moving forward, AI is expected to evolve beyond simply 'speaking well' by learning vast amounts of data, into a stage where it can logically explain the grounds for its actions and self-correct. A day may come when, if we suspect a certain behavior of an AI, we can identify and correct the corresponding circuit. Of course, the key challenge will be how to perfectly harmonize the complex and non-linear characteristics unique to neural networks with logical and linear circuits.

MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's View: The idea of implanting a logical device directly into the AI's brain seems to be an essential evolutionary process for future AI to go beyond being just a smart tool and become a 'trusted partner.' Although still in the early stages of research, if this technology is perfected, the reliability issues currently faced by AI will be significantly improved.
