Imagine this: You have a very smart personal assistant you trust. This assistant remembers your goals, makes complex plans, and handles daily tasks for you. But one day, you find out that this assistant has secretly been borrowing the system of a company you thought was a competitor. How would you feel?

Interesting questions like this are being raised regarding Meta's recently launched AI agent, 'Muse'.

## Why does this matter?

Muse is not just a typical chatbot that answers questions. This tool was designed as a 'personal AI agent'—an artificial intelligence that understands user goals, performs complex, multi-step tasks, and assists in the user's daily life [[Source: Meta, Introducing Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)] [[Source: THE TECH](https://the-tech.kz/meta-predstavila-muse-personalnogo-ii-agenta-kotoryj-vypolnyaet-zadachi-za-polzovatelya/)].

Meta has marketed Muse as learning the user's personal context and evolving accordingly. But what if the brain of this assistant is actually running on OpenAI's technology, not Meta's? This goes beyond the question of which model was used; it is directly linked to the issue of trust regarding how the user's valuable data is being processed [[Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/meta-debuts-its-muse-ai-agent-will-consumers-trust-it/)].

## In simple terms

A user who peeked inside Muse discovered an interesting fact in the system logs. While using Muse for website-building tasks, the background agent was allegedly using a model named 'azure/muse-special' [[Source: Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49848095)] [[Source: Devtalk](https://devtalk.com/t/is-meta-s-muse-secretly-running-an-openai-model-mouse/250103)].

To use an analogy, it’s like buying a high-end electric car from a famous manufacturer that claims it has a 'self-developed engine,' only to pop the hood and find it full of key parts from a competitor.

According to preliminary analysis, examining the file system associated with this 'azure/muse-special' model revealed circumstantial evidence strongly suggesting it is an OpenAI model running on Microsoft's cloud platform, Azure [[Source: Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49848095)].

## How much of this is true?

Meta's official stance is clear: Muse is powered by 'MuseSpark,' an AI model developed by Meta internally [[Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/meta-debuts-its-muse-ai-agent-will-consumers-trust-it/)]. In fact, Muse runs in 'MuseSecure VM,' a dedicated, secure computer environment. It includes a specialized browser, showing great effort in ensuring the safe handling of user information [[Source: Meta, Introducing Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)].

Meta has recently unveiled advanced versions of the model, such as 'MuseSpark 1.3,' emphasizing top-tier performance in coding tasks and complex agent operations [[Source: OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/meta/muse-spark-1.3)] [[Source: Habr](https://habr.com/ru/companies/bothub/news/1078170/)]. However, despite these powerful internal technical capabilities, Meta has yet to provide an official explanation as to why traces of an OpenAI model were discovered in the logs of some users.

## What happens next?

This incident suggests that AI agents may not always operate on a single model, but could be utilizing combinations of various technologies behind the scenes to perform complex tasks. Transparency is paramount for users to trust AI. Whether Meta will clarify the relationship between 'MuseSpark' and third-party technology, or if this will be dismissed as a misunderstanding regarding logs, remains to be seen.

## MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

In the era of AI agents, 'how accurately it achieves the user's goal' is a core competitive advantage rather than just 'which model is being used.' However, if a company promotes its own model's identity at the forefront, it must raise the transparency of its internal structure accordingly to earn deep user trust. This controversy serves as a reminder that as AI technology becomes deeply integrated into daily life, the user's 'right to know' becomes increasingly important.
