Imagine this: You just took over the most successful cafe in your neighborhood. Customers line up every day, and cash is pouring in. However, for some reason, you feel extremely uneasy keeping your earnings in Korean money (the won). So every evening, when you close up shop, you convert all the won you earned that day into dollars and hide them securely in a safe. The value of the cafe keeps rising, but an odd situation unfolds where the won dries up in the neighborhood, and only dollars become scarce and valuable. 

This is exactly what is happening in the South Korean economy right now. In the past, when foreigners came to buy Korean stocks, they brought wads of dollars and converted them into won, naturally driving up the value of the won. It was common sense that when the stock market heated up, exchange rates would calm down. But that is now a thing of the past. A "cunning magic" is occurring where they buy stocks as usual, but behind the scenes, fearful that the currency's value will drop, they dump the won and hoard dollars. What on earth is going on in the foreign exchange market?

---

## Why It Matters

The reason this bizarre phenomenon is particularly painful is that it directly affects everyone's wallets. A rising exchange rate means that import prices—what we must pay to buy foreign goods—go up. When oil prices rise, delivery and transportation costs jump, and as the prices of wheat flour and various raw materials increase sequentially, our ordinary grocery bills are severely threatened. In fact, the government and foreign exchange authorities are deeply concerned that surging exchange rates could drive up import prices, increasing inflation burdens across the broader economy [1,539 Won by Day, 1,559 Won by Night... Why Do Exchange Rates Spike More at Night? | Daum | Kookmin Ilbo](https://v.daum.net/v/20260607050315992).

The situation is far more serious than one might think. Breaking through the psychological threshold of the '1,500 won exchange rate' is already placing a considerable burden on the government and financial authorities. When the foreign exchange market fluctuates this wildly, the stability of the financial market collapses, and it can snowball the anxiety of foreign investors ["Selling Korean Stocks and Buying Dollars"... Exchange Rate Stuck in 1,500s Amid Trump's Boiling Whims...](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/economy/12000047). Some even offer a terrifying forecast that the Q2 won-dollar exchange rate could head toward 1,600 won, the highest level since the past Asian Financial Crisis [Exchange Rate Heading Toward 1,600 Won... Q2 Won-Dollar at Highest Since Financial Crisis | BloomingBit](https://bloomingbit.io/feed/news/113713).

Furthermore, the recent craze for overseas stock investments by so-called 'Seohak Ants' (domestic retail investors directly investing in foreign stocks) is fierce. Driven by factors like the rise in U.S. stock prices, overseas securities investments by domestic residents have expanded by a staggering $89 billion [Goldman Sachs "Amid Weakness of the Korean Won... Tax Benefits for Individual Currency Hedging Likely to Emerge" | Seoul Economic Daily](https://www.sedaily.com/NewsView/2H0M8LASQH). $89 billion is an astronomical sum, amounting to about 120 trillion won. Because the won is not a reserve currency (a central currency for international settlements) used everywhere in the world like the dollar, you must give won to get dollars in the domestic foreign exchange market to buy U.S. stocks. With everyone desperately seeking dollars like this, there is simply no room for the exchange rate to go down.

## The Explainer

Why on earth is this happening? At the center of this phenomenon lies a financial technique called **'Currency Hedging'**. Put simply, currency hedging is 'a type of insurance that fixes future exchange rates at current levels in advance to avoid the risk of exchange rate fluctuations' [Hedged vs. Unhedged ETFs: Principles, Pros and Cons, and Investment Strategies](https://kbthink.com/etf/hedged-etf.html).

To use an analogy: Imagine you bought a 1 billion won apartment in Tokyo, Japan. You firmly believed the apartment's value would rise to 1.1 billion won in a year. As expected, the apartment's value went up, but what if the value of the yen was halved in the meantime? When you sell the apartment and convert the money back to Korean won, you end up taking a massive loss in an absurd turn of events. To prevent such unfair situations, making a solid promise with the bank when buying the apartment that "they will unconditionally exchange the money at the current exchange rate one year from now"—that is currency hedging.

In the past, foreign investors didn't bother buying this kind of insurance when purchasing Korean stocks. They firmly believed that if the stock market performed well, the value of the won would naturally rise, so they used an 'open currency risk' (currency exposure) strategy that left them exposed to exchange rate fluctuations. But now, foreigners are predicting a structural weakness in the won, expecting its value to continue dropping. Therefore, they have entirely changed their investment approach by preemptively hedging against the won's decline when buying Korean stocks, or urgently buying dollars right away [Foreigners Seeking a 'Windfall' from Exchange Rate Hikes... Turning to 'Currency Hedging' due to 'Structural Weakness of the Won'](https://v.daum.net/v/20251125163629663).

As a result, the beautiful old formula where thriving stocks and exports led to a falling exchange rate has been completely shattered [Exchange Rate Heading Toward 1,600 Won... Q2 Won-Dollar at Highest Since Financial Crisis | BloomingBit](https://bloomingbit.io/feed/news/113713). Under the old normal, if foreigners bought domestic stocks in droves, the exchange rate should fall. However, because the 'supply and demand burden' (imbalance of supply and demand)—where foreigners fiercely buy dollars to hedge their currencies while simultaneously buying stocks—is added, a contradictory phenomenon has become commonplace where exchange rates remain stagnant or even rise despite stock purchases [Stuck in the 1,470s? Won Weakens in Tandem with Weak Yen and Supply-Demand Burdens - Handsome Tax Saver's...](https://therainbowinfo.com/1470원대-고착화-엔화-약세·수급-부담에-원화-약세-동조/). In fact, recently, even on days when the KOSPI index soars and foreigners scoop up stocks, the proportion of days where the exchange rate actually drops has plummeted to the 50% range ['Vicious Cycle' of Won Weakness as Foreign Investors Increase 'Currency Hedging'](https://marketin.edaily.co.kr/News/ReadE?newsId=01420246645319688).

## Where We Stand

The current foreign exchange market is truly walking on thin ice. Despite foreign investors recently making a welcome net purchase of 300 billion won in Korean stocks, the market witnessed a bizarre phenomenon where the exchange rate, instead of stabilizing, actually closed slightly higher [Foreigners Finally Net Purchase 300 Billion Won in Korean Stocks... Exchange Rate Closes Slightly Higher](https://www.wowtv.co.kr/NewsCenter/News/Read?articleId=A202502060834). This is clear evidence that the power of funds poured into exchange rate defense or dollar purchases is much stronger than the funds coming in to buy stocks.

Adding insult to injury, global bad news has joined the fray. Volatility has reached an extreme, with the exchange rate jumping from 1,539 won during Korean daytime hours to as high as 1,559 won in overnight trading. This is because the dual blows of unstable international oil prices caused by escalating tensions in the Middle East and a global strong dollar phenomenon, where dollars are becoming scarce worldwide, hit simultaneously [1,539 Won by Day, 1,559 Won by Night... Why Do Exchange Rates Spike More at Night? | Daum | Kookmin Ilbo](https://v.daum.net/v/20260607050315992). When the sound of thunder from outside (external instability factors) grows louder, people instinctively experience an explosive psychological urge to seek the strongest bomb shelter: the 'dollar' [[Exchange Rate Forecast] The Suffering Won: Foreigner Exodus and Middle East Instability | KB Think](https://kbthink.com/investment/fx/daily/260522.html).

The sudden, bolt-from-the-blue impact of capital outflows cannot be ignored either. In the past, when an external shock hit the stock market, an outflow of 5 trillion won in foreign capital all at once triggered the fear of record-high exchange rates. When a crisis hits, investors are the first to line up to switch to the safe dollar. Paradoxically, the money that came to Korea during the good times when domestic asset values were high is now rushing out all at once, becoming a ticking time bomb for wild exchange rate swings [The Fear of Record-High Exchange Rates Brought by the 5 Trillion Won Exodus of Foreign Funds... - storytown77](https://storytown77.com/외국인-자금-5조-이탈이-불러온-역대급-고환율-공포ft/). Various economic broadcasts, including on YouTube, are also scrambling to seriously cover the emergency situation of the won, which is staggering after taking a direct hit from the largest-ever foreign stock sell-offs [Foreigners Sell 'Record High' Korean Stocks... Directly Hit Won Staggers... - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-6ILpTKHHjU).

What makes experts tremble most is the 'one-sided herding phenomenon'. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) issued a strong warning about the "possibility of a currency hedging herd," noting that the size of Korea's dollar assets exposed to currency risk is 20 times the entire domestic foreign exchange market [Korean Dollar Assets Exposed to FX Risk are 20 Times the FX Market... IMF Warns of "Currency Hedging Herd Possibility"](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20260118141303556). This is akin to the precarious sight of a small neighborhood reservoir overflowing with massive cruise ships meant for the ocean. Even the National Pension Service, which is responsible for the reliable retirement of citizens, has launched strategic currency hedging in earnest to prevent exchange rate fluctuation risks. With everyone scrambling to grab a life jacket (dollars) first, a farce is unfolding to the point where even the Bank of Korea diagnosed that "while there are plenty of foreign currency funds in the market itself, spot dollars available for immediate trading are woefully insufficient" ['Vicious Cycle' of Won Weakness as Foreign Investors Increase 'Currency Hedging'](https://marketin.edaily.co.kr/News/ReadE?newsId=01420246645319688).

## What's Next

As the situation took an unusual turn, an irate government and foreign exchange authorities urgently rolled up their sleeves. Holding an emergency review meeting, the government and the Bank of Korea issued a stern warning message that they will absolutely no longer tolerate the one-sided herding behavior of buying dollars or speculative acts aiming for superficial currency exchange gains [Gov't Holds Emergency Review Meeting on Surging Exchange Rates... "Strict Action Against Excessive Herding and Speculation"](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202606071710001). This is an effort to cool down the market's fear and anxiety through so-called verbal intervention.

However, the prescription of authority intervention cannot be a cure-all. Market experts believe that the natural breath of the market, such as a drop in oil prices or a recovery in investors' risk appetite, could softly alleviate the offshore dollar's strength. On the flip side, they cautiously analyze that if foreign exchange authorities intervene too aggressively and blatantly in the herding phenomenon, downside volatility could actually increase, potentially making the market even more frightened and anxious [[Exchange Rate Forecast] Won Shaken by Foreigners, Authorities Also Pondering Response | KB Think](https://kbthink.com/investment/fx/daily/260605.html).

Amidst this, an interesting forecast is catching the eye. Global investment bank Goldman Sachs predicted that as a clever move to defend against the persistent weakening trend of the Korean won, the government may roll out a 'tax benefit' system in the future that actively encourages general retail investors, like Seohak Ants, to engage in currency hedging [Goldman Sachs "Amid Weakness of the Korean Won... Tax Benefits for Individual Currency Hedging Likely to Emerge" | Seoul Economic Daily](https://www.sedaily.com/NewsView/2H0M8LASQH). It is a novel approach attempting to suppress the overall market demand for dollars and prevent exchange rate spikes by inducing individuals to use safe currency hedging products from financial institutions, instead of indiscriminately hoarding spot dollars directly in the foreign exchange market.

## AI's Take

The reliable formula of 'when stocks go up, the exchange rate goes down,' which had been a sturdy pillar of the South Korean economy for decades, now seems to have completely expired. As long as foreign investors firmly bet on the structural weakness of the won and thoroughly equip themselves with a 'currency hedging shield,' the fierce volatility in the foreign exchange market will continue to precariously shake our grocery prices and daily lives.

Ultimately, the answer lies in returning to the most honest basics. Beyond short-term market interventions or intimidation-style stopgap measures, the 'won' currency itself must be recognized on the global stage as an attractive and robust asset that people want to keep in their wallets. It is time to hasten a fundamental improvement of the Korean economy's constitution through technological innovation, formidable strengthening of export competitiveness, and transparent policies that investors can trust. Because, in the end, the exchange rate is the most honest report card that mirrors a nation's economic stamina.
