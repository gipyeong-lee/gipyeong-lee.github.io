Imagine this: you have an old smartwatch that has been sitting in a drawer for over a year. You paid a lot of money for it, but the features are limited and you're bored with the screen design. What if this watch could be reborn as a one-of-a-kind, stylish timepiece through just a few hours of "AI magic"?

Recently, a developer took a budget-friendly smartwatch called the "PineTime," which costs about $27, and conducted a very special experiment [Source: Hacking with Claude on a $27 Smart Watch · Mike Kasberg](https://www.mikekasberg.com/blog/2026/08/19/hacking-with-claude-on-a-27-smart-watch.html). They "hacked" the watch with the AI assistant Claude, transforming it into something entirely new [Source: Hacking with Claude on a $27 Smart Watch | Zeli](https://zeli.app/en/story/49374772).

### Why is this important?

When we think of smartwatches, we usually think of expensive products made by large corporations like Apple or Samsung. However, these devices can only be used within the limits permitted by the manufacturer. In contrast, the PineTime is a device that uses open-source firmware (the basic software that makes the device operate) [Source: Hacking with Claude on a $27 Smart Watch - Devtalk](https://devtalk.com/t/hacking-with-claude-on-a-27-smart-watch/248993).

The significance of this case lies in the fact that **"AI has broken down technological barriers."** In the past, modifying a device like this required deep, specialized knowledge of complex programming languages. Now, however, we live in a world where anyone can change a device to suit their own tastes simply by explaining their desired design to an AI and asking it to write the code.

### Simply put: AI is a reliable "technical tutor"

Let's use an analogy: think of hacking a device as "cooking." In the past, to cook, you had to learn everything yourself, from preparing ingredients to controlling the heat. But today's AI is both a top-tier chef and a kind tutor. If you just say what you want to do, it helps you step-by-step, preparing the ingredients and teaching you the recipe.

The developer used Claude and an open-weights model (AI models that are public and accessible to everyone) to work on the screen design [Source: Hacking with Claude on a $27 Smart Watch | Zeli](https://zeli.app/en/story/49374772). Using the code suggested by the AI, like putting together puzzle pieces, they completed a classic "Casio" style watch face in just a few hours [Source: Hacking with Claude on a $27 Smart Watch | Zeli](https://zeli.app/en/story/49374772). While they faced technical challenges, such as the small memory capacity of the device, they navigated them wisely through collaboration with the AI.

### Current Status: A project anyone can start

The results of this smartwatch hacking project are highly open. The developer has published an 'AGENTS.md' guide on GitHub, detailing their code and the process [Source: Hacking with Claude on a $27 Smart Watch | Zeli](https://zeli.app/en/story/49374772). This means that anyone with the motivation can now customize their own smartwatch. However, it is worth noting that unlike building a smartphone app, this type of work requires a more delicate technical approach due to the hardware's memory limitations.

### What happens next?

In the future, almost every electronic appliance we use could be transformed into "our own." It will become much easier than it is now to change the alarm sound of a rice cooker to your personal taste, or to customize the extraction method of a coffee machine. This case shows not just the customization of a watch, but the potential for consumers to reclaim control over products using AI as a tool. If you have a device gathering dust in a drawer, why not breathe new life into it with AI?

Technology is no longer the exclusive domain of experts. Just as AI tools like Claude transformed a $27 watch into a unique masterpiece, we now live in an era where anyone with an idea can become a "creative hacker" capable of designing their daily life.
