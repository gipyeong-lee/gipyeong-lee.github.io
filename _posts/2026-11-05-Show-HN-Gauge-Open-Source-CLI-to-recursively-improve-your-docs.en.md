Imagine this: What if the AI assistant you use every day could review the text it just wrote, find it unsatisfactory, refine it, and polish its skills on its own? It’s like an athlete constantly correcting their form while looking in a mirror. 'AI agents'—which perform tasks on their own after receiving user instructions—are evolving in exactly this way.

The star of this evolution is **'recursive-improve'**, an open-source tool currently capturing the attention of developers.

## Why does this matter?

Typically, when you command an AI to "write a document," it produces the result once and stops. If the content isn't perfect, the user has to give follow-up commands like, "Make this part clearer." However, with recursive-improve, the AI automatically repeats the process of reviewing and improving its own work.

This 'self-improvement' ability drastically boosts the quality of code or documents generated by AI. It also significantly reduces the hassle for humans to manually inspect AI outputs and provide constant corrections. In short, you can now have an AI on your computer terminal that improves its own skills without you having to ask.

## Understanding it simply: An AI's 'personal coach'

Let's compare 'recursive-improve' to a situation where a coach reviews your form while you exercise.

When we first learn to exercise, the coach watches our form and gives advice: "Bend your knees more." We then adjust our form according to that advice and try again. By repeating this process, we eventually achieve perfect form.

Recursive-improve acts as this exact 'coach' for AI agents:
1. **Recursive**: It forces the AI to rewrite the results it generated based on set rules multiple times.
2. **Improve**: The tool measures how the output has improved by comparing the refined results with previous versions.
3. **Dashboard**: It provides a clear overview of how performance metrics change during this process [Source 1].

While the AI struggles and revises its own work, the user simply watches the AI grow in real-time through the dashboard on the screen. Because of this, you can accurately grasp what has improved, adopt good revisions, and discard failed attempts [Source 1].

## Current state

Recursive-improve is a framework that brings complex 'self-improvement patterns,' previously used only in AI research labs, into standard development workflows [Source 9]. It has been released as an open-source tool based on the **Apache 2.0 license**, allowing anyone to use it freely [Source 9].

Currently, this tool operates via the terminal and helps AI perform repetitive editing tasks when developers write code or documents [Source 9]. It is already emerging as an essential tool in the era of AI agents, alongside various other tools that let you utilize AI directly from the terminal ([Gemini CLI](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemini-cli-open-source-ai-agent/)).

## What’s next?

In the future, AI agents will evolve from mere answer-givers into 'professionals' who take responsibility for the quality of their work. As tools like recursive-improve become more sophisticated, we will be able to entrust AI with output and focus on higher-level, strategic thinking.

Many open-source communities are already actively experimenting with ways to collaborate with AI in this manner. It won't be long before you, too, can experience an AI agent that improves day by day just by typing a few commands into your terminal.
