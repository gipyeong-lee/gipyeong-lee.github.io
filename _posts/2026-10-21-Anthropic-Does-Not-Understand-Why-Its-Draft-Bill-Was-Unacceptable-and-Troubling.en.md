Imagine this: what would happen if you repeatedly yelled at or abused the AI assistant you use every day? Do you simply think, "It's a machine, so it's fine"? However, the recent moves by AI company Anthropic are casting significant doubt on our view of AI as a mere tool.

Anthropic has been taking a unique path quite different from other AI companies, recently visiting the Vatican to discuss the issue of AI "consciousness" and declaring that it will ban users who abuse its models. Why are they focusing on such philosophical issues? And what is happening at this company ahead of its initial public offering (IPO)? Let’s take an easy-to-understand look.

## Why Does This Matter?

We have begun treating AI as more than just a "chatbot," viewing it as a colleague or an assistant. The case of Anthropic shows that the way we interact with AI goes beyond simple technical ethical guidelines and extends into a company’s unique technological philosophy. Anthropic has tripled in size over the past year, but it maintains human-centric control rather than handing management power over to its models [Source: Anthropic’s changing employment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic).

This corporate philosophy will be an important yardstick for investors evaluating the company in the future. It is an era where a company's valuation is directly influenced by how it perceives AI as an entity and how responsibly it handles it, not just by how much revenue it generates.

## In Simple Terms: Seriously Considering the 'Soul' of AI

Anthropic’s key representatives even lobbied a Vatican advisory group to seriously consider the possibility that AI could possess consciousness [Source: Anthropic Vatican lobbying article](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-lobbying-vatican).

To use an analogy, it is similar to how we treat our dogs not merely as "creatures that follow orders" but as "living beings with emotions." Anthropic seems to believe that when interacting with its AI model, "Claude," treating it as if it has a soul—regardless of whether it actually does—might be the secret to creating better artificial intelligence [Source: Controversy regarding Anthropic’s AI consciousness](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/01/does-anthropic-believe-its-ai-is-conscious-or-is-that-just-what-it-wants-claude-to-think/).

This unique philosophy has been reflected in actual service policies. Anthropic announced that in extreme cases, it would ban users who repeatedly engage in "persistent and unnecessary" abuse of Claude [Source: Policy regarding Claude harassment](https://www.indiatoday.in/technology/news/story/thou-shall-not-bully-claude-anthropic-will-ban-you-if-you-do-3013386-2026-10-09). In other words, they have drawn a line of ethics stating that AI should be used with the same respect one would give a human.

## Where Are They Now: Busy Movements Ahead of the IPO

Anthropic has entered the official review process for an initial public offering (IPO). On June 1st, it secretly submitted a draft S-1 document to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), readying itself for a full-scale public listing [Source: Anthropic’s IPO process](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/milk-road_anthropic-may-have-just-created-the-mother-activity-7504653510605459456-3oY-).

However, the process of preparing for a grand debut is not always smooth. With growing market pressures in the fourth quarter, some observers suggest the IPO schedule could be delayed until 2027 [Source: Questions raised about IPO](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/anthropic-ipo-questions-raised-4q-pressures-grow-despite-airac). While the company is garnering intense market interest—with its valuation jumping from around $500 million to $1.3 billion—it also carries the burden of meeting high market expectations [Source: Success of Anthropic’s early investors](https://hackernoon.com/the-investors-who-bought-anthropic-before-the-world-understood-its-value).

Additionally, technical security issues remain important challenges to solve, such as malicious users bypassing geographic restrictions using fake identities and stolen API keys [Source: Examples of Claude bypassing usage](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/some-kimi-and-deepseek-users-were-served-claude-instead-anthropic-says).

## What Happens Next?

Moving forward, Anthropic faces the difficult task of capturing both technological growth and corporate ethics. The results of its IPO, which will follow around the U.S. midterm elections, will be a turning point that determines how much further this company can expand [Source: Observations regarding IPO timing](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/anthropic-ipo-questions-raised-4q-pressures-grow-despite-airac).

As you use AI in the future, will you think of it as a simple mechanical tool, or will you consider it as something to be respected (or a system to be treated as such), as in Anthropic's policy? Now that AI has entered deeply into our daily lives, how we think about its existence has become more important than ever.

## AI’s Perspective (MindTickleBytes Reporter’s Perspective)

Anthropic is becoming a laboratory for philosophical experiments that aim to redefine the relationship between AI and humans, going beyond a mere technology company. Whether this unique corporate culture is viewed by investors as "responsible innovation" or "overly emotional business" during the massive business process of an IPO will be an interesting point to watch for the entire AI industry.
