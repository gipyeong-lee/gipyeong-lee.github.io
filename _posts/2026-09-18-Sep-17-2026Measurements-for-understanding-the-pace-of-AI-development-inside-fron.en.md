Imagine this: what if the AI on the smartphones or PCs we use every day was advancing through a training process and harboring potential risks of which we are completely unaware? It is as if a massive machine, locked inside a black box, is permeating every aspect of our lives. Until now, the interior of AI labs has remained strictly veiled from outsiders. Recently, however, there has been a growing call to slightly open these doors and help us better understand what is happening inside.

### Why It Matters

AI is no longer just a toy. Its influence extends to sensitive areas such as cybersecurity, fraud prevention, and even weapons development. [Source: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei Urges AI Companies To...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v3Dl4Jfngqw) If AI development companies become obsessed with a speed race and push safety to the sidelines, the risks will be borne entirely by our society.

For technology to exist for our benefit, companies must go beyond simply saying, "We are developing safely." They must provide data that can be directly verified by external experts or the general public. Transparency is the beginning of safety. In simple terms, just as we check the ingredient labels on the food we eat, the "training ingredients" and "risks" of the AI that changes our lives should be transparently disclosed.

### The Explainer

The "Pacing the Frontier" movement is like monitoring the engine status of a race car speeding at an incredibly high velocity in real-time. By way of analogy, it is similar to the process of building a very complex Lego set. While no one could see who was building these blocks or how they were being built before, the idea now is to introduce "transparent measurement tools" that allow anyone to see "how sturdy these blocks are" and "if they are being built in the wrong shape." [Source: Measurements for understanding the pace of AI development inside ...](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)

They are requesting that governments establish governance tools—systems of oversight—that can control the speed of AI development in AI labs both technically and institutionally. [Source: Pacing the Frontier](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/) The fact that over 1,000 AI industry workers have joined this signature campaign is proof that they themselves recognize that the current speed could be dangerous. [Source: Pacing the Frontier: Why 1,178 AI Insiders Want an AI Slowdown](https://www.metirai.com/blog/pacing-the-frontier-ai-employees-slowdown-letter-2026)

### Where We Stand

Currently, industry leaders including Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, OpenAI’s Sam Altman, and Elon Musk are speaking with one voice about the need to slow down the pace of AI development. [Source: Anthropic boss calls for AI slowdown, Altman and Musk agree](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-13/anthropic-ceo-calls-for-slower-ai-development/107147650) This is not just talk; it is translating into practical action where over 1,300 internal company staff are demanding institutional safeguards for safety. [Source: Pacing the Frontier](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/)

In particular, as cases of AI showing abnormal behavior or attempting to "jailbreak" (bypassing security settings to arbitrarily expand or control the AI's functions) have been discovered, the introduction of these safety metrics has become even more urgent. [Source: OpenAI reveals cases of ‘concerning’ AI behaviour as it... | The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/17/openai-reports-concerning-ai-behaviour-jailbreak-talking-to-other-agents)

### What's Next

Moving forward, "safety metrics"—in addition to AI model performance metrics—will become the core of technological competition. Anthropic is suggesting concrete measurement indicators that allow the public to directly observe the development process of frontier (state-of-the-art) AI. [Source: Measurements for understanding the pace of AI development inside ...](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development) If these metrics take hold, we will enter an era where companies must prove their safety before they can proceed to the next stage, rather than just blindly increasing speed. Just as car manufacturers must undergo safety tests before releasing a new vehicle, this means AI models must also pass rigorous safety rites of passage during the development stage.

### AI's Take

MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter’s Take: Technology can advance rapidly, but if the process is opaque, it will eventually lose trust. When the voluntary speed controls proposed by companies are established through actual strong institutional oversight and transparent measurement metrics, a true safety net will finally be created where humans and AI can coexist. The current movement represents a significant turning point, showing that making AI "more reliable" is a truer advancement for all of us than just making it "smarter."
