## Why Does This Matter?

The AI chatbots and generative models we use daily have grown smarter as they grew larger. It was previously necessary to astronomically increase the number of adjustable internal connection networks—parameters (the unit representing the size of an AI model's neural network connections)—to 2 trillion or 3 trillion to mimic complex human reasoning abilities. However, as models grew massive, server maintenance costs skyrocketed, speeds slowed down for ordinary users, and side effects like exorbitant power consumption emerged. It was akin to paying astronomical heating bills every month just to maintain a giant mansion.

GLM-5.4, released by Z.ai, is drawing attention because it directly breaks through this 'limit of scale' [Source 1, Source 7]. Instead of blindly increasing parameter counts physically, it adopts a method where **existing brain structures are passed through twice to encourage deep thinking** [Source 6, Source 7]. This maintains a relatively modest physical size while unleashing advanced intelligence rivaling colossal monster models of 2T to 3T parameters [Source 1, Source 6].

In addition, stepping away from the previous text-only generation, this model has acquired vision (image input capabilities), opening pathways for both developers and the general public to access everyday creative work much more cheaply and powerfully [Source 10, Source 11]. With reduced cost burdens and drastically improved performance, this upgrade can be called a 21st-century milestone that accelerates the mass adoption of AI services [Source 5, Source 10]. This carries profound significance, rewriting the efficiency standards for the future of AI beyond just a simple model update.

## Easy Understanding: 'Loop Transformer' and 'Generalized Coding'

So, what exactly is GLM-5.4's core technology, the **Loop Transformer (an architecture that induces deep thinking by cyclically repeating the model's neural network layers)**? Let us explain with an easy-to-understand analogy.

> **An Easy Analogy:**
> When an ordinary AI reads a difficult mystery novel, it quickly skims the pages once and tries to guess the culprit right away. If the book is too thick (few parameters), it easily misses contextual nuances and points the finger at the wrong suspect. Therefore, until now, methods tried to solve problems by making a single book 10 times thicker.
> 
> On the other hand, GLM-5.4's 'Loop Transformer' reads through the pages once, and then **meticulously reviews and revisits the most crucial chapters (Layers, neural network processing stages) from the beginning again**. By looping through 28 core layers twice (Loops 28 layers twice at 750B scale), it corrects missed clues and logical errors on its own [Source 5, Source 6]. Without needing to build a physically massive library, it becomes a master detective through clever reading methods.

Another concept to note is **Generalized Coding (a concept extending code from a programming language into a universal tool controlling video, games, 3D design, etc.)** newly introduced by Z.ai [Source 1, Source 7].

> **Simply Put:**
> Until now, 'coding' was considered the exclusive domain of programmers typing English commands (`if`, `else`, `print`, etc.) on a black screen to build programs. However, to GLM-5.4, coding is not merely a software production tool. After envisioning what should appear on screen, the AI writes sophisticated **code** to render video files inside the computer, assemble interactive games, and directly draw 3D solid models and mechanical blueprints [Source 7, Source 13]. In other words, code has evolved into an 'all-purpose digital language' connecting humans and computers.

Ultimately, these two innovations serve as sturdy wings helping AI think deeper with fewer resources and handle a wider range of creative works.

## Current Status: What Has Changed and How?

According to blog specifications and official announcements, GLM-5.4 stands on a technically robust foundation [Source 2].

* **Overwhelmingly Efficient Loop Structure:** Adopts a design that executes 28 layers twice based on an approximate 75B (75B/750B) parameter scale [Source 2, Source 5, Source 11]. Thanks to this innovative recurrent structure, it delivers intelligence comparable to massive models reaching 2T to 3T parameters [Source 1, Source 6].
* **Integration of Visual Intelligence:** Unlike its predecessor, GLM-5.3, which was a 'text-in, text-out' model capable of handling text only, GLM-5.4 introduces visual capabilities fully into its main coding plan model, effortlessly handling image recognition and multimodal tasks (the ability to simultaneously process multiple types of information such as text and images) [Source 10, Source 11].
* **Massive Memory Capacity:** Supports an expansive context window (the maximum range of conversation and documents an AI can remember and understand) of up to 1 million tokens (the unit of text and data fragments an AI can process at once), preventing it from losing track even when entire long research papers or massive source code bases are fed in [Source 11].
* **Cost-Effectiveness and Pricing:** Already available to users via Z.ai Chat and GLM Coding Plan subscription services, with subscriptions starting at $18/month [Source 5, Source 11]. Notably, coding plan credits consumed during output generation dropped significantly from 24 credits in the previous GLM-5.3 to 16 credits, enabling high-performance AI at a lower cost [Source 5]. Product Lead Zixuan Li spread news of a 50% quota boosting for coding plans via X (formerly Twitter), garnering enthusiastic responses from developers [Source 2, Source 10].

However, the fact that official public benchmarks (performance evaluation metrics) and open weights (an open policy releasing internal model weight files) have not yet been released at launch implies that thorough, real-world user validation is still required [Source 5]. Although servers experienced slight overloads on launch day due to explosive interest [Source 15], market expectations show no signs of cooling down. Users maintain high interest in how much this massive efficiency will be felt in real-world workflows.
