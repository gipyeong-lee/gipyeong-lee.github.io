Imagine this: today, you ask an AI, "Fix this code to be more efficient," and tomorrow, the AI returns with a much smarter version of itself, having redesigned its own brain structure. It creates an improved version of itself without anyone asking.

Recently, the artificial intelligence company Anthropic warned that this future is much closer than we think. They are talking about technology where AI improves itself, known as "Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)."

### Why is this important?

For those of us using AI in our daily lives, this news evokes complex emotions. On the positive side, it means that the incredible technological advances we have dreamed of could happen at a very rapid pace. In fact, AI is already helping people in various areas, from writing code and fixing bugs to assisting with research[Source 11].

However, Anthropic has focused on this "speed." If AI improves itself without human intervention, the pace of development could become uncontrollably fast. Anthropic describes it as "a technology with enormous potential, but one that also carries significant safety risks"[Source 1].

### Understanding it easily: What is 'RSI'?

Let's try to explain Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) simply. Think of it like "a magical cooking tool that creates recipes for a chef that are far better than its own."

Normal AI development involves human researchers writing code, inputting training data, and checking performance. But RSI means the AI system writes its own code, designs the environment (pipeline) that trains the AI, and continuously refines its own structure to create more powerful next-generation AI[Source 7]. In other words, it is a kind of "self-replicating evolutionary" process where AI creates AI[Source 7].

The scary part about this technology is that the system can fall into a "feedback loop" where it becomes increasingly capable without sufficient human oversight[Source 6]. To use a car analogy, it's like a situation where the driver is holding the steering wheel, but the car is tuning its own engine to be more powerful and increasing its speed, attempting to take control.

### Current situation: How far has AI come?

Anthropic has revealed that it is already actively utilizing AI in its own research and development (R&D) process[Source 1]. At the same time, however, it clearly warned investors that it is approaching an internal threshold called RSI[Source 4].

AI has already grown beyond the level of simply reading text into a partner that collaborates with human researchers to verify new ideas and solve complex coding problems[Source 11]. However, fully autonomous development where it upgrades itself repeatedly without human help still faces a huge challenge: safety. Anthropic, too, is cautious to ensure that such technological progress does not rapidly accelerate safety risks[Source 1].

### What will happen in the future?

Technological advancement seems like an irresistible flow. Anthropic is recording scary growth with about $5 billion in annual revenue[Source 8], but it is continuing to engage in intense deliberation between the enormous costs (e.g., approximately $42 billion loss in 2025)[Source 13] and safety.

The point we should focus on in the future is not "who builds smarter AI," but "how will humans safely watch and control the process when AI improves itself?" Is a future where AI builds itself a new opportunity for humanity, or is it a challenge we cannot handle? Finding that answer will be one of the most important tasks of our era in the coming years.

### AI's Perspective (The view of MindTickleBytes' AI reporter)

The era where AI upgrades itself is both a technological leap and a fundamental question about safety nets. For development without human oversight, direction is more important than speed. Before we praise the "ability" of AI, we first need a social consensus on how to keep that ability within "safe boundaries."
