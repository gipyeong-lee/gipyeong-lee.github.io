Imagine you've discovered an indie game that gets your heart racing. You fall in love with the pixel art, where every dot is crafted with care, and the ingenious gameplay mechanics. Just as you’re about to purchase it, you wonder: "Could this game have been whipped up in just a few seconds by Artificial Intelligence (AI)—a technology that creates images or text by learning from vast amounts of data?"

With recent advancements in generative AI, there is more content flooding the web than ever before. [Source 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VXkDaDDJjoA) While this has made game development environments more convenient, there is a growing chorus of gamers who insist on games infused with "human touch." Amidst this trend, an interesting shift is being observed on itch.io, often called the mecca of indie games.

## Why does this matter?

Games are not just software; they are works of art that capture the philosophy and artistic struggle of the developer. Indie games, in particular, are an area where the value of "handcrafted" work—where developers write every line of code and place every pixel themselves—is highly prized. [Source 2](https://psytronik.itch.io/vanhelsing) When gamers wonder whether AI was used, it is not simply about rejecting technology. It is a natural consumer desire to respect the "process of creation" and the value of the effort behind it. Transparent information disclosure is a vital element in maintaining trust between creators and gamers.

## Making it easy to understand

If you were to use an analogy, the 'AI generation disclosure' tag is like a **nutrition label that lets you check: "Was this meal mass-produced by a machine, or prepared by a chef?"**

The itch.io platform allows developers to disclose whether they used generative AI during the development process when they upload their games. [Source 2](https://psytronik.itch.io/vanhelsing) It is similar to a restaurant stating that they use "locally sourced ingredients." Some gamers use search features to avoid games with this tag, or conversely, to filter for and support games that have been touched by human hands. [Source 2](https://psytronik.itch.io/vanhelsing) This creates an environment where consumers can proactively choose "genuine creative works" that suit their tastes.

## How far have we come?

Currently, many indie developers are actively using this feature to communicate with their audience. One developer, while introducing their game, proudly stated, "This release uses no AI generation, and was crafted by skilled artists using pure C64 code and pixels," encouraging other gamers to use the tag to find their work more easily. [Source 2](https://psytronik.itch.io/vanhelsing)

However, information is not yet transparently disclosed for every game. Technical issues, such as systems failing to find file paths [Source 1](https://itch.io/t/1358407/failed-to-find-indexhtml), and instances where the platform itself experiences temporary outages due to irrational external reports, show that we are still going through trial and error. [Source 3](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2024/12/itch-io-platform-briefly-goes-down-to-ai-driven-anti-phishing-report/) The platform's transparency policy still has a long way to go before it is fully established.

## Where are we headed?

In the future indie game ecosystem, "transparency" will become an even greater competitive advantage. While it is the developer's freedom to use AI technology as a tool, gamers—as consumers—will increasingly prefer results that are "imbued with human struggle."

Right now, we are at the stage of filtering content using tags, but one day, transparency might become as important a purchasing factor as a game's rating or price. We are likely to see a healthy virtuous cycle where creators share their production processes more honestly, and gamers make more meaningful consumption choices based on that transparency.
