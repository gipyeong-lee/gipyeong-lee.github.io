Imagine this: You have worked hard to start a restaurant, and large franchise corporations that already dominate the market demand that the government "significantly tighten the standards for opening new restaurants." While they claim it is "for the sake of food safety," what if their underlying calculation is actually to prevent new competitors from entering the market?

A similar debate is currently raging in the Artificial Intelligence (AI) industry. Leading companies like OpenAI and Anthropic are calling on the government to slow down or manage the pace of AI development. David Sacks, who served as the White House AI and Cryptocurrency Czar, has raised strong questions about these demands.

## Why Does This Matter?

AI technology is now moving beyond mere lab results and deeply embedding itself into our daily lives and industries across the board. On the surface, it may seem like a responsible act for core AI developers to argue that "the pace must be slowed down." However, beneath this surface lies a complex problem involving not only the safety of the technology but also the competitive structure of the market.

In simple terms, if the government were to enact very strict regulations based on these requests, what would happen? Large corporations that already possess vast capital and manpower can afford to meet these regulatory standards, but startups or latecomers just starting out would find it difficult to even enter the market due to the high regulatory barriers. This could ultimately lead to the monopolization of technological innovation by a specific few companies [Source 11, Source 13].

## Analogy: The 'Regulatory Capture' Strategy

David Sacks explains this situation using the political and economic term "regulatory capture." To use a simple analogy: imagine a lord of a castle who has already built high walls, requesting the state to "make the rules for building castle walls much harder from now on." The large company with the already-high walls can easily meet the new rules, but those attempting to build a new castle would be unable to even come near the area because of the complicated new regulations.

Sacks points out, "If companies want to slow down their development for the sake of their own safety, they can do it themselves without needing government help." His criticism questions why they seek to use the power of the government to force the same pace onto their competitors [Source 16, Source 8]. Regarding Anthropic's call for an AI pause, Sacks lashed out, stating it "is essentially inviting nationalization" [Source 7].

## Current Situation

OpenAI and Anthropic are currently supporting statements calling for the government to provide tools to regulate the speed of frontier AI development [Source 5]. They argue that the current regulatory framework fails to keep up with the speed of technological progress and that the government must intervene to drive international cooperation [Source 6].

On the other hand, David Sacks, Chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, views these claims by companies as an pursuit of strategic profit hidden behind the name of "safety" [Source 3, Source 11]. He maintains the position that if companies have the courage to stop themselves, they should simply do so, and they should not use government regulation to bind their competitors [Source 16].

## What Will Happen Next?

The government is facing a deep dilemma between the fear that AI technology might surpass humans and the desire to continue technological innovation [Source 14]. Sacks' criticism is expected to intensify public and media scrutiny regarding what kind of regulatory framework the government will build, and whether it is truly for the safety of humanity or to protect the interests of specific companies.

The point we need to focus on going forward is not the question of "who decides the speed of AI," but the more difficult task of "how to ensure fair competition while securing safety for humanity" [Source 12].

## AI's Take

As important as the pace of technological development is the environment of fair competition in which that technology develops. It is a time for meticulous and transparent discussion among tech companies, the government, and our society to ensure that safety regulations do not paradoxically become barriers that stifle innovation.
