Imagine this: What if an AI could solve in just 21 hours a task that would take researchers months of work in a complex scientific laboratory? The sight of an AI solving a complex biological puzzle that humanity has struggled with for decades feels like something straight out of a science fiction movie. Recent news from the AI company Anthropic makes us feel that this future is rapidly approaching. The [report that Anthropic’s AI model made a significant discovery in the lab](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/anthropic-says-its-biology-lab-has-already-found-something-big/) has sent ripples through the entire scientific community.

Anthropic revealed that it operates its own "wet biology lab" in the Bay Area. This is a cutting-edge space designed not just for analyzing information in a database, but for AI models to plan and perform physical experiments themselves. [Anthropic’s lab uses AI models to conduct physical experiments](https://techbeat.co/story/anthropic-says-claude-found-crispr-like-enzyme-system-in-21-hours).

## Why does this matter?

The core of this discovery is that it proves AI can go beyond the role of a simple "knowledge summarizer" or "chatbot" and become a **"primary agent of scientific inquiry."** Until now, AI has mostly functioned at a level where it learns from existing papers to summarize answers; now, it is entering a phase where it understands the laboratory environment and finds practical scientific clues in the physical world.

This is important to the general public because of the speed of scientific and technological advancement. Challenges that could fundamentally change human life, such as gene editing or new drug development, could be drastically accelerated with the help of AI. Although still in the early stages, the very fact that it found a new enzyme we were unaware of offers hope that a new horizon is opening in biotechnology. [This discovery shows that AI can participate meaningfully in scientific inquiry](https://superintelligencenews.com/ai-fields/large-language-models/claude-discovery-anthropic-biolab/).

## Easy to understand: The 'DNA editing tool' found by Claude

What the AI model 'Claude' found is a so-called 'enzyme system.' To use an easy analogy, the DNA in our bodies or in viruses is like a massive "instruction manual" containing the blueprint of life. Here, enzymes act as 'editors' that cut, remove, or modify specific parts of this manual.

The enzyme discovered this time appears to have functions very similar to **CRISPR (a technology widely known as gene editing that allows genes to be corrected as desired)**. [This system has the ability to cut, copy, and paste DNA](https://superintelligencenews.com/ai-fields/large-language-models/ai-biology-lab-anthropic-claude-enzyme-discovery/).

In short, Claude found a highly sophisticated 'editing tool' within the vast DNA manual of a bacteriophage (a virus that eats bacteria), which spans tens of thousands of pages. Researchers didn't even know what this tool existed for, but Claude analyzed the data and accurately identified this unknown system in just 21 hours. [Claude identified this system in 21 hours](https://techbeat.co/story/anthropic-says-claude-found-crispr-like-enzyme-system-in-21-hours).

## Current situation: We know 'what', but not 'how'

However, we need to watch the situation calmly when we hear this news. Although Anthropic announced that it "made a significant discovery," [it is still unclear what biological function this enzyme system actually performs or why it exists](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/anthropic-says-its-biology-lab-has-already-found-something-big/).

Even Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei honestly admitted that [nobody yet knows what this system actually does](https://decrypt.co/379128/anthropic-claude-found-something-big-dna-doesnt-know-what). Furthermore, this discovery has not yet been published as a formal paper that has gone through peer review (the scientific community’s mutual verification process), and verification by independent third-party researchers has not taken place either. [Therefore, its scientific importance and concrete evidence remain uncertain](https://yougonews.com/science/anthropic-says-ai-helped-its-biology-lab-make-a-big-discovery/).

## What happens next?

The next steps will be a time of verifying whether this discovery can be usefully applied in the actual field of life sciences. Anthropic is expected to characterize the discovered enzyme and confirm how it differs from existing biological understanding. [Anthropic plans to examine the discovered protein in more detail in the lab](https://www.the-independent.com/tech/claude-anthropic-biology-dna-crispr-b3056156.html).

If this trend continues, AI may soon go beyond being a 'research assistant' in the lab and start performing the role of 'lead researcher.' [Claude is already contributing increasingly to research and development processes](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/6167945-anthropic-says-its-model-claude-is-helping-to-build-the-next-version-of-itself).
