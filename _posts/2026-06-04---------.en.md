## Introduction: The Invisible Wave Threatening Our Wallets

Imagine this. You've been saving up for months for the overseas trip you've dreamed of for a long time. On the morning you're finally about to pay for your flights and hotel, news breaks that the exchange rate has skyrocketed. Overnight, your travel expenses have jumped 10% or 20%. A trip that could have cost 1 million won has magically, but unpleasantly, turned into 1.2 million won. 

Or consider a local cafe owner. The price of coffee beans imported from overseas shoots up overnight, leaving them wide awake wondering if they should raise the price of an Americano by 500 won starting tomorrow. 

Like this, the exchange rate (the ratio at which two countries' currencies are exchanged) is not just a dry, boring number in the economic section of the evening news. It is like a massive wave that controls everything from our daily grocery prices to corporate survival and the entire national economy. Gentle waves allow a ship to sail peacefully, but a sudden tsunami can sweep everything away. 

Recently, as these waves showed signs of unusual turbulence, the government, which acts as the helmsman of our economy, issued a strong warning message. Koo Yun-cheol, Deputy Prime Minister for Economic Affairs (the top official overseeing national economic policy) and Minister of Finance and Economy (the government department in charge of national finance and economic policy), recently expressed a strong willingness to intervene in the market regarding the unusual expansion of volatility in the foreign exchange market, stating, "We will immediately take necessary measures against excessive herd behavior" <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol "Immediate response to excessive herd behavior"… vigilance against expanded exchange rate volatility](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260604_0003655855)]</sup> <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol "Immediate action in case of excessive exchange rate herd behavior"… strengthened vigilance in the foreign exchange market](https://www.msn.com/ko-kr/news/national/ebs-6월-모평-국어-작년-수능보다-쉬워-작년-6월과-유사/ar-AA24MJpb)]</sup>. 

What exactly is happening in the economic market right now that has prompted the government to show such high vigilance? From a layperson's perspective, I will explain the true meaning of this complex economic situation and the government's statements in an easy and detailed manner.

## Why It Matters

Just as we normally worry about living costs when looking at the price tag of an apple or a carton of milk at the supermarket, there are moments when a massive entity like a nation must worry about the value of its money. If the value of South Korea's currency, the 'won,' rises or falls too quickly compared to the 'dollar,' the global standard currency, the entire national economy suffers from severe motion sickness. Simply put, if the value of money fluctuates wildly, neither exporting companies nor importing companies can plan for tomorrow.

The word Deputy Prime Minister Koo Yun-cheol mentioned with the most concern is **'herd behavior (or market bias, a phenomenon where investors, driven by anxiety, concentrate their trades in one direction all at once, causing the market to lose its balance).'** 

To use an analogy, it's like a large cruise ship. On a massive cruise ship floating in the middle of the ocean, if passengers are freely scattered across different floors and rooms, the ship maintains its balance and sails safely. But suppose a loud explosion is heard from outside the ship or a terrifying school of sharks appears. What happens if hundreds of terrified passengers suddenly flock to the right side of the ship to survive? The ship will suddenly tilt sharply to the right and risk sinking.

The foreign exchange market (a massive financial market where different currencies are bought and sold) operates exactly the same way. When shocking news comes from the outside, investors are gripped by fear and rush to buy the asset they believe is the safest on Earth (usually the US dollar). With everyone wanting only dollars and trying to sell off the won, the won's value plummets and the dollar's value skyrockets uncontrollably, creating an extreme tilt, or 'herd behavior.'

To prevent such a disastrous situation, Deputy PM Koo emphasized, "In a situation of high external uncertainty, we are keeping a close watch with a high sense of vigilance to ensure that anxiety does not spread" <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol immediate action in case of excessive exchange rate herd behavior… strengthened vigilance in the foreign exchange market | Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026060466047)]</sup>. The key phrase here is **'anxiety.'** People often think of the economy as the realm of complex math or statistics, but in reality, the economy is much more frequently governed by people's 'psychology.' When the panic that "exchange rates are going to skyrocket!" begins to spread through the market, that psychology itself becomes the cause, creating a terrifying vicious cycle that actually drives up exchange rates. The government is trying to strongly block this psychological panic from spreading like a virus early on.

## The Explainer: Why Did the Storm Suddenly Arrive?

So, what exactly happened in the outside world that caused passengers (investors) to be so extremely anxious and show signs of flocking to one side of the ship? The cause that shook our sturdy sailing ship was essentially a perfect storm of two major global events and one specific domestic situation occurring simultaneously.

### 1. The First Wave: Explosion of Geopolitical Conflict Between the U.S. and Iran
The most shocking event that recently froze global financial markets was the armed conflict in the Middle East. In early June, an unprecedented event occurred when Iran launched an airstrike on Kuwait Airport <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol “Close watch on exchange rates, immediate response to excessive herd behavior”... Won/Dollar exchange rate 15...](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10763300)]</sup>. The Middle East is like a massive 'oil warehouse' responsible for the world's energy. When the dark shadow of war looms over this region, with missiles flying and bombs exploding, the global economy becomes extremely tense. 

When extreme risk situations like war strike, major global investors start selling off highly volatile risky assets like stocks or currencies of emerging countries. And they fiercely hoard only one thing—the 'US dollar,' the safest money on Earth that won't become scrap paper no matter what crisis hits—just like panic-buying emergency rations for war. As a result, the value of our money (the won) drops sharply, and the dollar's value skyrockets, putting immense pressure on the exchange rate.

### 2. The Second Wave: America Raising Trade Barriers
Around the same time, unsettling news also arrived from across the ocean in the United States. The Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR, the core government agency overseeing US trade negotiations and policy) suddenly announced a plan to impose additional tariffs <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol “Close watch on exchange rates, immediate response to excessive herd behavior”... Won/Dollar exchange rate 15...](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10763300)]</sup>. 

Tariffs are taxes levied on imported goods. The US declaring high taxes on imported goods is simply equivalent to saying, "We will now make other countries' goods expensive so we won't buy them, and we will only use goods made within our country." A country like South Korea, which makes a living by exporting cars and semiconductors, is bound to take a direct hit when the US raises trade barriers like this. If export paths narrow, it is obvious that the fundamental strength of the Korean economy will weaken, and investors, expecting the Korean economy to become unstable, will rush to dump Korean money and switch to dollars. This is also a very powerful factor causing exchange rates to soar.

### 3. The Empty Breakwater: Market Closure Due to June 3 Local Elections
While house-sized storms were raging outside, unfortunately, our home base, which was supposed to defend against the storm and absorb the shock, was temporarily closed. June 3rd was the day of the nationwide June 3 local elections, and as a result, the regular foreign exchange market in Seoul was also closed for the holiday <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol “Close watch on exchange rates, immediate response to excessive herd behavior”... Won/Dollar exchange rate 15...](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10763300)]</sup>.

Imagine this. There is a massive dam protecting an entire village, but on a national holiday, everyone who manages the dam has locked up and gone home. However, on that very night, an unprecedented, massive torrential downpour (the Middle East bombing and the US tariff bomb) pours down upstream of the dam. 

While the Seoul foreign exchange market was closed and the domestic defense system was halted, an absolute emergency occurred in the offshore market (a market where our money is traded in overseas financial hubs, rather than domestically), which operates 24/7 regardless of Korean time. Just as the water level rises rapidly in the absence of a dam manager, in overseas markets, without a buffer zone to protect the value of Korean money, panic snowballed, and exchange rates shook violently.

## Where We Stand: The Giant Water Cannon Held by the Firefighter

Amidst this 'perfect storm' (the worst economic situation where multiple bad events overlap at once) of military conflict in the Middle East, US trade barriers, and the temporary closure of the domestic market, the economic firefighter, Deputy Prime Minister Koo Yun-cheol, stepped up to prevent the exchange rate from uncontrollably collapsing off a cliff in one direction. 

His statement, made with a stern expression, that "we will immediately take necessary measures against excessive herd behavior," is called a **'verbal intervention'** in foreign exchange market terminology <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol immediate action in case of excessive exchange rate herd behavior… strengthened vigilance in the foreign exchange market | Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026060466047)]</sup> <sup>[[Koo Yun-cheol "Immediate response to excessive herd behavior"… vigilance against expanded exchange rate volatility](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260604_0003655855)]</sup>. 

To use an analogy: It is like a police officer firing a loud warning shot into the air and sternly warning, "If you move any further, I'll really shoot!" before actually pulling the trigger with live ammunition to catch a vicious thief. It shows a firm determination to forces fueling exchange rate hikes for speculative purposes in the market, or people engaging in blind dollar hoarding out of excessive fear, that "the South Korean government will mobilize massive financial resources to normalize the market's direction, even by force."

The 'necessary measures (live ammunition)' the Deputy PM refers to here mean the government pouring massive amounts of dollars (foreign exchange reserves) stockpiled in its vault for emergencies directly into the market. When there are too many people trying to buy dollars and the dollar price skyrockets, if the massive warehouse called the government suddenly releases billions of dollars into the market, the scarcity of dollars decreases, and the skyrocketing exchange rate magically calms down. 

This is like a sturdy **shock absorber** attached to a car's wheels. When a car is running, it is perfectly natural for the body to shake slightly due to uneven roads. In a market economy, it is healthy and natural for exchange rates to go up and down slightly every day. However, when you suddenly hit a giant sinkhole (geopolitical crisis), if a powerful shock absorber called the government doesn't kick in on time, the car can flip over completely. The government has clearly declared its principle that it will not interfere with the natural movements of the market, but will only intervene when an extreme 'herd behavior' that threatens to completely ruin the market appears.

Of course, the key to a fundamental resolution lies in global issues occurring outside our country's market. ## AI's Take

The core of this situation, as seen by MindTickleBytes' AI reporter, lies not in numbers but in the 'human mind.' Vague market anxiety can threaten the economic ecosystem much faster and more violently than the speed at which visible economic indicators deteriorate. 

As seen in countless economic crises in the past, fear itself is the most dangerous economic epidemic. It is common for the anxiety of one person to infect two, eventually throwing the entire market into panic and causing even perfectly healthy companies to go bankrupt. Therefore, the government's recent 'verbal intervention' appears to be a kind of **'economic psychological quarantine'** and an essential reassuring measure for the public and investors, rather than just a warning to frighten the market. We hope this wise measure of setting up a firewall before the embers of anxiety spread into a massive fire will successfully soothe the market.
