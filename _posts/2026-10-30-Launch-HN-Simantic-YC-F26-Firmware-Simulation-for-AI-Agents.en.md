Imagine this: You wake up in the morning and tell your AI agent, "Design a custom small robot and make it work." Previously, to create the 'firmware' (the basic software that controls hardware) that acts as the robot's brain, an engineer would have had to purchase circuit boards, perform countless tests, and endure the arduous process of loading code onto physical devices. Now, an era is coming where you can complete this entire process in the digital world without needing physical equipment.

Simantic, a startup recently selected for the Y Combinator Fall 2026 batch, is at the center of this change. [Source 1](https://foxplug.com/launch/simantic) [Source 6](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/simantic)

### Why is this important?

The embedded and firmware industry is the foundation of modern technology. From electric vehicles and smart appliances to robots, most of the electronic products we use are governed by this firmware. However, traditional hardware design has faced a major barrier: it is difficult to even test without a 'physical device.' This is because, until the physical device is ready, it is impossible to verify if the software code is functioning properly. [Source 4](https://lemstudio.co/yc-companies/simantic-33838)

Simantic is accelerating the 'era of AI designing hardware' by solving these problems. By allowing firmware to be executed and tested in a virtual environment without the need for physical hardware boards, it has dramatically increased product development speed and reduced costs. [Source 1](https://foxplug.com/launch/simantic) [Source 8](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/simantic)

### Making it easy to understand

Let's compare Simantic's technology to a 'filter' in a photo editing app.

In the past, to change the color tone of a photo, you had to develop the photo and re-take it, or go through complex chemical development processes. But now, you can just tap a filter button in an app and see in advance how the photo will change in a virtual environment, right?

Simantic is similar. Even without an engineer soldering circuit boards (boards) and connecting wires, you can use the virtual simulation environment provided by Simantic to pull up a 'digital board' and test whether the firmware is working well. [Source 11](https://simantic.dev/docs) It's like a developer assembling a robot in a virtual game space and immediately checking how their software reacts on a real device.

### Current Status

Founded in 2025, Simantic is currently a small team of four, but their background is powerful. The co-founders are veterans who directly developed electric vehicles, AI chips, and humanoid robots at global hardware technology companies such as Tesla, Neuralink, and Simbe Robotics. [Source 2](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shong0504_y-combinators-fall-2026-batch-starts-next-activity-7503961062501666816-49HE) [Source 3](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/simantic/jobs) [Source 8](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/simantic)

Currently, Simantic provides a firmware simulation environment targeting microcontroller units (MCUs, ultra-compact computer chips) like the STM32, and users can execute their code without physical boards through a dedicated CLI (command-line interface) tool. [Source 11](https://simantic.dev/docs)

### What will happen in the future?

Hardware engineering will be the biggest battlefield for AI. Once solutions like Simantic become universal, individual developers or small teams will be able to design innovative robots or smart devices without needing tens of millions of won in experimental equipment. The world is approaching where AI agents will test tens of thousands of hardware combinations on virtual boards overnight, and present optimized blueprints to human engineers in the morning.
