It has become a part of our daily lives to ask our smartphone's AI assistant for the weather every morning and to entrust it with complex tasks. But what if one day, the companies building this incredible technology reached out to the government and said, "Please help us, and please regulate us"? Recently, the two giants of the artificial intelligence (AI) industry, OpenAI and Anthropic, have been making such moves, capturing public attention.

## Why is this important?

AI technology is already deeply embedded in our lives. We live in an era where AI writes code, solves complex mathematical problems, and creates creative content [Source: Banandre](https://www.banandre.com/blog/openai-navier-stokes-millennium-problem-ai-proof-controversy). However, it is highly unusual for the companies at the forefront of developing this technology to call for "speed control" and "government management" themselves.

This shift sends us two important signals. First, AI technology has now reached a level of social influence and risk that is too great for individual companies to handle alone. Second, even though the AI industry is receiving massive investments, it is facing an unstable situation where no one knows where the end lies. If these companies falter, the AI services that have become the foundation of our daily lives could also be jeopardized [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/26/its-not-about-anthropic-vs-openai-anymore/).

## Simply put

Why are these smart AI companies trying to hold the government's hand? To help you understand, let's compare it to making a "super car" that is extremely powerful but difficult to control.

At first, they put all their effort into going faster than anyone else, but as the car became too fast, it became dangerously difficult for the driver to control. Now, the companies want to slow down themselves, but they cannot stop because they are competing with each other. If the government steps in at this point and says, "We will create speed limit rules and conduct safety inspections," it provides a "justification" and a "shield" for the companies to comfortably focus on safety [Source: SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/21/politics-hits-data-centers-openai-falls-behind-anthropic-and-now-ai-is-too-big-to-fail-quietly/).

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei recently publicly declared, "We need to slow down the rate at which AI models improve their capabilities" [Source: NakedCapitalism](https://www.nakedcapitalism.com/2026/09/openai-anthropic-ai-freeze-doomers-trump-bannon-sanders.html). Industry moguls such as Sam Altman of OpenAI, Elon Musk, and Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind have joined the ranks in agreement [Source: NakedCapitalism](https://www.nakedcapitalism.com/2026/09/openai-anthropic-ai-freeze-doomers-trump-bannon-sanders.html).

## Current situation

The AI industry is currently in the midst of a massive investment wave worth approximately $1 trillion. This is an enormous amount, comparable to the economy of a small city. However, it is still uncertain when this massive amount of money will turn into profit [Source: The Currency Analytics](https://thecurrencyanalytics.com/technology/anthropic-openai-and-the-1-trillion-ai-race-nobody-wants-to-pause-293671). Given the situation, OpenAI and Anthropic are facing similar problems and risks. If they fail, the repercussions will be beyond imagination [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/26/its-not-about-anthropic-vs-openai-anymore/).

To solve this, companies are discussing the following measures:
- **Government Financial Intervention**: The US government is considering taking a financial stake in leading AI labs like OpenAI or Anthropic [Source: YouTube(Sam Altman is Panicking...)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hwrGL5ZYHOw).
- **Introduction of Strong Regulations**: AI companies are discussing the introduction of new regulations that require government review before releasing advanced models to the public [Source: TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/openai-anthropic-google-meta-urge-us-to-slow-ai).

## What will happen in the future?

Moving forward, the AI industry is expected to shift its focus from "fast growth" to "safety and management." If the government becomes deeply involved in the AI industry, AI companies will move beyond being simple tech startups and reach a position similar to national infrastructure. While this has the advantage of making the AI services we use safer, it also carries the risk of entrenching a structure where a few giant corporations and the government monopolize the future of AI [Source: Mint](https://www.livemint.com/ai/artificial-intelligence/openai-vs-anthropic-sam-altman-dario-amodei-11772880227944.html).

Readers, which do you think is closer to our future: 'slow but safe AI' or 'fast but dangerous AI'?
