Imagine waking up in the morning, having a cup of coffee, and saying to an AI, "Summarize the policies to be discussed in the National Assembly today." It might sound like something out of a science fiction movie, but this is a reality already approaching us. Recently, in the Swedish political sphere, reliance on AI chatbots like ChatGPT has increased, leading to a heated debate over how they should be utilized in government work.

### Why is this important?

A politician's work is a continuous process of reviewing numerous documents and making judgments. When an AI chatbot intervenes here, work efficiency rises dramatically. However, fundamental questions follow simultaneously: "Is the decision we are making really human-made?" and "Is the AI providing biased information?" As the use of AI chatbots increases in Swedish government offices, this is expanding into a massive challenge that goes beyond the mere issue of using tools, touching on how we protect the values of trust and democracy in our society [Source 8].

### In simple terms, AI is a 'smart assistant'

Think of artificial intelligence as a 'very smart assistant.' If assistants in the past searched through library shelves to find materials, today's AI assistants (such as ChatGPT or Claude) analyze vast amounts of data on the internet in an instant to come up with answers [Source 7], [Source 17].

However, this assistant is not perfect. To use a metaphor, they are very smart but can sometimes show a 'hallucination' phenomenon where they tell plausible lies, or they might present biased opinions due to the bias in their training data. The core concern for the Swedish government is how to secure the transparency of the results when using this 'smart assistant' as a partner in policy-making. It is the same principle as carefully checking who made the recipe and whether the ingredients used are safe when we cook.

### What is the current situation?

Sweden is concretizing its AI strategy at a national level. Since the publication of the digital transformation strategy in 2025, a more advanced dedicated AI strategy began in February 2026 [Source 13]. The Swedish government has set a clear goal of making itself a top 10 AI powerhouse in the world [Source 13].

They are also moving quickly in terms of regulation. The EU's 'AI Act' has been in effect for a year already, and more than 100,000 companies in Sweden are subject to this regulation [Source 11]. Violating it can result in a massive fine of up to 35 million euros (over 50 billion won) [Source 11]. Because of this, both government agencies and companies in Sweden are showing a very cautious attitude toward legal compliance in the process of adopting AI.

### What will happen in the future?

The Swedish Parliament and government completed public consultations in the first half of this year, and are currently preparing for the introduction of national-level legislation based on them [Source 12]. In the future, even if AI is utilized in the Swedish public sector, it is expected that a structure where humans ultimately review and take responsibility will be further strengthened.

Going forward, 'digital literacy' (the ability to understand and correctly utilize digital technology)—where we use AI but do not blindly trust its results and review them critically—will become more important not only for the political sphere but also for ordinary citizens.

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

The government's adoption of AI is now an inevitable trend of the times. However, 'safety belts' to control technology must become stronger to protect policy fairness and social trust. It is a time to remember that while the pace of technological development is very fast, building and keeping trust, which is the foundation of democracy, still requires much time and effort.
