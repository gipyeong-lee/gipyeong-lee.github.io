Behind the artificial intelligence (AI) services we use in our daily lives, massive amounts of capital are in motion. Have you heard the recent news that Anthropic, the developer of the AI model 'Claude,' is preparing for a large-scale Initial Public Offering (IPO)—where a company goes public and sells shares to the general public—valued at $2 trillion? [[Source 1](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/very-intentional-steve-eisman-thinks-anthropic-hiding-something)] However, ahead of this flashy debut, sharp criticism has emerged from the financial market.

Steve Eisman, a renowned investor who was the real-life inspiration for the movie 'The Big Short' and is famous for predicting the subprime mortgage crisis, has raised serious suspicions against Anthropic. He claims that Anthropic intentionally omitted its most important recent financial figures—those from the third quarter—in the 'prospectus' it submitted to investors for its stock market listing. [[Source 1](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/very-intentional-steve-eisman-thinks-anthropic-hiding-something), [Source 2](https://biztoc.com/x/4c4eafe786c59a3f)]

### Why Is This Important?

Usually, when we buy stocks, it is a matter of course to check if the company is making good money. Because financial information for pre-IPO companies is not fully disclosed, investors judge the health of a company through IPO documents.

If a company deliberately removes its most recent report card—the third-quarter figures—from the document, investors are bound to suspect, "Are they hiding poor performance that they are embarrassed to show?" Eisman strongly argues that by omitting Q3 figures, Anthropic tried to cover up a sharp slowdown in the company's growth. [[Source 2](https://biztoc.com/x/4c4eafe786c59a3f)] If this is true, it creates a fatal information imbalance for the many investors who are planning to invest based on the company's value.

### In Simple Terms

Imagine this: You are selling a used car, and you’ve been to a repair center three times in the last month due to engine failure. However, on the vehicle condition report you show the buyer, you conveniently leave out the records for the last month and only list the perfect records up until the previous month. The buyer would mistakenly think, "The car is in perfect condition."

The 'intentional omission' that Eisman claims is exactly this situation. It is a suspicion that the company is 'cherry-picking'—a strategy of highlighting 'past data' showing the company is soaring while hiding the most recent 'unfavorable performance data.' [[Source 1](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/very-intentional-steve-eisman-thinks-anthropic-hiding-something)]

### Where Do They Stand?

Anthropic is currently one of the core companies in the AI market attracting attention by receiving large-scale investments from giant corporations like Google. [[Source 3](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/thebuzz24_google-commits-up-to-40-billion-to-anthropic-activity-7453594870537056256-G9Mk)] As such, Anthropic's management status is a hot topic of interest for investors around the world. [[Source 4](https://biztoc.com/)] Since Steve Eisman is famous for being the first to spot the bubble in the market during the financial crisis, his aggressive comments are causing ripples that cannot be taken lightly in the market. [[Source 1](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/very-intentional-steve-eisman-thinks-anthropic-hiding-something)]

### What Is Next?

Whether Anthropic actually intentionally omitted information or if it was simply a time lag in accounting procedures will only be known when more information is disclosed in the future. However, one thing is certain: 'transparency' is vital for a company ahead of an IPO. Investors are now watching to see what additional explanations Anthropic will offer, and how the market will react when the omitted third-quarter results are actually disclosed. It is recommended that you carefully follow the news related to Anthropic's performance as it is released.
