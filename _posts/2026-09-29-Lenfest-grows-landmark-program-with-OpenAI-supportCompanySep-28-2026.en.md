Imagine this: This morning, a reporter at a local newspaper was struggling with hundreds of pages of public records and data. The task of analyzing this vast amount of material, which would have taken days in the past, is now being tackled with the help of AI, allowing the reporter to extract key content and turn important civic information into an article in just a few hours.

This change is not just imagination. It is happening in the newsrooms of the local media outlets we turn to every day. Recently, the Lenfest Institute for Journalism announced that it is further expanding its ambitious project to strengthen local journalism using artificial intelligence technology [Source: Lenfest Institute Expands AI Fellowship With $5M OpenAI Backing](https://insideai.news/news/ai-in-business/lenfest-ai-collaborative-and-fellowship-program/13138/).

### Why does this matter?

Local news is the "watchdog of grassroots democracy," delivering stories closest to our lives—such as neighborhood affairs, school issues, and local politics. However, many local newspapers are struggling due to digital transformation and cost issues. This support is significant not only because it provides funding, but because it puts "the tool of technology" directly into the hands of journalists. If reporters can move away from repetitive tasks and focus on in-depth field reporting, the quality of our local news will ultimately improve.

### Simple explanation: AI is a reliable "research assistant"

In simple terms, this program is like a service that hires a "digitally literate AI research assistant" for a newsroom.

How about this comparison? If a reporter of the past was a chef who had to prepare and cook all ingredients themselves, a reporter who has adopted AI is a chef working with two or three competent sous-chefs. If the sous-chefs finish the complex prep work (organizing vast data) in an instant, the chef can focus entirely on creating the "flavor (the value and originality of the article)," which is the essence of cooking.

The Lenfest Institute's program dispatches actual technical staff to newsrooms to help reporters learn how to apply AI in the field [Source: OpenAI Adds Up to $10 Million for Lenfest AI Fellowships...](https://www.citybiz.co/article/910450/openai-adds-up-to-10-million-for-lenfest-ai-fellowships-opening-door-to-more-local-newsrooms/).

### Current status: How far has it come?

Launched in October 2024 with the support of OpenAI and Microsoft, the program has had a successful first two years [Source: Five news orgs join Lenfest AI Collaborative & Fellowship Program](https://www.lenfestinstitute.org/institute-news/five-news-organizations-lenfest-ai-collaborative-fellowship-openai-microsoft/). It has already brought about tangible changes by deploying technical staff to 11 major newsrooms, including The Baltimore Banner and The Philadelphia Inquirer [Source: OpenAI Adds Up to $10 Million for Lenfest AI Fellowships...](https://www.citybiz.co/article/910450/openai-adds-up-to-10-million-for-lenfest-ai-fellowships-opening-door-to-more-local-newsrooms/).

With OpenAI's recent decision to provide an additional $5 million in direct funding and $5 million in software credits, the range of local media outlets that can benefit is expected to widen even further [Source: Lenfest Institute Expands AI Fellowship With $5M OpenAI Backing](https://insideai.news/news/ai-in-business/lenfest-ai-collaborative-and-fellowship-program/13138/), [Source: OpenAI Expands Lenfest AI Collaborative Program | AIToolly](https://aitoolly.com/ai-news/article/2026-09-29-openai-expands-lenfest-ai-collaborative-and-fellowship-program-with-major-funding-and-engineering-su).

### What’s next?

Through this expansion, the Lenfest Institute plans to go beyond individual newsrooms and create a platform where the entire local media ecosystem can share and utilize AI technology [Source: Lenfest Institute expands AI Collaborative with OpenAI ...](https://www.lenfestinstitute.org/institute-news/lenfest-institute-openai-ai-fellowship-expansion/). In the future, as we read local news, we may encounter data-driven, in-depth insights more often than we ever could before. It will be a process of proving that technology doesn't replace journalists, but that journalists who are adept at using technology can build a better world.
