Imagine this: you hand your favorite song and lyrics to an AI, and a two-minute music video is produced in an instant. The surprising part is that this AI didn't learn from and stitch together existing movie scenes. Much like a painter picking up a brush to draw on a canvas, the AI picked up the brush of "coding" and drew every single frame itself.

The music video production capability recently showcased by **Claude Opus 5.5** is truly unprecedented. Moving beyond simple image generation, we will now take a deep dive into an example that demonstrates a high level of "intelligence" in video creation.

### Why does this matter?

The AI video technology we have seen so far was primarily a method of learning countless videos and imitating their styles. However, Claude Opus 5.5 walks a completely different path. This AI chose not to be a "data-based model" that makes video, but a "program" that writes the code to generate it.

Why is this significant? Technically, it's because **"artistic control"** has fallen into our hands. Existing video generation models made it difficult to perform detailed edits because the AI created the video arbitrarily; however, with a code-based approach, it is possible to implement animations exactly as the user intended. A new era has opened where even non-engineers can command AI to perform complex graphic tasks. ### Easy to understand: How AI paints

The process by which Claude Opus 5.5 creates a music video can be compared to calling a **"robot chef."**

If we ask a robot chef to "make pasta," the robot doesn't make the pasta itself, but writes an "elaborate recipe (code)" and issues commands to the kitchen automation system. Claude Opus 5.5 is the same. When a user says "make a music video," the AI directly writes the code that instructs the screen on what to draw, utilizing graphic libraries (a set of tools for drawing with a computer) like `p5.js` or `Three.js`. [[Source: Claude Opus 5.5 Made This Music Video From One Prompt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ShfA4KRLSyM), [Source: GitHub - tuzhechen2005/opus-video-skills](https://github.com/tuzhechen2005/opus-video-skills)]

The recently viral 'LGTM (Looks Good to Me)' music video divided the 156-second video into 9 chapters and meticulously drew a total of 3,760 frames using JavaScript code. To achieve a watercolor feel, they utilized a library called 'p5.brush,' and it is astonishing that this entire process was carried out by the AI's single command execution without human intervention.
