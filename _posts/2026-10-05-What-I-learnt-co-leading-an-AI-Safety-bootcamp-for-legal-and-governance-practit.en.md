Imagine this: How would you feel if the judge ruling on your unfair case today, or the lawyer on the legal team tasked with protecting your privacy, were using artificial intelligence (AI) without having any idea how it makes its decisions?

We already encounter AI in our daily lives. However, the technology behind it remains a complex "black box" (a system whose internal structure is unknown). Recently, 19 legal and governance (a management system where various stakeholders cooperate to set rules to achieve shared goals) experts from 12 countries around the world gathered for a 5-day AI safety bootcamp. [Why legal experts participated in an AI safety bootcamp](https://theneuralfeed.com/article/what-i-learnt-co-leading-an-ai-safety-bootcamp-for-legal-and-governance-practiti/VAdvpUaa) Why have they started digging into the core of the technology, even though they are not engineers?

## Why is this important?

AI is not just a simple tool; it is now deeply involved in society’s decision-making systems. Companies, courts, and governments are already using AI to carry out hiring, judicial rulings, and policy decisions. [Why legal experts participated in an AI safety bootcamp](https://theneuralfeed.com/article/what-i-learnt-co-leading-an-ai-safety-bootcamp-for-legal-and-governance-practiti/VAdvpUaa)

If the people making decisions in these fields do not understand why AI came to a certain conclusion, the results may not be fair. Experts who have received AI safety training can accurately identify the technical limitations and potential for errors in AI, enabling them to draft legislation or contracts that protect technology from infringing on citizens' rights. In other words, their efforts to bridge the gap between technology and the law are the front line in protecting your privacy and fundamental rights.

## Simply put: Open the black box called AI

The biggest reason AI is difficult to understand is that the process by which AI learns on its own is very different from human thinking. This is called a "black box." Plausible answers come out on the surface, but it is difficult to know what is happening inside.

To use an analogy, imagine a chef (AI) who makes a very complex dish. We receive the delicious food (result), but we do not know what ingredients (data) the chef put in, or in what proportions they were mixed to create that flavor. [The purpose of an AI safety bootcamp](https://theneuralfeed.com/article/what-i-learnt-co-leading-an-ai-safety-bootcamp-for-legal-and-governance-practiti/VAdvpUaa) Education for experts is a process of opening the chef's kitchen and learning the technical principles of how ingredients react.

Through this, participants demystify the "black box" and gain concrete technical vocabulary to communicate with engineers when discussing policy. It is like learning how to inspect the hygiene and safety of a kitchen with the chef. [Educational content of an AI safety bootcamp](https://ml4good.org/)

## Current situation: Where are they learning?

As voices advocating for AI safety grow louder, specialized educational programs for experts are also increasing.

* **BlueDot Impact**: Operates talent accelerators for artificial intelligence safety and social resilience and offers various courses. [About BlueDot Impact](https://bluedot.org/)
* **ML4Good**: Helps experts transition their careers so they can participate in the AI safety field and lowers the technical barriers to enable high-level policy discussions. [ML4Good Program](https://ml4good.org/)
* **SASH**: Provides fellowships and bootcamps for AI safety and governance work. [SASH Program](https://www.aisafety.sg/programs)

Through this education, legal professionals do not just learn words written in law books, but study how actual AI systems are designed and where safety defects occur. [AI safety learning for legal experts](https://theneuralfeed.com/article/what-i-learnt-co-leading-an-ai-safety-bootcamp-for-legal-and-governance-practiti/VAdvpUaa)

## What will happen in the future?

The pace of technological development is very fast. It is now practically impossible for legal professionals to design a safe society without knowledge of technology. Just as it is difficult to make traffic laws without knowing how a car is structured, it is highly likely that legal and governance experts possessing AI safety knowledge will become a required qualification in the future.

If these efforts continue, courts and governments will be able to make more transparent and responsible decisions when dealing with AI. It is no exaggeration to say that protecting your rights depends not only on technological advancement but also on the efforts of legal professionals to understand that technology.
