Imagine this: You start your busy morning, and as usual, you command your AI assistant, "Summarize today's meeting materials for me." But instead of a helpful response, you get a cold 'error message.' Thinking it might just be you, you ask other AI friends, but they either don't respond or react sluggishly.

Today, many AI services that people worldwide rely on have stopped working as if by mutual agreement. Why are our usually smart AI companions struggling so much?

## Why Does This Matter?

For many, AI has become a part of daily life. From developers writing code to office workers and students writing documents, countless people use ChatGPT or other AI models as essential tools.

When multiple AI services stop at once, it transcends mere 'inconvenience.' Workflow can grind to a halt, and critical data may become inaccessible in crucial moments. This incident highlights how deeply we have become dependent on the 'invisible infrastructure' of massive AI systems.

## Easy Explanation: An Analogy for AI Outages

An AI service going down is much like 'the checkout system of a giant library being paralyzed.'

AI, powered by sophisticated technologies like Transformers (an AI architecture that understands relationships between words in a sentence), processes vast amounts of data rapidly. However, if this 'library' is suddenly flooded with far more users than usual, or if there is a problem with one of the core components of the library system—the 'classification scheme' (servers and components)—the entire system may stutter or cease functioning altogether.

Especially with this incident where other AI services are simultaneously affected, many users speculate it might be a 'domino effect' where users flock to other services as one AI platform goes down [Source: ChatGPT and Codex Is Down | Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49550640).

## Current Situation: How Far Has It Spread?

According to OpenAI's official status page, 'Elevated Error Rates' are occurring across ChatGPT and Codex (coding assistance AI) services, with the issue persisting for at least four hours [Source: ChatGPT and Codex are currently down for some users - 9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/23/chatgpt-and-codex-are-currently-down-for-some-users/), [Source: Elevated errors across ChatGPT and Codex - OpenAI Status](https://status.openai.com/incidents/01M1KWEDH417T2CF44YYHZDFCR).

The scope is quite broad. For Codex, which assists with coding, the impact extends beyond web services to local command-line tools (CLI) used by developers, editor extensions, and even the Codex components within the desktop version of ChatGPT [Source: OpenAI Confirms Service Degradation Hitting ChatGPT and Codex...](https://www.unite.ai/openai-confirms-service-degradation-hitting-chatgpt-and-codex-users/).

Furthermore, user reports indicate that beyond ChatGPT and Codex, other popular AI chatbots like Claude and Grok are also experiencing connection failures or performance drops [Source: ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok Are Down - MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/03/chatgpt-claude-and-grok-are-down/).

## What Happens Next?

Service recovery may take time. As a user, the best course of action is to check the connection status, retry periodically, or keep an eye on the official status pages of the service providers [Source: Is Codex Down? Fix Access Denied, 429 & Failed Requests](https://shardstitch.com/radar/is-codex-down-request-failed-recovery/).

This phenomenon highlights how critical infrastructure stability becomes as AI technology advances. In the future, AI service companies will likely strive to build more robust server distribution and response systems to prevent such simultaneous outages. If AI services are unavailable, we recommend having patience rather than repeatedly attempting to reconnect.

## AI's Perspective

AI is, in the end, a system powered by human-created software. This outage serves as a reminder that while AI often feels like magic always at our side, there is complex server infrastructure operating in the background. Perhaps it is wise not to rely solely on AI, but to occasionally think about 'what we can do without it.'
