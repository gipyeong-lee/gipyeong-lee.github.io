# AI Firm Anthropic Paid $1.63 Million to Trump-Connected Lobbying Firm... Why Is It Controversial?

Imagine finding out that the tech giant behind the AI chatbot you use every day has funneled millions of dollars into a political lobbying firm tied to high-ranking White House officials. It sounds like a scene from a grand political thriller, but it is actually playing out right now in Silicon Valley and Washington D.C.

Anthropic, the developer of the popular conversational AI model Claude (designed to be helpful, honest, and harmless), has recently found itself under heavy political spotlight ahead of federal lobbying disclosures and its upcoming initial public offering (IPO) [Source 1, Source 2, Source 3, Source 7]. Let's explore why an AI company dedicated to technological development joined hands with Washington lobbying firms, and what story lies behind the scenes.

---

## Why Does This Matter?

To us, AI is a convenient assistant, writing tool, and sometimes a coding partner. However, as AI technology advances, it becomes entangled with massive policy stakes such as national security, semiconductor chip supplies, and government contracts.

To put it simply: just as automobile companies in the past focused solely on building high-performance cars to drive on roads, today's tech companies' fates depend heavily on what bills pass in government ministries and parliaments that set the rules for the global road network. Anthropic's injection of massive funds into political circles to communicate has a simple purpose: to establish safety guards so that regulatory and policy directions favor the company amid the massive wave of global AI disruption. In this era, it has become crucial for the general public to understand how AI pricing, service stability, and the health of the AI industry are driven by these political dynamics.

This clash of interests goes beyond a single company's marketing strategy. As AI establishes itself as national infrastructure, a single government policy decision can dictate billions of dollars in value. Consequently, tight communication between Silicon Valley tech companies and Washington D.C. is no longer a choice, but a prerequisite for survival.

---

## Making It Simple: The Meeting of AI Companies and Washington Lobbying

Political lobbying is often associated with shady backroom deals, but within the American political system, lobbying is also a legitimate communication channel for companies to explain their technological vision and regulatory needs to policymakers.

Let's analogize Anthropic's situation: imagine a large construction company planning to build a massive new city, hiring professional government affairs experts (lobbying firms) to actively convince the head of urban planning or high-ranking Defense Department officials that "our apartments have earthquake-proof engineering and are eco-friendly."

According to federal lobbying disclosure records, Anthropic paid a total of $1.63 million across six quarters to two lobbying firms closely connected to U.S. President Donald Trump’s political network [Source 1, Source 2, Source 16, Source 17]. Among them, $740,000 went to a lobbying firm named Continental Strategy [Source 1, Source 2, Source 17]. This firm is led by a former chief of staff to U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and features White House Chief of Service Susie Wiles' daughter as a partner [Source 1, Source 2, Source 14].

This lobbying activity did not happen by chance. It took place at a sensitive time coinciding with debates over AI usage in the Pentagon linked to a memo authored by Secretary Marco Rubio, alongside shifts surrounding former NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang [Source 2, Source 14, Source 16, Source 17]. Federal disclosure documents show that the lobbying firm even included the White House National Security Council (NSC) on its list of targeted government agencies for 2026, demonstrating how deeply Anthropic's voice reaches within Washington [Source 12]. This clearly proves that tech companies are stepping beyond mere software development to become core players in foreign policy and security strategy.

---

## Current Status: A Giant Nearing IPO and the Reality of Massive Costs

Standing at the center of political controversy, Anthropic is simultaneously undergoing a massive economic transition. Recently, Anthropic drew intense public attention by filing hundreds of pages of S-1 documents for its U.S. stock market listing [Source 3, Source 8].

The numbers in the disclosed documents are nothing short of astonishing:
- Anthropic recorded a massive revenue of $17 billion [Source 8].
- However, despite this, the company still remains in the red [Source 8].

Why is that? Due to the global AI boom, user demand massively exceeded the computing resources (servers and AI chip processing power) that the company had pre-secured [Source 8]. To provide seamless service to customers, Anthropic had to spend an additional $5 billion solely to secure short-term computing resources [Source 8]. Such colossal infrastructure investment and operating costs starkly highlight the structural challenges facing the AI industry.

Adding to this, ahead of its stock market listing, Anthropic announced it will donate 5% of its total shares to a "Fund for the Public Good" to demonstrate the company's public-interest value [Source 7]. While this is an expression of its intent to pursue commercial success while upholding AI safety and public interest simultaneously, both Wall Street and Washington are watching the tech giant's moves with bated breath. Balancing corporate profit-seeking with public responsibilities will be the key to the IPO's success or failure.

## What’s Next?

Over the coming months, the environment surrounding Anthropic is expected to become even more intense:

1. **IPO and Market Evaluation**: As Anthropic navigates the listing process—with valuations projected between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion—attention is focused on how the market will evaluate its financial structure, which consumed massive capital over a short period [Source 3]. Investors will rigorously verify sustainable revenue-generating capability alongside top-line growth.
2. **Enhanced Political and Regulatory Scrutiny**: A key watchpoint will be how lobbying activities through firms connected to core Trump administration figures translate into actual results regarding future government AI regulation, defense contracts, and semiconductor supply disputes [Source 1, Source 2, Source 12, Source 16]. Policy shifts could reshape the entire AI industry landscape.
3. **Realization of the Public Fund**: Investors will continue to scrutinize how the promised 5% equity allocation to public interest impacts actual corporate governance and shareholder value [Source 7]. This will serve as a major benchmark for social responsibility models among other big tech companies.
