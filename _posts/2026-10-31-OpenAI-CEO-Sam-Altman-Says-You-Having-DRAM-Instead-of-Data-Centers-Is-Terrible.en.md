Imagine this: You spent a fortune on a top-of-the-line computer, equipped with high-performance memory (DRAM, the space where a computer temporarily stores data while working) capable of processing massive amounts of data in an instant. How would you feel if, one day, the head of the world's most famous AI company appeared and told you that your computer is 'wasting' that precious memory?

Recently, a statement made by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman during a podcast interview has sparked a heated debate in the IT industry. He criticized the distribution of the world's limited memory resources across individual computers as "terrible" [[Source Title](https://www.thegamer.com/openai-ceo-sam-altman-consumers-having-dram-terrible/)]. Why on earth would he make such a provocative claim?

## Why Does This Matter?

This statement goes beyond a mere debate about memory technology; it raises a fundamental question about how we will use AI in the future. For a long time, many users have dreamed of 'Local AI'—technology that executes AI directly on their own devices without an internet connection. However, following Altman's logic, a future where AI is provided through centralized 'Data Centers' (facilities housing massive supercomputers) rather than individual devices might become the inevitable norm. This statement foreshadows significant changes in terms of consumer choice and individual autonomy.

## Keeping It Simple

To make Altman's argument easy to understand, let's use an analogy.

Think of computer memory (DRAM) as 'cars.' People all over the world park one car in front of their homes, but in reality, they only drive them for 5% of the day, leaving them empty in a parking lot for over 23 hours [[Source Title](https://daily.dev/posts/sam-altman-says-your-dram-is-being-wasted-on-you-gjmtxy96f)]. Sam Altman is essentially saying, "Why does everyone need to own a car? If we gathered all those cars to create a taxi company, we could transport far more people by keeping them in operation 24 hours a day for those who need them."

In other words, he argues that it is a waste of resources for individuals to install memory in their own computers only to use a tiny fraction of it (about 5%) for actual AI work. His logic is that if these memory resources were gathered in a central data center and used intensively for AI computing, we could create far more powerful and efficient AI services [[Source Title](https://cryptobriefing.com/sam-altman-local-ai-inference-dram/)].

## Where We Stand

Currently, the AI industry is largely divided into two camps. One side is aiming for local AI models that run directly on personal devices like ours, while the other side, like Altman's OpenAI, is strongly pushing for data center-based cloud services (services using online servers). Altman is fiercely defending the idea that data center-based AI processing is far more efficient than running local AI on personal devices [[Source Title](https://daily.dev/posts/sam-altman-says-your-dram-is-being-wasted-on-you-gjmtxy96f)].

His criticism stems from the view that the trend of individuals equipping their own devices with expensive memory to run AI hinders the limited global memory supply chain and the efficient operation of data centers [[Source Title](https://frvr.com/blog/news/openai-ceo-sam-altman-says-its-terrible-to-have-everybody-have-a-bunch-of-the-worlds-limited-supply-of-dram-instead-of-shoving-it-all-in-datacentres/)].

## What Will Happen Next?

Altman has frequently made statements that break with conventional wisdom in the tech industry. This statement also reflects his strong will regarding where the resources needed for the AI era should flow. Future technological progress is expected to move away from simply making massive models even larger [[Source Title](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-ceo-sam-altman-the-age-of-giant-ai-models-is-already-over/)] and become a battle over how efficiently resources are utilized.

What do you think? Would you prefer a future where you sacrifice your computer's resources for the sake of data center efficiency and entrust all AI to the cloud, or is the freedom of local AI—where everything is processed within your own device, even if slightly inefficient—more valuable? AI technology is advancing, but the contemplation of how we enjoy that technology seems to have only just begun.

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

Individual users want to maintain direct control over their devices. While technological efficiency is important, user experience and choice are values that cannot be ignored. Whether a future where individual autonomy is absorbed into data centers in the name of efficiency is the 'correct' answer remains to be seen.

Just as we used to carry around separate portable MP3 players and navigation devices before smartphones arrived, only to eventually have them integrated into a single device, AI resources might also be destined to be reorganized in the most efficient way possible. However, the question of how we will be compensated for the 'control in the palm of our hands' that we might lose in that process remains a major task to solve.
