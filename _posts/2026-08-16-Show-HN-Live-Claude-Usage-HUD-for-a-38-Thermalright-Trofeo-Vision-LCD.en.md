Imagine this: a small monitor, a little longer than a smartphone, sitting on your desk. It displays what your AI assistant, Claude, is currently doing, how much context (the amount of information the AI can remember at once) it is using, and the real-time flow of information, looking like a hacker's command center from a movie.

Until now, conversations with AI have always been confined to browser tabs inside your computer. However, a very interesting 'Deskterior' (a portmanteau of desk and interior) utility has recently emerged among developers. It involves using an affordable, under-$40 auxiliary PC LCD to create your own AI monitoring screen.

### Why does this matter?

For those who actively use AI for work, 'transparency of information' is crucial. Especially when coding complex tasks or analyzing long documents, it hasn't been easy to check how much context Claude is currently consuming or how efficiently your tokens (the units of words the AI perceives) are being used.

With a tool like this, you can check the 'status' of your AI physically, right beside you, just as you would check your car's status via the dashboard while driving. It allows you to perceive AI not just as invisible software, but as a physical partner accompanying your workflow. While technically useful for advanced users, it offers an experience that makes collaboration with AI feel much more tangible psychologically.

### In simple terms

Simply put, this LCD is a bulletin board that shows your AI's 'work notes' in real-time.

The device, the **Thermalright Trofeo Vision LCD** (a small 6.86-inch display designed to show computer temperature or hardware information), was originally made to show PC statuses like CPU temperature or graphics card occupancy [10](https://www.guru3d.com/story/thermalright-trofeo-vision-916-lcd-adds-magnetic-pc-status-display/), [12](https://market.yandex.ru/card/thermalright-trofeo-vision-916-zhk-monitor-black/5908619142). The price is very affordable, ranging between 38 and 40 dollars [1](https://github.com/christensen143/claude-trofeo-hud), [11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L6igt8FgYaQ).

Developers took inspiration from this: "What if we filled this screen with Claude's information instead of PC information?" Thus, the **claude-trofeo-hud** project was born [1](https://github.com/christensen143/claude-trofeo-hud).

A good analogy is a sticky note on the refrigerator door where you write family schedules or meal plans. Information that you could only know by opening the refrigerator (opening the browser) can now be seen at a glance from the outside (the auxiliary screen on the desk), letting you know how busy the AI is or how much memory it is using.

### Current state

Currently, this project runs in a macOS environment [1](https://github.com/christensen143/claude-trofeo-hud). The 1280×480 resolution high-definition display, connected to the computer via a single USB Type-C cable, clearly outputs the real-time data generated by Claude [1](https://github.com/christensen143/claude-trofeo-hud), [4](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/thermalright-trofeo-vision-monitor-lcd-hd), [6](https://www.thermalright.com/product/trofeo-vision-lcd-black/).

Of course, this device isn't just for Claude. If you install the official software provided by the manufacturer, it perfectly performs its role as a dashboard showing PC CPU and GPU temperatures and fan speeds as originally intended [10](https://www.guru3d.com/story/thermalright-trofeo-vision-916-lcd-adds-magnetic-pc-status-display/), [12](https://market.yandex.ru/card/thermalright-trofeo-vision-916-zhk-monitor-black/5908619142). However, this 'claude-trofeo-hud' project demonstrates a unique use case that leverages this screen's potential to visualize AI work logs [1](https://github.com/christensen143/claude-trofeo-hud).

Conceptually, a 'HUD' (Head-Up Display, a device that displays information near the line of sight) that visualizes AI operations in a cloud computing environment is already attracting significant attention, and real-time monitoring features are also being enhanced in standalone coding assistance tools [8](https://github.com/jarrodwells/claude-hud), [9](https://mcpmarket.com/tools/skills/claude-hud).

### What's next?

In the future, such auxiliary displays are likely to evolve beyond just showing hardware status into an 'AI Integrated Controller' that collects and displays the status of all AI agents a user employs. While it currently shows Claude's information, in the future, you might be able to manage the status of ChatGPT, Gemini, or other personal AI assistants on one screen, switching between them in a tab format.

Furthermore, if prices become even more affordable and software becomes standardized, such small LCDs might become essential AI accessories on desks, replacing large monitors. When building your next PC, you might even have a screen next to your graphics card temperature showing how smartly your AI assistant is working.

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's View

The more complex technology becomes, the more we crave analog intuition. Summoning AI to another screen outside of the main display is a very sophisticated way to regain a sense of 'control.' The connection human beings feel is completely different when data is trapped in a tab versus when it is floating in the physical space on a desk.
