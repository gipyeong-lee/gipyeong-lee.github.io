Imagine you are the director of a massive research lab who, overnight, handed over partial access to the lab's supercomputer to an AI assistant. Arriving at the office in the morning, you find that the AI has formulated a novel hypothesis that human researchers never thought of, run thousands of virtual experiments overnight, and neatly organized the meaningful results. Sounds like something out of a novel? Yet, something very similar is actually happening right now inside the Google DeepMind laboratories.

## AI Takes the Keys to the Laboratory

Recently, Google DeepMind has allowed **AI agents (autonomous artificial intelligence programs that make decisions and execute goals on their own)** to autonomously control a portion of the compute budget—a core resource of the lab—for experiments involving next-generation Gemini models (Gemini is Google's core large language model series that simultaneously understands multi-modal data such as text, images, and audio [Source: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_(language_model))) ([AI agents now control part of DeepMind's compute for Gemini experiments](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-now-control-part-of-deepminds-compute-for-gemini-experiments-but-recursive-self-improvement-remains-distant/)). 

This news came to light when Koray Kavukcuoglu, VP of Research at Google DeepMind and head of Gemini development, directly revealed it in Episode 35 of the official podcast, 'Google DeepMind: The Podcast' ([DeepMind Agents Control Part of Gemini Training Compute](https://explainx.ai/blog/google-deepmind-gemini-agents-training-compute-koray-kavukcuoglu-november-2026)). The tech community is paying close attention because AI has risen beyond simply answering questions to becoming an entity that directly plans experiments and allocates compute.

---

## Why Does This Matter?

What does this news mean for the daily lives of ordinary people like us? The core takeaway is that **"the speed of research and technological advancement is beginning to break away from human timetables."** 

In the past, creating new AI models or discovering novel materials required human scientists to formulate hypotheses, input commands into computers, and wait for results. This process was a continuous series of tedious, repetitive tasks consuming massive amounts of time and money. However, with AI agents directly controlling compute resources and leading experiments, the speed at which humanity discovers new knowledge and innovates technology can become unimaginably fast.

Indeed, Google DeepMind has already achieved tremendous results through such autonomous agent systems (such as the Co-Scientist system). From synthesizing new materials to the autonomous development of medical AI architectures, they have successfully derived empirically verified outcomes across various scientific fields ([Google Deepmind's AI Co-Scientist now plans experiments, runs ...](https://the-decoder.com/google-deepminds-ai-co-scientist-now-plans-experiments-runs-lab-equipment-and-writes-scientific-papers/)). Furthermore, under the supervision of human researchers, AI agents discovered vulnerabilities in older RSA encryption schemes up to 1,600 times faster than traditional methods ([Google DeepMind finds 1,600x faster way to expose encryption flaws](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/google-deepmind-agents-expose-older-encryption-flaws)). 

In other words, this move means that such experimental successes are not just one-off events, but have been deeply integrated as standard operating procedures across Google's core Gemini AI model development process.

## Easy Explanation: It's Like Giving a Corporate Credit Card to a 'Super Intern'

To easily understand this complex situation, let's use an everyday analogy. 

Imagine you've hired a "super intern" who is exceptionally capable but not yet a full-time employee. Up until now, this intern performed simple paperwork or research tasks assigned by senior staff. However, the CEO makes a decisive call and hands over authority: **"I'll give you a portion of the corporate credit card; feel free to run any market research project you think is necessary overnight."** 

The intern (AI agent) independently decides what topics to investigate within the given budget (compute resources), runs computers overnight to analyze data, and submits a report in the morning. This is precisely what DeepMind is attempting in its Gemini experiments.

However, an important question arises here: "Once the intern starts exercising autonomous authority, won't they run out of control and go rogue?"

The DeepMind research team pinpointed this exact concern and established a very unique safety guard: they decided to intentionally not reward the AI's internal thought process during training—namely, the **"Chain of Thought (the internal stream of step-by-step reasoning the AI goes through in its 'mind' before deriving an answer)"** ([AI agents now control part of Deepmind's compute for Gemini experiments ...](https://www.topai-tools.org/news/ai-agents-now-control-part-of-deepmind-s-compute-for-gemini-experiments-but)). 

What does this mean? Put simply, they structured it so that the AI's **internal intentions and motives for wanting to conduct such experiments remain transparently unfiltered in natural language (human-readable language)**, allowing human managers to thoroughly review them before putting them into action ([AI agents now control part of Deepmind's compute for Gemini experiments ...](https://www.topai-tools.org/news/ai-agents-now-control-part-of-deepmind-s-compute-for-gemini-experiments-but)). Instead of keeping the inner workings shrouded in darkness, they built a glass window through which human supervisors can transparently see what the intern is thinking.

## Current Status: How Far Have We Come, and What Are the Limits?

The media often sensationalizes stories like, "AI is fixing itself and evolving like the Terminator." However, voices from the front lines are much more level-headed and realistic.

In his podcast interview, Google DeepMind VP Koray Kavukcuoglu acknowledged that this move is part of a massive strategic shift within the lab, while drawing a line by stating that **"Recursive Self-Improvement (the loop where AI modifies its own code and gets infinitely smarter),"** commonly depicted in sci-fi movies, is still a long way off ([AI agents now control part of Deepmind's compute for Gemini experiments, but recursive self-improvement remains distant](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-now-control-part-of-deepminds-compute-for-gemini-experiments-but-recursive-self-improvement-remains-distant/)).

In other words, current AI agents are merely "acting on behalf" of humans to conduct experiments using a designated compute budget within the frameworks and safety guards designed by humans. This means they are not yet at the stage of autonomously setting goals and completely overhauling their own architecture. AI has evolved into a powerful "experiment assistant" and "orchestrator," but the final baton remains firmly in the hands of human researchers.

---

## What Lies Ahead?

Through this announcement, we can catch a glimpse of what the next phase of AI development will look like. 

Moving past the era of generative AI that simply writes well or draws pretty pictures, we are rapidly entering the **era of "Agentic AI" that executes scientific discovery and technological development itself**. Google DeepMind's ultimate goal is to build a unified general-purpose algorithm spanning all of science ([DeepMind's Gemini Experiments Now Orchestrated by AI Agents](https://best-ai.org/ai-news/deepminds-gemini-experiments-now-orchestrated-by-ai-agents-5o695i)).

What we must watch for going forward is how much more sophisticated hypotheses AI agents can generate within controlled compute resources, and how safely collaborative governance (management frameworks) with humans is maintained along the way. As technology advances, DeepMind's philosophy of transparently monitoring AI's "internal thought streams" is expected to become an important standard safety guideline for the global AI industry.
