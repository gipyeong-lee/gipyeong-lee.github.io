Imagine you are preparing a special birthday card for a loved one. In the past, you might have spent a long time deliberating among beautiful cards at a stationery store, but these days, you can complete a perfect card, from the illustration to the message, in just one minute by simply saying a few words to artificial intelligence (AI, computer technology that mimics human intelligence). It is truly convenient. However, don't you feel a bit empty in a corner of your heart? Recently, young artists have appeared who are questioning this "emptiness within convenience."

### Why is this important?

We are living in an "age where everything is AI." Looking at the YC Startup Directory, which shows the flow of tech startups, almost all startups, excluding the hardware sector, are operating with AI technology as their core engine [Source 1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49689696).

This flow is also strongly surging in the field of creation. Just a few lines of prompts (commands given to AI) can produce results in an instant that would take an entire team to work on overnight [Source 5](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-ai-startup-making-creatives-obsolete-or-more-powerful-than-ever-352b273e5b). However, can this efficiency truly perfectly replace the essence of creation? For those who value narratives such as "who made this and with what heart" more than simply churning out results quickly, the current AI-centric technological development is approaching as a major concern.

### Simply put

By way of analogy, the current AI craze can be compared to "high-speed instant food." It is fast and filling, but similar to how one misses "home-cooked meals" full of care. In fact, in university game development clubs and arts-related communities, considerable resistance is sensed against environments where AI takes the lead in creative activities [Source 1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49689696), [Source 2](https://hacknux.blogspot.com/2026/09/new-ask-hacker-news-story-ask-hn-would.html).

What they want is not an unconditional rejection of AI technology itself. They are wary of the unique senses and efforts that only humans can possess evaporating in the process of creation. For example, some small business owners are choosing hand-drawn posters instead of uniform images created by AI, seeking results imbued with the "scent of humans" [Source 17](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-04/ai-boom-technology-backlash-policy-changes/107036814). It is the same logic as placing higher value on furniture made by a master craftsman with great care than on products mass-produced identically by machines.

### How far has it come?

Then, should AI technology disappear from our side? Of course not. What Gen Z (the generation born after the mid-1990s) is demanding is not the abolition of AI, but its appearance as a "wise helper" that complements human creativity [Source 4](https://www.theedadvocate.org/why-gen-z-is-turning-against-ai-the-surprising-backlash-explained/).

However, current startups in the market tend to overly emphasize only the "efficiency of AI." Because of this, voices are growing that policies and technology development containing philosophical reflections on human creators' rights or unique work processes should be prioritized [Source 4](https://www.theedadvocate.org/why-gen-z-is-turning-against-ai-the-surprising-backlash-explained/). A relationship of "symbiosis," where technology does not replace us but helps us discover greater possibilities through technology, is still lacking in reality.

### What will happen in the future?

In the field of creation, it is very likely that two flows, "AI-utilized services" and "human-centered handmade services," will coexist in the future. While the fear that AI will swallow up all creation clearly exists, paradoxically, the value of "results made directly by people" will become scarcer and more precious.

What we must pay attention to now is not just simple technological development. Social consensus on how to make AI naturally permeate our daily lives and how to firmly protect the rights of creators so that uniquely human creativity does not disappear will become important. Moving beyond the binary deliberation of "should I use AI or not," how to preserve our human aspects within technology will become the core battleground for next-generation business.
