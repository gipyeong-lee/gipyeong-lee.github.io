Imagine this: today, you had a long conversation with the AI chatbot 'Claude.' You refined important work emails and solved complex coding problems. Feeling quite satisfied with the conversation, you casually clicked the 'Like' (feedback) button next to the chat window.

But suddenly, a worry crosses your mind: **"Does this feedback button I just clicked mean my chat content is flowing somewhere and being used as material for AI training?"** Today, we will thoroughly explore the relationship between AI training and personal information—something many users are curious about—and uncover the truth behind the 'feedback' button.

### Why is this important?

Every day, we share secret work stories or deeply personal concerns with AI. If this data were used as training material for AI companies without any filtering, it’s only natural to feel anxious that your important information might be mixed into responses provided to someone else in the future. For AI service users, 'data sovereignty' has now become as important as the convenience of the technology.

### Understanding it easily: Feedback is AI’s 'Error Note'

Providing feedback to Claude is like creating an **'error note' for the AI, letting it know, "This response was good" or "This response was wrong, you need to fix it."** [Source: RLHF Training Principle](https://www.anthropic.com/research/training-a-helpful-and-harmless-assistant-with-reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback)

Although AI may seem to become perfect on its own, it is actually continuously 'learning' and becoming smarter through this human feedback. The feedback buttons we click become the essential material that makes AI models kinder and more useful tools. [Source: Claude User Permission Settings](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/anthropic-gives-claude-users-more-201113498.html)

### Current situation: Can I choose regarding my data?

Fortunately, Anthropic, the creator of Claude, has provided mechanisms for users to directly choose whether their data is used.

1. **Rights of consumer plan users**: Individual users on Free, Pro, and Max plans can directly decide whether their chat data is used for AI training. [Source: Data Usage Policy](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage), [Source: Data Usage Control](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/anthropic-gives-claude-users-more-201113498.html)
2. **How is feedback processed?**: If you provide feedback, Anthropic completely separates your user ID from the data before processing it. This means it is used for training in a state where it is impossible to know 'who' sent it. [Source: Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996868-is-my-data-used-for-model-training)
3. **Are connected external files safe?**: When using an external service like Google Drive connected, simply having it connected does not mean all that data is trained. Clicking feedback does not automatically include the original data from connected external services, so you can rest assured. However, remember that if you manually copy and paste content into the chat window, that content may be included. [Source: External Data Processing Policy](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023580-is-my-data-used-for-model-training)
4. **Enterprise users are separate**: Business (Team, Enterprise), API users, and government service users maintain separate data policies from general consumers. [Source: Data Usage Policy](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)

### What lies ahead?

While AI companies need vast amounts of data to create smarter models, the level of demand for user privacy protection is also steadily rising. In the future, it is expected that we will move beyond simple checkboxes to allow 'data training permission settings,' toward directions where adjustments can be made granularly by conversation topic, or where more powerful privacy protection technologies are applied.
