## Why Does This Matter?

Every day, we encounter countless forecasts through news, YouTube, and communities. Statements like "This coin will skyrocket next year" or "Self-driving cars will completely dominate the roads in five years." Yet when time actually passes, few people seriously track who said what or whether it actually came true. Human memory conveniently forgets wrong predictions and remembers only the correct ones.

In this situation, artificial intelligence cold-heartedly verifying the archives of collective intelligence carries several important implications.

First, we can receive an **objective report card on tech and market trend predictions**. We can identify patterns of what technologies were underestimated in the past and what trends were accurately foreseen within a community gathering the brightest developers and engineers from around the world.

Second, it demonstrates that **AI has moved deeply into the realm of 'fact-checking' beyond being a mere summarization tool**. While it is nearly impossible for a human to sift through millions of comments accumulated over decades to determine authenticity, modern AI systems can structure and verify them in just a few hours.

## Understanding Easily: How AI Verifies Past Predictions

To easily understand how this service works, let's use an analogy. 

Imagine this. You have **the world's most memory-efficient and sharp-eyed library librarian**. This librarian has read every single one of millions of visitor logs accumulated in the library (Hacker News) from 2008 to the present. Out of those, they carefully pick out sentences where people wrote, "Such-and-such will happen next year." 

Not stopping there, this librarian roams the newsstand of the world every morning (Google searches and public records) to check whether those predictions actually came true. They grade them: "Ah, ten years ago someone wrote, 'Cloud servers will become mainstream in the future'? Looking at actual records, AWS dominated the market. This prediction is a 100-point correct answer!"

Indeed, the technical structure of this service is very interesting. The developer stated that it took only 4 to 6 hours to build this web application [Source: Show HN: Analysis of Hacker News predictions since 2008](https://hnpredictions.com/). In a Google Cloud environment, they combined the Vertex API (a toolkit to easily leverage Google's AI models) and various Gemini models to analyze and summarize text [Source: Show HN: Analysis of Hacker News predictions since 2008](https://hnpredictions.com/).

Especially interesting is the **duplicate prediction handling process**. Since multiple people can make the exact same prediction around the same time, the system first goes through a 'clustering' process to group them together, and then ranks them based on three criteria [Source: Show HN: Analysis of Hacker News predictions since 2008](https://hnpredictions.com/):

1. **Impact**: How large of a ripple effect did the prediction actually have on the world?
2. **Boldness**: Given the atmosphere at the time, how unconventional and challenging to common sense was the claim?
3. **Specificity**: Rather than talking vaguely in the clouds, how specifically did it pinpoint the target?
