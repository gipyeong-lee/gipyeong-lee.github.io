Imagine a lawyer who needs to review a complex contract spanning hundreds of pages. In the past, they would have had to spend several nights staying up with their team, comparing clauses one by one. But now, they only need to tell the AI, "Find the clauses in this contract that are unfavorable to our company and analyze the risks." We are entering an era where AI doesn't just summarize content, but takes responsibility for the entire flow of legal work itself. At the center of this is the new, legal-specialized AI: **GLM-Legal**.

### Why is this important?

Many of the AI tools we have encountered so far have remained at the level of "answering questions." However, the legal field is different. Legal tasks such as contract review, evidence analysis, and compliance (the process of verifying that regulations are being followed correctly) are "flows" themselves, where numerous steps are intertwined [Source 10].

Legal professionals no longer want just a tool to search for knowledge; they want a "colleague" they can trust with complex practical tasks. Agentic systems like GLM-Legal, which judge and act on their own, do not just provide one-off responses. Instead, they understand the user's complex goals, decompose them into executable sub-steps, and derive results [Source 12]. This will create an environment where lawyers and paralegals can step away from simple repetitive tasks and focus on more strategic decision-making.

### Easy to understand

To understand GLM-Legal, you first need to understand the concept of an "agent." Let's use an easy analogy. If previous AI were "library librarians" who looked up answers in encyclopedias, agentic AI is a "professional assistant" who manages entire projects and handles the actual work.

GLM-Legal is a legal-specialized model developed by Z.ai, built on the powerful brain of **GLM-5.3-Flash** [Source 10]. This AI has learned the methods veteran lawyers use to handle their work:
1. **Goal Decomposition**: When a user says, "Review this contract," the AI breaks it down into small steps such as "extract key clauses," "verify legal risks," and "check consistency with other contracts" [Source 12].
2. **Tool Invocation and Self-Check**: It directly queries necessary legal databases and goes through a "Self-check" process to verify whether there are errors in the output it has created [Source 12].

Because it follows such a systematic process, it can show high reliability in areas like law, where even small mistakes are not tolerated.

### Current situation

GLM-Legal is already proving its capabilities in real-world legal work environments. It proved its competence by recording a 19.2% pass rate across all subjects in the 'Harvey LAB' test, a performance evaluation metric for legal AI used by experts [Source 9]. This is a very significant achievement as a specialized legal model.

Technology is constantly evolving. In the current market for open-weight models (models where users can directly modify and utilize the model's information), models like the GLM series are demonstrating powerful performance, competing for the top spot in overall scores [Source 13]. However, these models are still in a stage where they act as powerful "agents" helping experts' judgment, rather than completely replacing professional legal advice.

### What will happen in the future?

The future legal AI market will be a competition not of parameter size (the figure determining the model's intelligence), but of how well it is tailored to a specific field (Domain Alignment) [Source 19].

We will soon see "end-to-end" legal work workflows where AI manages everything from receiving client information, document creation, contract comparison, litigation support, and even electronic signatures [Source 2][Source 4]. This goes beyond a simple technological advancement; it has the high potential to lead to the "democratization of legal services," lowering the cost of legal services and allowing more people to receive professional legal support.
