Imagine this. There is a column you enjoy reading every morning. It is written by a famous columnist known for keen insight and witty sentences. But one day, how would you feel if their writing felt somehow dull and clichéd compared to usual? This is exactly what recently happened at the prestigious media outlet, *The Atlantic*.

In reality, the piece was not written by a human reporter but by the AI 'Claude.' [Source: PaulClaudeGigot:WhyIAmRightAboutAI- The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/08/paul-claude-gigot-why-i-am-right-about-ai/688434/) Today, we will use this intriguing incident to examine where AI writing capability currently stands and why the human touch remains decisive.

## Why It Matters

As generative AI (artificial intelligence that creates new content such as text and images by itself) technology has advanced rapidly in recent years, articles or columns written by AI have appeared in the press with increasing frequency. This phenomenon sometimes leads readers to mistakenly believe that "AI is now able to think and write with depth similar to a human."

However, this case poses a very important question: Can we really call a piece written by AI a 'good article'? AI may be faster and more accurate than humans at simple information delivery. However, sharp criticism that cuts through situations and brilliant humor that makes people laugh are still very difficult tasks for AI. This incident clearly demonstrates what we should be wary of when utilizing AI in the media industry.

## The Explainer

This column was written to mimic the style of Paul Gigot, the opinion editor of the Wall Street Journal. [Source: WhyIAmRightAboutAI](https://politomix.com/the-atlantic/2392199/why-am-right-ai/) To put it simply, it is like placing an order with an AI saying, "Speak like Paul Gigot."

Technically, this is called 'Style Transfer.' Think of it as similar to applying a 'filter' in a smartphone photo app that mimics the style of a famous painter. An AI can replicate a painter's style to create a new picture, but it does not understand why that picture is moving or what the core artistic spirit of the painter is.

The same was true for this article. It mimicked the formal style, but the core insight that would permeate the entire piece or the wit that would entertain the readers was completely missing. The content was so banal and ordinary that, in the end, a human editor had to manually add humor to save the piece. [Source: PaulClaudeGigot:WhyIAmRightAboutAI- The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/08/paul-claude-gigot-why-i-am-right-about-ai/688434/)

## Where We Stand

Currently, AI technology is advancing brilliantly. Various AI chatbots speak numerous languages, including Korean, and engage in natural conversations with people. [Source: ChatGPT vs Claude Korean... | FindSkill.ai— Master any skill with AI](https://findskill.ai/ko/blog/chatgpt-vs-claude-hangugeo-bigyo-eoddeon-aiga-hangugeoreul-deo/) However, we must keep in mind that articles written by AI are fundamentally based on 'statistical probability.'

AI is a master of the 'probability game,' choosing the word most likely to follow based on the context. Because of this, it can create very elaborate and sophisticated sentences, but the 'actual experience of a person' or 'sincere contemplation' that fills the space between those sentences does not exist. As the *Atlantic* case clearly proves, there is a very high risk that articles written by AI remain 'hollow shells' that look good on the surface.

## What's Next

The media industry will continue to utilize AI as a simple auxiliary tool. Repetitive tasks such as correcting grammar or building the framework of a draft will be left to AI, but the core competencies of columnists and reporters—'original perspective' and 'heartfelt messages'—will remain the domain of humans for the time being.

Now, rather than being obsessed with distinguishing whether a piece was written by AI or a human, we must cultivate the discernment to examine what deep value that article gives us. In the end, it is entirely up to us to find true insight amidst the massive amount of text that AI pours out.
