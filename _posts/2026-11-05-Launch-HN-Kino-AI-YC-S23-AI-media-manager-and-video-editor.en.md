Imagine you are working on a complex video editing project. You are organizing the timeline, a colleague sitting next to you is leaving real-time feedback, and simultaneously, an 'AI assistant' is searching through the numerous video clips shot yesterday to find the necessary footage and placing it on the timeline. This process, which used to be cumbersome because everyone had to work separately and exchange files, now happens in real-time on a single screen.

'Kino AI,' which has recently been gaining attention in the video editing industry, is showcasing this very future. ### Easy Understanding: 'Video Lego Assembly' with AI

To explain the working principle of Kino AI simply, you can compare it to 'assembling Lego blocks.' If traditional video editing was a process where the user had to find and assemble thousands of blocks (video clips) one by one, Kino AI is like having a very smart AI assistant sitting next to you.

Kino AI is built based on 'Remotion' (a tool that uses web technology to create videos programmatically).
