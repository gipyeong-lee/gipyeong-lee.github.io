Imagine this: You follow a famous blogger you enjoy. Their writing flows naturally and clearly, as if a friend next to you were sharing an interesting story over coffee. Now, what if you discovered that this blogger is actually a company developing a specific AI model? And what if the AI service they created, "Claude," produced answers that felt quite different from that blog's style?

In the world of artificial intelligence, an interesting "temperature gap" in style has been observed. Why do the public-facing writings published by global AI firm Anthropic feel so different from the responses provided by their flagship AI assistant, Claude?

## Why does this matter?

We live in an era where it is becoming difficult to distinguish between human-written and AI-written text. What we must focus on in this context is "intent." The distinct gap between the writing Anthropic uses to describe its products and the language Claude uses with users is evidence that the company is strictly separating its brand voice from the persona of its AI. Simply put, the company is intentionally designing its blog to build a friendly, professional image, while engineering the AI to function as a polite, practical assistant. This serves as a first step toward understanding that as we communicate with AI in the future, it is moving beyond a simple mechanical answering device to possess a customized persona infused with corporate philosophy.

## An easy explanation

Anthropic's official writing style is very clear and fluent. It uses a comfortable tone that is easy to read, almost as if a seasoned blogger were conversing with the reader [Reference: WhyisAnthropic'spublicwritingstylesounlikeClaude's?](https://cmart.blog/claude-writing/). To use an analogy: if Anthropic's blog is like an explanation from a "smart and eloquent friend next door," then the AI Claude is like a "prepared assistant waiting to answer anything accurately."

Anthropic defines Claude [Reference: Claude](https://claude.com/) as a "safe, accurate, and secure AI assistant." Users ask this assistant to solve complex problems, analyze data, and write code [Reference: Claude](https://claude.ai/). An assistant must be polite and efficient rather than an emotional blogger. Perhaps that is why even Anthropic's staff seem to make an effort not to use Claude's specific response style in their own public writing. It is similar to a professional chef speaking like a polite critic when explaining their culinary philosophy, but precisely handling efficient tools while in the kitchen.

## Where are we now?

Currently, Anthropic's official materials are clearly distinguished from Claude's writing style [Reference: WhyisAnthropic'spublicwritingstylesounlikeClaude's?](https://cmart.blog/claude-writing/). This is not simply a limitation of the AI model; it indicates how Anthropic views Claude as a tool. Anthropic even occasionally treats Claude as if it were a "person," and in internal guidelines, they have even noted, "This document was written with Claude as the primary audience" [Reference: Talking to Claude: Why Anthropic treats its chatbot like a person—and not a tool - America Magazine](https://www.americamagazine.org/short-take/2026/02/12/talking-to-claude-why-anthropic-treats-its-chatbot-like-a-person-and-not-a-tool/).

Of course, the AI service Claude is also continuing to evolve. Anthropic expects that the code written by Claude has already reached a level nearly equivalent to that of humans, and anticipates even better performance next year [Reference: Anthropic says something unsettling has been happening to Claude](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/anthropic-says-something-unsettling-happening-103500529.html).

## What happens next?

As AI services become increasingly sophisticated, each company will struggle more intensely over what kind of "persona" to grant their AI. While "smart answers" have been the priority thus far, "what tone and atmosphere (tone and manner) to communicate with" will become a key competitive advantage. Much like Anthropic intentionally maintaining different voices for its blog and Claude, future AI is highly likely to showcase varying conversational styles depending on the personality of the brand we choose. What we must pay attention to is not just the content of the AI's answers, but the attitude with which those answers approach us.

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's perspective

Ultimately, this difference shown by Anthropic signifies that AI is evolving beyond an era where it answers everyone in the same tone, moving toward a stage where it forms its own independent language infused with the purpose of the service and the philosophy of the company. AI is now settling by our side not just as a tool for delivering information, but as a personality representing a company's brand values.
