Imagine this: You have an AI assistant that costs over $20 a month to use. At first, it felt reliable, as if this friend was seamlessly helping you with your work. But what if one day, it started leaving traces on your output saying, "I, the AI, made this"? Or what if it started doing less of the heavy lifting than you expected?

With generative AI services flooding the market, even those already paying for subscriptions are deeply contemplating, "Is this still worth the money?" Notably, news is circulating that a wave of cancellations is occurring among Claude users. What exactly is driving them away?

### Why does this matter?

We now live in an era of working alongside AI. Subscription services have become more than just the right to use a model; they have become a crucial factor in determining the efficiency, security, and nature of our work. Advanced AI models like Claude are not just simple chatbots; they are deeply involved in practical tasks such as coding, document writing, and data analysis. Therefore, changes in service policy or performance differences directly imply changes in how we work. Assessing the tools we subscribe to and choosing the services that are truly necessary for us is now an essential digital skill.

### Simple explanation: AI 'memory' and 'watermarks'

To use a simple analogy, Claude is like a very smart "intern." However, this intern has recently been causing users to reconsider in two respects.

First is the **AI watermark**. According to a [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/claude-users-cancel-subscriptions-citing-anthropic-new-ai-watermark-2026-8) report, some users are concerned about the AI watermark inserted into outputs. This is a sign proving that content was generated by AI, and users worry that having a mark indicating their creative work or code was generated by AI might negatively affect contracts with clients or professional settings.

Second is the **change in performance and usability**. According to one analysis, Claude's "Thinking" metrics declined over the first three months of this year, while API request volume spiked. [One tech blog's analysis](https://akoskm.substack.com/p/why-i-cancelled-my-claude-code-and) describes this situation as being like the "steam locomotive era," where AI companies are struggling between performance and efficiency due to server capacity issues, impacting the user.

Additionally, Anthropic recently integrated the memory systems of Claude chats and its work agent, "Claude Cowork." While Anthropic explains that ["no matter where you work, Claude starts based on what you already know"]([ZDNET](https://www.zdnet.com/article/anthropic-claude-and-cowork-share-memories-now-unless-you-opt-out/)), such integration may not feel entirely comfortable for all users in terms of personal data management.

### Current situation: How do I cancel?

If you have made up your mind to cancel your subscription, the method depends on your sign-up path.

*   **Web (claude.ai) or Desktop App**: After logging in via a web browser, click on your profile name or initials in the bottom left corner and navigate to the "Settings" menu. [[Source: Anthropic Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8325617-cancel-your-pro-or-max-subscription)]
*   **Android App**: If you signed up through an Android device, you must tap your profile initials in the top right corner of the app, then enter the "Billing" menu to cancel your subscription. [[Source: Anthropic Help Center](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/8325617-how-do-i-cancel-my-claude-pro-subscription)]

Note that as of June 15 this year, usage for programs like the Agent SDK or "claude-p" is managed separately from general conversational subscription quotas. [[Source: Note.com](https://note.com/honest_salvia397/n/n007b6847c126?hl=en)]

### What's next?

The AI market is currently seeing a wider range of user choices with the emergence of powerful competing models like OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol. [[Source: XDA Developers](https://www.xda-developers.com/cancelled-claude-6-months-daily-use-what-went-wrong/)]. Moving forward, users will move into an era of choosing models that best fit their work environment, have transparent security policies, and produce results aligned with their objectives, going beyond simply finding the "smartest AI." Rather than being tied to a specific model, it is a time when an "AI multi-persona" strategy of combining optimal tools according to your work type is necessary.
