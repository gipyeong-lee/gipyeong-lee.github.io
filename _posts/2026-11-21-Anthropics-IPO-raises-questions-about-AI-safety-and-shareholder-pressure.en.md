Imagine a world where the artificial intelligence (AI) technology we use every day advances dazzlingly, but the innovative companies creating that technology go public on the stock market and manage the money of countless shareholders. What choices will companies make between the solemn promises made at founding to prioritize technical safety above all else and the cold pressures of capital demanding massive returns for investors? This question is no longer a hypothetical scenario, but the hottest reality facing today's global tech ecosystem.

Recently, the global tech community has been buzzing with a massive event unprecedented in the history of artificial intelligence. Anthropic, one of the frontrunners in generative AI, has successfully entered the Nasdaq market and made its glamorous stock market debut [Source 2, Source 18]. This has drawn global attention as the first pure-play artificial intelligence company to be listed on a stock exchange [Source 10, Source 19]. However, behind this dazzling IPO celebration, fundamental questions and concerns are erupting among the general public, policymakers, and technical experts regarding the potential risks of AI and whether the pace of rapid data center construction should be slowed [Source 2].

---

## Why It Matters

Artificial intelligence, which has penetrated deep into our daily lives, has now gone beyond mere curiosity or toys to become a core social infrastructure supporting modern society. However, as AI models become more advanced, warnings about potential risks of getting out of human control and catastrophic risks are also intensifying [Source 19]. Anthropic's current listing goes beyond a mere individual company's entry into the stock market; it is directly connected to all of our lives as the first historical test bed where the public good of "AI development for the benefit of humanity" directly clashes with the capitalist instinct of "maximizing shareholder profits" [Source 5].

To use an analogy, it is like a car manufacturer declaring that it will not compromise on safety even if release is delayed slightly to thoroughly verify airbags and advanced braking systems for passenger safety, but then jumping into a battlefield where tens of thousands of shareholders demand immediate, massive dividends and stock price surges. If safety verification processes for AI are neglected or pushed to the back burner due to Wall Street's intense profit pressures, the fatal side effects will fall squarely on the public using the technology. For this reason, this IPO is not merely an interest of Wall Street investors, but a momentous social event that citizens around the world must watch with open eyes.

---

## Understanding Simply: The Tug-of-War Between Safety Guardrails and the Stock Market

To help understand, let's use a more concrete analogy. From its inception, Anthropic started out with a firm philosophy: "Safety comes before reckless speed. We will build safe AI that does not harm humanity." To realize this, the company was legally designed as a "Public Benefit Company" that must consider public missions alongside profit pursuit [Source 8]. 

However, astronomical funding is required to scale up the company and continue building cutting-edge AI research and massive data centers used by tens of millions of people. Ultimately, Anthropic chose an initial public offering (IPO) opening its doors to the stock market, pricing shares at $83 and raising approximately $8.1 billion on a pre-fee basis (an enormous amount reaching trillions of won) [Source 7, Source 18]. Starting trading on the Nasdaq market under the ticker symbol "ANT," the company sparked an enthusiastic response with its stock surging compared to the offering price by the close of its first trading day [Source 10, Source 18].

A structural tension arises precisely at this point. The moment a company goes public, its actual owners expand from founders or a small group of philosophical partners to an unspecified majority of public shareholders [Source 4, Source 8]. Regardless of the company's lofty philosophy, shareholders care most about how well the company is making money right now and how much their stock will rise. 

This is similar to a doctor who vowed to prioritize patient life and morality above all else, becoming the director of a large commercial hospital funded by massive capital, submitting huge performance reports to shareholders every month and suffering from sales pressure. Early investors and members of the AI safety community are expressing deep concern precisely at this point [Source 4]. 

---

## Current Situation: Glamorous Debut and Mixed Market Reactions

Anthropic's Nasdaq debut sent massive shockwaves through global financial markets. The stock opened above its $83 offering price, delivering massive wealth to some early investors and stakeholders while sparking unprecedented shockwaves across the broader market and reigniting AI bubble debates [Source 6, Source 7, Source 18]. Safety community figures—including Jaan Tallinn, an AI doomer (an expert warning of existential risks from AI) who recognized Anthropic's potential early on and led its $124 million Series A investment—are quietly raising voices of concern behind the glamour of this listing [Source 4]. 

The core of their worry is clear: amid intensifying short-term profit pressures from shareholders after going public, Anthropic's AI safety research and rigorous control measures, which it had maintained strictly since its founding, could be pushed to the background [Source 4, Source 14]. In fact, market reactions did not end with simple cheers. Immediately following the stock market debut, the stock surged during intraday trading, but subsequent heavy selling by retail investors brought heavy supply, creating mixed conditions across the broader stock and asset markets [Source 12]. This vividly shows that the market is confused, simultaneously weighing the massive potential of a giant AI company named Anthropic and its equally massive risks.

Critics and industry experts point out that while Anthropic captured the symbolic title of being a bona fide "first publicly traded AI-exclusive company," truly rigorous tests begin now [Source 10, Source 15]. No one has yet proven the answer to how to catch two rabbits at once: the founding promise to prioritize public interest and the continuous profit generation demanded by Wall Street's cold capital [Source 5, Source 14].

---

## What Lies Ahead

Anthropic's trajectory over the next few years will serve as the most crucial litmus test measuring the double standards and ethical benchmarks of the global artificial intelligence industry. First, the key will be how well the company can leverage its legal apparatus as a public benefit company to fend off shareholders' demands for short-term profit and pour consistent, unwavering budgets into AI safety research [Source 8, Source 14]. Second, having secured massive funding, how transparently it controls potential catastrophic risks arising from developing more advanced next-generation AI models will become the subject of sharp scrutiny from the public and policymakers [Source 2, Source 19].

While the pace of technological advancement far outstrips our imagination, institutions to wisely control that technology and preemptively defend against social side effects have always lagged a step behind. Anthropic's entry into the stock market is a powerful signal announcing that AI no longer remains a pure research subject inside a laboratory, but has jumped into the center of the massive capitalist market. Readers should note both how dazzling revenues AI companies post in the future and keep a sharp eye on whether they are truly guaranteeing our safety.
