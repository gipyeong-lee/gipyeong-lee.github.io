Imagine this: You have used your hard-earned money to buy shares in Anthropic, a promising AI company. Naturally, as a shareholder, you want the company to move in a better direction and hope to have a voice in board decisions. However, investors who carefully read the Initial Public Offering (IPO) documents were in for a surprise. They discovered that the authority to elect the board of directors—who are supposed to represent the shareholders' interests—does not lie with general shareholders, but with a mysterious "Trust" that few had even heard of.

As Anthropic's move toward a public listing becomes tangible, market interest is extending beyond simple AI technological prowess or revenue figures. A unique governance structure is coming to light, where the owners of the company—the shareholders—cannot actually control it.

### Why It Matters

The stock we invest in fundamentally includes "the right to reflect my opinion to monitor management and receive dividends from profits." However, the structure Anthropic is preparing diverges from typical publicly traded companies. Even if you buy shares in Anthropic, the authority to appoint the board of directors, the body effectively responsible for monitoring management, is held by a trust consisting of only three people. [Source: TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324928/20260819/anthropic-ipo-buyers-get-no-board-control-super-voting-founders-three-member-trust-govern.htm)

This means it is extremely difficult for general retail shareholders to voice their opinions on the company's future direction. Given Anthropic's nature as a Public Benefit Corporation (a corporate form that prioritizes the public good), this appears to be an attempt to control the risks of AI and fulfill social responsibilities; however, from an investor's perspective, questions regarding management transparency are inevitable. [Source: LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7517755855996383232)

### The Explainer

In simple terms, you can compare this structure to "running a school." If a typical company is a school where students vote to elect a student body president (director), Anthropic is a system where "three guardians of the school's founding philosophy (the Trust)" appoint the board of directors.

On top of this, Anthropic is empowering its founders. They are demanding a structure where 7 founders hold 50.1% of the voting rights on most management issues. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/25/anthropics-founders-seek-voting-control-ahead-of-ipo/)

To summarize, a double-lock system is in place: **"The Trust elects the board, and the founders exercise 50.1% of the voting power."** While this Trust holds only 5% of the total shares, it holds the powerful key of authority to elect the board of directors, which will determine the company's future. [Source: Wall Street Journal(Moomoo)](https://www.moomoo.com/news/post/1001538271/what-to-know-about-the-obscure-private-trust-looming-over), [Source: TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324928/20260819/anthropic-ipo-buyers-get-no-board-control-super-voting-founders-three-member-trust-govern.htm)

### Where We Stand

Currently, Anthropic is gauging investor reactions by disclosing this governance structure in its IPO filings ahead of the listing. While the market is focusing on its massive revenue growth, it is also raising warning voices regarding the "ballooning" phenomenon where costs are inflating like a balloon and its massive losses. [Source: Wall Street Journal(Moomoo)](https://www.moomoo.com/news/post/1001538271/what-to-know-about-the-obscure-private-trust-looming-over), [Source: Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/world/business/anthropic-ipo-5-points-to-know-behind-its-2-trillion-bet-42-billion-loss-and-warning-of-existential-ai-risks-to-humanity/articleshow/134565030.cms)

Some investors are questioning the market's expected valuation of $2 trillion, seeing the gap between the company's disclosed massive computing resource spending and its profitability. Investor anxiety is centered on whether the company's presented technical vision can actually translate into real profits. [Source: Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-ai/anthropic-ipo-2-trillion-valuation-loss-risks-10899549/), [Source: ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/aftershock-anthropic-ipo-investors-mark-valuation-down-15-trillion-growth-slowdown-sinks)

### What's Next

Anthropic's IPO is expected to become an economic benchmark for the entire AI industry. The key is how this "Long-Term Benefit Trust" will actually balance controlling AI risks and shareholder interests in board operations after the IPO, or if it will simply remain a tool to reinforce the founders' influence. Investors must now carefully watch not just the technological capabilities, but the influence this "invisible shadow board" will have on corporate operations. If conflicts of interest between the trust and shareholders arise, the repercussions are expected to pose a significant challenge for the public market as a whole.
