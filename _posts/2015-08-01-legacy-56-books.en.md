<p>
<span>
Everything can be interpreted in one way or another. There is no such thing as inherently good or bad events. Good, bad, helpful, harmful, excellent, ugly—the one who interprets it all is, in the end, yourself. However, one must realize that from the moment you interpret something, you are trapping yourself within that interpretation. In the end, you become captivated by your interpretation, and you begin to see things only from the vantage point where that interpretation emerges. In short, interpretations, or value judgments based on them, bind you so that you cannot move. Yet, without interpreting, one cannot organize a situation. This is the dilemma of interpreting life.
</span>
</p>
<p>
<span>
- Excerpt from the words of Nietzsche -
</span>
</p>
<p>
<span>
<br>
</span>
</p>
<p>
<span>
Indeed. Humans begin to form their own interpretations of things. And then, they become captured by those interpretations. It is not that interpretation itself is bad. However, I believe one must be careful not to let emotions cloud why that interpretation was formed in the first place. Because we are human, we cannot exclude 'emotion', but 'emotion' can cause us to misinterpret the intentions of others. Interpretations based on 'emotion' usually cause problems. Furthermore, interpreting others through hearsay leads to 'misunderstanding'. If you re-interpret information that has already been interpreted by someone else, that information is already tainted.
</span>
</p>
<div>
<span>
<br>
</span>
</div>
<div>
<span>
Therefore, when interpreting something, one should base it on what one has directly seen and heard, and interpret it through one's own objective judgment.
</span>
</div>

<p>
<b>
The interpretation of an interpretation breeds misunderstanding.
</b>
<br>
</p>
