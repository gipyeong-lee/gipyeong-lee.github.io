Imagine this: This morning, you asked AI to summarize meeting minutes to increase your work efficiency. A moment later, the AI provides a summary organized in smooth sentences. But suddenly, you have a thought: 'Can I really trust this text? Or did the AI just make it up?'

As AI-generated content becomes part of daily life, we are becoming more sensitive to 'who or what created this information.' Amidst this trend, Anthropic's AI model, Claude, is attempting a significant change. It is leaving an invisible mark on the content it creates, indicating that it was 'AI-generated.'

## Why is this important?

If the origin of the information we encounter in daily life is unclear, there is a risk of believing false information as fact or misunderstanding the depth of the information. This is especially true in education or copyright management.

Anthropic's Claude embeds machine-readable marks into the content it generates, clearly stating that this information was created by AI [Source: [How Claude marks AI-generated content](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content)]. This allows us to receive a more transparent context regarding the information we consume.

## In simple terms

It is easy to think of this mark as a type of 'digital seal' or 'invisible watermark.'

By way of analogy, it is similar to how invisible camera model names or shooting times are saved as data inside a photo when you take it with a photo app. When we view a photo, that information is not visible, but when necessary, we can open the file properties to find out when and with what device it was taken.

Likewise, text generated by Claude contains data that is invisible to the human eye but, when read by a machine, allows it to immediately recognize, "Ah, this text was written by Claude, an AI model!" [Source: [How Claude marks AI-generated content](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content)]. Through this, information transparency in digital spaces is significantly enhanced.

## Current situation

Currently, AI-generated content is being actively used in various places. Its scope has expanded from simple summaries to writing scripts for social media like Instagram [Source: [4 Claude Prompts That Generated 1.4M Instagram Views](https://instantdm.com/blog/claude-prompts-that-generated-instagram-views)], or creating complex data analysis and charts [Source: [What Is Claude 3.5 Sonnet?](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/claude-sonnet-anthropic)].

However, at the same time, efforts to distinguish whether a text was written by AI or not are fierce. Conversely, 'text humanizer' services are emerging that refine AI-written text to have more of a human touch [Source: [Humanize AI](https://humanizeaitext.ai/)]. There are even AI detector services in the market actively competing to verify if a text was written by AI [Source: [AI Detector - Accurate AI Checker](https://originality.ai/)]. As such, technical efforts to distinguish between content created by AI and content written by humans are becoming more complex.

## What will happen in the future?

As technology advances, tools to verify AI-generated content will become more sophisticated. The machine-readable marks introduced by Claude this time are highly likely to become a standard for building greater trust in the process of AI sharing information.

AI safety research companies like Anthropic are focusing on building reliable, understandable, and controllable AI systems [Source: [Newsroom Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news)]. In the future, it appears we will enter an era where AI and humans interact more safely and healthily through transparency devices like this one, amidst the diverse results AI creates.
