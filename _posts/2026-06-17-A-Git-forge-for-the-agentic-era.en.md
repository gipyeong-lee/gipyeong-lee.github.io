Imagine this. You wake up in the morning, open the work smartphone app you use every day, and find that the annoying screen-freezing bug you experienced last night has completely disappeared. You might be moved, thinking, "Did the development team stay up all night fixing it?" but in reality, not a single human was involved. While we were asleep in the early hours, artificial intelligence (AI) autonomously read user error reports, pinpointed the problematic code, fixed it, thoroughly completed the testing, and finished the automatic update. A world where AI autonomously finds and accomplishes what needs to be done, much like a magical unseen helper—isn't it truly fascinating?

Right now, behind the scenes of the global software industry, massive "digital civil engineering projects" are underway to turn such movie-like imagination into everyday reality. The virtual online spaces where human developers once gathered to work are being completely torn down and rebuilt from the ground up to match the overwhelming speed and working methods of AI. This is an inevitable phenomenon occurring as AI evolves from a simple "assistant tool" for coding into an independent worker, or an "agent." Let's take a step-by-step, easy-to-understand look at the massive seismic shift taking place in the software development world and why globally renowned big tech companies are staking their lives on building this new infrastructure.

## Why It Matters

What if the voice assistant on your smartphone went beyond simply transcribing your words, and instead autonomously opened a restaurant app, completed a reservation, and meticulously logged the event in your calendar? An exactly identical revolutionary change is happening in the world of software coding. And this is by no means just a "complex story for IT developers." This massive shift is a monumental event that will completely transform the speed and cost at which the countless mobile apps, banking systems, and online shopping mall programs we use every day are built and modified.

In the past, the entire process of writing computer code, finding errors among millions of lines of code, and carefully fixing them was strictly tailored to "human speed." It was the speed of a human drinking coffee, thinking deeply, gathering in a conference room with colleagues for a meeting, and then typing on a keyboard. However, today's AI can churn out massive amounts of code tens or hundreds of times faster than a human. In the brief three minutes it takes for a human to brew a cup of coffee, AI has acquired the incredible ability to read and fix tens of thousands of lines of code.

This is exactly where a fatal bottleneck occurs. Existing coding infrastructure—the online systems where developers gather code, review it, and collaborate—was never designed to handle such incredible speeds and overwhelming workloads in the first place. Cursor, a startup developing a famous AI-based coding tool, pointed out that "code is being generated faster than any existing infrastructure was built to handle" and defined this explosive period as the "Agentic era," where AI thinks and acts autonomously [Cursor · Origin](https://cursor.com/origin).

When this agentic era—where AI thinks for itself and takes initiative like a human—fully unfolds, the waiting time for innovative services or useful apps to hit the market will dramatically shorten. Even a small, underfunded one-person startup in your neighborhood will be able to exert a massive impact, as if it had hired dozens of skilled developers. This ultimately means that our everyday lives as consumers will evolve much faster and more abundantly than they do now.

## The Explainer

To fully grasp this monumental change, let's first translate two unfamiliar terms frequently used in the industry—"Git Forge" and "Agentic"—into everyday concepts we can easily understand.

First, what exactly is a "Git Forge"? Simply put, "Git" plays a role similar to the "track changes" feature in common word processors or a "save point" in a video game. It is a core system that meticulously records when, by whom, and how complex code was modified. And a "Forge" refers to a massive online factory or digital headquarters where all these countless code records are gathered together. Famous services like GitHub or GitLab are prime examples of a "Git Forge." Numerous developers around the world log into this online office every day to check the code they have worked on and merge it into one large program.

Second, what does "Agentic" mean? Expressed very simply, it means "the tendency to act autonomously and proactively." The simple chatbot AI of the past was like a passive robot that only moved when I gave a specific command like, "Bring me a glass of water." In contrast, agentic AI is an incredibly smart, highly perceptive secretary that grasps the situation and brings water, washes the empty cup, and stocks the fridge with bottled water for tomorrow, just by me casually mentioning, "I'm feeling a bit thirsty today."

The same goes for the coding world. Beyond merely writing a single line of code as instructed by a human, an agent AI autonomously combs through disgruntled bug reports from users, finds and fixes the broken code, meticulously reviews whether there are issues elsewhere, and finally, cleanly updates the documentation on its own.

By using an analogy, the current uproar in the software industry becomes very easy to understand.

Up until now, a "Git Forge" was a traditional analog office where humans walked around handing over paper approval documents and gathered in conference rooms to drink coffee and discuss. But suddenly one day, tens of thousands of AI secretaries—who never get tired and work at the speed of light—started showing up to work at this old office all at once. Naturally, the narrow hallways tailored to walking humans and the slow approval systems (existing server infrastructure) could no longer handle the massive volume of work and approval requests these countless AI secretaries pour out every second. It's a scene of utter chaos, with documents getting jammed in doors and approval boards flying through the air.

Therefore, the software industry is currently boldly tearing down the existing buildings where humans used to work, and completely designing and building a "high-speed dedicated office exclusively for AI"—a "Git Forge for the agentic era"—where AI secretaries can fly freely on electromagnetic waves, process tens of thousands of coding tasks in 0.1 seconds, and confer amongst themselves [Cursor · Origin](https://cursor.com/origin).

## Where We Stand

So, how far along is this digital construction project to build a massive "AI-exclusive office"? Huge global big tech companies and agile, innovative startups are waging a fierce turf war to seize the initiative, and reality is already moving quickly, far surpassing our imagination.

GitHub, the home for developers worldwide and the largest coding repository, unexpectedly released a preview version of an astonishing technology called "GitHub Agentic Workflows," a joint project between the GitHub Next lab and Microsoft Research, in February 2026 [GitHub Agentic Workflows Complete Guide - AI Automates Issues and PRs...](https://knightk.tistory.com/296). Inside this new system, the previously described AI agents take up residence and live entirely within GitHub's automation tools (GitHub Actions). And without human prompting, they autonomously and logically categorize newly raised problems (issues) by severity, sharply review code written by humans with eagle eyes, automatically add test code to prevent bugs, and effortlessly update related guide documents to the latest status [GitHub Agentic Workflows Complete Guide - AI Automates Issues and PRs...](https://knightk.tistory.com/296).

This is a tremendous technological leap. Eddie Aftandilian, Principal Researcher at GitHub, emphasizes that this is not merely the addition of a convenient new feature to an existing service. He declared that it marks the beginning of a completely new software engineering paradigm called "Continuous AI," where machines think for themselves and manage code, far surpassing the traditional systems (CI/CD, Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment) that used to automatically assemble and deploy code in the past [GitHub Agentic Workflows Complete Guide 2026 — AI Agent CI/CD Automation...](https://knightk.tistory.com/410).

Competitor GitLab is also not missing out on the massive wave of the times. GitLab announced a major platform update designed squarely for the "agentic engineering era." In particular, for large enterprises like banks or hospitals where security is life or death, they declared they would fully support the lightning-fast working speed of AI agents while simultaneously providing an intricate control and security system ensuring that human administrators—the sovereigns of the system—never lose control [GitLab: Built for the agentic engineering era](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/gitlab-transcend-announcements/).

Beyond the movements of giant corporations, unconventional attempts starting from a blank slate on an entirely new foundation are also drawing attention. The aforementioned startup Cursor is boldly discarding sluggish human infrastructure and has officially announced the launch of an entirely newly built platform for the agentic era named "Origin," and is currently accepting sign-ups for its waitlist [Cursor · Origin](https://cursor.com/origin).

Furthermore, in the open-source (free, public code) camp where developers around the world participate voluntarily, a project named `a5c-ai/forge` is gaining significant attention. From the very beginning, they are minimizing human intervention and building a decentralized code platform tailored solely for AI agents (agent-native distributed codeforge). They have completely integrated a large-scale collaboration system directly into the platform, where a massive number of agents can communicate with each other while writing code [a5c-ai/forge: git-backed agent-native distributed codeforge, agentic...](https://github.com/a5c-ai/forge).

Of course, there are areas of concern as well. Allowing AI to freely tinker with critical computer code on its own without any restrictions is a terrifying prospect that could potentially lead to massive financial accidents or hospital system paralysis. That is why powerful control and verification mechanisms are rapidly developing alongside it to safely reign in this immense power.

Sonar, a company specializing in code quality verification, has suddenly introduced a new inspection methodology called the "Agent Centric Development Cycle" (AC/DC for short). This is a cutting-edge technology that strictly monitors and verifies whether the code written by an AI agent is as consistent as if written by a trained human expert, whether the logical decision-making process was transparently recorded, and above all, whether the code was written in a trustworthy manner safe enough for us to use with peace of mind [Code Verification for the AI Era | Sonar](https://www.sonarsource.com/).

Lastly, tools acting as "guardrails" that prevent accidents and ensure AI does not make bizarre mistakes while performing complex tasks are proving remarkable results in practical field applications. The Python-based "Forge" framework created by developer Antoine Zambelli is an excellent example of this [GitHub - antoinezambelli/forge: A Python framework for self ...](https://github.com/antoinezambelli/forge).

To use an analogy, it is like installing "bowling bumpers" that securely block both sides so the ball does not fall into the gutter for a young child going to the bowling alley for the first time. When this guardrail technology was applied, even a lightweight, small-scale AI (an 8B parameter model, meaning a light model with about 8 billion adjustable neural connections) capable of running on a standard personal laptop rather than a massive supercomputer miraculously saw its success rate in autonomously completing complex tasks jump from a mere 53% to an astounding 99% [Forge Review: 8B Local Model Hits 99% on Agentic Tasks](https://andrew.ooo/posts/forge-guardrails-self-hosted-llm-agentic-review/). A novice AI that used to wander around making mistakes half the time transformed into a veteran expert that executes perfectly 99 times out of 100. These technologies are performing the crucial role of a sturdy "harness" (a strong safety belt structure that prevents falls at a construction site) that helps AI safely handle complex, real-world production tasks such as developing new features or meticulous code reviews [Forge—Agentic Coding Harness from forgecode.d - TeamDay.ai](https://www.teamday.ai/harness/forgecode).

## What's Next

The future software development site will no longer be the exclusive domain of human engineers staring intensely at a laptop screen in a dark room and manually typing out the alphabet. Human developers will evolve into the role of creative "master architects" who draw the big picture of what to build and issue core directives.

Then, countless AI agents will act as powerful and reliable "workers" who take those instructions, draw complex blueprints in 0.1 seconds, and stack bricks day and night to instantly complete magnificent digital buildings. In this new era where the generation speed of pouring out code has completely overwhelmed the capacity of existing systems [Cursor · Origin](https://cursor.com/origin), the adoption of "AI-exclusive offices" called agentic Git forges is no longer a choice but an essential condition for the survival of IT companies.

Before long, we will face a completely new world where, when a critical error occurs in a frequently used financial app or messenger, there is no need to wait in frustration for the responsible human developer to come to work in the morning, drink coffee, and turn on the computer. An astonishing daily reality is already unfolding before our eyes, where artificial intelligence instantly detects errors in real time, gathers among itself in agent-exclusive offices at faster-than-light speeds to discuss solutions, and automatically deploys a perfectly repaired, normal version to the smartphones of tens of millions of users in just five minutes.

## AI's Take

If past IT innovations were about putting "faster and better tools" into human hands, like a calculator or an excavator, we have now entered an era of building a massive "new stage" where the tools themselves can work freely and run wild. It also means that AI is beginning to be recognized as a complete and reliable virtual colleague, going beyond a mere typewriter that just types out code on our behalf.

Humans will now focus entirely on fundamental value creation—"what" and "why" to build—instead of agonizing over "how" to write the code. A change in infrastructure means a change in the way we work, and when this massive Forge for agents is finally completed, the pace of software development will dazzlingly accelerate into unknown territories that humanity has never experienced before.
