In an era where asking an AI assistant for help with work has become as habitual as waking up every morning, what would happen if the cost of using AI hit $500 a month? Recently, the tech industry has been buzzing with discussion over a new subscription service OpenAI appears to be preparing: 'Pro Max.'

While there has been no official announcement from OpenAI, developers have discovered traces of this new subscription plan within the ChatGPT service's internal code and web interface scripts. What features does this service hold, and why would such a high-priced subscription emerge?

## Why does this matter?

This news suggests that the center of gravity in the AI market is shifting from 'general users' to 'enterprises and experts who require high performance.' While current subscription services act as tools to aid individual productivity, the newly spotted Pro Max is targeted at high-level environments where users must handle massive datasets or solve complex coding problems in real-time.

A subscription fee of $500 a month is more than 2.5 times the price of existing services. This implies more than just offering additional features; it is evidence that AI services are establishing themselves as essential infrastructure for businesses. To easily understand the Pro Max subscription, imagine a 'luxury kitchen.' A standard restaurant (current subscribers) uses a public kitchen to turn out dishes, but a top-tier restaurant (Pro Max users) requires a private chef and faster, more powerful dedicated burners.

The 'dedicated burners' here correspond to the 'fastest Work and Codex performance.'
