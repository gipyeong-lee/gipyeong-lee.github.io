Imagine this: You have important digital spaces your family knows nothing about, or perhaps a final, heartfelt letter you want to share. If you were to leave this world suddenly, what would happen to this precious information?

Recently, many people have been struggling with managing digital assets and passwords. While 'Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)' serves as a powerful shield during your lifetime, it often becomes a major barrier that prevents family members from accessing a deceased person's accounts [Source: Funeral.com, Inc.](https://funeral.com/blogs/the-journal/how-to-create-a-digital-vault-for-passwords-and-2fa-before-you-die). A unique idea has emerged to solve this dilemma: introducing 'Seal.'

## Why Should You Prepare?

In the digital age, we live surrounded by countless accounts and passwords. If your beloved family can access your smartphone or devices after you pass away, it can be a great help in preserving cherished memories, settling subscription services, or retrieving important contacts [Source: Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/advice-password-recovery-after-death/).

However, many people feel that such preparations are vague or are reluctant to write down their passwords due to security concerns. Seal is an attempt to clearly and securely solve the task of 'posthumous delivery' of digital information.

## In Simple Terms, What Is This App?

You can easily think of Seal as a 'digital-age time capsule' or a 'sealed letter envelope.'

When developing this app, the creator contemplated a method as reliable as a 'physical key' in the software world, just as we use physical keys to open our front doors [Source: Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49763311). It is essentially taking the analog method—putting a letter and password in a paper envelope to be opened only under specific conditions—and transferring it directly into the digital world.

This app is currently available for free on iPhone and iPad [Source: El Ecosistema Startup](https://ecosistemastartup.com/seal-app-para-que-tu-familia-abra-tus-secretos-cuando-mueras/). Users can create 'sealed envelope'-style digital data containing letters, photos, audio, videos, or important password files they wish to pass on to their family. This data is designed to be opened only on the device of a trusted person you have designated when you are no longer here [Source: Seal Messenger](https://sealmessenger.com/).

## How Does It Currently Work?

Seal is an open-source project designed to work only on the user's device without going through servers [Source: Seal Messenger](https://sealmessenger.com/).

Typical cloud services store your information on external servers, which can lead to anxiety about data breaches. However, since Seal uses a method where the user directly controls their data, there is no need to pay separate subscription fees, and individual digital secrets can be stored safely without external servers [Source: Seal Messenger](https://sealmessenger.com/).

## Future Challenges

The emergence of Seal poses an important question: 'Who is responsible for my digital assets?' Moving forward, this type of digital legacy management service will become more commonplace. However, users should always remember to back up their data securely on their own and establish a plan to deliver information to their designated recipients in advance, regardless of the service being used [Source: Funeral.com, Inc.](https://funeral.com/blogs/the-journal/how-to-create-a-digital-vault-for-passwords-and-2fa-before-you-die).
