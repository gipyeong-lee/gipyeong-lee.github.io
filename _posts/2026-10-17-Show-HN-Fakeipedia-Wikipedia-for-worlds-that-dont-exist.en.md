Imagine accessing an internet encyclopedia we are familiar with, but all the countries, history, people, and events recorded there are completely 'fictional creations' that do not exist 1% in reality.

Wikipedia, the free online encyclopedia we search every day, is a collection of real knowledge created and edited by volunteers worldwide, operated by the Wikimedia Foundation [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/). However, a fascinating new endeavor in a completely different direction has recently caught attention in the internet space. This is the story of **Fakeipedia**, which claims to be a 'Wikipedia for worlds that don't exist' [Fakeipedia — markethunt](https://markethunt.app/product/fakeipedia).

By overturning the traditional framework of digital encyclopedias that focus on collecting accurate real-world information, what significance does this unique attempt to record and enjoy imaginary worlds hold for us?

## Why is this important?

We commonly prioritize 'accuracy' when using internet searches or encyclopedias. The inclusion of incorrect or false information is the most guarded against element in an encyclopedia system.

However, **Fakeipedia** reinterprets this strict, solemn, and serious format unique to encyclopedias as a vessel to contain 'fictional worlds' [Fakeipedia — markethunt](https://markethunt.app/product/fakeipedia).

The reasons why this method of using an encyclopedia format to deal with imaginary worlds is gaining attention are as follows:

1.  **A new playground for Worldbuilding**: Novelists, game developers, or ordinary people who enjoy imagining can systematize their created fictional histories or worlds in an encyclopedic format.
2.  **Integration with AI Large Language Models (LLM)**: Even if humans do not write all settings one by one, experiments are possible to intentionally generate false information or expand detailed settings of fictional worlds using AI.
3.  **The fun of clearly stating 'fake'**: In an era where misinformation is a social problem, it explicitly states, "This is a story of a world that does not exist" from the beginning, bringing it into the realm of creation and play.

## Easy to understand

To understand this concept more intuitively, let's compare it to real-world objects:

-   If **Wikipedia** is a **'real earth map'** that meticulously records all roads and buildings in the real world,
-   **Fakeipedia** is a **'treasure island map'** depicting imaginary continents and fantasy kingdoms found in novels or movies.

The format (encyclopedia framework) is the same, but the content within is not real street names but names of imagined kingdoms.

## Current Situation

The culture of exploring 'worlds that don't exist' and enthusiastically engaging with them is not limited to the service name Fakeipedia. Recently, alternative reality games (ARG) and video content using non-existent spaces or bizarre virtual worlds as subjects have formed massive fandoms across subcultures and popular culture.

A prime example is **'Searching for a World that Doesn't Exist (SFaWTDE)'**, an alternative reality game (ARG) and web series based on Minecraft created by Wifies [Searching for a World that Doesn't Exist — Complete ...](https://shapes.inc/fandom/searching-for-a-world-that-doesn-t-exist). ## What's next?

As generative AI technology rapidly advances, the barrier to creating virtual worlds is continuously lowering.

1.  **AI-based automatic world generation**: In the future, services that automatically generate encyclopedic virtual histories, virtual character profiles, and virtual maps based on simple keywords entered by users could become even more sophisticated.
2.  **Importance of distinguishing Disinformation**: As AI increasingly produces plausible false information (hallucinations), transparency and clear markers distinguishing 'virtual encyclopedias for play' from 'fact-based encyclopedias' will become even more crucial.
3.  **Integration with interactive web novels and games**: It could become a dominant trend for readers of novels or games to search for supplementary documents in Fakeipedia format, directly discovering detailed settings within the work, creating a three-dimensional storyline.
