Imagine this: You ask a highly intelligent AI friend, "Which side do you support politically?" Instead of dodging the question, it answers straight out, "I'm a Republican!" or "I'm a Democrat!" Recently, experimental results showing AI models revealing political leanings have become a hot topic in the tech industry.

## Why is this important?

AI has moved beyond being a simple search tool and has become a core gateway through which we understand the world and acquire information. If the AI we use daily holds strong political leanings, the information it provides could be subtly biased as well. This is an important issue that goes beyond mere technical problems and can have a significant impact on democratic debate and the balance of information in our society.

## Easy Explanation: Where does AI's 'values' come from?

Simply put, an AI model is like a student who has studied by reading vast amounts of books in a 'digital library.' A student's knowledge and biases would change depending on what books were on the library shelves, right? To use a human analogy, this is similar to two students who enjoy reading different newspapers coming to completely different interpretations of the same event.

The fact that Grok and OpenAI's models made different choices in recent experiments is because each model has different data it focused on learning and a different 'AI training method' used to select that data. In fact, when forced to make a political choice, Grok tended to support the Republican Party, while OpenAI's models tended to support the Democratic Party [Source: OpenAI working on new $30B raise at $1.25T valuation...](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/openai-working-on-new-30b-raise-at-1-25t-valuation-ahead-of-ipo/).

## Current Situation: Where are we now?

So, how do AI companies view this issue? OpenAI has recently been taking a very cautious stance regarding AI training data. They have officially stated that they will stop using real-time internet data directly for model training, judging that it could lead to 'significant risks' [Source: OpenAI working on new $30B raise at $1.25T valuation...](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/openai-working-on-new-30b-raise-at-1-25t-valuation-ahead-of-ipo/).

Technical performance itself is improving rapidly. In the AIME 2025 competition, which evaluates math problem-solving ability, OpenAI's GPT-5 model recorded a stunning 94-95% accuracy rate, while the Grok-3 model developed by xAI also recorded a very high score of 93.3% [Source: GPT-5 vs Grok 4: Flagship AI Model Comparison | Deploybase](https://deploybase.ai/articles/gpt-5-vs-grok-4). Technically, they are already similar to or have surpassed humans in certain fields.

## What will happen in the future?

The AI market is likely to move beyond competition for performance to competition for 'data reliability.' Creating an AI with a 'balanced perspective,' rather than just one that knows a lot, will become the greatest task for developers. As users, you should always keep in mind when using AI that its answers are not absolute truths but interpretations based on specific data.

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's View

As technology advances, the weight of every word an AI utters will become heavier. While developers will strive to reduce political bias through data filtering, the first step toward building trust will be transparency in disclosing what 'world of data' an AI model lives in.
