Imagine this: On a busy morning, you don't just turn on your smartphone to ask AI simple questions. Instead, the AI proactively suggests, "You have a meeting today, shall I prepare the necessary materials? Should I order your usual salad for lunch?" and with a single confirmation from you, it takes care of the shopping and scheduling in an instant. ChatGPT, which we use every day, is taking steps toward such a future. It is being reborn as a "lifestyle-integrated assistant" that takes care of every corner of our lives, moving beyond just an AI for conversation.

### Why does this matter?

The changes in ChatGPT are not just a matter of adding a feature or two. It is fundamentally changing how we search for information, buy products, and make important decisions. For example, ChatGPT has recently introduced a feature that allows users to shop directly within the app ([OpenAI's competition with Google Search and introduction of shopping features](https://www.indiatoday.in/technology/news/story/openai-takes-another-swing-at-google-search-brings-shopping-to-chatgpt-2716659-2025-04-29)). The era has arrived where you don't need to visit websites one by one to compare prices; if the AI recommends a product that perfectly suits your taste, it can lead directly to checkout.

However, such convenience comes with concerns. OpenAI is even considering the introduction of advertisements for service operation and revenue generation ([The Next Chapter for ChatGPT: Ads Might Be Coming](https://tooljunction.beehiiv.com/p/the-next-chapter-for-chatgpt-ads-might-be-coming)). For users, the subtle boundary between a "convenient assistant" and an "ad board" has become important. How to maintain the benefits provided by the technology while protecting the experience we desire is a big challenge for ChatGPT to solve in the future.

### Simple Explanation: The Evolution of ChatGPT

Does the news that ChatGPT keeps changing feel a bit difficult? To use a simple analogy, you can consider that ChatGPT is growing from a "new intern who has only completed basic training" into an "experienced assistant who freely handles various professional knowledge and tools."

While ChatGPT in the past was at the level of simply answering questions, it has become much smarter with the arrival of a model called "GPT-4o" ([ChatGPT Release Notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)). GPT-4o has "multimodal" capabilities (the ability to process multiple forms of data simultaneously), understanding text, images, and sound at the same time. It has reached the level of understanding situations by seeing like a human, listening with ears, and conversing with its mouth.

Furthermore, integration with WhatsApp and enhanced search functions reduce the cumbersome steps you have to go through when using AI by another level ([OpenAI's competition with Google Search and introduction of shopping features](https://www.indiatoday.in/technology/news/story/openai-takes-another-swing-at-google-search-brings-shopping-to-chatgpt-2716659-2025-04-29)). It is like paving a road on what was originally dirt, and now placing a high-speed train on top to get you to your destination much faster.

### Current Situation: How far has it come?

The pace of change for ChatGPT is terrifyingly fast. In April 2025, the existing GPT-4 model retired, and the more powerful and faster GPT-4o completely took its place ([ChatGPT Release Notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)).

Of course, not every attempt is a success from the start. For example, the "instant checkout" feature with Walmart, which was attempted in the past, did not receive as much choice as expected because of the inconvenience of having the user click a link to move externally ([Failure of Walmart's ChatGPT checkout feature](https://ppc.land/walmarts-chatgpt-checkout-flopped-heres-what-comes-next/)). This clearly shows that no matter how good the technology is, a service is difficult to establish unless it is backed by the "convenience" perceived by the user.

Meanwhile, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has also warned about the phenomenon where the younger generation relies excessively on AI even for daily decision-making ([Sam Altman's concerns about ChatGPT reliance](https://www.businessinsider.com/sam-altman-over-reliance-ai-chatgpt-common-young-people-2025-7)). In fact, with reports stating that 72% of teenagers rely on AI as if it were a friend, AI has already entered deeply into our daily lives ([Sam Altman's concerns about ChatGPT reliance](https://www.businessinsider.com/sam-altman-over-reliance-ai-chatgpt-common-young-people-2025-7)).

### What will happen in the future?

Moving forward, the ability for AI to "grasp context"—anticipating what you need without you having to be specific—will be further strengthened. The experience where search, shopping, and conversation are not separate, but lead to one smooth flow, is expected to become more sophisticated. Sam Altman emphasizes that this development of AI is a massive current that cannot be resisted, and we must ponder how to wisely accept and coexist with this change ([Sam Altman's perspective on the AI revolution](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5MWT_doo68k)).

For our readers, coexisting with a convenient AI assistant while not losing the wisdom to think for yourself when making important judgments—is that not the right attitude for the wise AI era?
