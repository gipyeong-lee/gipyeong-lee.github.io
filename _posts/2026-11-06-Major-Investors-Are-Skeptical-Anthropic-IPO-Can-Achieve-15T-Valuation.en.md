Imagine this: You hear news that the AI service you use daily for work or study is making its debut on the stock market. How would you feel if headlines poured in claiming the company's value would reach an astonishing $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion?

The hottest topic in the AI industry recently is undoubtedly the Nasdaq IPO of the AI company Anthropic. This massive story became official on October 26, 2026, when Anthropic submitted its initial public offering (IPO) paperwork to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)[17]. With the listing expected in November, the market is watching this 'big event' with complex eyes, a mix of expectation and worry[16].

### Why is this important?

Why is a simple listing announcement attracting so much attention? It is due to the scale. The public listing of major AI companies like Anthropic can serve as a catalyst to supply tens of billions of dollars in fresh capital to the market[4]. It provides investors with attractive new options beyond traditional stocks or cryptocurrencies[5]. However, there is also concern that if too high a price is set, it could pose risks to the market as a whole[16].

### Easy to understand: How is corporate value calculated?

What is corporate value? Simply put, you can think of it as a 'price tag' that combines a company's technical prowess, growth potential, and the money it will earn in the future. To use an analogy, it's like setting the salary for a promising rookie player; instead of looking just at current stats, you calculate in advance the probability of winning and the popularity the player will bring to the team.

Anthropic is famous for developing the AI model Claude. Investors and investment banks highly value the AI world this company will build in the future and have attached an astronomical IPO target price tag of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion[7, 16].

However, much like someone saying housing prices are "too expensive" because of a bubble, some major investors are expressing skepticism about whether Anthropic can prove this level of value immediately upon listing[15]. This is partly because Anthropic has a structure where it must carry out a unique mission—'developing AI for the long-term benefit of humanity'—rather than just being a company chasing profit[6]. In other words, the core challenge is how to simultaneously catch two rabbits: profit maximization and the ethical value of technology.

### Current Status: Towards the Nasdaq Debut

Anthropic has nearly finished preparations for its listing. It officially submitted its IPO paperwork on October 26, disclosing its financial status, and investors are confirming the company's rapid growth rate through these documents[17].

However, how public and expert concerns about 'potential dangers of AI' or 'technological safety' will affect the corporate valuation remains a point of debate[9]. Nevertheless, Anthropic is cruising toward its Nasdaq debut in November 2026[16].

### What happens next?

Anthropic's IPO will serve as a barometer for how the entire AI industry will be treated in the stock market going forward. Investors are watching with bated breath to see if Anthropic can actually demonstrate growth commensurate with the high value it has set, or if it will face a cold assessment by the market after listing. For the general public, it will be an interesting point where AI technology is proven as more than just convenience, but as massive economic value.
