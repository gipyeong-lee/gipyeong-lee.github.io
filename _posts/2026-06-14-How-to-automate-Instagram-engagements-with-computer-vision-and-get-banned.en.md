## The Explainer: Robots in Invisibility Cloaks vs. Advanced AI Guards

Exactly how do these modern automation bots utilizing 'computer vision' work, and conversely, how does Instagram know with ghostly precision whether it's a human or a cold machine touching the phone?

### The Mechanism of Computer Vision Bots: Robots That 'Read' Screen Pixels
Old, traditional macro programs were built simply to blindly click specific coordinates on a monitor screen (e.g., width 500 pixels, height 800 pixels). However, because the Instagram website or app features a 'Dynamic UI' (a screen design that flexibly adapts to situations) where the positions of buttons constantly change depending on the user's screen size or smartphone model [How to automate Instagram engagements with computer vision (and get banned)](https://blog.florianherrengt.com/how-to-automate-instagram-engagements.html), this simple coordinate-clicking method quickly breaks down and clicks empty space if the page changes even slightly.

To solve this fatal flaw, hackers brought out 'computer vision' technology. Simply put, a bot equipped with this technology captures and analyzes the entire smartphone screen as a real-time photo, much like human eyes. When a programmer instructs, "find the red heart-shaped pixel pattern somewhere on the screen," the bot accurately locates the heart icon within the complex picture and moves the mouse cursor directly to it. It elaborately mimics human vision and hand movements.

### Instagram AI Detector: Finding the 'Uncanny Valley' of Behavior
Hearing the explanation above, it might sound like robots can perfectly deceive humans, but the reality is different. To use an analogy, imagine an incredibly sensitive and smart guard (the Instagram AI detector) standing at the entrance of the world's most exclusive private club you run.

One night, a robot (computer vision bot) wearing an invisibility cloak and holding a forged fake ID lines up to enter the club pretending to be human. The robot is confident it has perfectly mimicked human appearance. But before the guest even opens its mouth, the guard instantly realizes it is not human and kicks it out of the club. How on earth did they know?

The answer lies in the fact that 'humans never move as perfectly as machines.' Real people scroll through the app at uneven speeds because they are reading text; their mouse cursors or fingers don't move in perfectly straight lines but tremble slightly or draw curved arcs. The time intervals between clicking the like button vary every single time. They might swipe past one photo in 3 seconds, but stare at a friend's photo for 10 seconds before double-tapping the screen to leave a heart.

On the other hand, the robot's behavior is so 'perfect and mechanical' that it actually arouses suspicion. The mouse cursor shoots toward the button in a mathematically calculated, perfectly straight line of shortest distance, clicks likes at a breathtakingly constant interval without even 0.5 seconds of error, and follows thousands of unknown people a day without sleeping for 24 hours. These are behavioral patterns that a human could never perform.
