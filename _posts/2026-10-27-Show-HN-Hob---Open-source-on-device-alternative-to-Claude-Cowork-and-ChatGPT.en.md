Imagine this: You wake up, turn on your computer, and tell the AI, "Please summarize and organize my project documents." Usually, this task involves sending your documents across the internet to a giant corporation's server for processing. What if those documents contain sensitive personal information or important company secrets? You might hesitate to use AI out of security concerns.

Recently, a new attempt to alleviate such worries has been unveiled. It's called **'Hob.'** It's a new open-source desktop app where AI operates directly within your computer, instead of on a cloud server.

### Why is this important?

Most AI work tools we have used until now were 'cloud-based.' Your files had to be sent to the servers of the companies operating the AI models for it to understand the content and return results. While convenient, this had limitations: it was difficult to use in environments where corporate security is paramount or where internet connectivity is unstable.

The arrival of Hob is changing this landscape. 'Working on your computer' means there is no risk of data leaking to the outside. In other words, a very powerful alternative has emerged for users who prioritize personal privacy and security. ### Understanding it simply

To understand Hob, let's compare it to a **'digital private tutor.'** 

Existing cloud-based AI is like hiring a tutor from a distant tutoring academy. You write down the problems and send them to the academy, and the instructor checks the content and sends back a reply. But Hob is like inviting a private tutor directly into your home study. All the studying happens right there on your computer (the study), with no need for data to leave the house (an external server).

Technically speaking, Hob runs AI brains like Gemma 4 or gpt-oss—which are 'open-weight models' (models using the AI core architecture released by AI developers)—directly on your computer hardware. Thanks to this, you can utilize the intelligence of AI without a cloud connection. ### Current status

Hob is a project recently released as open-source by developer Christian. The app supports macOS, Windows, and Linux environments, and it follows the MIT license, which anyone can use for free. ### What will happen in the future?

Hob is a symbolic case showing how rapidly the local AI ecosystem is growing. Users now go beyond mere AI convenience and attach importance to sovereignty—asking, 'Am I in control of my data?' If more high-performance models can be run lightly on personal PCs in the future, the era will come where almost all work is safely processed on your own computer without heavy cloud dependency.
