Imagine this: You ask an AI at work this morning to "summarize this week's meeting materials," and it invents information that wasn't there, creating a completely different report. You might just laugh it off, thinking, "That was a close one." But what if this mistake didn't happen in personal work, but in a military operation where the fates of nations were at stake?

A shocking incident within the US Department of Defense and military field has recently been revealed by foreign media. Last spring, the US military reached the point of nearly conducting an armed operation against a Chinese vessel sailing in the Middle East, only to cancel it at the last minute. The fuse for this tense operation was none other than a "fake report" generated by artificial intelligence.

### Why is this incident important?

This event serves as a massive wake-up call for every organization introducing AI into their operations. We often think of AI as a smart assistant, but sometimes the answers it presents with such confidence can be baseless fiction. While artificial intelligence excels at structuring logical narratives based on the data we provide, its ability to discern whether that information is factual is not yet perfect.

Especially in domains where "trust" is paramount—such as military, security, finance, and medicine—when incorrect information generated by AI leads to actual actions, it can go beyond simple errors to cause diplomatic disasters or national crises like war. As the trend of prioritizing the use of AI in global security strategies accelerates, the question of how much to trust and how to verify AI-generated data has become a critical task directly linked to all of our lives. [[Source: HapnGo](https://hapngo.org/)]

### Easy to Understand: What is AI 'Hallucination'?

First, we must understand the "Hallucination" phenomenon, the culprit in this incident. Artificial intelligence hallucination refers to a phenomenon where AI generates false information as if it were true, constructing statistically plausible sentences regardless of whether they are factual.

To put it simply, it’s like a storyteller with a flashy way of speaking who has browsed all the books in a library but doesn't accurately remember the content. If the questioner asks, "Are there nuclear weapon components on the Chinese ship?" the storyteller combines words seen in the library without fact-checking and fabricates a "plausible" story in a convincing tone. The US military analyst in this case also received this "plausible story" generated by the AI chatbot and believed it to be real intelligence. [[Source: Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/report-us-almost-boarded-chinese-ship-over-hallucinated-ai-arms-report/)]

### Current Situation: The Tensity Before the Operation

The details of the incident are as follows: While analyzing information using an AI tool, an analyst from the US Special Operations Command produced a shocking report that a Chinese ship passing through the Middle East was transporting components related to a nuclear weapons program. This report immediately became the basis for military action, and the US military prepared an armed operation to detain the vessel. [[Source: Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/report-us-almost-boarded-chinese-ship-over-hallucinated-ai-arms-report/)]

It was a tense situation, with military aircraft actually sortieing, just moments before the operation was to commence. However, fortunately, in the process of re-verifying the reliability of the information, US defense authorities discovered that the content was "entirely false." According to officials, this incident was a dangerous moment that nearly started a war and had a high potential to lead to an actual armed conflict between the US military and China. [[Source: CNN Politics](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/18/politics/us-military-ai-false-intelligence-china-ship)]

### What happens next?

Since this incident, concerns about the safety of AI systems within the Pentagon have reached an extreme high. There are also movements to re-examine the speed at which AI is utilized in operational fields immediately. Moving forward, the construction of powerful guardrails—where AI-provided answers are cross-verified and humans make the final judgment rather than accepting the AI's output at face value—will be more important than ever. It has become clear that as technology advances, we must not rely on AI and become lazy, but rather cultivate "AI Literacy" to verify information more meticulously. [[Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/ai-hallucination-nearly-triggered-us-military-operation/)]

AI is merely our tool; the responsibility must always remain in human hands.
