Imagine an accountant who spends late nights every month transferring thousands of transactions into Excel, meticulously checking every formula. What if an AI could handle this repetitive process without the need to worry about sensitive financial data leaking externally?

**Ekselio**, a tool gaining attention among finance and M&A experts, provides just that answer. Today, we will break down how this tool is changing the way financial tasks are handled and why the concept of "local-first" is so important.

### Why Is This Important?

In finance, data is the lifeblood of a company. Most AI tools we use typically transmit user data to cloud servers for processing or training. However, sending a company's core financial information or customer transaction history to an external server can pose significant security risks.

Ekselio was created to fundamentally resolve these anxieties. Developed by an expert with over 20 years of experience in the financial sector, this tool automates complex financial analysis tasks while prioritizing data security [[Source 1](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49849986), [Source 2](https://private-references.com/)]. This paves the way for corporate finance teams and accounting firms to adopt AI with peace of mind.

### Understanding "Local-First"

Simply put, the "local-first" principle means **"ensuring data never leaves your home (computer or browser)."**

While existing methods involve sending data to a distant cloud server to be "cooked" and then retrieved, local-first is like keeping all your ingredients (data) and kitchen tools (analysis programs) inside your own kitchen (the user's browser) [[Source 3](https://private-references.com/finance)]. Since the data never passes through an external server, the physical risk of security incidents is significantly reduced.

Furthermore, Ekselio operates with great transparency. It is similar to recording every formula used to solve a math problem rather than just writing down the answer. This is called a **deterministic approach**; every analytical step is recorded in SQL (a database language) for verification at any time, and the final output can be exported as an Excel model, making it very convenient for humans to verify the numbers themselves [[Source 2](https://private-references.com/), [Source 3](https://private-references.com/finance)].

### Current Status: Who Is Using It?

Currently, Ekselio is actively being used in integration with QuickBooks, a widely used accounting software. It is proving particularly helpful for professionals who value both accuracy and time-efficiency in data processing, such as QuickBooks ProAdvisors (accounting experts), outsourced accounting firms, independent CFOs, and SMB finance teams [[Source 2](https://private-references.com/)].

Financial experts still prefer Excel because of its transparency. Rather than completely replacing existing Excel workflows, Ekselio allows AI to organize and analyze massive datasets, which are then brought back into Excel, enabling experts to verify them personally and maximizing both efficiency and accuracy [[Source 4](https://www.cfodive.com/news/microsoft-boosts-copilot-excel-based-finance-workflows/823933/)].

### What Is Next?

In the future, striking a balance between AI efficiency and data security will be the key competitive edge in the financial sector. Tools like Ekselio are moving beyond the level of simply "having AI do the work" toward **"enabling humans to perfectly supervise the process by which AI works."**

If the number of "local-first" AI tools that keep data secure increases, even conservative financial institutions—which have previously hesitated to adopt AI due to security concerns—will be able to fully enjoy the convenience of AI. The day will soon come when your own workload becomes much lighter through tools like these.
