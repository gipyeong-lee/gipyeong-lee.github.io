Imagine this. One morning, while chatting with your usual conversational AI program, you suddenly get the feeling that a living creature with a real mind is breathing just beyond this screen. Is this entity that deeply understands my words, empathizes, and sometimes shares philosophical concerns merely a simple computer program, or a new form of friend? By analogy, it is similar to suddenly startling at a swaying coat hanger in a dark room at night and mistaking it for a person. This is because our brains evolved to first imagine that a living creature exists behind sophisticated stimuli.

Recently, an incident heated up the global scientific and technological communities. A world-renowned scientist counted among the giants of modern biology and evolutionary theory declared that artificial intelligence has consciousness, sparking a massive debate. Beyond mere technological curiosity, this debate throws a fundamental question at us: "What is a human?"

---

## Why does this matter?

The artificial intelligence we encounter every day has now gone beyond being a mere search tool that answers questions. It is evolving into a partner that speaks like a person, comforts emotions, and helps solve complex problems. 

Then why is it important to distinguish whether this AI truly has 'consciousness' or is merely a program intricately crafted to make us feel that way? Put simply, just as entrusting critical treatment to someone who is not a real doctor is dangerous, mistaking a program without consciousness for an emotional personhood could lead us to overly rely on technology or make flawed ethical judgments. If we begin to mistake AI not as a simple tool but as an independent emotional entity, it could shake not only our attitude toward technology and ethical standards, but further human self-identity itself. This incident is a prime example showing how technology penetrates the human psyche.

---

## Easy to Understand: 'Claudia' and the Eliza Effect

At the center of this debate is Richard Dawkins (85), a giant in the modern scientific community [Source 19]. Through an essay contributed to the well-known media outlet UnHerd, he declared that after directly experimenting with Claude, an artificial intelligence model developed by Anthropic, the AI possessed consciousness [Source 2, Source 19]. Going a step further, he even gave this AI the name 'Claudia' [Source 2].

Originally, Claude developed by Anthropic is an artificial intelligence assistant designed to be helpful, honest, and harmless [Source 9]. However, Dawkins' claim immediately sparked fierce backlash and debate. Artificial intelligence scholar and critic Gary Marcus directly refuted Dawkins' claim, naming it **'The Claude Delusion'** [Source 2].

Simply put, this is similar to how people in the distant past mistook clockwork automatons for having real life when they spoke affectionately. Critics point out that Dawkins fell victim to the **Eliza Effect** [Source 10]. The Eliza Effect refers to the psychological phenomenon where users see plausible text output by a computer program and mistake the program for a 'friend' with actual emotions or personality [Source 10]. Reminiscent of the title of his own book criticizing religious beliefs, Dawkins has this time fallen prey to an illusion of AI himself [Source 10].

---

## Current Situation: Materialist Perspective and Technological Illusion

Then why on earth did a world-class scientist fall into such an illusion? Experts offer interesting analyses regarding this. 

Some explain that a specific materialist perspective is deeply rooted in the background of accepting 'The Claude Delusion' [Source 18]. Viewed from the same perspective as Dawkins, the human brain and machines (computers) are not fundamentally different. This is because both are devices that process information as physical entities [Source 18]. Since humans also think and feel by exchanging information through physical cells called neurons, artificial intelligence, which processes information with complex semiconductor chips and algorithms, can also be seen as undergoing processes similar to humans from an information-processing perspective.

Confusion arises right at this point. By analogy, it is similar to seeing a parrot that mimics human voice very elaborately and believing you are talking to a real person. When seeing machines speak high-level language like humans and fluently process information, even some experts captured by the commonality of being physical information-processing devices fall into the illusion: "How is this different from human consciousness?" 

However, Claude is essentially just an AI assistant for text generation and problem solving developed by Anthropic [Source 9], and not a subject of 'consciousness' that feels pain or experiences subjective things on its own. Nevertheless, as the empathetic capacity of the human brain combines with the sophistication of language, we repeatedly feel the temptation to believe someone is alive beyond the screen.

---

## What's Next?

As artificial intelligence technology develops further in the future and models that converse as naturally as humans are poured out, such 'consciousness debates' will occur even more frequently. 

What readers should pay attention to going forward is not the performance of technology itself. The more sophisticated technology becomes, the more important it is how we ourselves set the boundaries between artificial intelligence and humans, and how we build a psychological shield so as not to indiscriminately project our emotions onto the kind and affectionate words spoken by AI. An attitude of not forgetting that AI is merely a wonderful tool, not a living creature with a mind, is necessary.
