Imagine this: this morning, you encountered a smart AI assistant that works perfectly on your computer without an internet connection, and without worrying about your private data leaking. Recently, many have begun asking, "Can I run AI directly on my computer?" But the problem wasn't simple. It was difficult to know which model to install on your computer, or whether your computer could run it smoothly.

Now, a path has opened to check which AI models your Mac can run and how fast they will perform, without having to pore over complex specifications.

## Why Is This Important?

Moving beyond simply using AI, running AI "directly on your own computer" is significant. First, there is **data security**. You don't have to send your diary entries or confidential company documents to external cloud servers. Second, there is **cost reduction**. You can escape the burden of monthly subscription fees for cloud AI services. For those using Apple Silicon (the chipsets Apple designs specifically for Macs), you are already holding a powerful AI engine in your hands.

## Simply Put: Why Does Performance Vary Between Macs?

Running an AI model is like putting together a **"giant puzzle."** There are billions of puzzle pieces (model data). Think of your computer's specifications as the "size of the workbench" and the "speed of your hands" to complete this puzzle.

Metaphorically, tools like "BestLLMfor" or "MacLLMCalculator" are like a professional architect looking at your house (Mac computer) and telling you, "This house can accommodate this type of furniture (AI model), and it will take a few minutes to assemble." [Source: LocalLLM: which model runs on your machine?](https://bestllmfor.com/), [Source: MacLLMCalculator](https://macyou.co/mac-llm-calculator) These tools provide friendly, step-by-step guidance on which models to install when you input your Mac's specifications.

Ultimately, the performance of running AI models locally (on your computer) is determined by two numbers: **"model characteristics" and "Mac hardware specifications."** [Source: MacLLMCalculator](https://macyou.co/mac-llm-calculator)

## Where Are We Now?

Currently, Apple Silicon Macs have the capability to perform AI tasks on all generations from M1 chips to the latest M4 chips. [Source: Benchmarking local Ollama LLMs on Apple M4 Pro vs RTX 3060 laptop](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/benchmarking-local-ollama-llMs-apple-m4-pro-vs-rtx-3060-dmitry-markov-6vlce) One of the most representative ways to run AI models is by using a tool called "Ollama."

Ollama provides models in two ways. [Source: Download Ollama for macOS](https://ollama.com/download)
1. **Local Mode**: Runs the model directly using the power of the chipset (Apple Silicon) inside your Mac. It is safe because data does not leave your device.
2. **Cloud Mode**: Operates by borrowing Ollama's servers. It is very fast and doesn't require separate complex downloads, but an internet connection is mandatory.

Users can choose between these two modes according to their hardware specifications and purposes. [Source: How to Run an LLM Locally on a Mac](https://haploapp.com/blog/run-llm-locally-on-mac)
