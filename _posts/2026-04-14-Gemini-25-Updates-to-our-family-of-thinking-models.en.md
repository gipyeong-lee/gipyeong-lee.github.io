Imagine this. When you're given a very difficult math problem, do you immediately blurt out the first number that pops into your head? Or do you write down the solution process on paper, think to yourself, "Ah, I should solve it like this," and then give the answer? Until now, most AI has been closer to the former. It was a method of immediately providing the most statistically plausible answer as soon as a question was received. However, Google's newly introduced AI, **Gemini 2.5**, has started to provide answers after organizing its 'thoughts' and weighing the logic, just like the latter. [Gemini 2.5: Our newest Gemini model with thinking](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-model-thinking-updates-march-2025/)

Developed by Google DeepMind, Gemini is a **multimodal** artificial intelligence that simultaneously understands and processes various forms of information, including not only text but also images, audio, and video. [Gemini: A Family of Highly Capable Multimodal Models](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/gemini/gemini_1_report.pdf) It is also a powerful successor that inherits the technological prowess of Google's past AI models, LaMDA and PaLM 2. [Gemini (language model) - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_(language_model)) Through this update, Gemini 2.5 has evolved beyond a simple 'answer machine' into a 'thinking model' with the ability to reason on its own.

## Why is this important?

The most embarrassing moment when using AI is when it confidently presents incorrect information as fact. This is technically called **hallucination**. 'Thinking models' like Gemini 2.5 drastically reduce these mistakes because they go through an invisible internal reasoning process before outputting an answer. [Gemini 2.5: Updates to our family of thinking models – ONMINE](https://onmine.io/gemini-2-5-updates-to-our-family-of-thinking-models/)

In simple terms, before clicking the answer button, the AI takes time to review by asking and answering itself, "Is my logic correct? Are there any variables I should consider in the next step?" [Gemini 2.5: Updates to our family of thinking models](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/gemini-2-5-thinking-model-updates/) To use an analogy, it's like a child who used to answer impulsively now calmly reading the problem to the end, checking the solution process, and then starting to speak. This internal 'thinking process' proves its worth in tasks that require meticulous steps, such as solving complex math problems, advanced programming coding, and extensive data analysis. [Gemini thinking | Gemini API | Google AI for Developers](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/thinking)

## Understanding deeper: AI's 'Thinking Budget'

One of the amazing features of Gemini 2.5 is that users can directly set a **'Thinking Budget'** for the AI. [Gemini 2.5: Updates to our family of thinking models](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/gemini-2-5-thinking-model-updates/) This is a kind of guideline that determines how much time and resources the AI will spend 'deliberating' to solve a specific problem.

Shall we compare this to cooking?
*   **When boiling simple ramen (simple question):** There's no need to spend time worrying about a complex recipe. In this case, it's enough to set a low 'thinking budget' and get an answer very quickly.
*   **When preparing a 5-course meal for an important guest (complex problem):** Everything from the harmony of the menu to the order of ingredient preparation and cooking time must be precisely calculated. In this case, you can set a high 'thinking budget' to encourage the AI to think deeply enough and produce the best results.

In this way, Gemini 2.5 is very efficient because it can adjust how deeply it deliberates depending on the importance of the situation. [Gemini 2.5: Updates to our family of thinking models](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/gemini-2-5-thinking-model-updates/)

## Introducing the Gemini 2.5 Family: From Pro to Flash-Lite

Gemini 2.5 is divided into three models according to the user's purpose and environment. [Gemini (language model) - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_(language_model))

1.  **Gemini 2.5 Pro:** This model acts as the smartest 'brain.' It is currently available as a formal version, overwhelmingly breaking existing performance measurement standards (benchmarks) in complex reasoning and coding abilities. [Gemini 2.5: Our newest Gemini model with thinking](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/gemini-model-thinking-updates-march-2025/), [Gemini 2.5: Updates to our family of thinking models - Solega Blog](https://blog.solega.co/gemini-2-5-updates-to-our-family-of-thinking-models/)
2.  **Gemini 2.5 Flash:** A model that balances speed and efficiency. With this update, its **'agentic tool use'** capability has been greatly improved. [Continuing to bring you our latest models, with an improved Gemini 2.5 Flash and Flash-Lite release](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/continuing-to-bring-you-our-latest-models-with-an-improved-gemini-2-5-flash-and-flash-lite-release/) This means that the AI doesn't just answer, but its ability to find the necessary tools and directly perform complex chain operations has evolved dramatically.
3.  **Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite:** The youngest model to newly join the family. It is an economical model that dramatically lowers usage costs while maintaining performance, showing its potential currently in the preview stage. [Gemini 2.5: Updates to our family of thinking models](https://www.engineering.fyi/article/gemini-2-5-updates-to-our-family-of-thinking-models)

These models are like transportation options you choose depending on the situation. It's similar to choosing a powerful large truck (Pro) when moving heavy luggage, a highly maneuverable motorcycle (Flash) when moving quickly in the city, and an electric scooter (Flash-Lite) when moving light luggage frequently and cheaply.
