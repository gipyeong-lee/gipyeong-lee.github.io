Imagine you are having coffee with a friend, trying to decide what to have for dinner. You pull out your smartphone, open ChatGPT, and ask, "Recommend some light dishes for dinner tonight." Surprisingly, an ad for a meal kit delivery service appears right below the response. It feels as if it were listening to your dilemma.

As ChatGPT recently introduced ads for Free and Go plan users, conversations with AI have now become a significant data source for ad targeting. How exactly is your conversation delivered to advertisers? Today, we will look at what this new change means.

## Why Does This Matter?

Until now, AI services were primarily structured around subscription fees or were free in exchange for using our data to train the models. Now, however, a new revenue model called "advertising" has been added. This means the context of the conversations we have is becoming a criterion for ad exposure.

Crucially, personal information is being utilized. Email addresses and phone numbers provided at sign-up can be shared with advertisers, allowing them to show you ads related to your ChatGPT conversations on other platforms. ([Ref 2](https://techsifted.com/posts/chatgpt-ads-pilot-2026/)) This goes beyond simple "personalized recommendations," meaning the scope of user identification and tracking by corporations has expanded.

## Simple Understanding: An Ad Board with a Smart Memory

In simple terms, ChatGPT's ad system is like an **"ad board with a smart memory."**

Some analysts explain that ChatGPT uses a method similar to "Contextual Advertising"—a way of showing ads related to the topic of conversation—by grasping the context of the chat and combining it with user information. ([Ref 5](https://www.progressiverobot.com/2026/08/16/chatgpt-ad-personalization-privacy-policy/)) This process differs slightly from existing complex behavioral tracking. While traditional ads remembered "which sites a user visited," ChatGPT remembers "what conversations a user had" with the AI and displays ads perfectly suited to that context.

Advertisers can use a feature called "Audiences" to encrypt and upload their own customer lists (emails or phone numbers) to OpenAI. Then, when a specific user asks a question, ChatGPT delivers the corresponding ad precisely based on that information. ([Ref 16](https://searchengineoptimization.blog/article/chatgpt-ads-gets-audience-uploads-for-email-and-phone/?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block))

## Current Situation

ChatGPT is currently testing its ad model in earnest.

* **Exposure Rate**: When a user asks a query with commercial intent, there is approximately a 25.94% probability that an ad will be displayed below the response. ([Ref 18](https://seranking.com/blog/chatgpt-ads-study/))
* **Ad Format**: There is only one ad slot per response, which is determined by combining the user's past conversations, current topic, and previous ad interactions. ([Ref 18](https://seranking.com/blog/chatgpt-ads-study/))
* **Personalization Options**: Note that even if a user turns off personalization settings, ad targeting can still function. ([Ref 1](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/chatgpt-target-ads-prompts-email-170013093.html))

Of course, not all advertisers are welcoming this method. Because it lacks detailed demographic information (age, gender, etc.) compared to existing ad platforms, there is still skepticism among marketers regarding its effectiveness. ([Ref 4](https://strategicmarketingtribe.com/marketing-news/b/chatgpt-ads-openai-marketing-storybrand))

## What Happens Next?

Given the large initial investment required for ChatGPT, advertisers will likely continue to solidify its position as an enterprise marketing channel. ([Ref 4](https://strategicmarketingtribe.com/marketing-news/b/chatgpt-ads-openai-marketing-storybrand))

For users, this might lead to more convenient and relevant information, but we must remember that our digital footprint is being recorded and utilized in greater detail as a trade-off. In the future, when chatting with AI, you should be careful not to enter overly personal information (such as phone numbers or private concerns) and stay wise by continuously checking how the service's privacy settings change.

## MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

We must not forget that when AI services are provided for free, the real price we pay is our "data." The introduction of ads is an inevitable step for OpenAI to secure profitability, but in an era where the context of our conversations becomes a tool for advertising, we need a smarter form of "digital sovereignty." Taking an interest in how your conversations are used is the first step for a wise AI user.
