Imagine this: You wake up in the morning and tell your smartphone AI, "Summarize today's meeting materials," but the AI accidentally uploads your personal messages to the company server. Or, what if the AI learns from your writing and mimics your tone or thoughts to others?

In the past, AI seemed like a mere novelty, but now it has become a core part of our daily lives. In this era, we need "agreements" to manage AI so that it is not just smart, but also does us no harm. This is the world of "AI policy" we will explore today.

### Why Is This Important?

Going beyond the mere fact that technology is good, "how to manage it" has become a key task for nations and corporations. AI governance (a framework for how AI systems are managed and supervised) is not just about limiting technology. It is a process of determining who is responsible for AI results, what should be regulated, and when to apply these rules during the development process [Source: AI policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_policy).

These policies are essential protective shields for us to trust and use AI safely. Without such rules, it would be impossible to know who to hold accountable when companies indiscriminately train AI on personal data or when damages occur due to AI errors. As technology becomes deeply embedded in our lives, social safety nets are essential.

### Simply Put: Making 'Traffic Laws' for AI

To use an easy analogy, AI policy is similar to creating traffic laws when automobiles first appeared.

When cars first emerged, they were just fascinating, but as they got faster and traffic increased, accidents became frequent. People installed "traffic lights" and set "speed limits" to maintain order. AI policy is the same. As the powerful engine of AI makes our society run too fast, it is a process of installing traffic lights and signs to create an environment where everyone can coexist safely without major accidents.

Expert Charlotte Stix has pointed out that terms like "trusted AI," "responsible AI," and "ethical AI" have had their meanings continuously change over time and are commonly mixed up [Source: AI policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_policy). This means the field is still a nascent area that is being defined. Policymakers are doing the complex and important work of clarifying these terms and setting guidelines for developers.

### Current Status: The Whole World Is Moving

AI policy has now become a global issue, going beyond the problems of any single country. Governments around the world are racing to build fences for the safe use of AI.

1. **European Union (EU)**: By adopting the "AI Act," a common legal framework for AI, in 2024, it is becoming a global leader in regulation [Source: AI policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_policy).
2. **Global Trend**: Starting in 2025, governments worldwide began actively monitoring and managing AI systems, models, and data pipelines [Source: Top 10 New AI Regulations and Policy Updates (US, EU, Asia)](https://www.index.dev/blog/ai-regulations-and-policy-updates-us-eu-asia).
3. **Tracking and Observation**: The U.S., U.K., and OECD member countries are continuing to monitor changes in AI regulation and governance and seeking cooperation in their respective environments [Source: AI Policy Pulse](https://www.aipolicypulse.com/).

As such, each country is creating laws suitable for its own situation, while also cooperating internationally to create common guidelines. In particular, in December 2025, the U.S. White House announced a policy notice emphasizing a national AI policy framework, showing that government intervention is becoming more active [Source: Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/).

### What Will Happen in the Future?

AI policy will become more segmented and concrete in the future. While it started with vague ethical regulations like "let's make AI safe," it is now evolving toward practical and concrete regulations and questions of liability, such as "What data was used for training?", "Was data leaked?", and "Was there bias in the decision-making process?" [Source: Top 10 New AI Regulations and Policy Updates (US, EU, Asia)](https://www.index.dev/blog/ai-regulations-and-policy-updates-us-eu-asia).

We will encounter the words "AI regulation" or "AI policy" in more news in the future. This never means that AI technology is stagnating or that development is stopping. Rather, you should understand it as a process of building a firmer foundation for AI to take deep root in our society and be loved for a long time.

### MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

AI policy is not a "brake" that holds back technology. Rather, it is a "seatbelt" that must be equipped to run safely at higher speeds. As AI becomes smarter, our society will need tighter and more rational agreements so that we can trust and fully utilize that technology.
