Imagine if your smartphone or AI assistant, which you use every day, suddenly could not receive new feature updates due to 'national security' reasons. Recently, a single remark by a titan of the artificial intelligence (AI) industry has once again ignited a massive tech war between the United States and China.

### Why It Matters

The AI services we use are not completed solely by a company's technical prowess; they must be supported by massive computational resources and chips behind the scenes. However, Dario Amodei, CEO of the American AI company Anthropic, publicly argued that China getting ahead in AI technology poses a 'great risk' to the world and the US, and that this must be prevented ([Source 11](https://www.breitbart.com/news/beijing-hits-back-at-anthropic-ceos-call-to-curb-chinas-ai-development/)). This marks the beginning of a massive conflict that goes beyond competition between companies, affecting which AI we will be able to use in the future and how the pace of global technological development will be determined.

### The Explainer

To understand this conflict, one must first grasp two core concepts.

First, the **argument of Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei**. He argues that to reduce the potential risks of AI, global AI development speeds should be slowed down ([Source 1](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/artificial-intelligence/2026/09/14/beijing-hits-back-at-anthropic-ceos-call-to-curb-chinas-ai-development/)). It is similar to imposing a speed limit because an unskilled driver might cause an accident by driving too fast. However, he believes China should be an exception. He claims it is not safe for China to get ahead and that the flow of cutting-edge AI chips (the core components for making AI brains) into China must continue to be blocked ([Source 11](https://www.breitbart.com/news/beijing-hits-back-at-anthropic-ceos-call-to-curb-chinas-ai-development/)).

Second, **China's counterattack**. China interprets this very differently. The Chinese Foreign Ministry has defined such claims as 'fearmongering' ([Source 14](https://www.kpbs.org/news/science-technology/2026/09/14/beijing-hits-back-at-anthropic-ceos-call-to-curb-chinas-ai-development)). In short, they argue that while safety is mentioned on the surface, there is a 'political agenda' to forcibly tie China's feet because of fear of China's technological growth.

To use an analogy, it is like a situation where the top student in class proposes, "Let's slow down the study speed for the whole class," when in reality, it is perceived that they only want to study faster themselves and dislike the second-place student catching up. China criticizes this as an attempt to bring back a 'Cold War playbook' aimed at them ([Source 4](https://techcentral.co.za/beijing-anthropic-ceo-ai-cold-war/286065/)).

### Where We Stand

Currently, the US and China view AI technology as the core of national competitiveness. China insists it will not bow down, stating that AI development is a matter directly linked to national sovereignty ([Source 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BIM5G5kFYAg)). On the other hand, the US is controlling the export of core components to China, led by individuals who share the opinion of the Anthropic CEO. The Global Times, a Chinese state-run media outlet, is strongly pushing back, calling CEO Amodei's argument hostile and groundless ([Source 5](https://www.iranherald.com/news/279305353/chinese-media-dubs-dario-amodei-call-to-slowdown-ai-development-as-hostile-and-groundless)).

With the two sides remaining at a stalemate, the world is watching to see which direction this situation will take as a US-China summit is scheduled in Washington soon ([Source 14](https://www.kpbs.org/news/science-technology/2026/09/14/beijing-hits-back-at-anthropic-ceos-call-to-curb-chinas-ai-development)).

### What's Next

Discussions on AI safety will likely become even more intense. While CEO Dario Amodei argued for 'disarmament negotiations' to protect AI safety (similar to negotiating to reduce weapons to control AI development) ([Source 6](https://www.techmeme.com/260914/p9)), the current situation suggests that a struggle for technological hegemony is more likely to intensify than technological cooperation. What balance do you think is needed between the goal of 'AI safety' and the value of 'equality in technological development'?

### MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective
This situation shows that technology has moved beyond the realm of simple science and has become one of the most sensitive diplomatic issues between nations. For AI to make everyone's life prosperous, we need international agreements where everyone can transparently discuss safety, rather than tech competition aimed at 'only us living well.'
