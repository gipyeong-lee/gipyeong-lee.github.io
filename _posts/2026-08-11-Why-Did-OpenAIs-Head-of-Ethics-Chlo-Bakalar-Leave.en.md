Imagine this: the artificial intelligence (AI) we use every day is like a very smart child capable of dangerous behavior. What if there were only one "guardian" to teach and monitor this child on what is right and wrong, and what actions might harm society? And how would we feel if even that sole guardian left the company?

Recently, a similar situation unfolded at OpenAI, one of the most influential companies in the AI industry. News broke that Chloé Bakalar, OpenAI's only dedicated AI Ethics Lead, has left the company. [Source 6, Source 9]

### Why Is This Departure Important?

The situation is too serious to dismiss as merely an executive leaving a company. Chloé Bakalar was not just a figurehead. As OpenAI’s sole dedicated ethicist, she played a key role in managing the social issues and ethical dilemmas that could arise as technology advances. [Source 3, Source 6, Source 9]

AI models like the ChatGPT we use learn from massive amounts of data every day. In this process, biased information can be included, and there is a possibility that they may inadvertently produce harmful responses. In this context, an "Ethics Lead" is like installing brakes on a car that has none. The fact that this person has disappeared raises questions about whether the company is neglecting systems to monitor safety and ethics compared to the pace of its technology development. [Source 5] These concerns are amplified when considered alongside recent internal turmoil, such as the hacking incidents OpenAI has faced. [Source 2]

### In Simple Terms: The Relationship Between AI and an Ethics Lead

To easily describe the role of an ethics lead, think of a "safety official" at a race track. If a race car (AI technology) is speeding around the track, the safety official constantly checks for hazards, cars breaking rules, or risks to spectators.

Chloé Bakalar was the head of this safety team. In a 2023 panel discussion, she emphasized the impact of technology on humans and empathy, stating, "The ability to make people feel listened to, understood, and recognized is very important in areas like healthcare." [Source 3] In other words, she was the central pillar ensuring that technology not only produces "smart results" but moves in a direction that "does not harm humans and can be trusted."

### Current Situation: Questions Left Behind

Currently, neither Chloé Bakalar nor OpenAI has provided an official explanation for her departure. [Source 1, Source 4] She joined in August 2025 and left in July 2026, less than a year later. [Source 6, Source 8, Source 9] What is more concerning is the fact that a successor has not yet been named. [Source 1, Source 6]

OpenAI has already seen leadership changes related to safety systems, including members of the Superalignment team (a technology that aligns superintelligent AI with human values). [Source 4] With Bakalar's departure, some analysts are suggesting that OpenAI has effectively lost its experts dedicated to ethical issues. [Source 3] The fact that a vacancy remains after the departure of an expert with a brilliant background—having served as a senior ethicist at Meta—is highly significant. [Source 8]

### What Should We Watch For in the Future?

The pace of technological advancement will only accelerate. The ethical vacuum that occurs when companies focus solely on development speed can return as a social cost at any time. What we need to watch is when and how OpenAI fills this void. The key will be whether they can grant the new dedicated ethicist more authority and independence than before, rather than simply filling a seat.

AI has now become an inseparable part of our daily lives. We must continue to ask how tightly the companies creating this technology are fastening the seatbelt of "social responsibility," beyond just "technical sophistication."

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

Technology races ahead without hesitation, but the safety mechanisms to control it are becoming increasingly loose. The departure of an ethicist feels not like a simple personnel change, but like proof that AI companies are prioritizing commercial achievements over technical integrity.
