Imagine this: you wake up in the morning and tell your smartphone AI, "Organize my schedule for the day and summarize my important emails," and the AI instantly produces a perfect result that even reflects your personal preferences. We are witnessing amazing advancements in AI technology every single day. However, this rapid change sometimes brings fear with it. What if this technology is misused by someone? What if it becomes too smart to control?

Recently, a very interesting and important voice has emerged from the artificial intelligence industry. Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic, the maker of the AI model 'Claude', has made a radical proposal: we must intentionally slow down the pace of AI development. [Source 5](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-ai-development-rcna597383)

## Why It Matters

We have welcomed the fact that AI performance improves every day. However, CEO Amodei warns that the current pace of development is moving ahead of safety research, which manages the potential risks inherent in AI. [Source 11](https://kfoxtv.com/news/nation-world/anthropic-ceo-urges-ai-firms-slow-model-advances-let-safety-catch-up-dario-amodei-elon-musk)

This goes beyond just a "technological development" issue. As AI advances, potential risks such as cybersecurity threats or the serious possibility of misuse against humanity grow. [Source 8](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-ceo-says-time-slow-143736155.html) In other words, this proposal is a declaration by an industry obsessed with technological competition to "pause for a moment and look back." It also means we must lay a "secure foundation" before AI is fully integrated into our daily lives.

## The Explainer

Let’s use an analogy. The current AI development race is like a car race going at an incredible speed. The industry is only looking at who will reach the finish line first, with their foot all the way down on the accelerator. However, CEO Amodei points out that "if we keep increasing the speed like this, we won't even have time to check the performance of our brakes."

Simply put, while it is true that AI has become very smart through technologies like **Transformer (a core AI structure that understands the relationships between words in a sentence)**, "perfect safety devices" to immediately stop the AI if it decides to do harm or causes an unexpected error have not yet been sufficiently verified. [Source 16](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/09/12/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-calls-on-ai-companies-to-slow-down-ai-development-amid-superint) In a 3,800-word essay, he detailed these concerns and proposed that AI companies and governments establish a three-stage framework to adjust development speed based on the model's capabilities and safety verification results. [Source 6](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/12/tech/anthropic-ceo-essay-ai), [Source 17](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/anthropic-ceo-urges-ai-companies-slow-model-development_n_6aa56897e4b0298845cc9294), [Source 18](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/ai/ai-insights/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-calls-for-slower-ai-progress-warns-of-catastrophic-risks/articleshow/134142323.cms)

## Where We Stand

In a post on his website, CEO Amodei repeatedly emphasized, "We need to slow down the rate at which we improve the capabilities of AI models." [Source 6](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/12/tech/anthropic-ceo-essay-ai) He added, "Technological progress will of course still feel fast, but we must use that precious time we have gained wisely." [Source 16](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/09/12/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-calls-on-ai-companies-to-slow-down-ai-development-amid-superint)

Industry titans are aligning with these arguments. It is highly encouraging that Sam Altman and Elon Musk have voiced support for this proposal. [Source 15](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/article/dario-is-right-musk-and-altman-back-anthropic-ceo-on-slowing-ai-down-182008851.html) However, as many companies are still competing for technological hegemony, the extent to which a practical "speed limit" will be consistently observed globally remains a challenge.

## What's Next

Moving forward, we should be more attentive to how robustly "safety standards" have been established that match AI's "performance," rather than just how much that performance has improved. With this proposal, discussions on government-level AI regulation and policy development are expected to accelerate. Readers will frequently encounter terms like "AI safety governance" or "standard safety frameworks" in the news. Just as much as the convenience technology provides, monitoring whether it is being used safely is the true path to becoming a "smart consumer."
