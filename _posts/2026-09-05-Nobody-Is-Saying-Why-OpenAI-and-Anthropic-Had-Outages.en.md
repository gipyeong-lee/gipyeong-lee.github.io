Imagine this: It’s a busy morning, and you ask your chatbot to summarize important meeting materials, but there’s no response. Thinking it’s just an internet issue, you try other AI services, but they are frozen too. What if it wasn't just bad luck, but a bizarre event where the global AI chatbots you rely on all went paralyzed at once?

Recently, exactly this happened. Key models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX AI suffered service outages at almost the same time [Source 2](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/nobody-saying-why-major-chatbot-outage), [Source 14](https://www.indiavision.com/national/nobody-is-saying-why-openai-and-anthropic-had-outages-today/606602/), [Source 16](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2026/09/03/openai-anthropic-spacexai-outages/). But here is the stranger part: the companies operating these services are staying silent about this massive incident.

## Why Does This Matter?

AI has moved beyond a simple toy and become a core tool for work. As our reliance on AI grows, these service outages go beyond mere inconvenience to become major risks that halt productivity. In particular, the fact that services from multiple companies stopped "simultaneously" raises concerns that there might be a massive, shared technical defect or infrastructure problem we don't know about. However, the situation where companies hide the cause makes users rethink whether they can trust these services with their data.

## Simplified: The 'Common Plumbing' Problem of AI

To use an analogy, think of AI services as a giant apartment complex. Each AI company operates its own apartment. Suddenly, the main water pipe for the entire complex bursts, and water stops coming out in every unit.

Why did the apartments stop simultaneously? One hypothesis is the "common infrastructure" problem. Modern AI models share massive plumbing, such as data centers, power grids, or specific cloud servers. If a problem occurs in this common plumbing, functionality can be paralyzed for different companies at the same time. It’s the same principle as when a building’s main substation fails, causing electricity to go out in all apartments in the complex at once.

## Current Situation: Outage in the 'Labyrinth'

Currently, ways to verify this situation are very limited. We only notice outages through user reports and the official status pages of each company [Source 16](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2026/09/03/openai-anthropic-spacexai-outages/). In Anthropic's case, they reported a "partial outage" starting at 6:23 AM (PT) on Thursday, which included major models such as 'Claude Mythos 5.1', 'Claude Fable 5.1', and 'Claude Opus 5' [Source 1](https://www.wired.com/story/nobody-is-saying-why-openai-and-anthropic-had-outages-today/).

The problem is that Anthropic and other related companies are avoiding specific comments on the precise circumstances or cause of this outage [Source 1](https://www.wired.com/story/nobody-is-saying-why-openai-and-anthropic-had-outages-today/). While there is a record of "what happened," "why it happened" remains shrouded in fog.

## What Happens Next?

Because the speed of AI technological advancement is so fast, there is much criticism that infrastructure aspects like security or stability are not keeping up. This incident may be a clear example of how closely intertwined the AI industry is as companies pursue economies of scale.

There are two points we need to watch moving forward. First, will the companies release transparent technical reports regarding this event? Second, if systems continue to be paralyzed simultaneously, can our society continue to trust AI as an emergency business tool? Reliability is just as important as the technology becoming smarter.

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

This incident shows that no matter how fragmented technology appears to be, the reality is that it is tightly bound within a massive technological ecosystem. If companies want to gain user trust, they must clearly disclose the causes of outages. "Silence" causes greater anxiety for users than any error message.
