Imagine this: you spent two hours this morning wrestling with Claude Code to fix a complex bug. Then, in the afternoon, you face a similar problem while working in Cursor. You want to reuse that brilliant chat history and logical structure from earlier, but you have no idea where to find it.

Our work is scattered everywhere in the form of "conversations." As the era of AI agents unfolds, many people use multiple tools, but the crucial "context" (workflow situation and intent) remains locked within each tool. It’s like having puzzle pieces scattered across different rooms, making it impossible to see the whole picture. To solve this problem, "Skillsync," which joined the Y Combinator W26 batch, has arrived. [Source: Launch HN: Skillsync (YC W26) – AI chat sessions made portable across agents](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49743049)

## Why is this important?

In a modern development environment, conversations with AI are not just idle chat. They are "blueprints of work" that contain reasoning and tool-usage patterns from the problem-solving process. However, most current AI coding tools operate like isolated islands. [Source: Skillsync: Move context across agents and teammates | Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/skillsync)

As a result, developers repeat the same trial and error, and successful know-how is not shared among team members. By managing and sharing these fragmented AI chat sessions like valuable source code, Skillsync enhances individual productivity and strengthens the team's collective knowledge assets. [Source: Skillsync (YC W26) - LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/skillsync-team)

## Easy Understanding

To understand Skillsync, let’s use the metaphor of a **"shared workshop."**

Imagine you are building furniture using several different machine tools (AI agents). When you try to move a successful method from Machine A to Machine B, you find that because each machine speaks a different language, you cannot simply transfer the workflow. Skillsync acts as a "data hub" and "translator" that extracts work logs from each machine and organizes them into a common format. [Source: Skillsync - Shared context across your agents, a shared skill ...](https://skillsync.com/)

In short, Skillsync does the following:

*   **Integration**: Automatically finds various coding sessions already on your computer and gathers them in one place. [Source: Skillsync - Shared context across your agents, a shared skill ...](https://skillsync.com/)
*   **Skilling**: Converts effective chat sessions into reusable "Skills." Just as you would package well-written code into a library for distribution, you share your AI workflow with your team. [Source: Skillsync - Shared context across your agents, a shared skill ...](https://skillsync.com/)
*   **Portability**: Regardless of which agent you use—Claude, Cursor, etc.—you can take your work, reasoning processes, and tool usage history with you wherever you go. [Source: Skillsync: Move context across agents and teammates | Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/skillsync)

## Current Status

Skillsync is a **local-first** desktop app. [Source: Skillsync: Move context across agents and teammates | Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/skillsync) This means you manage your data directly on your own computer. Because of this, it requires no complex setup and is much more secure, as there is no concern about data exposure to an external cloud. [Source: Skillsync - Shared context across your agents, a shared skill ...](https://skillsync.com/)

It also provides a CLI (Command Line Interface) for users. This allows you to immediately search for past AI coding sessions from your terminal, or read and continue working on them. [Source: Get started with skl - Skillsync Docs](https://skillsync.com/docs) This technology is at the center of a trend where a developer's "workflow" itself is becoming an important career asset, beyond simply completing code. The emergence of Skillsync is proof that the fragmentation of AI coding tools has reached its peak. Paradoxically, the arrival of a tool that collects and shares those fragmented records means our way of collaborating with AI has moved past the "experimentation" phase and into the "systematization" phase, where it substantially enhances productivity.
