## Why It Matters

Whenever you watch economic news, words like "FOMC" or "benchmark interest rate" appear frequently. You might have wondered, "The US is deciding its own country's interest rates, so why is the interest on my loan here in Korea fluctuating?"

To use a metaphor, the US benchmark interest rate is like a massive "gravity" pulling the global economy. When gravity strengthens (when the US raises or maintains high interest rates), money spread across the world gets sucked into the US like a giant black hole to receive even a little more interest. If the US does not lower interest rates and keeps them tied tight at a high level, Korea cannot easily lower its own rates either, to prevent funds invested in our country from flowing out overseas like an ebb tide in pursuit of higher interest.

This ultimately connects directly to our daily lives. Maintaining high interest rates for a long time means that the interest on the mortgages or overdrafts we must repay every month continues to weigh heavily on our shoulders. Conversely, the interest we receive when diligently saving in the bank could become quite attractive. From the perspective of companies, they hesitate to make new investments because the cost (interest) of borrowing money to build factories and hire new employees becomes too expensive. In this way, a single decision made in a US meeting room creates a chain of massive waves that reach our salary accounts, real estate markets, and even stock portfolios.

Above all, the reason this situation is receiving intense attention from the global media is that "economic logic" and "political logic" are clashing head-on and fiercely in the ring. Currently, President Donald Trump is making blatant and strong demands for "rate cuts" toward the Fed `[워시 첫 FOMC 독립성 시험대…인플레 4.2%·트럼프 압박 '협공' - 뉴스1](https://www.news1.kr/world/international-economy/6197494)`. Politicians usually prioritize elections and approval ratings. Therefore, they tend to prefer the magic medicine of "rate cuts," which makes borrowing easier and releases a lot of money into the market, making the economy look good in the short term.

However, the most important and only mission of a central bank is to stabilize the value of money—that is, "prices"—without being shaken by political winds. Whether the newly appointed Chair Kevin Warsh will surrender to the intense pressure from the powerful president or trust the cold economic data and protect the independence of the central bank is the most thrilling point to watch in this meeting.

## The Explainer: Brake or Accelerator

To understand the current situation more clearly, let's compare the national economy to a "car" driving along.

The weapon called the "benchmark interest rate" held by the Federal Reserve (Fed) is the brake and accelerator pedal that adjusts the speed of this car. Raising interest rates is like stepping hard on the brake. Since interest becomes expensive, it makes people close their wallets instead of borrowing money to buy things, cooling down the engine of the overheated car (economy). Conversely, lowering interest rates is like stepping on the accelerator. Since interest becomes cheap, it becomes easier to borrow money, so people increase their spending, and the slowed-down economy gains vitality and picks up speed again.

Currently, the US benchmark interest rate stays at a fairly heavy level of 3.50-3.75% `[워시 첫 FOMC 독립성 시험대…인플레 4.2%·트럼프 압박 '협공' - 뉴스1](https://www.news1.kr/world/international-economy/6197494)`. Simply put, the driver called the Fed is keeping the brake stepped on quite firmly.

In normal road conditions, the car's speed (the trend of price increases) should normally decrease gradually when the brake is stepped on this strongly. In this situation, President Trump's argument is, "It seems the speed has decreased enough now, so quickly take your foot off the brake and step on the accelerator again (lower the interest rate)!" It is a strong political order to forcibly breathe vitality into the economy.

However, the reality shown by the dashboard in the Fed's bridge is chillingly different. Despite stepping on the brake with all their might, the terrifying monster called "inflation (price increase)" is not slowing down but rather racing even more fiercely. The number on the dashboard is a whopping 4.2%. In such a precarious situation, if they were to step on the accelerator (lower the rate) because they couldn't withstand the immediate political pressure, the car called the economy would lose control and inevitably plummet off a cliff (hyperinflation). This is the fundamental dilemma why Chair Kevin Warsh cannot blindly lower interest rates and has no choice but to drop the heavy anchor of a "freeze."

## Where We Stand: 3-Year Inflation Peak and Sudden Turn of the Doves

So, specifically, how serious is the situation with US prices? Looking into the meaning behind the numbers, the reason why the Fed is so nervous and breaking out in a cold sweat becomes clear.

Currently, the overall inflation figure in the US reaches 4.2%, which is skyrocketing at the fastest pace in a whopping three years `[워시 첫 FOMC 독립성 시험대…인플레 4.2%·트럼프 압박 '협공' - 파이...](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202606151227362273)`. If the number 4.2% doesn't quite hit home, think of it this way: a grocery basket that you could fill comfortably with 100,000 won last year now requires 104,200 won to fill the same way, even if you just stay still and breathe. On top of this, the "Consumer Price Index (CPI)," which represents the price level we directly feel in our daily lives when buying restaurant meals or daily necessities, recorded 3.8% as of last April. This is a terrible record, the highest since May 2023 `[워시의 첫 FOMC, 6월 17일 밤 — 인플레 3.8%와 '인하 없는 2026'](https://maxetf.tistory.com/200)`.

To use an even more relatable analogy, when you are determined and go on a diet, doesn't a frustrating "plateau" come at some point where no matter how much you starve and sweat while exercising, your weight stops dropping? That is exactly how the US inflation rate is right now. It is in a dizzying situation where it cannot break the magic wall of 3% and go down, but instead is bouncing back up like a spring `[워시의 첫 FOMC, 금리 인하가 아닌 인상 논의로 출발...? : 세이브티커](https://saveticker.com/news/148711)`. Furthermore, as even the labor market holds up so firmly that salaries continue to flow into people's pockets and money circulates without rest in the market, prices show no sign of falling.

In the face of such suffocating objective data, President Trump's reckless demand for a "rate cut" is being regarded as an unreasonable tantrum that is going completely against the direction indicated by economic reality `[워시 첫 FOMC 독립성 시험대…인플레 4.2%·트럼프 압박 '협공' - 뉴스1](https://www.news1.kr/world/international-economy/6197494)`.

As the situation is like this, the atmosphere in the Fed's meeting room is also freezing over like thin ice. Usually, in economics or financial news, those who softly argue that interest rates should be lowered to save a struggling economy are called "doves," while those who strictly argue that interest rates should be raised and the money supply tightened to firmly catch prices even if it means swallowing a bitter pill are called "hawks."

However, even the gentle "doves" on the committee, who only a few months ago were raising their voices to lower interest rates to save the economy, are quietly shaking their heads in front of the 4.2% inflation flames that show no sign of stopping. Even they have now withdrawn their support for a rate cut and have turned into sharp hawks, saying, "On the contrary, depending on the situation, we might even have to raise the interest rate once more to firmly put out the fire" `[워시의 첫 FOMC, 금리 인하가 아닌 인상 논의로 출발...? : 세이브티커](https://saveticker.com/news/148711)`. The discussion about lowering interest rates has vanished, and the "hike card" that had been tucked away in a drawer is stealthily appearing back on the table.

## What's Next

So, what conclusion will be reached in this complex tangle of threads? The Federal Reserve, the central bank of the US, will hold the FOMC regular meeting behind closed doors for two days from the 16th to the 17th, and then announce the final interest rate policy to the world in the early morning of the 18th, Korean time `[G7 개막·멕시코 만나는 홍명보호·워시 첫 FOMC…이번주(15...) | 뉴스1](https://www.news1.kr/society/court-prosecution/6196748)`.

In fact, seasoned economic experts and the media are already speaking with one voice regarding the "result itself" to be announced this week. In a situation where the pressure of inflation is so fierce as to swallow everything, an irresponsible rate cut is impossible from the start, and it is a foregone conclusion that an **"interest rate freeze is certain"** at the current level of 3.50-3.75% `[워시의 첫 FOMC…인플레 압력에 '금리동결 확실시' | 한국경제](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202606172969i)` `[워시 첫 FOMC 독립성 시험대…인플레 4.2%·트럼프 압박 '협공' - 뉴스1](https://www.news1.kr/world/international-economy/6197494)`.

Then, why are investors worldwide losing sleep and waiting with bated breath for the results of this meeting even though the obvious result of a freeze is already out? It is because the real point to watch is not the "rate number" to be announced tomorrow itself, but the **"subtle nuances of the message"** the Fed will put out after the meeting and the **"Dot Plot,"** which contains the future economic outlook like a code.

To put it simply, the "Dot Plot" is a kind of "future interest rate treasure map" where the 19 core members of the Fed anonymously place dots to show their inner thoughts, such as, "I think interest rates will stay around this number at the end of this year and next year." Market participants take this June's rate freeze for granted. However, they will be focusing all their attention and looking through a magnifying glass at whether the dots on this Dot Plot, newly submitted by members including Chair Warsh, have stealthily moved up compared to last time (a secret hint that they will raise interest rates more in the future) or if they stay in place `[워시의 첫 FOMC, 6월 17일 밤 — 인플레 3.8%와 '인하 없는 2026'](https://maxetf.tistory.com/200)`.

Also, it is a matter of great concern whether the Fed will **completely remove phrases related to "rate cuts" from the official statement** released immediately after the meeting `[워시의 첫 FOMC…인플레 압력에 '금리동결 확실시' | 한국경제](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202606172969i)`. If the words "rate cut" vanish from the statement, it is equivalent to the Fed sending a silent but powerful warning to the market: "We are overwhelmed just catching prices right now, so don't even dream of us lowering interest rates for a considerable period ahead." What words Chair Warsh will carefully choose in the press conference he will hold in front of cameras for the first time since taking office, and how firm and unwavering an attitude he will show toward the pouring political pressure, will be the most massive compass determining the direction of the future global stock market and our wallets.
