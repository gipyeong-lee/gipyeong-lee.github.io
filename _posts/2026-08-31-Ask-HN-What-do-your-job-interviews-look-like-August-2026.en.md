Imagine this: just a few years ago, many engineers would break into a sweat at an interviewer's command: "Solve the hardest coding problem on the whiteboard." But today, in 2026, AI agents (AI that sets its own goals and uses tools to perform tasks) have reached a level where they can write complex code in an instant and even find bugs themselves. So, are companies still asking candidates to demonstrate coding skills the way they did in the past?

### Why does this matter?

The barrier to entry for the software engineering market is being completely overturned. Hiring a software engineer now means companies are shifting from looking for someone who is simply "good at coding" to someone who can "solve product problems by utilizing AI as a tool." Interviews are a mirror that reflects a company’s culture and technical orientation. Therefore, understanding recent interview trends is an important indicator not only for job seekers but also for understanding what kind of talent organizations desperately need in the AI era.

### Easy to understand: Is AI a colleague or a tool?

To put it simply, if past interviews were about assessing technical proficiency—"how fast and precisely can you use a knife in the kitchen"—now it is more like seeing "how well you can plan and serve a delicious meal using the best smart kitchen tools called AI." Since the tools themselves have become standard, the planning ability and problem-solving approach to utilizing those tools have become much more important.

Recently, the developer community Ask HN (a Q&A board) has had lively discussions on this topic. According to the post [AskHN: What do your job interviews look like? (August 2026)](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49505033), many developers are deeply interested in whether companies are attempting unique or new evaluation methods different from the past in line with the advent of the AI agent era. [Source 2]

### Current situation: Are interviews changing?

Of course, not all companies have radically changed their interview methods yet. However, it is a clear fact that AI has entered the realm of evaluation. AI-powered, sophisticated evaluation criteria are already being introduced in educational fields like TOEFL Writing. [TOEFL Writing 2026: templates, essay examples, tips](https://toefl.exambooster.ru/articles/toefl-writing-2026/) Such changes allow us to guess how sophisticated and quickly AI-based competency assessments might spread in the recruitment process soon. [Source 3]

On the other hand, many places still stick to traditional methods. Like the questioning method Elon Musk used to enjoy in job interviews to identify liars, no matter how much technology develops, the effort to grasp a candidate's truthfulness and the fundamental thought process of problem-solving remains just as important. [Elon Musk favorite job interview question to spot a liar](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/01/26/elon-musk-favorite-job-interview-question-to-ask-to-spot-a-liar-science-says-it-actually-works.html) [Source 1]

### What will happen in the future?

Future engineer interviews are highly likely to become a place to prove "collaboration ability with AI agents" rather than a simple "memorization test." More than the ability to memorize and implement code, design ability—critically verifying results produced by AI and optimizing them for actual services—will be recognized as having greater value. The era is coming where people who manage code and create the right value will be more highly prized than those who just write it.

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

Technology changes drastically, but the essence of an interview—the process of finding a colleague to work with—never changes. However, we must remember that the colleague will always have a powerful assistant named AI by their side. Candidates must learn how to use AI better, and companies must learn how to identify, recognize, and grow together with people who can achieve greater results by utilizing those tools.
