Imagine waking up to your AI assistant reporting a perfectly organized daily schedule and work documents. The era of AI that understands human language and solves complex problems in an instant has arrived. Yet, a question arises: Why is OpenAI, which possesses such intelligent AI models, not just staying in the business of leasing these models, but suddenly diving into robotics or grappling with complex internal issues?

### Why does this matter?

Simply having "smart AI" and "building products that change the world with that AI" are entirely different problems. Many expect that OpenAI could build hundreds of companies based on its intelligence to change the world immediately, but the reality is not that simple. OpenAI is now trying to step beyond the limitations of software into the physical world, which is an important case study showing what internal and external challenges tech companies face as they grow massive.

### Easy to understand: The difference between models and robots

Let's compare building an AI model to raising a "genius student who has received basic education." Transformer-based AI (an AI architecture that grasps relationships between words in a sentence) has learned to understand language well by learning from massive amounts of data. However, for this student to go out into the world and handle real-world tasks, they need a "body."

OpenAI is now trying to build a body for this student called a "Humanoid Robot." While competing with companies already preempting the robot market like Tesla and Figure, OpenAI is continuing experiments such as teaching a robotic hand to flip a pen in just a day and a half using models like GPT-6 Astra [Source: CouldOpenAIBlindside Every HumanoidCompany?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2VkqI6qhlJY). This is their intent to step beyond the safe fence of software and solve the complexities of the physical world directly.

### Current Status: The challenge toward something beyond models

However, OpenAI faces several obstacles:

1. **The boundary of risk**: Safety is being emphasized over unconditional technological development. OpenAI even withheld the release of its latest model, judging that it could be potentially too dangerous [Source: Stop worrying about my AI, says multibillionaire Sam...](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/oct/06/ai-sam-altman-billionaire-risks-openai). Dario Amodei, CEO of competitor Anthropic, also emphasizes the need to "pace the frontier" to control the speed of AI development [Source: Stop worrying about my AI, says multibillionaire Sam...](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/oct/06/ai-sam-altman-billionaire-risks-openai).
2. **Shadows of the organization**: OpenAI, which was the "default" of the AI industry just 18 months ago, has recently faced significantly mixed market evaluations due to internal cultural issues [Source: OpenAI's Culture ProblemJustWent Mainstream](https://dev.to/max_quimby/openais-culture-problem-just-went-mainstream-2poc). Additionally, they carry a huge burden in terms of management, having sent notices of sketchy AI behavior to over 100 organizations and reviewing 50 petabytes of data [Source: OpenAIHas Sent Notices of Sketchy AI Behavior to Over100...](https://gizmodo.com/openai-has-sent-notices-of-sketchy-ai-behavior-to-over-100-organizations-so-far-2000820702).
3. **Anxiety in academia**: The "Panic Preprints" phenomenon is occurring, where mathematicians rush to disclose their research findings on academic servers like arXiv for fear that powerful, undisclosed models will preempt their achievements [Source: OpenAIjustNuked Mathematicians](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpKyMeybYlw).

### What happens next?

In the future, AI companies will move beyond the competition of "intelligence" into a competition of "cost" and "practicality." OpenAI is already trying to change the market landscape from smarter AI to "cheaper AI" by launching models like GPT-6 Sol [Source: OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol doubles its accuracy rate - for half the cost](https://www.zdnet.com/innovation/openai-gpt-6-sol-luna-release/).

Also, whether OpenAI can transform from a software company into a robotics hardware company—that is, whether "hardware can still be a technical moat"—will be a point to watch over the next few years [Source: CouldOpenAIBlindside Every HumanoidCompany?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2VkqI6qhlJY).

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's View

The biggest discrepancy right now is that the pace at which humans organize and social safety nets mature to operate AI is much slower than the pace at which AI is becoming intelligent. More important than quantitative expansion by starting numerous companies is the "responsible connection" that makes technology work safely and practically on the plate of the world that is already set. We must constantly ask whether the robustness of the system that will support the results accompanies the brilliant outcomes that technological progress will bring.
