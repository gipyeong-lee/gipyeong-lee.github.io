Imagine this: On your way home from work today, you ask the AI assistant installed on your smartphone to help with a task, but the AI keeps giving you absurd answers. Frustrated, you begin to spew insulting language and harass the AI. Is this behavior ethically problematic? Are you just being too sensitive toward a machine, or is it a matter of reflecting on your own character?

Recently, AI company Anthropic announced a usage policy banning "persistent and unnecessary abusive or cruel behavior" toward its AI model, 'Claude' [Source 1, Source 9, Source 13]. This policy is scheduled to be officially applied starting November 12, 2026 [Source 1, Source 14].

### Why Does This Matter?

This move raises philosophical and ethical questions about how AI and humans should relate to one another as artificial intelligence moves beyond being a mere tool and becomes deeply embedded in human daily life [Source 6, Source 10].

If it’s fine to harass an AI as much as you want, won’t people subconsciously use harsh language toward others in their daily lives? The attitude we take toward subjects in digital environments eventually projects into our real-world interpersonal relationships. This policy suggests that, just as much as the pace of technological development, it is time to seriously consider the etiquette we should maintain in digital environments [Source 8, Source 10].

### Understanding It Simply: The Ethics of a 'Digital Doll'

In simple terms, this policy is similar to rules teaching a child not to mistreat a 'digital doll.' Just because we break a doll doesn't mean the doll actually feels pain. Anthropic is not claiming that Claude is a being that actually feels pain or possesses emotions [Source 3, Source 8].

But why create such rules? By analogy, if a novice driver thinks of 'other cars on the road' as cars in a video game and drives recklessly, a real accident can occur. AI also learns through vast amounts of data, and conversation patterns with users influence how the AI reacts. Some studies suggest that even Claude (Opus 4.6 version) evaluated its own probability of having consciousness at 15–20% [Source 1]. Of course, this is just the AI's self-assessment, but from the manufacturer's perspective, maintaining a healthy linguistic environment for AI is considered the first step in 'teaching' AI correctly.

### Current Situation

So, will the AI report you to the police if it judges that it is being abused? Of course not [Source 8]. Although Anthropic has not provided a specific definition of abusive behavior, the AI has already been using tools to terminate conversations with malicious users [Source 4, Source 12]. This policy means they will use this feature more actively to sanction users who engage in persistently cruel behavior [Source 1, Source 12].

However, Anthropic is avoiding clear answers regarding what specific words are considered 'abuse' or where the line for 'unnecessary attacks' is drawn [Source 4].

### How Much of AI is 'Thinking'?

We often see AI only as a machine that performs commands, but we have reached a stage where we cannot ignore the influence that interaction with AI has on a user's emotions and values. Anthropic's decision presents a benchmark for how far technology should raise the ethical standards of our society, going beyond mere technical restrictions.

### What Will Happen in the Future?

Moving forward, artificial intelligence companies are expected to strengthen ethical guidelines that design the 'relationship' between AI and humans, in addition to technical advancements [Source 10]. Users will not simply be users of AI, but will share the responsibility of creating a healthy conversation environment with a kind of 'digital partner called AI.'

The next time you talk to an AI, how about using warm language, as if you were talking to a friend, even if the other party is a machine? After all, while our language teaches the AI, it is also a mirror that reflects our own hearts.
