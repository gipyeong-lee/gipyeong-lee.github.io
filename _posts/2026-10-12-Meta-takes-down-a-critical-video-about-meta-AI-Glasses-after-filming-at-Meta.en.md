Imagine this: You use a company’s smartphone, and you film a video in front of their office asking employees, "Is this phone actually any good?" But as soon as you upload the video, it disappears instantly from the social media platform operated by that very same company. How would that make you feel?

What happened recently regarding Meta’s AI smart glasses is similar to this scenario. As a platform company deletes content critical of its own products, a heated debate is arising over "how far the platform's power to censor should go."

## Why It Matters

The social media platforms we use are no longer just playgrounds; they are like public squares where information flows. But what happens if the owner of this square turns off the speakers whenever they hear a story that puts them in a bad light?

This incident goes beyond just "a video being deleted." It starkly demonstrates how easily giant platforms we use daily, like Instagram or Facebook, can control content critical of their own products or businesses. This poses a fundamental question about whether users can truly trust these platforms, given the values of "transparency" and "accountability" that tech companies claim to uphold.

## The Explainer

The deleted videos are known to involve two major cases. The first is a video by Dutch program creator Roel Maalderink, who visited Meta's Amsterdam office, had employees try on the AI glasses, and interviewed them about their honest thoughts [Source: Meta deletes video criticizing its own glasses [Source 4], [Source 15]]. The second is a review video filmed at Meta headquarters by tech influencer Brandon Dalio, which analyzed the product's privacy concerns and functional limitations in detail [Source: Meta warning on video deleting criticism of its AI glasses [Source 6]].

In short, it is **"as if a landlord saw a video criticizing them filmed in their own front yard and deleted it immediately because they didn't like it."** In this process, Meta immediately deleted those contents from its platforms, Instagram and Facebook [Source: Meta deletes video criticizing its own glasses [Source 4], [Source 9]].

Such behavior can also trigger what is known in tech terms as the "Streisand effect." This refers to the phenomenon where the more one tries to hide information, the more it piques public curiosity and causes it to spread even further. In fact, after this deletion, this issue became a topic of conversation for many, resulting in even greater criticism of the company's transparency [Source: Critical video deleted after filming at Meta headquarters [Source 5], [Source 9]].

## Where We Stand

Currently, Meta has not provided a clear explanation for this action, and the deleted videos are no longer searchable on the platform [Source: Meta deletes video criticizing its own glasses [Source 7], [Source 16]].

Of course, from Meta’s perspective, they could cite unauthorized filming on private property or violations of product promotion policies as reasons for the deletion. However, users and critics are interpreting it as "censorship of content that doesn't suit the company's taste" [Source: Meta warning on video deleting criticism of its AI glasses [Source 6], [Source 9]]. The tech community is in agreement that this incident will serve as an important warning to future AI tool developers and users [Source: Meta warning on video deleting criticism of its AI glasses [Source 6]].

## What's Next

We will live lives increasingly intertwined with AI in our daily routines. In that process, countless AI products will flood the market. However, what is important is not the technology itself, but a free and healthy ecosystem where that technology can be evaluated and criticized.

This incident is expected to lead to stricter social standards regarding how platform companies should handle critical assessments of their own products. Users will now look more carefully at just how "neutral" the information provided by platforms really is. What do you think? Is technology without criticism truly safe for us?

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

It is by no means a sustainable strategy for a company to erase voices of criticism that are unfavorable to them just because they are the owners of the platform. The true power of technology is at its strongest when it even embraces criticism and uses it as an opportunity for improvement. Wouldn't it be better for a mature tech company to adopt an attitude that accepts even the sharp advice their product receives as an asset to the platform?
