Imagine this: tomorrow morning, the AI on your smartphone becomes smarter than ever and decides to handle your complex tasks for you. You're in a situation where you could meet this new AI with the press of a single button. But suddenly, the government puts on the brakes, saying, "Wait a minute; this technology is so powerful that it could be dangerous, so don't release it all at once—disclose it slowly."

This is something that has actually happened recently in the artificial intelligence industry. We'll break down the situation surrounding OpenAI's next-generation AI model, 'GPT-5.6,' what it means for us, and why the government intervened.

### Why Is This Important?

This incident demonstrates that AI has moved beyond simple laboratory-level technology and has become 'core infrastructure' with a direct impact on our society's security and cyber environment.

In the past, it was taken for granted that whenever a new AI model was released, it would be made publicly available for anyone to use immediately. However, now that AI has become so intelligent, concerns have grown that it could become a tool for cyberattacks if utilized maliciously by someone. The fact that the [Trump administration](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/trump-administration-asks-openai-stagger-release-new-model-security-concerns) put the brakes on OpenAI demonstrates the government's strong will that 'safe implementation' is just as important as the speed of AI technological advancement.

### Easy to Understand: What Is a 'Staggered Release'?

Is the term 'staggered release' a bit unfamiliar? Simply put, you can compare it to a **'vaccination.'**

When a new medicine is developed, instead of injecting everyone simultaneously, it's similar to vaccinating people in order while checking their health status. [The method requested by the government this time](https://digg.com/tech/p15oldm4) means that instead of showing GPT-5.6 to the public all at once, it will be used first by pre-approved users, and then the scope will be expanded while carefully reviewing whether there are any issues.

[GPT-5.6](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/gpt-5-6-june-2026-release/) is expected to make tremendous progress in the fields of reasoning ability and task automation. To use an analogy, if the AI so far had been a 'student with good fundamentals,' this model is leaping to the level of an 'expert who analyzes complex problems and devises strategies on its own.' Before releasing such an intelligent expert to the world, the administration intends to preemptively plug any potential cybersecurity holes.

### Why Is This Measure Necessary?

As AI performance has improved dramatically, AI now handles everything from system control and code writing to complex data analysis, going beyond simple text generation. To use an analogy, if the AI until now had been a 'well-spoken library librarian,' it has now become an 'engineer capable of designing buildings and inspecting security on its own.' As the engineer's abilities are superior, the scale of damage caused when this is misused is also incomparable to the past. For the government, now is the time when establishing a safety device called 'prevention of potential accidents' has become more important than anything else, along with the convenience of technology.

### Current Situation: What Has Changed?

GPT-5.6, which was initially expected to be [released in June 2026](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/gpt-5-6-june-2026-release/), has now [shifted its direction](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/957372/openai-will-delay-gpt-5-6-after-trump-administration-request) from the existing full-disclosure release method to a staggered release method at the government's request. [OpenAI CEO Sam Altman](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/trump-administration-asks-openai-stagger-release-new-model-security-concerns) conveyed this decision to employees through an internal company Q&A session, and the company has decided to accept it.

For now, it appears that preventive inspections will be carried out by managing access rights per customer. Furthermore, looking at the industry flow, it seems the atmosphere is for OpenAI to focus on substance rather than rushing, and it is reported that they are also considering [postponing the IPO until next year or later](https://www.cnbc.com/video/2026/06/25/openai-will-stagger-gpt-5-point-6-release-following-trump-admin-request-for-review-source.html).

### What Will Happen in the Future?

There is a high possibility that this kind of tug-of-war between AI companies and the government will become a daily occurrence in the future. This is because, while technology advances day by day, the process of taming that technology so that it does not threaten society as a whole is essential.

What our readers should pay attention to is **'how meticulously security verification is carried out.'** You should not just wait, but watch how safely and intelligently it comes to our side. As AI develops, the methods to control and manage that AI will also develop, which is the only way we can safely enjoy the benefits of technology in the AI era.
