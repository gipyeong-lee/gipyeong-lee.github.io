Imagine a laboratory where microscopes, sample-handling robotic arms, and precision laser equipment move in unison, performing experiments like a coordinated team. Until now, connecting these devices to AI required engineers to write custom, dedicated code for each machine. It was a highly inefficient process, much like hiring a separate translator for every person speaking a different language.

Recently, however, the AI company Anthropic offered a clue to solving this complex puzzle: the **Model Hardware Standard (MHS)**.

## Why is this important?

If AI in our daily lives has primarily been about reading and responding to text, it is now stepping into the stage of physically moving objects in the real world. Anthropic has decided to provide a standardized set of drivers so that AI agents (AI that autonomously plans and executes tasks) can safely and easily control various equipment in fields like scientific research and advanced manufacturing ([Source 1](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/08/anthropics-new-hardware-standard-lets-ai-agents-control-the-physical-world/)).

This is not just a matter of convenience. It means that when scientists develop new drugs or experiment with complex chemical reactions, they can reduce the time spent on manual equipment operation and focus entirely on the "research results." Simply put, just as we control complex household appliances with a single universal remote, AI can now operate complex lab equipment via a standardized interface ([Source 2](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/27/anthropic-pushes-into-physical-world-with-new-standard-to-help-ai-agents-operate-machines.html)).

## In simple terms

Think of it this way: previously, microscopes "spoke" Microscope and robotic arms "spoke" Robotic Arm, meaning AI had to learn each individual language to communicate with them. If there were 100 devices, you would have needed 100 translators.

MHS has created a "common language" that these devices can use. By using standardized commands like 'Read' or 'Move,' AI can issue instructions regardless of the device type ([Source 4](https://fortune.com/2026/08/27/anthropic-makes-first-move-into-physical-ai-with-universal-standard-for-scientists-manufacturing/)). Thanks to this, experts no longer have to struggle with writing custom code for every single machine. It has become significantly more efficient for AI agents to operate robotic arms, precisely align lasers, or perform protein analysis ([Source 8](https://byteiota.com/anthropic-model-hardware-standard-physical-ai/)).

Crucially, MHS is **model-agnostic**. This means not only Anthropic's own "Claude" model, but also OpenAI's models and other open-source AIs can use this standard to control equipment ([Source 4](https://fortune.com/2026/08/27/anthropic-makes-first-move-into-physical-ai-with-universal-standard-for-scientists-manufacturing/), [Source 11](https://techstartups.com/2026/08/27/anthropic-launches-model-hardware-standard-to-let-ai-agents-control-physical-machines/)). This is the result of Anthropic attempting to expand into the physical world based on the Model Context Protocol (MCP), an open standard for connecting data sources that they introduced previously ([Source 4](https://fortune.com/2026/08/27/anthropic-makes-first-move-into-physical-ai-with-universal-standard-for-scientists-manufacturing/)).

## Current Status

Anthropic has currently released a Research Preview of MHS and is testing the technology with a select group of scientific laboratories and advanced manufacturing companies ([Source 3](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-hardware-standard-research-preview), [Source 6](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/anthropic-announces-new-model-hardware-standard-for-ai-agents-plans-open-source-release-with-safety-guidance20260828112959/)).

The standard currently aims to support equipment commonly used in research settings, such as cameras, robotic arms, microscopes, centrifuges, and pipettes ([Source 13](https://modelhardwarestandard.com/)). While still in its early stages, it is in the process of building an environment where numerous pieces of equipment can be connected to AI to safely operate complex tasks ([Source 10](https://coursiv.io/blog/model-hardware-standard)).

## What’s Next?

If MHS becomes widely adopted, the "smart lab" we once imagined will become a reality. Moving beyond simple operation, multiple devices will be able to communicate with each other and function organically. Anthropic plans to open-source this technology, and it is expected that more developers will participate, leading to safer and smarter manufacturing and research environments ([Source 6](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/anthropic-announces-new-model-hardware-standard-for-ai-agents-plans-open-source-release-with-safety-guidance20260828112959/), [Source 18](https://aninews.in/news/business/anthropic-announces-new-model-hardware-standard-for-ai-agents-plans-open-source-release-with-safety-guidance20260828112959/)). An era is approaching where AI does not just stay on digital screens, but contributes to solving humanity's scientific challenges by directly controlling the physical equipment we touch.
