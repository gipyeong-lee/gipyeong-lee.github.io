Imagine if the "security locks" protecting the deepest layers of the smartphones, laptops, and the countless internet services you use every day were broken in hundreds of places. A startling event recently occurred in the field of artificial intelligence related to just this.

An OpenAI AI agent pipeline (a system where AI performs tasks by stepping through processes on its own) discovered 948 security holes in the "Linux kernel" (the core software of the operating system), which is the foundation for computing across the world [[Source 1](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/openai-agents-linux-kernel-vulnerabilities-exploits), [Source 10](https://www.linux.org.ru/news/security/18414552/page1)]. It reportedly didn't just stop at finding the risks; it even autonomously created "patches" (repair code for software) to resolve them immediately [[Source 2](https://forklog.com/en/openai-identifies-948-vulnerabilities-in-linux-kernel/)].

## Why is this important?

Linux may not be a household name, but it is effectively the foundation for nearly every technology that powers modern society. The Android smartphones we use, cloud servers, and even various home appliances all operate based on Linux. The fact that hundreds of "privilege escalation vulnerabilities" (security flaws where ordinary users could hijack administrator privileges) were hidden in this core foundation is a very serious issue [[Source 2](https://forklog.com/en/openai-identifies-948-vulnerabilities-in-linux-kernel/)].

Previously, humans had to review vast amounts of code one by one to find such errors, which consumed enormous amounts of time and money. However, this experiment proved the possibility that AI can audit system safety much faster and more efficiently than humans [[Source 6](https://dzen.ru/a/auxEvmaSGStr5KF2)].

## Easy to understand: The arrival of the AI repairman

To use a simple analogy, this event is like **"an AI performing a full inspection on tens of thousands of door locks in a massive apartment complex in just one day."**

1. **AI's filtering process:** The AI agent initially identified about 2,308 suspicious pieces of code. This is like walking through an entire apartment complex and spotting signs of failure in door locks [[Source 6](https://dzen.ru/a/auxEvmaSGStr5KF2)].
2. **Actual intrusion attempts:** To test if security was truly penetrable, the AI verified 1,724 of those pieces and finally identified 948 confirmed "security holes" [[Source 6](https://dzen.ru/a/auxEvmaSGStr5KF2)].
3. **The repairman's role:** It didn't stop at discovery; it automatically created a "repair kit" (patch) tailored to the flaws in each lock [[Source 10](https://www.linux.org.ru/news/security/18414552/page1)].

Notably, it even discovered 10 new attack techniques previously unknown to the world, making it a landmark moment for the security industry, confirming that AI can be both a powerful "shield" and a "spear" at the same time [[Source 1](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/openai-agents-linux-kernel-vulnerabilities-exploits)].

## Current status: Cost and efficiency

Currently, this task was performed in a single automated run, which incurred a non-trivial API cost of $1.2 million [[Source 2](https://forklog.com/en/openai-identifies-948-vulnerabilities-in-linux-kernel/), [Source 6](https://dzen.ru/a/auxEvmaSGStr5KF2)]. While many experts are impressed by AI's astonishing analysis speed, it is not yet a task that anyone can perform daily, considering the massive computational costs required. However, it is clear that it has opened the floodgates for AI to be fully deployed in the field of system security.

## What will happen in the future?

It is highly likely that, in the future, it will become a standard procedure for companies and software developers to entrust "security audits" to AI agents before releasing new features. This is because AI can fill in the minute gaps in vast codebases that human security experts often miss. However, a new challenge remains for us: ensuring that security information discovered by AI is not exposed to malicious hackers first.
