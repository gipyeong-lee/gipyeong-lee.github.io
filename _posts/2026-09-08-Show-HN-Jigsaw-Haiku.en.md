## A weary daily life, a line of poetry in a single puzzle piece

Imagine this. You have spent all day living in the midst of complex information on computer screens and smartphones, and after getting off work, you sit quietly in your room with a warm cup of tea. You turn on your smartphone and start a puzzle game with some calm background music. What if, as you assemble the pieces one by one, not only is a picture completed, but a short yet deeply resonant line of poetry appears on the screen?

Recently, a game that appeared on the tech community 'Hacker News' has caught people's attention. It is 'Jigsaw Haiku'. [Source 2](https://zeli.app/story/49568162) [Source 4](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49568162) The biggest charm of this game is that it is not a game where you have to clear levels quickly, but rather one that delivers an artistic experience called 'Haiku' to the user through the familiar form of a puzzle.

## Why does this matter?

When we think of 'digital technology', we often think of faster processing speeds or the ability to handle complex data. However, technology sometimes becomes a 'comma' that stimulates human sensibility and calms the mind. Jigsaw Haiku shows that among the many games today that compete with complex features, even a very simple method can give users emotional satisfaction.

In short, it is like stopping for a moment in a busy daily life to look at the scenery. This game is very special in that technology can become a tool to slow down the pace of daily life and look into the inner landscape.

## Easy to understand

The principle of 'Jigsaw Haiku' is actually very simple. It combines the 'jigsaw puzzle' (a puzzle that divides a picture into several pieces and puts them back together), which we are familiar with, and 'Haiku', a Japanese structured poem.

Haiku is a traditional Japanese poetic form, usually consisting of 17 sounds (on) in a 5-7-5 pattern. [Source 8](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/하이쿠) This short poem often includes devices like 'kigo' (season words) and 'kireji' (cutting words) to leave a deep impression.

To use an analogy, it is similar to the excitement of finding a piece of paper when we were hunting for treasure as children. In Jigsaw Haiku, a puzzle piece is both a part of a picture and a word or phrase of a poem. As the user puts the pieces together one by one, the scattered sentences find their place, and in the end, one can read a completed poem. It is as if a puzzle is completed as a picture, and at the same time, a heart-touching poem is finished. [Source 2](https://zeli.app/story/49568162)

## Current situation

Currently, 'Jigsaw Haiku' is operated as a web-based game service that users pursuing artistic puzzle experiences can visit. The 'aesthetics of brevity' inherent in the Haiku form blends perfectly with the 'piece matching' of jigsaw puzzles, making the time users stay in front of the screen more meaningful.
