Imagine this. A business owner who has run a small parts factory their whole life. Labor is becoming increasingly scarce, and when they look into automating the factory, they lose sleep at night because of the massive costs of complex machine installation and the daunting prospect of hiring experts. What if they could fit robot arms together just like assembling Lego blocks, and operate the robots as easily as using a smartphone app?

German robotics startup 'RobCo' is turning this dream into reality. They recently secured $100 million in investment and have declared their full-scale expansion into the US market ([German robotics startup RobCo raises $100M and expands to US](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/start-ups/german-automation-start-up-robco-munich-raises-100m-us-expansion)).

## Why does this matter?

Even in Germany, a manufacturing powerhouse, many factories are located in sparsely populated areas on the outskirts of cities, leading to a serious labor shortage ([Sequoia Capital's analysis of RobCo partnership](https://sequoiacap.com/article/partnering-with-robco-accessible-automation-for-the-physical-world/)). This is not a problem unique to Germany. Manufacturing sites around the world are facing production setbacks because they cannot find skilled workers.

RobCo's technology doesn't just sell 'robots'; it acts as a way to lower the 'barrier to automation.' They have made it so that you don't need to overhaul an entire factory or rely on expensive external experts to introduce robots. This is expected to not only increase manufacturing efficiency but also strengthen the competitiveness of the entire manufacturing ecosystem by allowing small and medium-sized factories to enjoy the benefits of cutting-edge automation technology ([Analysis of RobCo robot market value](https://sifted.eu/articles/robotics-valuations-europe-us)).

## Understanding it easily

To understand RobCo's technology, let's use two metaphors.

First, it is a **'Lego-style robot.'** While existing industrial robots were like massive structures custom-built for a factory, RobCo's modular robots are like Lego blocks ([RobCo's autonomous industrial robotics technology](https://www.rob.co/)). Since arm joints can be assembled and arranged according to the necessary functions, they can respond quickly to changing work environments ([ROS Goss podcast interview](https://www.dwightco.com/podcast/from-europe-to-the-us-meet-robco)).

Second, it is **'automation as easy as taking off and putting on a school uniform.'** RobCo provides software that allows robots to be controlled without complex coding knowledge ([ROS Goss podcast interview](https://www.dwightco.com/podcast/from-europe-to-the-us-meet-robco)). Just as we edit photos or shop with a smartphone, users can teach robots what to do through an intuitive interface.

In addition, RobCo has adopted a 'shared-risk' model. It is a structure where the company and the customer share the financial and operational burden felt by the customer when introducing robots, helping manufacturing sites experiment with new technologies more safely ([ROS Goss podcast interview](https://www.dwightco.com/podcast/from-europe-to-the-us-meet-robco)).

## Where do they stand?

Headquartered in Munich, RobCo has grown into one of the most notable robotics companies in Europe ([Report on German robotics startup RobCo's investment](https://www.siliconrepublic.com/start-ups/german-automation-start-up-robco-munich-raises-100m-us-expansion)). The $100 million in funding secured this time will be used to bring their physical AI roadmap to life and solidify their position in the US market ([RobCo's latest funding round](https://theaimag.net/french-robotics-unicorn-exotec-unveils-new-global-hq-imaginarium-in-lille-northern-france-a-hub-for-retail-technology-innovation/)). They are currently proving their results by collaborating with experts in various manufacturing fields, including the automotive industry ([RoboDK blog: Automation in the automotive industry](https://robodk.com/blog/advanced-automation-automotive-industry/)).

## Future outlook

RobCo's entry into the US will be an important testbed to show how the trend of manufacturing automation will change. Beyond competing to simply improve robot performance, the key is how 'friendly' a robot can be made. Products we use in the future will be produced faster thanks to modular automation technologies like RobCo's. Also, it is highly likely that the roles of manufacturing workers will change from simple physical labor like carrying heavy loads to technical tasks of commanding and managing robots.

## AI Opinion

RobCo's modular approach is a key strategy for lowering the barrier to robot adoption. If robots move away from the perception of being complex and expensive equipment and become tools that can be expanded as needed, automation will become a daily reality even for small and medium-sized enterprises. Such flexible automation systems combined with physical AI will become the standard for future manufacturing sites.
