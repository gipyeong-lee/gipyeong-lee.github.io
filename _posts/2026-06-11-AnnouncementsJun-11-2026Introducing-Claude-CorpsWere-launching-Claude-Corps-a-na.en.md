### Why It Matters

We have often seen numerous giant IT companies donate massive amounts of money or software, claiming to give back to the world. Usually, these donation activities stop at merely tossing over 'free passes' or license accounts, saying, "We'll let you use our company's latest software for free for the next year." Of course, that in itself is a wonderful act of sharing, but unfortunately, the reality on the ground at nonprofits is very cold. No matter how good the tools given are, if they don't know how to turn them on right now or how to apply them to their organization's unique tasks, they will eventually end up as useless burdens sitting on computer desktops.

Simply put, it's like handing a bunch of top-tier ingredients to someone who doesn't know how to cook at all and expecting a wonderful dish. No matter how good the ingredients are, if you don't know how to cook them, they will just spoil in the refrigerator.

In this regard, Anthropic's new approach is completely different from typical charity work. They are not merely providing cold technology or system access. Anthropic has decided to dispatch people—trained 'coaches'—directly to nonprofits across the country, which are often in the blind spots of the latest technology and always thirsty for efficiency [Anthropic launches Claude Corps fellowship for nonprofits | Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropic-launches-claude-corps-fellowship-for-nonprofits-6e8df3a0). 

Going beyond simply giving money and access, they are investing 'people' to firmly bridge the vast gap between technology and the field. This is the most crucial and heart-pounding differentiator of the Claude Corps program. Technology is always cold and complex, but ultimately, it is the warm touch of human hands that makes that technology meaningful. If nonprofits receive careful help from these coaches to utilize artificial intelligence more effectively in their work [Anthropic announces 'Claude Corps' to teach nonprofits to use AI more effectively - Yahoo News UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/anthropic-announces-claude-corps-teach-130127973.html), it means that the overwhelming efficiency previously enjoyed only by the top 1% of Silicon Valley companies will deeply permeate local libraries, environmental protection groups, and poverty relief organizations.

### The Explainer

Then how exactly does this 'Claude Corps' program operate? Let me give you a more specific analogy. 

Imagine a wealthy person built a state-of-the-art, premium gym in the neighborhood, claiming to improve the health of local residents, and just mailed them 'free memberships.' Seniors who have never been to a gym are bound to be baffled by the shiny treadmills and complex strength training machines. If used incorrectly, they could get seriously hurt. Eventually, no matter how good the equipment is, people will stop visiting the gym. 

But Claude Corps is completely different. It doesn't stop at just throwing the doors of the state-of-the-art gym (AI model) wide open. It is exactly like assigning a '1:1 personal trainer (PT)' to every resident, who kindly teaches them how to use the equipment and carefully designs a workout plan suited to their individual fitness levels. So that nonprofit activists don't get lost in front of the complex machine called artificial intelligence, the dispatched young coaches stick right by their side, step-by-step teaching them customized AI usage tailored to the organization's essential tasks—from automating Excel data organization to writing promotional emails for sponsors.

This massive newly-launched project is not the unilateral move of a single company but has a very unique and stable structure where three different organizations have formed a solid partnership to operate organically. Within this strong alliance, Anthropic plays a pivotal role by providing a massive fund of $150 million, spearheading the overarching strategy, and generously granting full access to 'Claude,' its most powerful and intelligent language model [Introducing Claude Corps \ Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-corps).

Then who exactly are these coaches who will be dispatched directly to the fierce front lines of nonprofits to act as 1:1 trainers? 

Anthropic has filled this special fellowship with young, passionate professionals in the early stages of their careers—in other words, those who have just taken their first steps into society or are desperate for professional growth [Introducing Claude Design by Anthropic Labs \ Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-design-anthropic-labs). Only those with a burning passion to spread the warm benefits of AI to local communities across the US will join [Introducing the Services Track and Partner Hub of the Claude Partner Network \ Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/services-track-partner-hub).

To use an analogy, these young professionals are like 'foreign language interpreters' or 'friendly local guides.' To the general public, the complex and cold language of technology used by Silicon Valley engineers sounds like completely unknown alien gibberish. On the other hand, activists in the field of nonprofits have their own tight-knit, unique language dealing with the hardships of the field and complex social issues. The dispatched coaches play the great role of building a sturdy bridge between these two worlds and smoothly translating the difficult language of technology into a very practical and easy language that can be used immediately in the field.

### Where We Stand

So why is Anthropic starting such a program at the astronomical cost of $150 million? Is it simply a good deed to nicely package the company's image?

Surprisingly, Anthropic's ultimate goal aims for a place much larger and more profound. The 'Claude Corps' program goes beyond declaring help for nonprofits; it was unveiled to the world in tandem with the announcement of a macro policy framework (a framework and direction for establishing consistent laws or policies) to deeply address the fundamental impact the massive wave of artificial intelligence will have on 'how we work' and 'jobs' [Introducing Claude Corps \ Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-corps).

Anthropic intends to closely observe and study under a microscope, right in the field, the moment of collision when highly advanced AI technology is first introduced to offices or job sites—that is, the social changes and work environment disruption that inevitably occur [Claude Corps: Anthropic launches team to teach nonprofits to embrace AI | AP News](https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-ai-claude-corps-daniela-amodei-b1c130a08417d13e1256f8982d233b0e). 

Regarding this massive social science experiment that goes beyond simple volunteer work, Daniela Amodei, a key figure at Anthropic, left a very sharp and meaningful insight. She asserted: 

"If we don't study it (social disruption) firsthand, transparently publish it, and continuously discuss it, we will never be able to understand what the social disruption that will come in the future will look like." [Claude Corps: Anthropic launches team to teach nonprofits to embrace AI | AP News](https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-ai-claude-corps-daniela-amodei-b1c130a08417d13e1256f8982d233b0e)

When a new disruptive technology appears in the workplace, someone is inevitably bound to be frightened. It's the heavy anxiety of 'Will my lifelong job be completely replaced by AI?' or 'Will the excellent work methods I've adhered to for decades become useless relics overnight?' Amodei's remark is a strong warning that this vague anxiety across society must never be simply covered up or ignored. 

The true hidden purpose of this massive investment is to precisely and academically study the vivid process of friction itself—how people's work processes are specifically broken down and reassembled, whether artificial intelligence pushes humans out, or rather assists human work to improve performance—when AI is deployed into the most unpredictable work environment of nonprofits.
