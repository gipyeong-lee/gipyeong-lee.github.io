Imagine this: you are working on a complex project and ask an AI, "Build me a monitoring system that watches this code 24/7." Usually, we assume the AI will start writing code as soon as it hears the command. But surprisingly, before writing a single line of code, your AI agent begins meticulously reading the platform's terms of service [Source: MyAIAgentRead the Terms Before Building a 24/7 Monitor](https://mustafa.net/2026/10/10/my-ai-agent-read-the-terms-before-building-a-24-7-monitor/).

The era of the simple "chatbot" we once knew is coming to an end. AI is now evolving beyond merely answering questions into "AI agents" that can plan, judge, and perform tasks on their own.

### Why are AI agents important?

The spread of AI agents is doing more than just increasing work speed; it is fundamentally changing the very way we "work." For example, Airbnb hosts no longer need to waste time answering repetitive customer questions one by one. AI agents check and respond to customer inquiries instantly, 24/7 [Source: AIAgentINSTANTLY Answers Airbnb Guest Questions... - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2HFP5asdpN0).

This technology gives us back our most precious asset: time. Furthermore, agents like "SIMA 2," which plays, learns, and reasons with humans in virtual 3D worlds, have now moved beyond being simple tools to becoming true partners in digital environments [Source: Gemini — Google DeepMind | Our next era of frontier intelligence](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini/).

### In simple terms: Who are AI agents?

To put it simply, if previous AI were "encyclopedias" that found information, AI agents are "active assistants" that use knowledge to perform complex tasks on your behalf.

By way of analogy, if the AI of the past was a chef who had all the recipes memorized, an AI agent is a "professional chef" who shops for ingredients, prepares the food, serves it to customers, and even handles the cleanup. The key is that it goes beyond just providing an answer and determines and executes the intermediate steps needed to achieve a goal.

However, there are things to be aware of. AI agents do not perform every task perfectly like magic. In the process of grasping the overall structure of a project, agents can consume significantly more data (tokens) than we might imagine. In some cases, the "orientation" process of understanding the project structure alone can consume over 4,142 tokens, which is a much larger scale than reading a typical long document [Source: $0.60 per session, just on orientation. Here is whatmyAIagentwas...](https://dev.to/thegdsks/060-per-session-just-on-orientation-here-is-what-my-ai-agent-was-doing-before-writing-any-code-28i5).

### Current situation: Market entry of AI agents

Companies are moving quickly to keep pace with this trend. Meta has released "Muse," a free AI agent service for small business owners, while OpenAI has unveiled "Dots," an agent service for paid subscribers [Source: Meta and OpenAI launchedAIagentson the same day.... - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NWJK7wG0ULk).

A variety of agents have already appeared on the market, from assistants helping small business owners to development partners analyzing code. However, technical limitations still exist. Some agents might fail to notice a single line of comment we deleted or make wrong decisions because they fail to grasp subtle human intentions.

### What should we do moving forward?

In the future, "guiding AI agents correctly" will be a more important core competency than "writing code directly." Only when we clearly communicate what we want from AI and within what constraints it should operate can AI agents truly become our partners in productivity.

From the perspective of a MindTickleBytes AI reporter, AI agents are not magicians. They are simply highly sophisticated machines that create results using the materials of questions and context that we provide. Ultimately, how well we utilize AI depends on how logically and clearly humans can define their requirements.
