Imagine this. How would it feel if the maker of the artificial intelligence (AI) assistant you chat with every day to help with work and brainstorm ideas suddenly appeared on Nasdaq, the center of global finance, and swept up tens of trillions of won in funds in a single day? It might feel like a scene from a sci-fi movie, but this is what actually happened. Artificial intelligence (AI) company Anthropic has taken its historic first step on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol 'ANT' [Source 4, Source 5].

This listing goes beyond simply entering the stock market for a single company; it is a massive event showing how the global tech market and investors evaluate the future of artificial intelligence. Far exceeding the initial IPO price guidance range of $70 to $78, the price was finalized at $83, successfully raising a record-breaking $81 billion (over 110 trillion won) [Source 6, Source 13]. On its first day of trading, the stock was expected to open around $105—surging 27% above the IPO price—drawing intense interest from investors [Source 12].

## Why This Matters

To the general public or average office workers, a company's listing and multi-trillion-won valuation might seem like a distant story full of numbers. However, Anthropic's Nasdaq debut is an important compass to gauge the sustainability of the AI services we use and the speed of technological changes coming into our lives.

Simply put, for the AI chatbots we use daily to remain stable and get smarter, a massive invisible engine and fuel must be continuously supplied. The immense capital Anthropic raised from the market becomes the driving force to keep that engine running and upgrading. Conversely, if market evaluation falters, it could also affect the cost structure and accessibility of the AI benefits we enjoy. In other words, this listing is the most vivid evidence of how the pace of technological advancement intertwines with Wall Street's capital power.

## Easy Explanation: An AI Company's IPO is Like Investing in a Massive Factory

To put it simply, Anthropic's Nasdaq listing is akin to revealing a colossal intelligence factory—providing top-tier customized advice in real-time to tens of millions of people worldwide—in the public square of the stock market, and expanding it into collective ownership by more people.

What we need to pay attention to here is how this factory operates. Instead of directly owning and operating its own data center buildings, Anthropic relies on massive external computing and infrastructure [Source 14]. For example, it is similar to a delivery company renting state-of-the-art external infrastructure by paying hefty fees instead of buying trucks or warehouses directly. In 2025 alone, computing and infrastructure costs reached $7.3 billion, and in the first six months of 2026, it already spent $8.9 billion [Source 14]. On the other hand, the amount spent on purchasing its own real estate or equipment was a mere $482 million [Source 14].

This structure is like running a large theme park while leasing the ride facilities themselves for enormous rental fees. As visitors (users) increase explosively, revenue rises steeply, but at the same time, ride maintenance costs (infrastructure costs) skyrocket along with it.

## Current Situation: Astronomical Costs Masked by Phenomenal Revenue Growth

So why did the market go wild? The answer lies in its explosive growth potential. Anthropic reported approximately $17 billion in revenue for its recent third-quarter results [Source 5]. This is an astonishing 1,127% surge compared to the same period last year, and a remarkable 47% growth compared to the previous quarter (Q2) [Source 5].

However, behind the dazzling revenue lies the shadow of massive expenditures. During the same period, Anthropic recorded a net loss of $2.329 billion (over 3 trillion won) [Source 5]. Due to astronomical infrastructure costs and R&D investments, the scale of the deficit is also substantial. Nevertheless, market investors focused on Anthropic's overwhelming growth speed rather than its losses. In fact, in this IPO, investor order oversubscription was explosive, reaching a staggering 7 times the supplied stock volume [Source 13].

The company's liquid assets also reached $146.6 billion, providing a solid defense in terms of short-term financial stability [Source 5]. Some projected, comparing it to the valuation recorded by Elon Musk's space and AI company SpaceX (about $1.8 trillion), that Anthropic's corporate value would reach between $1.3 trillion and $1.8 trillion, with first-day expectations peaking at up to $1.8 trillion [Source 2, Source 6, Source 8].

Anthropic's successful Nasdaq debut is expected to have a profound impact on the tech industry at large. First, the channel for companies developing and servicing large-scale artificial intelligence models to raise massive funds from public capital markets has opened much wider. Second, investor expectations are likely to become much stricter. No matter how much revenue grows by over 1,000%, they will continuously be required to prove how efficiently they can control astronomical deficits and infrastructure costs and convert them into a profitable business model. What readers should watch moving forward is how much Anthropic increases the efficiency of next-generation AI models and reduces the burden of infrastructure costs based on the massive $81 billion it secured [Source 6].
