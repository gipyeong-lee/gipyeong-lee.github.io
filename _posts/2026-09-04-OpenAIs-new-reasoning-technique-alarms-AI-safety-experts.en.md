Imagine this: You are writing a highly complex business plan at work and ask an AI for help. Until now, AI has been accustomed to 'sequential thinking,' where it thinks step-by-step, much like a chef preparing ingredients and cooking according to a recipe. But what if the AI suddenly starts changing the cooking order on its own, or mixing several ingredients in its head all at once to arrive at a completely new conclusion we didn't expect?

Recently, OpenAI unveiled 'recurrent depth,' a new reasoning technology to be applied to its next-generation AI model, 'Astra,' sparking a heated debate among the AI industry and safety experts. [Source 1](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/openais-new-reasoning-technique-alarms-ai-safety-experts/)

## Why It Matters

It is certainly welcome news that the AI we use is becoming smarter. However, what happens if we can no longer understand the 'process of becoming smarter'? Existing AI reasoning models had a relatively clear 'order' in how information was input and output. [Source 8](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/chatgpt/articles/openai-reasoning-technique-alarms-ai-201914302.html) But this new technology seeks to fundamentally change how AI reaches conclusions.

If AI begins to think in ways we cannot predict, it becomes very difficult to prevent AI malfunctions or biased judgments in advance. This goes beyond the issue of AI performance and touches on the issue of trust—how much can we trust and rely on AI that has become deeply integrated into our lives?

## The Explainer

Does the term 'recurrent depth' sound a bit difficult? Let’s use a very simple analogy.

If traditional AI is a **'chef,'** the newly introduced method is closer to a **'painter.'** A chef follows a 'sequential process' of slicing ingredient 1, stir-frying ingredient 2, and boiling ingredient 3. If we peek into the kitchen from the outside, we can quickly tell what the chef is doing.

On the other hand, 'recurrent depth' is similar to a painter who applies paint to a canvas, then continues to layer over it to capture a form. After receiving input, the AI continuously reprocesses its internal state within its 'hidden space.' [Source 2](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/openai-s-new-recurrent-depth-ai-technique-sparks-safety-concerns) In other words, it doesn't just think once and finish; it circles and refines information in its head until it reaches a conclusion.

Simply put, while traditional AI followed a set path, an AI using this technology is like finding the optimal exit while circling in a maze. This allows the AI to solve more complex problems, but it makes real-time external monitoring of the AI's 'thought process' as difficult as finding the original sketch under layers of paint on a finished canvas. [Source 2](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/openai-s-new-recurrent-depth-ai-technique-sparks-safety-concerns)

## Where We Stand

This technology is currently raising significant concerns among AI safety experts. [Source 10](https://en.ammonnews.net/article/94883) In fact, OpenAI even temporarily halted training for several weeks during the development of Astra and other future models to account for safety issues. [Source 11](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-astra-first-ai-model-with-critical-cyber-abilities/)

OpenAI has stated that it has resumed work after strengthening internal control and security systems to reflect these safety concerns. [Source 11](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-astra-first-ai-model-with-critical-cyber-abilities/) However, experts continue to question how difficult it is to fully control AI as it becomes more complex.

## What's Next

In the future, it might become much harder for us to directly verify 'why and through what path' an AI reached its answer. Instead, we will face the challenge of more strictly verifying the AI's 'output.' Our society will need constant consensus on whether the safety control measures OpenAI claims to have strengthened will actually be effective, and whether the performance gains brought by this new reasoning method are worth the risks we must bear.

While technological advancement is important, isn't ensuring that such progress stays within our control the true measure of 'intelligence'?
