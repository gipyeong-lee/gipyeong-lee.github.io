Imagine this: your company has ambitiously introduced an AI assistant to boost work efficiency. But one day, management suddenly says, "Why are we using another company's assistant when we have one made in-house? It costs money and makes data management difficult, so let's focus more on our own tools."

This is actually happening among the tech giants of Silicon Valley. Meta and Microsoft have begun "cleaning up" their internal AI tools. Let's break down why they made this decision and what this news means for us.

### Why is this important?

While it might look like a simple internal policy of "use our company's product," this issue is a significant signal of the strategic stance these massive tech companies are taking toward generative AI.

AI has now gone beyond experimental tools to become a core asset that dictates work productivity. For companies, the monthly AI usage fees are snowballing. In Microsoft's case, the estimated cost they were set to pour into Claude usage reached approximately $1 billion [Source: GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9109975/meta-and-microsoft-reduce-employee-use-of-claude-ai). We have entered an era where cost management determines business success or failure.

### To put it simply, by way of analogy

'Claude' is a high-performance AI chatbot developed by Anthropic [Source: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_(AI)). It has been loved by many office workers for its ability to converse like a human, write complex code, and summarize long documents.

Let's use an analogy. Suppose we use a smartphone photo editing app and, because we like the filters of a specific app, we pay for it monthly. Then, the smartphone manufacturer starts providing a high-level photo editing feature of its own for free. What would the company want in this situation? They would want employees to use internal functions to manage data safely and reduce costs leaking to the outside.

The same applies to Meta and Microsoft. It is not that they are denying Claude's performance, but a strategic choice to have employees utilize self-developed tools like Copilot more, build their own technical expertise, and increase cost efficiency [Source: Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/news-microsoft-meta-cut-internal-claude-use/).

### What is the current situation?

Meta and Microsoft are systematically reducing the degree to which employees rely on Claude [Source: Finance Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/meta-microsoft-scale-back-internal-185842649.html). Microsoft has already officially requested that employees restrict their use of Claude and devote more time to their own AI tools [Source: TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/meta-and-microsoft-want-to-stop-their-employees-using-claude).

However, they haven't blocked all access entirely. Industry experts analyze that both companies are not completely severing ties with Claude [Source: Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/news-microsoft-meta-cut-internal-claude-use/). They are in a phase of inducing selective use in line with the company's strategic direction rather than indiscriminate use, while still allowing external tools to be used in parallel when necessary.

### What will happen in the future?

Moving forward, companies will accelerate the building of an 'AI self-sufficiency' environment. Beyond the stage of borrowing other companies' tools, securing an independent AI ecosystem that can safely learn and utilize internal corporate data is, in itself, a powerful competitive edge. As the AI tools you use at work continue to increase, I hope you will keep an eye on how companies manage data and what innovative proprietary tools they introduce in the future.

### AI's Perspective (MindTickleBytes AI Reporter)

It is a very rational choice for giant corporations to lower their reliance on external tools in terms of efficiency. However, because the pace of AI technological advancement is so fast, there is also a risk of missing out on more innovative external technology if they insist solely on their own tools. In the end, companies will be on a constant tightrope walk between the 'stability of internal development' and the 'innovation of external tools.'
