Imagine this: you sit down at your computer in the morning, and the complex tasks you worked on yesterday have already been organized by AI, with meeting minutes and next steps automatically recommended. An era is coming where the trivial work processes we used to miss due to the limitations of human "memory" come together to create your own smart work assistant.

[Screenpipe](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/screenpipe), which was recently selected for the S26 batch of Y Combinator, the most prominent startup accelerator in Silicon Valley, is painting just such a future. It is not merely a screen recording tool; it is a tool that remembers your work habits and creates "context" for AI.

## Why does this matter?

Have you ever felt frustrated while using AI, thinking, "The AI doesn't know my work style well, so I have to explain the situation every single time"? Corporate work is complex and sophisticated. Countless "ways of working" that are not organized in company wikis or CRMs (Customer Relationship Management systems) are already embedded in your screen and conversations.

Screenpipe turns this "hidden context" into data that AI can understand. According to [Source 6](https://screenpipe.com/blog/screenpipe-v2-13-yc-s26-may-changelog), the richest work context we possess is not in documents, but on the screens we look at every day. For an AI agent (an AI that makes judgments and performs tasks on its own based on user instructions) to automate work, it must first know how that work is done. Screenpipe acts as the bridge.

## Easy to understand

To understand Screenpipe, imagine "an AI's diet." Let's say delegating tasks to an AI agent is like "hiring a chef." But this chef doesn't know what your kitchen looks like or what cooking tools you usually use.

Screenpipe is a 24/7 recording device installed in your kitchen (your computer). According to [Source 1](https://github.com/screenpipe/screenpipe), this tool constantly records what you see, what you say, and what you do.

In simple terms, it is closer to a **secretary who organizes your memories** than just a recording tool. But if everything were saved as video, your computer's storage would run out quickly. That is why Screenpipe uses a much smarter method. According to [Source 10](https://explainx.ai/blog/screenpipe-yc-s26-local-work-memory-agents-july-2026), instead of saving everything by the second, it captures the screen and information only when specific "events" occur, such as app switching, mouse clicks, or typing stops. It is like a veteran photographer who only chooses to take photos of important moments.

Our days are filled with countless pieces of information. Screenpipe is not like high-resolution CCTV that records everything; it is more like a secretary with an excellent memory who carefully notes down the flow of key tasks over your shoulder. These organized memories become a solid foundation for AI to perfectly emulate your methods.

## Current status

Currently, Screenpipe was founded in 2024 by Louis Beaumont and is operated by a team of six based in San Francisco [Source 3](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/screenpipe). According to [Source 4](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/screenpipe-yc-s26-local-work-memory-agents-july-2026), it has already gained massive popularity among developers, recording over 20,000 GitHub stars (an indicator of developer project preference).

Users can securely manage all data generated on their device locally (inside the device without passing through a cloud server) [Source 1](https://github.com/screenpipe/screenpipe), [Source 9](https://github.com/screenpipe/screenpipe/releases). Looking at [Source 13](https://mcprepository.com/screenpipe/screenpipe), it is already in a state ready for immediate use by connecting with over 100 apps, including AI agents like OpenClaw and Hermes.

However, since it records the screen, concerns about privacy may exist. As seen in [Source 15](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41695840), some in online communities have pointed out that a cautious approach is needed regarding the recording of others' data or private meeting content.

## What will happen in the future?

The future Screenpipe is painting extends beyond the "recording individual" to the "recording organization." In [Source 12](https://x.com/screenpipe), the team proposes a vision where screen data of all members is centralized, and hundreds of AI agents process work 24/7 based on that data. The message, "Don't hire 500 people, record 12 and hire 500 AI agents," clearly illustrates the future of work. Just as someone who meticulously keeps a daily diary can write an autobiography very easily later, a world is approaching where the entire organization records its work methods, allowing AI to learn company culture and perform work on their behalf.

Moving forward, Screenpipe is expected to evolve beyond simple recording into an automation environment where the user can execute anything just by speaking [Source 16](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/y-combinator_screenpipe-yc-s26-lets-you-record-how-you-activity-7482811226582867968-zym2).

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

The emergence of Screenpipe clearly shows that the key connecting link to transition into the AI agent era is "everyday personal recording." We need to keep an eye on whether their attempt to provide rich context to AI while maintaining privacy will accelerate a future where countless tasks can be completed with "a single word." Ultimately, technology is moving in a direction that complements human memory to allow us to focus on more creative work, rather than replacing humans.
