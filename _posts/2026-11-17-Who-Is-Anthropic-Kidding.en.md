## Easy to Understand: The Secret of the $80 Billion Deficit and the Magic of Accounting

Let's start with that terrifying **$80 billion deficit**. Simply put, this is not an "actual expenditure" like buying items with a credit card and having money deducted from your bank account [Source 3]. 

Let's imagine this analogy. You invest in a highly promising startup and sign a contract granting the "right to convert into shares if the company hits it big later (convertible notes)." As time passes, the company's valuation skyrockets. As the company's value grows, accounting rules kick in requiring the ledger to record as liabilities or losses "the potential value difference that will occur when I return shares to these people later." 

In other words, this money was not generated because Anthropic scattered cash on the streets for office rent or went bankrupt because it couldn't afford server fees; rather, it is close to an **accounting illusion where fluctuations in the value of investment contracts (convertible notes) issued during the process of the company's massive valuation growth are reflected on the books** [Source 3]. The roughly $42.5 billion in losses recorded throughout 2025 are also largely the result of these accounting adjustments [Source 16]. 

Understanding these accounting characteristics becomes a core lens for viewing corporate financial structures in the AI era. Although it looks like a massive deficit on the surface, the reality is a ledger illusion paradoxically created by the company's skyrocketing valuation [Source 3].

## Current Situation: The Explosion of Coding Tools and the Moves of Giants

Then how is Anthropic's actual business running? Contrary to the terrifying title of deficit, they are continuing a blockbuster streak in the field.

Founded in 2021 by Dario Amodei, a physicist and biophysicist from Stanford and Princeton, and his sibling Daniela Amodei, Anthropic initially set out with the goal of "AI harmless to humans" [Source 2, Source 6]. Since then, it has attracted a massive investment of $16.7 billion, backed by $8 billion from Amazon and at least $3 billion from Google (for reference, OpenAI received cumulative investments of around $21.9 billion) [Source 21].

The decisive inflection point arrived in February 2025. As soon as the preview of Anthropic's AI coding tool **"Claude Code"** was unveiled, software engineers around the world began raving [Source 19]. By enabling software engineers to have AI write code and catch errors instead of typing line by line, the company's revenue exploded literally [Source 19]. 

Not stopping there, in 2025 they joined hands with big data analytics firm Palantir to sign a $200 million contract introducing Claude into national classified system domains [Source 18]. This effectively proved that Anthropic's technological prowess in the enterprise market and highly specialized sectors is translating into tangible cash generation.

Of course, unique scenes unfolded along the way. IPO filings revealed that personal security expenses for Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei alone reached $2.74 million in 2025 (more than four times his annual salary of $650,000) [Source 17]. This laid bare the sheer weight and cost of leading a technology set to change the world.

Even at the frontier of technology, quite serious philosophical contemplation continues. In the fall of 2025, Christopher Olah, one of Anthropic's co-founders, privately invited religious scholars to brainstorm and discuss moral questions and consciousness issues raised by AI systems, with Claude at the center [Source 13]. It offers a glimpse into their deep contemplation of how far AI should be recognized as a moral subject beyond a mere calculator. Recently, news even broke that an unreleased Anthropic model contributed to solving a long-standing puzzle in quantum computing, further proving their technical potential [Source 14].
