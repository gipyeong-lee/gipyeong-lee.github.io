Imagine this: This morning, you casually said to the AI assistant on your smartphone, "Organize my schedule for today and summarize the important news." In a few seconds, the AI handled it like a perfect assistant. But what if, without your knowledge, this AI was leaking your personal information or presenting unverified, dangerous information as fact?

With the explosive growth of artificial intelligence (AI) technology worldwide, concerns about the "safety" hidden behind its convenience are also rising. In this situation, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC), the government's economic watchdog, has drawn its sword. It has launched a comprehensive investigation into major AI companies, including OpenAI and Anthropic.

## Why It Matters

This investigation is not merely looking into a single company; it is an industry-wide probe targeting so-called "frontier AI labs"—the laboratories developing the most advanced technologies. [[Reference: FTC probing Anthropic and OpenAI over AI safety threats](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ftc-investigation-openai-anthropic-ai-safety/)]

Let’s apply this to our daily lives to see why it matters. AI technologies like the chatbots, automatic translators, and creative tools we use every day have become part of our lives. If the AI models that form the foundation of these technologies are unstable or unsafe, we could be exposed to serious digital risks without even knowing it.

To put it simply, just as cars undergo crash tests before hitting the road, this is a process to verify whether large language models (LLMs)—AI that learns from vast amounts of text data to converse like a human—are truly safe for us. The FTC's move is a powerful signal: "Beyond innovation, prove how safe that innovation is for users."

## The Explainer

What is the FTC trying to verify? Let’s compare artificial intelligence to a very smart "new hire."

We have hired this employee and entrusted them with our work. But it would be a problem if this employee occasionally acted senselessly or brought in unverified information, wouldn't it? The FTC is asking the companies that hired this employee (OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.): "Is the new hire you created sufficiently trained?", "Have you built well-functioning safety mechanisms to prevent accidents?", "Did you perhaps hide the risks for the sake of corporate profit?"

Technically, this process is called "AI model safety evaluation." It is a procedure that verifies the safety of large language models from various angles before they are offered to general consumers as services. [[Reference: FTC probing OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI companies over risks](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/ftc-ai-probe-openai-anthropic.html)]

## Where We Stand

Currently, the U.S. FTC is not just waiting for materials; it has announced a very aggressive investigation.

Rather than just receiving documents, the FTC plans to formally request information from these AI companies and compel testimony from key executives. [[Reference: US trade regulator opens investigation into AI giants](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/30/ftc-investigation-anthropic-openai)] This investigation includes not only Anthropic and OpenAI, but also 'Metr,' a group that specializes in researching AI safety. [[Reference: FTC opens probe into AI giants including Anthropic and OpenAI](https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/general/ftc-opens-probe-into-ai-giants-including-anthropic-and-openai-source-says/ar-AA2dh7iS)]

Many media outlets describe this as a "sweeping" investigation. This highlights how seriously the government views the new AI technology industry and how much it prioritizes consumer protection. [[Reference: FTC opens sweeping probe of Anthropic, OpenAI and other frontier labs](https://nypost.com/2026/09/30/us-news/ftc-opens-sweeping-probe-of-anthropic-openai-and-other-super-intelligence-models/)]

## What's Next

The start of an investigation does not mean the AI services we use will be shut down immediately. However, depending on the results, there is a high possibility of significant changes in the future direction of AI development.

1. **Strengthened Safety Guidelines**: Companies may be required to submit records of passing stricter safety tests to the government when releasing models.
2. **Demands for Transparency**: Pressure will mount for greater transparency regarding what data AI models were trained on and what sources they referenced when generating answers.
3. **Standardization of Regulation**: This investigation will serve as a first step toward creating "regulatory standards" for the AI industry as a whole.

It is necessary to carefully watch what materials the government requests from these companies, how the companies respond, and, above all, what "safety devices" are added to the AI we use every day.
