## A Deeper Look: Deleting Personal Information is Like Erasing with a Eraser

Shall we compare the process of deleting personal information to the task of "erasing a drawing with an eraser"?

Data brokers are managing your information like "books piled up in a public library." You must formally go to the library director (data broker) and request, "Please dispose of this book (my information)." [How to Remove Yourself from Data Broker Sites](https://www.privacy.com/blog/how-to-remove-yourself-from-data-broker-sites) Existing services used a method of hiring an "agency" to make this disposal request on your behalf. On the other hand, recently emerged open-source agent tools utilize "intelligent automation assistants" that figure out the library's disposal procedure (protocol) for you and automatically send a deletion request. [How to Remove Yourself from Data Broker Sites](https://www.privacy.com/blog/how-to-remove-yourself-from-data-broker-sites)

Beyond simple automation, these agent tools have features such as recording which requests were sent in SQLite (a light and powerful database engine) format or allowing you to check the results directly on your computer (local host). ## Where do we stand now?

There are currently three main ways to delete personal information.
1. **Using paid services**: They cost money but are the most convenient. [Incogni vs. DeleteMe Comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7S5NMrxCvY)
2. **Manual deletion**: It is the most certain, but it takes a very long time because you have to figure out the deletion protocol for each site. [How to Remove Yourself from Data Broker Sites](https://www.privacy.com/blog/how-to-remove-yourself-from-data-broker-sites)
3. **Open-source automation**: This is the method drawing attention among technically savvy users recently.

Especially for those living in California, you can use a legal device called the 'Delete Act (DROP)' to delete data much faster.
