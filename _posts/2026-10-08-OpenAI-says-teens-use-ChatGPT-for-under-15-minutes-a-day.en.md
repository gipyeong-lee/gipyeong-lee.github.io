Imagine this: When your child or niece is staring at their smartphone in their room, parents often worry, "Are they playing games again?" or "Are they just watching social media all day?" With generative AI (artificial intelligence) recently entering the educational landscape, concerns like "Will my child become addicted to ChatGPT and lose the ability to think for themselves?" are growing louder.

Recently, OpenAI, the developer of ChatGPT, released its first report on the actual usage status of ChatGPT by teenagers, seemingly in response to these public concerns. Are our children really trapped in the swamp of AI chatbots, unable to get out?

### Why is this important?

AI has now become a part of our daily lives, going beyond a mere technical tool. AI as a learning tool holds great potential while simultaneously raising concerns about excessive immersion or safety. Organizations related to digital welfare, such as 'Common Sense Media,' have even warned that ChatGPT could pose an 'unacceptable risk' to teenagers.

In this context, OpenAI's disclosure of this data is very significant. It allows us to verify how children are actually using AI through concrete indicators rather than having vague fears.

### Easy to understand: The magic of 15 minutes

The data was much calmer than expected. According to the report, the average daily usage time of ChatGPT by teenagers was recorded at **less than 15 minutes** [[Source: OpenAI Says Teens Average Under 15 Minutes a Day on ChatGPT](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-openai-says-teens-average-under-15-minutes-a-day-on-chatgpt/)]. So-called 'heavy users' who use it continuously for more than 3 hours accounted for less than 2% of the total [[Source: OpenAI's First Teen ChatGPT Report Says Median Teen Uses the App Under 15](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openais-first-teen-chatgpt-report-says-median-teen-uses-the-app-under-15)].

In simple terms, ChatGPT is closer to a 'digital reference book' that is used briefly to find necessary information or solve homework, rather than addictive content like games or social media (SNS) that teenagers have to be glued to all day.

What is particularly interesting is the quality of learning. It was confirmed that children using the 'ChatGPT for Teens' version sent on average **2.7 million more** learning-related messages than children using the regular version [[Source: OPENAI: TEENS AVERAGE UNDER 15 MINUTES A DAY ON CHATGPT](https://stockhub.kr/en/news/news_a85395b0afb0)]. The fact that about 1.2 million teenagers utilized the 'Learning Visualizations' function (a feature that shows complex concepts as graphs or charts) over one week also shows that this tool is being actively used as an academic aid [[Source: OpenAI Says Teens Average Under 15 Minutes a Day on ChatGPT](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-openai-says-teens-average-under-15-minutes-a-day-on-chatgpt/)].

### Current situation: Efforts for safety

Of course, just because the data is positive doesn't mean we can just stand by. OpenAI is also strengthening safety measures for children. A system that automatically recommends breaks when usage time becomes long has been introduced, and children who have actually experienced this are showing positive reactions [[Source: OpenAI says teen ChatGPT use limited but research finds it an](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwz0vrmxkvy4o)].

In addition, the company is continuing policy efforts, such as providing age-appropriate versions of the technology and reinforcing safety guidelines to ensure a safer environment [[Source: OpenAI says it is rolling out new safety measures for... - CBSNews](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/openai-chatgpt-under18-safety-controls-teens/)].

### What will happen in the future?

ChatGPT is now set to penetrate the education market even deeper through features like 'College Planner,' which helps with college entrance exams or high school coursework [[Source: OpenAI Says Teens Average Under 15 Minutes a Day on ChatGPT](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-openai-says-teens-average-under-15-minutes-a-day-on-chatgpt/)].

To use an analogy, it is similar to the vague anxiety we had when smartphones first appeared. What we need to pay attention to in the future is not 'usage time' itself, but the 'quality of usage.' Whether AI remains a tool that degrades children's thinking skills or becomes a catalyst that ignites children's intellectual curiosity as a personalized private tutor depends on how we educate and guide them about AI.

### AI's Perspective (MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective)

Technology itself is neither good nor evil. The 15 minutes of teenagers shows the possibility that AI can become a 'guide to knowledge,' not a 'shortcut to knowledge,' for children. What is important is digital literacy education. It is time for parents and the educational field to think together so that children can proactively utilize AI as a tool.
