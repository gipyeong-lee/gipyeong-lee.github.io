Imagine this: on a busy morning, all you have to do is tell your AI assistant, "Organize the materials needed for this afternoon's meeting and share them at the scheduled time." A world is coming where AI handles the operation of your computer to complete tasks, sparing you from the tedious process of sitting down, opening windows, searching, and saving files yourself. On September 3, 2026, OpenAI unveiled **GPT-6 Astra**, a next-generation AI model capable of autonomously using a computer to perform complex tasks [Source 5, Source 10, Source 12].

### Why Does This Matter?

Until now, AI has functioned more like a "consultant," primarily answering questions we input or writing text. But GPT-6 Astra is different. It is an "agent" model capable of independently resolving tasks ranging from software engineering and scientific research to everyday duties via web browsing [Source 13].

Simply put, if existing AI was like finding a book in a library and summarizing its contents, GPT-6 Astra has the ability to log into the library computer, access the necessary databases, and even process that data to create and save a new report file. In actual tests, this AI showed itself to be faster than an average human at scheduling DMV appointments, searching for job postings, and finding apartment listings [Source 14]. This signifies an evolution beyond simple information retrieval, toward AI functioning as an agent (an AI that autonomously achieves goals) capable of performing practical tasks in the digital world on our behalf.

OpenAI has classified this model as 'Critical' in the cybersecurity domain, pridefully stating that it is the safest and best-aligned (matching AI goals with human values) model released to date [Source 5, Source 14].

### AI Picks Up the Toolbox

To use an analogy for GPT-6 Astra, it is like giving a student who has just finished writing training a 'toolbox called a computer.' AI can now autonomously select and combine tools to produce results for tasks that previously required our manual instructions.

In fact, this model recorded a high bug-detection rate of approximately 61.3% in code review evaluations, outperforming previous powerful models like GPT-5.6 Sol (59.0%) and Opus 5 (50.2%) [Source 3]. This means we can expect the effect of having a highly meticulous, skilled worker inspecting our code line by line. We have gained a "smart colleague" who can provide practical, significant help with our work.

### Current Status: Can Everyone Use It?

Unfortunately, you cannot use GPT-6 Astra right away. OpenAI is currently providing priority access to 'vetted defenders' through a program called 'Daybreak,' and plans to expand this scope to more users via 'Daybreak Blue' within the next few weeks [Source 6].

The performance assessment is quite impressive. The model recorded a remarkable score of 100% on 'ExploitBench,' 98% in the 'FrontierMath' Level 4 evaluation, and 99.9% on the 'ARC-AGI-3' benchmark, quite literally setting a new standard for cutting-edge AI [Source 13]. However, the usage price is set between $10 and $50 per million tokens [Source 10]. Some analysts suggest that while the price is higher than previous models, the premium comes not from the performance itself, but from its 'reliable accuracy' and 'efficient token management' [Source 11].

### What Comes Next?

GPT-6 Astra signals that we have moved past the era of simple text-generating AI and into an era where AI increases practical productivity by utilizing human tools. Just as cases are emerging where AI independently carries out the entire process of YouTube video production, from planning and scriptwriting to utilizing video editing tools [Source 8], the way we work will rapidly shift toward contemplating "what to ask the AI to do" rather than "how to process it."

As OpenAI emphasizes that this is not only about benchmark scores but also the "safest model," it will be interesting to see how safely and usefully this smart AI will establish itself in our daily lives once it is released to the general public.
