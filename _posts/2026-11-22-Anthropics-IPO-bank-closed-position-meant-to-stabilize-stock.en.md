## Why Is This Important?

AI is already a familiar tool to us—easy to think of as a clever assistant that polishes writing or assists with coding. But the ecosystem of companies building AI is an entirely different story. Until now, countless artificial intelligence startups have relied heavily on massive investments or cloud infrastructure from Big Tech giants to stay afloat.

Anthropic's Initial Public Offering (IPO)—the process by which a company first offers its shares to the public and lists them—is **a historic event as the first pure-play AI-centric company to publicly sell stock to the general stock market** [Source 5]. In other words, it has stepped onto its first major test stage, escaping the shadow of mega-capitalists to face direct evaluation from retail and institutional investors worldwide. This report card serves as a giant compass gauging how sustainable a business model all AI technologies in the world possess, and how the technological investments impacting our society will be evaluated.

To put it into perspective, it is like an expedition team that left for an uninhabited island finally stepping foot into a massive Olympic stadium watched by the whole world. If this stage successfully establishes itself, more AI innovation companies will be able to achieve dazzling growth based on public capital. Conversely, if the market exhibits instability, it could serve as a critical turning point that sends a chill across technology stocks overall.

## Understanding Simply: The Stock Market, IPOs, and the Metaphor of a 'Stabilization Mechanism'

The process of a company listing its shares on the stock market for the first time can be compared to the following scenario:

Imagine a world-famous artist holding a massive art exhibition of works they poured years of heart and soul into, opening an auction house to sell fractional ownership of those pieces to the public for the first time. This is an **IPO**. Even before the auction begins, countless collectors from around the world flock in, lining up with the thought, "That work is going to be worth an astronomical amount."

Through its Nasdaq market debut—including underwriters' (investment banks and other stock issuance managing firms) additional share purchase options—Anthropic successfully raised a record-breaking amount of capital: **a staggering $93.5 billion (over 130 trillion won)** [Source 5, Source 11]. This was clear proof of how high public expectations are for AI's future potential.

However, what happens once the art auction concludes and actual trading begins? Because massive amounts of money pour in all at once, prices can shoot sky-high in an instant, only to plummet like a roller coaster on even the smallest rumor. It resembles a chaotic scene where countless people rush to exit an exhibition hall simultaneously.

At times like this, safety devices are needed to prevent prices from fluctuating too drastically. As reported by Reuters journalist Echo Wang, during Anthropic's IPO process, there was a strategic measure utilizing **closed positions by banks** to stabilize the stock price [Source 8]. Simply put, financial underwriters played a role behind the scenes to control volume and set up defensive walls, preventing the stock price from crashing instantly or being distorted by speculative forces. Metaphorically speaking, it is very similar to the role of tugboats firmly securing a massive cruise ship with sturdy ropes as it safely enters a narrow harbor amid a rough storm.

## Current Situation: A Dazzling Debut and the Cold Reality of the Market

The scene at the NASDAQ MarketSite in New York was charged with excitement [Source 14]. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and President & Co-Founder Daniela Amodei rang the celebratory bell alongside Nasdaq CEO Adena Friedman, taking a dazzling first step [Source 14].

As trading began, the market's response was explosive. Anthropic successfully closed its first day up **about 17%** over its initial public offering price, with closing prices topping $97 and driving its **total market capitalization to an astronomical figure of nearly $1.3 trillion (and $1.75 trillion on a fully diluted basis)** [Source 12]. Market experts like Paul Meeks expressed anticipation, suggesting that Anthropic's listing would once again fuel overall AI investment enthusiasm [Source 9].

However, the sweet celebration did not last long. Before the euphoria of the first day could even fade, the cold reality of macroeconomic conditions reared its head. On Friday morning ahead of the weekend, Anthropic's stock opened down **8%**, recording a steep **22% drop** from its IPO high and giving back a significant portion of the massive early gains [Source 7].

To make matters worse, the overall market sentiment was depressed. According to analysis by Deutsche Bank analyst Jim Reid, the S&P 500—a broad indicator of the New York stock market—fell for **six consecutive sessions**, marking its longest losing streak since April 2024, while tech-heavy indices also closed down 1.3% [Source 6]. The prevailing analysis is that the listing of a tech giant like Anthropic was insufficient to single-handedly dispel macroeconomic anxieties and fears of AI investment overheating [Source 6]. The cold selling pressure of the market was starkly exposed as the stock dropped as low as $87.26 right after the market opened [Source 8].

## What Lies Ahead?

Anthropic's IPO and the subsequent stock volatility offer several important implications for the future path of the AI industry:

1. **Passing the Litmus Test for the AI Sector**: The very fact that Anthropic successfully secured massive capital as the world's first pure-play AI publicly traded company provided powerful momentum to the entire tech ecosystem [Source 5]. It remains closely tied to the future moves of other private heavyweights like SpaceX, serving as a solid funding lifeline [Source 10].
2. **The Gap Between Illusion and Reality**: No matter how advanced the technology is or how much it changes humanity's future, it cannot buck massive economic waves like interest rate hikes or a general market downturn. Moving forward, investors will more fiercely scrutinize actual profit-generating capabilities and sustainability, alongside dazzling visions.
3. **Maturity Amid Volatility**: The initial surge followed by the steep drop represents unavoidable growing pains that a nascent tech market must endure. Stock price stabilization measures by banks and large-scale capital inflows/outflows vividly demonstrate that AI stocks are being incorporated as a major pillar of the real economy rather than remaining a mere thematic fad [Source 8].
