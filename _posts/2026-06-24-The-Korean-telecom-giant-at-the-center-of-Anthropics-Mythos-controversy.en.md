Imagine this: You have a voice assistant or a smart AI service that you use on your smartphone every day. How would you feel if, one day, the White House contacted the AI service provider and ordered, "Do not give your most powerful AI model to a major Korean telecom company"?

Surprisingly, this actually happened. Recently, a clash occurred where the global artificial intelligence (AI) industry collided head-on with international politics. Specifically, SK Telecom, a leading Korean telecommunications company, has had its access to the advanced US AI model 'Claude Mythos' restricted. This incident goes beyond a simple corporate conflict; it is a decisive case demonstrating how AI technology is becoming a 'geopolitical weapon' in modern society and why national security has become inseparable from AI development.

## Why Does This Matter?

AI has moved beyond being mere software and has become a core asset that determines a nation's competitiveness. In simple terms, AI is like modern 'oil' or a 'strategic commodity.' The servers our AI models pass through and the partnerships formed by the companies involved in their development have entered the realm of national security.

This incident highlights just how closely international politics and technological security are intertwined as Korean companies grow into key partners in the global AI ecosystem. For everyday users like us who utilize AI services, it suggests that service policies from global tech firms can change without warning, and furthermore, it reminds us of the importance of 'technological sovereignty,' where we must protect our own technology ourselves.

## Easy Explanation: The Secret of the 'Super Tutor'

'Claude Mythos' is a highly intelligent Large Language Model (LLM—AI that learns from vast amounts of data to converse like a human) developed by Anthropic. Simply put, it is a 'super-intelligence' with extensive knowledge. SK Telecom was a major investor in Anthropic and a solid partner performing the AI collaboration project 'Project Glasswing' together [[Source 8], [Source 11]].

But why did the US government try to break up this relationship? To use an analogy, it's like hiring an exceptional tutor, only to find out that the tutor is also secretly connected to others you are wary of. The US government took issue with the fact that SK Telecom has suspicious relationships with entities connected to the state within China [[Source 1], [Source 11], [Source 14]]. From the US perspective, it has taken 'techno-protectionist' measures to prevent advanced AI technology from flowing into countries that are hostile or competitive [[Source 10]].

## Where Do We Stand?

The current situation is moving very rapidly. Last week, following a direct request from the White House, Anthropic revoked SK Telecom's access to Claude Mythos [[Source 11], [Source 14]]. This is part of the export control measures implemented by the US government on AI models, reflecting the government's resolve to strictly limit the use of advanced AI technology by foreign nationals or specific companies [[Source 12]].

Meanwhile, Anthropic is making multi-faceted efforts to resolve this situation. According to recent reports, Anthropic has reportedly delivered a proposal to Howard Lutnick's side to end the US government's ban on the use of the Mythos and Fable AI models [[Source 4]]. While Anthropic emphasizes transparency as a company striving for a safe AI transition, this situation well illustrates the realistic geopolitical weight that the AI industry must bear [[Source 9]].

## What Will Happen Next?

Experts agree that this incident is just the beginning. It is highly likely that more AI technologies will be managed at a level equivalent to national secrets or strategic materials in the future [[Source 10]]. Korean companies now face the major challenge of needing to contemplate how to build a safe and transparent technology ecosystem amidst a complicating international political landscape while pursuing innovation in hand with global partners.

We will have to watch carefully for the time being how AI technology is utilized amidst the struggle for hegemony between nations. In an era where technology no longer just remains technology but has become a 'weapon' crossing borders, by what standards should we accept and protect AI?

## MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

While technological advancement is based on global cooperation, the logic of power that controls it always draws borders. We are now living in an era that requires 'digital literacy'—the ability to read not just the functions of technology, but also the political context surrounding it.
