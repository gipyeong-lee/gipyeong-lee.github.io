Imagine this: You wake up in the morning, tell your AI, "Help me with the work code I need to do today," and like a seasoned colleague sitting right next to you, it writes the code instantly and shows you a preview of the results. Because of this incredible experience, the AI chatbot Claude has become an essential work tool for many people around the world. But behind this enjoyment, a subscription bill arrives every month. Today, we want to talk about why Claude is still chosen by so many people even while being called "the worst in terms of pricing."

### Why does this matter?

AI services are becoming everyday tools, much like electricity or water. For those who frequently code, organize data, or write creatively, AI chatbots are a crucial factor that dictates work efficiency. However, AI companies mainly opt for subscription models, and users raise complaints, asking, "I want to pay for what I use, so why do I have to pay a fixed monthly cost?" [References 15](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48808413). In a reality where pricing policies simultaneously pressure the sustainability of services and the wallets of users, it is very important to understand why Claude remains a "pricey but desirable" existence.

### Easy Understanding: A High-End Private Tutor

To use a simple analogy, Claude is like a "high-end private tutor at a prestigious school." If other AI services are like community centers that anyone can easily access, Claude specializes in more professional and in-depth conversations.

What we should note here is Claude's "pricing structure." Claude currently operates five subscription plans, including a free plan: Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise [References 1](https://claude.com/). However, many users find this subscription model inconvenient. In particular, how many messages a user can send (message limits) is determined by which subscription plan they are on, rather than the model's performance [References 6](https://aizolo.com/blog/claude-4-8-sonnet-message-limit/). In other words, it is a structure where you have to pay a more expensive subscription fee not to use a smarter model, but to have more conversations.

For instance, in the case of the latest model, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, the price is set at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, and this cost includes the entire cost of 'thinking tokens'—the process by which the AI thinks for itself [References 16](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-3-7-sonnet). Users struggle while looking at this complex price tag, but ultimately press the payment button again because of its outstanding coding results and unique features like 'Artifacts' (a feature that provides real-time previews of code results) [References 2](https://undetectable.ai/blog/claude-vs-gpt-4/). It is similar to revisiting an expensive restaurant despite knowing the price, simply because you cannot forget the taste.

### Current Situation

Claude is an AI chatbot developed by the company Anthropic, and it also refers to the large language model (LLM—an AI structure that learns from vast amounts of text data to create natural sentences) that underpins it [References 5](https://www.pluralsight.com/resources/blog/ai-and-data/what-is-claude-ai/). Created by people like Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and AI researcher Amanda Askell, Claude displays excellent ability in grasping the context of conversations, summarizing, or carrying out creative planning [References 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ugvHCXCOmm4).

However, the service is not always perfect. Sometimes an error message like "This isn’t working right now" pops up, flustering the user [References 9](https://www.izoate.com/blog/how-to-fix-this-isnt-working-right-now-error-in-claude-ai/). This is because Claude does not run within the user's browser, but processes all tasks on Anthropic's servers and streams the results (a method of receiving data in real-time and displaying it on the screen) [References 11](https://www.digitbin.com/fix-claude-previous-response-still-running/). It is a structure where it becomes unusable if there is a problem with the server, but users are loyal enough to wait for it to return to normal while checking the service status page [References 10](https://status.claude.com/).

### What will happen in the future?

As can be seen in the Apple App Store, Claude's popularity is rising steeply [References 7](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/28/anthropics-claude-apple-apps.html). In the future, Claude will evolve beyond just a chatbot that collects expensive subscription fees into an AI specialized in even more diverse fields. It is already becoming professional enough to categorize and provide information on styles ranging from Mid-Century Modern to Wabi-Sabi for interior designers [References 12](https://beginnersinai.org/claude-for-interior-designers/).

However, users' complaints about pricing remain a challenge to be solved. Voices are growing that an API-based pricing model, where you pay for what you use rather than a fixed subscription fee, would be more attractive [References 15](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48808413). It will be an interesting point to watch how the current subscription model changes as the AI market matures further.

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

Even while setting up a high barrier called 'price,' Claude has succeeded in providing 'substantial value' that makes people want to get inside. For many users, AI now seems to have firmly established itself as an area of 'investment' to maximize work efficiency, rather than a mere cost.
