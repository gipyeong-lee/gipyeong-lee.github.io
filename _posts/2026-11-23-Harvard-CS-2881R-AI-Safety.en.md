Imagine this. One morning, you wake up, pick up your smartphone, and casually ask your AI assistant, "Please neatly organize my schedule for today and cleanly draft an important business email." In an instant, the AI crafts an email with astonishingly perfect sentences. But what would happen if this AI, contrary to your true intentions, started transmitting all of your company's highly confidential internal data to an external server or making unilateral decisions critical to our lives? As the intelligence of AI advances dazzlingly, we face a massive question we can no longer ignore: **"Can we truly fully control this rapidly expanding, uncontrollable intelligence?"**

This dizzying question is no longer a distant story or exaggerated scenario from a sci-fi movie. At Harvard University, one of the world's most prestigious universities, a formal course directly addressing this issue has been established, drawing fervent global attention [Source 15]. Let's step into Harvard's frontline lecture hall, which delves into the essence of "safety" and "alignment" deeply cast behind the explosive growth of artificial intelligence.

---

## Why Is This Important?

Artificial intelligence (AI) technology is evolving at an astonishing pace, breaking new records every single day. Chatbots have now moved beyond simply exchanging Q&As to establish themselves as all-weather problem solvers capable of reading vast research papers in one breath, performing high-level programming coding, and deftly analyzing complex business data. 

However, as technology grows more powerful and permeates deeper into our lives, the potential risks lurking behind it are snowballing. The process of correctly aligning artificial intelligence's intentions and purposes so that it does not harm humans—namely **"AI Alignment"** and **"AI Safety"**—is no longer the exclusive domain of a few genius developers. It has become the most urgent and crucial topic determining the safety, survival, and future of all of us living in modern society.

[Harvard CS 2881R: AI Safety], led directly by Professor Boaz Barak of the Harvard Department of Computer Science, was born out of this timely demand and urgency [Source 15]. Entering its second iteration in the Fall 2026 semester [Source 15], this course goes beyond teaching simple coding and programming skills; it serves as a cutting-edge base camp researching fundamental principles and defensive measures to ensure AI systems securely anchor themselves within our society and human values.

The course is held every Thursday from 3:45 PM to 6:30 PM (EST) in Room 2112 at 114 Western Avenue in Allston, Massachusetts [Source 1]. Here, students do not remain confined to theory; they directly experiment with, dissect, and rigorously analyze the behavioral patterns of actual state-of-the-art AI models.

## Easy to Understand: How to Teach Morals to an "Obedient Genius Assistant"

The concepts of AI safety and alignment might initially feel somewhat unfamiliar and abstract. To put it very simply, this can be described as **"the process of deeply instilling perfect safety rules and correct moral standards into a genius assistant who is the most capable and smart worker in the world, yet knows nothing about how the world works."**

Imagine this. You get a secretary who handles tasks faster and more perfectly than anyone else on Earth. One day, you give your secretary a very simple instruction: "Clean the inside of the building thoroughly." But what if this secretary interprets the word "clean" so extremely and ignorantly that they mistake all furniture and precious documents inside the building for trash and burn them all down? The secretary might have been 100% faithful to the command to "clean," but the result would lead to one of the worst catastrophes in human history. 

Similarly, making AI accurately understand the "true intent, context, and values" hidden behind explicit human instructions without misunderstanding is the core research task known as alignment. Metaphorically speaking, this is like putting a tight safety rein on a secretary so that they read their master's gaze and subtle nuances to avoid causing reckless accidents.

Harvard's CS 2881R course goes beyond such theoretical learning, generously providing practical assignments where students directly touch code and manipulate models with their own hands. For instance, in "Assignment 1" performed by students, they directly practice fine-tuning (the technique of finely adjusting a pre-trained model that has already learned vast amounts of data to suit specific purposes or safety criteria) and evaluation processes [Source 10, Source 11]. Through this, students are guided to physically experience through their entire bodies what mechanisms AI learns through and what critical flaws and side effects it reveals in the process.

A particularly talked-about highlight within the class is the **"Model Identity and Prompt Robustness Experiment"** [Source 6]. According to interesting experiments conducted by researchers and students, precise measurements were taken of the reaction figures shown by AI models when they were given completely different AI names (e.g., showing a 93% reaction when set as Meta's Llama model) or when a user instructed them without any system prompt, saying, "Bear in mind that you are Qwen created by Alibaba Cloud" [Source 6]. The warning these experiments give us is clear: they vividly demonstrate how easily an AI's identity can be shaken and its responses distorted by even a minor external input or fake name tag, and how massive a threat this can pose to future cybersecurity and social safety.

## Current Status: Beyond Quiet Laboratories into a Heated Arena of Global Debate

Harvard's AI safety course is by no means a one-off event or trend that opens once and ends. Following its successful debut in the Fall 2025 semester, it proudly welcomed its second season in the Fall 2026 semester, establishing an even more solid and systematic curriculum [Source 15]. 

The spectrum of knowledge covered by this lecture is broader and deeper than one might imagine. Going beyond simply writing code and catching bugs, it thoroughly encompasses heavy, philosophical, and future-oriented topics—such as whether AI has rights and to what extent ethical status should be granted if intelligence becomes advanced, as seen in Lecture 10, "AI Welfare," recorded on November 5, 2026 [Source 9]. Inside the classroom, the professor and bright-eyed students exchange fierce Q&As [Source 9], brainstorming from various angles about the massive social impact artificial intelligence will soon bring.

Of course, this movement is not limited to Harvard alone. Across academia and industry worldwide, AI safety has firmly taken root as a top-priority task that cannot be delayed for even a single day. Leading AI research and safety companies like Anthropic are dedicated day and night to designing safe AI systems that humans can trust, inspect inside (interpretability), and control at any time [Source 14]. 

In addition, heavyweights like world-renowned deep learning scholar Professor Yoshua Bengio are raising their voices strongly day after day regarding why AI safety must be taken seriously and thorough preparedness measures established [Source 12]. Furthermore, solidarity and movements centered around global institutions like BlueDot Impact to discover and nurture the next generation of AI safety experts are unfolding very actively worldwide [Source 13].

## What Lies Ahead?

As artificial intelligence technology penetrates deeper into every corner of our daily lives, the gap between "technology thoroughly and safely designed" and "technology running blindly without any devices" will widen as much as the difference between heaven and earth. Pioneer educational courses and research programs like Harvard University's CS 2881R are highly likely to spread fiercely to numerous universities, research institutes, and companies worldwide in the future.

The key future viewing points that we must keenly watch can be largely condensed into three:
1. **Establishment of Standardized Global Safety Guidelines**: Will strict and authoritative safety verification standards that developers must pass before AI models are officially launched into the world firmly take root legally and institutionally?
2. **Enhancement of Models' Intrinsic Robustness**: Will AI with strong identities that do not lose their minds or waver even under users' clever inductions or malicious prompt injections become universal?
3. **Improvement of Public-Wide AI Literacy**: Will not only developers but also ordinary members of the public acquire the literacy to accurately understand the clear limitations and potential risks of AI technology and utilize it wisely?

Artificial intelligence will gift us unimaginable convenience and leaps forward, but ultimately, holding the firm reins so that this dazzling gift does not turn into a massive disaster swallowing humanity is up to humans. We have no doubt that the fierce research and discussions originating from Harvard's small lecture hall will soon become the most reliable shield safely protecting the world we live in.
