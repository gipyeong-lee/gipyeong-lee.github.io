<h2>
2. Vision &amp; Objectives
</h2>
<ol>
<li>
<strong>
Invest in the Foundation
</strong>
— Structure, insulation, and waterproofing are difficult to fix later, so they are the top priority.
</li>
<li>
<strong>
Reduce Maintenance Costs
</strong>
— Use high-efficiency windows/equipment, and maximize natural lighting and ventilation.
</li>
<li>
<strong>
DIY Completion
</strong>
— Design the project with segmented tasks so that work can proceed on weekends and evenings.
</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p>
<span>
<p>
<strong>
Must‑Know
</strong>
If goals are vague, both the budget and schedule will falter. Establish decision-making criteria by distinguishing between <em>Need</em> and <em>Want</em>.
</p>
</span>
</p>
</blockquote>
<hr>
<h2>
3. Existing‑Conditions Assessment
</h2>
<h3>
3.1 Roof (Tiles &amp; Rooftop Waterproofing)
</h3>
<ul>
<li>
Check the condition of the waterproofing sheet under the tiles → Mark damaged areas.
</li>
<li>
Calculate the area requiring rooftop urethane/polyurea coating.
</li>
</ul>
<h3>
3.2 Exterior Walls
</h3>
<ul>
<li>
Detect condensation and leak points using a thermal imaging camera (rental).
</li>
<li>
Record insulation thickness / existing finish layer thickness.
</li>
</ul>
<h3>
3.3 Interior
</h3>
<ul>
<li>
Electrical wiring — Check for exposed wires and cracked insulation.
</li>
<li>
Plumbing — Record main pipe diameter and signs of backflow.
</li>
</ul>
<p>
Save photos, measurements, and notes in the <strong>Google Drive › Renovation › Survey</strong> folder.
</p>
<hr>
<h2>
4. Legal &amp; Structural Constraints
</h2>
<ol>
<li>
<strong>
Zoning/Floor Area Ratio
</strong>
: View documents at the district office’s architecture department.
</li>
<li>
<strong>
Load-bearing Wall Identification
</strong>
: Mark the location of wood structure trusses and beams.
</li>
<li>
<strong>
Rooftop Waterproofing Permitting
</strong>
: Keep the waterproof layer performance certification (for defect insurance).
</li>
</ol>

</h2>
