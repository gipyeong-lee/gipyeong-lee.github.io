Imagine trillions of tiny "machines" inside your body, moving without rest. They convert the food you ate for lunch into energy, carry oxygen from your lungs to every corner of your body, and throw themselves into battle when dangerous viruses invade. These marvelous nano-machines are called "proteins."

There is a fascinating secret to these proteins: their "shape" is their "ability." Just as a piece of paper can become a crane or an airplane depending on how you fold it in origami, a protein's function is determined by its complex, folded three-dimensional structure. Much like the jagged shape of a key must perfectly match a lock to open a door, the proteins in our bodies must have the exact right form to perform their roles.

However, cracking this "folding code" was a massive wall for humanity. For decades, scientists had to struggle with microscopes, spending years and millions of dollars just to figure out the shape of a single protein. Then, five years ago, an artificial intelligence emerged and solved this 50-year-old grand challenge in an instant. That protagonist is **AlphaFold**, the scientific AI developed by Google DeepMind that eventually went on to win the Nobel Prize. [AlphaFold: Five Years of Impact — Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphafold-five-years-of-impact/)

## Why is this important?

To put it simply, biology until now was in a state where we "could read the blueprint but didn't know what the finished machine looked like." While we had decoded the blueprint of our body's genetic information (DNA), predicting the actual 3D shape of the proteins created from that blueprint was mathematically nearly impossible.

If we want to create a new drug to attack cancer cells, we must know the exact shape of the cancer cell's protein to carve a "chemical key (drug)" that fits perfectly into its slot. Without knowing this shape, we have to rely on tens of thousands of trials and errors; but with the shape, precision strikes become possible. [AlphaFold five years on: What this new era means... | FEBS Network](https://network.febs.org/posts/alphafold-five-years-on-what-this-new-era-means-for-molecular-life-sciences)

With the arrival of AlphaFold2, everything changed. This system began predicting protein structures with such startling accuracy that it could estimate the position of individual atoms. [AlphaFold Five Years On: Real-World Impact, Limits, and What...](https://kiadev.net/news/2025-11-24-alphafold-five-years-impact/)

Francis deSouza, former CEO of Illumina, praised AlphaFold2 as "a monumental scientific discovery that demonstrates the powerful potential of AI to solve critical problems." [AlphaFold: Five Years of Impact | Francis deSouza](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/francisdesouza_alphafold-five-years-of-impact-activity-7399126185886150656--4Ok) Beyond mere technical progress, this means humanity has acquired a new weapon that can accelerate the speed of identifying disease causes and developing new drugs by tens or even hundreds of times. ## A Simple Analogy: From "Manual Mapping" to the "Satellite Navigation" Era

Let's use an analogy to make it easier. Protein research before AlphaFold was like a "manual task" where an explorer had to personally walk every alleyway of a massive metropolis to measure and record lengths just to draw a map. It could take a lifetime just to complete the map of a single city.

The advent of AlphaFold is like launching a high-performance satellite into the sky. Instead of sweating on the ground, you can snap 3D maps of every city in the world in an instant with the push of a button. Indeed, over the past five years, AlphaFold has become an essential tool used daily by more than 3 million researchers worldwide. Five years since AlphaFold was released, AI is no longer an option but a necessity in life science laboratories. ## What Lies Ahead?

AlphaFold's journey does not stop here. Scientists are now moving beyond knowing the shape of individual proteins to the more complex problem of "how these proteins talk to and bind with each other." Currently, AlphaFold is expanding its reach by supporting the prediction of "multimers," which are complexes where multiple proteins move together. [AlphaFold Protein Structure Database](https://alphafold.com/)

Expectations are particularly high in the field of drug discovery. While a new drug won't appear overnight, the very way biochemists design drugs has fundamentally changed. Therefore, it is highly likely that we will soon hear news of innovative treatments for diseases that have long plagued humanity, such as cancer, dementia, and genetic disorders. Even more remarkable is the fact that Google DeepMind released this powerful technology as "Open Source" for anyone to use for free.
