Imagine this: You’re reading a long report sent by a colleague this morning, and a thought suddenly occurs to you: "Did my colleague really write this, or did an AI?" Until now, we had to rely on mere intuition or writing style to guess, but things might look a little different from here on out. Anthropic, an AI company, has decided to attach a very special "tag" to its artificial intelligence model, 'Claude.'

## Why is this important?

In recent years, generative AI has become deeply embedded in every aspect of our lives, from work to our daily routines. However, as it has become increasingly difficult to distinguish AI-generated output from human-made content, concerns about distrust in information sources and the need for transparency have deepened. The European Union's AI Act has established strict transparency standards for AI-generated content to address these concerns. [Source: EU compliance, delivered globally: Anthropic to watermark... | Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/08/11/eu-compliance-delivered-tm-claudes-output-worldwide)

Anthropic's latest move goes beyond just meeting European regulations; it applies to all Claude users worldwide. [Source: Anthropic plans to add an invisible mark to AI text—as the industry scrambles to police AI slop | Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-claude-watermark-ai-text-police-ai-slop/) This means that wherever you are, text or files created by Claude will now contain an invisible "digital fingerprint." This will serve as a crucial standard for objectively identifying whether the information we are viewing has passed through the hands of an AI.

## Understanding Simply: The Principle of the 'Invisible Tag'

So, how does this "invisible watermark" work?

To use a simple analogy, it's similar to printing a very faint logo on the background of a document. However, Claude's watermark is much more intelligent. When an AI generates text, it faces countless crossroads in word choice; it embeds a microscopic pattern (a probability combination of word selection methods) that only a machine can read into the text while maintaining the same meaning. [Source: How Claude’s text watermarking works | Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)

This pattern is completely invisible to the human eye and follows the text even if you copy and paste it elsewhere. Even if a person modifies the sentences slightly, this trace often remains significantly intact. [Source: The End of Undetectable AI Text? Claude’s New Watermark Explained | Dev.to](https://dev.to/sylwia-lask/the-end-of-undetectable-ai-text-claudes-new-watermark-explained-45g2) However, if the text is very short, there may not be enough information to analyze for identification, and conversely, if the content is heavily deleted or intentionally rewritten, this marker can be erased. [Source: How Claude’s text watermarking works | Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark), [Source: Claude Invisible Watermark Enhances AI Text Transparency | The Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/08/11/claude-invisible-watermark/)

## Current Status: Where is it applied?

This feature is being fully applied to Claude models released after August 2, 2026. [Source: Anthropic says it will watermark text generated by its AI models | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-says-it-will-watermark-text-generated-by-its-ai-models/) This includes not only the Claude website (claude.ai) that we mainly use, but also all channels where Claude is utilized, such as the platform API for enterprise services, the developer tool Claude Code, and Claude Cowork. [Source: Claude is watermarking AI text: Everything you need to... - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EWGDJqlYlA8)

What is particularly interesting is that measures have also been taken for image files, not just text. Image files now include metadata compliant with C2PA (a global standard for certifying the provenance and change history of digital content), allowing for systematic identification of who created the file and whether it has been modified. [Source: Claude Invisible Watermarks— What They Detect... | explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/anthropic-claude-invisible-watermarks-c2pa-august-2026)

## Things to Note: The Watermark Doesn't Solve Everything

One important thing to remember is that this watermark only signals "AI generated this," and it is not evidence that the content is "the truth" or "correct." [Source: Claude’s Watermark Can Signal Contact. It Cannot Prove Guilt. | Kingy.ai](https://kingy.ai/blog/claude-watermark-ai-generated-content/)

The presence of a watermark cannot be used as clear evidence of plagiarism or malicious intent to deceive. [Source: Claude Now Watermarks Text Everywhere; Mark Proves Processing, Not Authorship | Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323873/20260811/claude-now-watermarks-text-everywhere-mark-proves-processing-not-authorship.htm) It simply sends a digital signal saying, "This sentence is a result generated or processed by Claude." This is why we must still critically review and personally verify information provided by AI.

## What's Next?

Moving forward, AI companies will competitively release more advanced identification technologies. People will gradually develop the ability to distinguish between AI-written and human-written text, and social consensus on how to ethically utilize content produced with the help of AI will be formed. What is certain is that as technology advances, efforts to use that technology responsibly and secure transparency will also come closer to us.
