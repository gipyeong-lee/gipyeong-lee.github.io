### Simple Explanation: What is an O'Neill Cylinder?

An 'O'Neill cylinder' is a blueprint for a space city proposed by physicist Gerard O'Neill in the 1970s. Simply put, imagine **laying a giant soda can on its side and building houses on the inner wall to live in.** [Source 6](https://island-three.gruberbuilds.workers.dev/)

This habitat doesn't just sit still; it rotates very rapidly. Like the principle behind clothes sticking to the wall of a spinning washing machine, this rotational force is used to create 'artificial gravity' on the inner wall.

However, there is a physical challenge. If you just spin one cylinder, it acts like a giant gyroscope (an instrument that maintains rotational force), making it very difficult to change direction. Therefore, an O'Neill cylinder **rotates two cylinders in opposite directions** to cancel out this torque. [Source 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Neill_cylinder) This allows it to maintain a stable orientation toward the sun while providing a stable living space for its inhabitants.

### Current Status: A Little Universe in the Web

The name of this newly released simulation project is 'Island Three'. [Source 6](https://island-three.gruberbuilds.workers.dev/)

*   **Scale**: It boasts a massive size of 6.4 km in diameter and 32 km in length. [Source 6](https://island-three.gruberbuilds.workers.dev/)
*   **Rotation Speed**: It is set to rotate once every 113.5 seconds. [Source 6](https://island-three.gruberbuilds.workers.dev/)
*   **Exploration**: Anyone can connect in real-time via a web browser to explore the habitat as if they were walking on the street. [Source 5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XGdK_t2ws2s)

If you visit the browser link (island-three.gruberbuilds.workers.dev) right now, you can experience what humanity might look like living in a physics-based space city built by AI in the future. [Source 4](https://www.promptzone.com/nadia_pham/claude-builds-walkable-oneill-cylinder-3cd7)
