### Why It Matters

The news of the 'prolonged US/Israel-Iran war' that we hear on the evening news every day is not just some other country's story across the ocean [LCCs Hit Hard by High Oil Prices and Exchange Rates..."Wouldn't Be Surprising If They Closed Down Next Month"](https://www.hani.co.kr/arti/economy/consumer/1262738.html). This complex geopolitical conflict has completely frozen the Middle East, the core supply chain of the world's oil, and as a result, global oil prices have begun to fluctuate wildly.

Why is this directly connected to our wallets? It's because airlines are fundamentally 'companies that fly through the sky carrying massive fuel tanks.' Aviation fuel (airplane-specific fuel) accounts for the largest portion of the total cost required for a plane to travel to its destination. However, as oil prices skyrocket, an anomalous situation is unfolding where the more planes they fly, the more their losses snowball.

In particular, LCCs (Low Cost Carriers), which have relatively weaker financial power compared to major airlines, are literally being pushed to the edge of a cliff. The reason we could lightly take off to Jeju Island or Japan on the weekends was thanks to these low-cost carriers, but painful laments are leaking out from within the industry that "it wouldn't be surprising if they closed down next month" [LCCs Hit Hard by High Oil Prices and Exchange Rates..."Wouldn't Be Surprising If They Closed Down Next Month"](https://www.hani.co.kr/arti/economy/consumer/1262738.html). What would happen if airlines collapse due to financial difficulties or fail to handle these uncontrollably swelling costs? Ultimately, popular routes will decrease or ticket prices will inevitably rise steeply, and this burden will be passed directly onto ordinary people like us trying to go on a sweet vacation.

### The Explainer: Two Crashing Waves

There are two main culprits currently suffocating airlines. They are the two massive waves of **'high oil prices'** and **'high exchange rates.'** Let's use an analogy from our daily lives to make it a bit easier to grasp just how terrifying it is when these two bad factors strike simultaneously.

**1. High Oil Prices: Taxi Fuel Costs Doubling Overnight**
Imagine you are a large taxi driver carrying passengers across the country every day. When meticulously calculating your monthly maintenance costs at the beginning of the year, you budgeted for the year based on gas prices being 2,000 KRW per liter. But one morning, you go to the gas station and find that the price of gas has suddenly more than doubled to 4,500 KRW. You wouldn't be able to help but sigh every time you grabbed the steering wheel.

This is exactly the situation Korean Air is experiencing right now. When Korean Air initially drafted its annual business plan, it ordinarily estimated oil prices at about $2.20 per gallon. However, with the outbreak of the Middle East crisis, they projected that the refueling unit price as of April 2026 would reach around $4.50 per gallon [Even Korean Air... Enters Emergency Management Due to Middle East Aviation Fuel Surge](https://www.donga.com/news/Economy/article/all/20260331/133644727/1). Fuel prices have become more than twice as expensive as their meticulously laid out plans.

The scale of this shock far exceeds the imagination of ordinary people. For Korean Air, which flies hundreds of massive passenger planes around the world, an increase in international oil prices of just $1 results in a staggering astronomical loss of 46.5 billion KRW over the course of a year [Korean Air Declares Emergency Management... Annual Loss of 46.5 Billion KRW for Every $1 Rise in Oil Prices (Comprehensive) - News1](https://www.news1.kr/industry/general-industry/6119983). 46.5 billion KRW is an enormous amount, equivalent to the airfare paid by millions of passengers evaporating straight into thin air.

**2. High Exchange Rates: The Hidden Tax Thinning Out Wallets**
On top of this, the second wave of 'high exchange rates' strikes consecutively. Aviation fuel is not something that can be paid for by swiping a card with Korean Won (KRW) at a local gas station. It must all be purchased from the global crude oil market using US 'Dollars'.

**Simply put**, it's a terrible situation similar to buying items from an overseas online shopping mall where the item price (oil price) itself has become incredibly expensive, and the dollar exchange rate needed to pay for it has also jumped from 1,000 KRW to 1,400 KRW. The rising fuel prices alone are backbreaking, but they are essentially suffering 'double taxation' because the fee (exchange rate) they have to pay when converting Korean money to dollars to pay for that expensive fuel has also skyrocketed [Korean Air Hits Emergency Management After Direct Hit from High Oil Prices... Aviation Industry in 'All-out Austerity' - Herald Economy](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10706412). When both terrifyingly high oil prices and high exchange rates persist simultaneously like this, it is an unavoidable mathematical conclusion that the overall profitability of the aviation industry will sharply deteriorate [Even Korean Air Raised Its Hands... Emergency Management Spreads in the Aviation Industry Due to the Middle East War | JoongAng Ilbo](https://www.joongang.co.kr/article/25416344).
