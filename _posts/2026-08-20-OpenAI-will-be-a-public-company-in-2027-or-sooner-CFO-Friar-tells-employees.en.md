One morning, you turn on your smartphone and say to your AI assistant, "Organize my tasks for today and prioritize them." In an instant, your schedule is perfectly arranged, and your assistant even reminds you of a meeting you almost missed. We live in the convenience of such services, but we often don't know much about how the company behind them operates or what its future holds. Recently, however, OpenAI, a giant in the AI industry, announced a concrete plan to transform into a "public company" where everyday people can become shareholders.

On August 19, OpenAI's Chief Financial Officer (CFO), Sarah Friar, announced during an all-hands meeting that the company will go public by 2027 [[Source: CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/open-ai-ipo-timing-2027-friar.html)]. This means that OpenAI, a name we hear frequently in the news, is finally preparing for its stock market debut.

### Why is this important?

Until now, OpenAI has grown as a private company through investments and funding from major IT firms. Going public means it will become a company whose shares anyone can buy. This is significant not only because it opens an opportunity for ordinary investors to invest directly in the front lines of the AI industry, but also because it symbolizes that AI technology is no longer an experimental lab-level technology, but is recognized as a pivotal business model in our economy.

Of course, a company preparing for an IPO must prove its performance and transparency. For users like us, watching OpenAI transform from a company that simply makes "cool AI" into a "robust enterprise" that generates sustainable profits will be an interesting point to observe.

### Simplifying it: Why now?

Why 2027? Let’s use an analogy: it is similar to the process of raising a young genius child. In the early stages, educating and raising the child (AI technology) costs a tremendous amount of money, but generates little profit. OpenAI is now at the stage where the child has reached adolescence and is beginning to be fully active.

It is said that some within the management team initially suggested rushing the IPO for late 2026 [[Source: Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/openai-could-go-public-before-2027-if-business-continues-to-inflect-cfo-friar-tells-staff/)]. However, CFO Sarah Friar preferred to take more time to fully prove the company's value before hitting the market in 2027 [[Source: Logicity](https://logicity.in/en/blog/openai-ipo-slips-to-2027-as-altman-cites-strategic-uncertainty)]. Simply put, the strategy is that appearing at a time when they can show the most impressive results will build greater trust with shareholders than forcing an early entry.

Of course, if the company's business growth accelerates faster than expected, passing an "inflection point," the possibility of going public earlier than 2027 remains open [[Source: Briefs.co](https://www.briefs.co/news/openai-eyes-2027-stock-market-debut-with-faster-timeline-pos/)].

### Where are they now?

Currently, OpenAI has a structure where massive costs are hidden behind flashy technology. Its projected operating loss for 2025 alone is $8 billion (over 10 trillion KRW), and its spending plan for the coming years is highly aggressive. It is expected to pour astronomical amounts into R&D and operations: $17 billion in 2026, $35 billion in 2027, and a staggering $45 billion in 2028 [[Source: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI)].

To handle this scale of costs, a stable source of funding is necessary. An IPO is a essential step for OpenAI to secure more capital and gain a technological edge over competitors (e.g., Anthropic, etc.) [[Source: Techbuzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/openai-targets-2027-ipo-as-cfo-reassures-staff-on-anthropic-race)]. OpenAI has already begun the paperwork for the IPO by submitting an S-1 registration statement to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) [[Source: Logicity](https://logicity.in/en/blog/openai-ipo-slips-to-2027-as-altman-cites-strategic-uncertainty)].

### What happens next?

There is less than two years left until 2027. What we should focus on going forward is how efficiently OpenAI converts these massive R&D costs into business revenue. Many people view OpenAI's IPO as the true opening of the AI era.

We are now facing an era where we must answer the question, "How does AI move our economy?" beyond just "How smart is AI?" After the IPO, it would be worthwhile to look forward to what new services OpenAI will introduce to change our daily lives with the influx of more capital. One thing is certain: AI will continue to dig deeper and more powerfully into our lives.

### AI's Opinion
OpenAI's IPO will be more than just one company's debut on the stock market; it will be a symbolic event signaling that artificial intelligence has fully established itself as core infrastructure for our economy. It is a massive milestone showing that AI technology has moved beyond the realm of research to become an essential element determining the survival and growth of businesses.
