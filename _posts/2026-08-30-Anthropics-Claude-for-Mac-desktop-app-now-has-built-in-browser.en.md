Imagine this: this morning, you say to your AI, "Find the three latest market reports related to the project we worked on last month, summarize them, and paste them into the document I've been organizing."

In the past, you would have had to turn on a search engine yourself, open the links one by one, read the content, and manually copy the key information back into your document. What if an AI could handle this repetitive, wrist-straining chore for you? Artificial Intelligence (AI) has started turning on the "browser" right inside your computer, navigating and acting on websites just like a human.

Anthropic, the AI safety and research company, announced an update to its AI assistant Claude's desktop app, "Claude Cowork," which now includes a built-in web browser. [Ref 1](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-built-in-browser) Claude has now evolved beyond an AI that simply exchanges text in conversations; it can actually visit, read, and click on websites on its own.

## Why is this important?

Until now, AI has largely been limited to analyzing text we provide or summarizing information we have already found. However, the fact that a "browser" has been integrated into AI signifies a decisive shift: AI can now "experience the internet world directly."

For general users, this is a major blessing. It increases the possibility of being freed from simple, repetitive tasks on the internet. Whether collecting data from multiple websites or finding and clicking specific buttons, Claude can now handle those processes instead of you having to do it all manually with your mouse. This is a significant shift in work productivity. [Ref 3](https://thenewstack.io/claude-built-in-browser-cowork/)

## Easy Understanding: From a Smart Librarian to a Competent Assistant

To use an analogy, if the existing Claude was a "smart library librarian," it has now become a "competent assistant who can freely roam the entire library, retrieve the necessary books themselves, and even take notes for you."

Previously, for AI to see web information, users had to copy and paste the page content or install separate "connectors" like browser extensions. [Ref 3](https://thenewstack.io/claude-built-in-browser-cowork/) But now, the Claude Cowork app has a browser built directly into its side panel. [Ref 1](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-built-in-browser)

When the AI needs to check a website while working, it opens the browser on its own, loads the webpage, reads the necessary text, clicks links, and even types into input fields. [Ref 1](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-built-in-browser) Just as humans surf the web by looking at a computer screen, AI has finally acquired eyes (vision) and hands (operation).

## Current Status

Anthropic's update is currently being rolled out sequentially for the Claude Cowork app on macOS, Windows, and Linux desktop environments. [Ref 4](https://www.phoneworld.com.pk/anthropic-adds-a-built-in-browser-to-claude-for-web-tasks/) [Ref 7](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260827-claude-cowork-built-in-browser/)

However, this feature is not immediately available to all users. It is currently being distributed to subscribers of Anthropic's paid plans—'Claude Pro', 'Max', and 'Team'—and is expected to gradually expand to more users over the coming week. [Ref 7](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260827-claude-cowork-built-in-browser/) Please note that this feature does not apply if you are using Claude by accessing the site directly via a web browser rather than using the desktop app. [Ref 7](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260827-claude-cowork-built-in-browser/)

## What will happen in the future?

As Claude gains the ability to manipulate the web environment directly, AI will gradually move beyond the category of a simple "chatbot" and take on the appearance of a "Digital Agent" (an intelligent proxy that performs specific tasks on your behalf). It will act as a secretary that expertly handles the websites we use every day, taking care of complex work processes for us. [Ref 5](https://gizmodo.com/download/claude-ai)

In the future, users will weigh how accurately and safely an AI can navigate websites and complete tasks to get the desired results, rather than just whether the AI is good at writing text.
