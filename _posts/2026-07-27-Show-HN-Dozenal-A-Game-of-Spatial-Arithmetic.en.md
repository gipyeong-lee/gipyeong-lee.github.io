Imagine this: what if you were to temporarily set aside the framework of the number '10' that we have taken for granted all our lives? Every moment—turning off the alarm in the morning, paying bus fare, checking the calendar—we live in a 'decimal' system that groups things by tens. However, throughout human history, a system called 'Duodecimal' (base-twelve), which views the world based on 12 rather than the familiar 10, has long existed.

Recently, an interesting puzzle game based on the duodecimal system, [Dozenal](https://dozenal.game/), was released. This game goes beyond simply solving problems; it allows you to naturally experience the world of an unfamiliar base system through the process of maneuvering numbers on a board.

## Why is 12 important?

Math is like a pair of glasses that allows us to see the world more precisely and structurally. The decimal system we currently use can be seen as a custom derived from the ten fingers humans possess. On the other hand, the number 12 has very special mathematical properties. Because 12 is divisible by 2, 3, 4, and 6, it is often much more efficient than 10 when dividing items or forming groups.

Studies show that computer-based geometric puzzle games can contribute to improving students' spatial reasoning skills [Source 7]. Spatial reasoning refers to the ability to mentally visualize or manipulate the structure of complex shapes or objects [Source 4]. Games like [Dozenal](https://dozenal.game/) do not merely involve repetitive calculation; they provide the brain with entirely new mathematical stimulation by requiring the player to solve puzzles that demand such spatial reasoning.

## In simple terms, what kind of game is it?

Metaphorically, this game is like 'number block matching.' The core goal of the game is to take various number tiles scattered on the board and combine them so that the final total is '12' [Source 8, Source 14].

Unlike the decimal system we use, in duodecimal arithmetic, 10 is uniquely represented as 'X' and 11 as 'E' [Source 2]. The 'Dozenal Society of America' has long recommended practicing with these symbols to effectively educate the public on the principles of the duodecimal system [Source 2, Source 10]. The process of moving tiles on a grid board to complete the final target of 12 is an intellectual puzzle that requires complex spatial reasoning skills beyond simple addition [Source 4, Source 8].

## What is the current situation?

Currently, [Dozenal](https://dozenal.game/) is available for anyone to enjoy directly in a web browser. It is highly significant that the efforts of organizations like the 'Dozenal Society of America,' which have long studied and taught the charms of the duodecimal system, have expanded through this game into a realm of play accessible to everyone [Source 10]. The attempt to bridge the gap between the everyday decimal system and the duodecimal system through the gentle medium of gaming well demonstrates the direction in which modern educational math games should be heading [Source 17].

## How will it change in the future?

Along with technological advancement, games are fundamentally changing the way we acquire knowledge [Source 7]. In the future, we will see a wider variety of puzzle games that incorporate specific mathematical principles into logical structures rather than just rote memorization of formulas. The process of discovering and experiencing principles directly through games will provide a powerful learning opportunity that makes math a 'fun adventure' rather than a 'rigid subject to study.'
