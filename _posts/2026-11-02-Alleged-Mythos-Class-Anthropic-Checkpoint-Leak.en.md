Imagine you have hired a brilliant assistant, but this assistant is so capable that you kept them inside your house for four months for fear that they might be exploited by villains. The recent "Claude Mythos" incident in the AI industry is exactly like that.

This AI, developed by Anthropic, was evaluated as "the best model we have created to date" from its internal testing phase. However, this immense capability became a major topic of discussion when it was exposed to the world through a data breach in March 2026 [Source 1](https://srccybersolutions.com/blog/email-security/anthropics-mythos-leak-is-a-wake-up-call-phishing-3-0-is-already-here).

### Why is this important?

While advances in AI technology make our lives more convenient, they can also become a more powerful "weapon" for those with malicious intent. Mythos shows incredible ability in defending against cyberattacks, but at the same time, it could be just as lethal if used by an attacker. The fact that the security of the financial, medical, and telecommunications systems we use daily could be swayed by a single AI is no longer a story from a distant land.

### Easy to understand

To put it simply, think of existing AI as a filter for a photo editing app. It changes the color tone of a photo a little bit. On the other hand, Mythos is like an expert who perfectly understands and reconstructs the meaning of objects within a photo.

To use a clearer analogy: if existing AI is a friend who can solve elementary school-level math problems, Mythos is an expert solving complex equations in a lab filled only with mathematicians. Anthropic restricted this "math expert" to researching only in a very safe laboratory, fearing that it might teach others not how to solve problems, but how to "manipulate them" [Source 5](https://thenextweb.com/news/dario-amodei-anthropic-race-to-the-top-nine-safety-decisions).

### Current situation: How is it being used?

Instead of releasing this model directly to the general public, Anthropic established a safeguard called "Project Glasswing" [Source 6](https://www.theepochtimes.com/opinion/anthropics-most-dangerous-achievement-6014959). Through this, they allowed only about 40 selected partners, including Google, Apple, and Microsoft, to use Mythos [Source 6](https://www.theepochtimes.com/opinion/anthropics-most-dangerous-achievement-6014959).

The results were astonishing. Using Mythos, these companies identified over 10,000 critical vulnerabilities in software that is essential to systems worldwide [Source 3](https://www.pandr.uk/company-check-anthropic-releases-ai-once-deemed-too-dangerous). They have successfully turned what looked like a dangerous power into a "security shield" that protects society.

### What lies ahead?

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei emphasizes that "cybersecurity safeguards" are more important than the speed of technology [Source 5](https://thenextweb.com/news/dario-amodei-anthropic-race-to-the-top-nine-safety-decisions). The Mythos model has already finished performance verification, and although its operation is sometimes briefly paused for unexpected operational reasons [Source 2](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/claude-mythos-5-uk-aisi-malware-test), it continues to evolve under safe control. The future launch of AI models will go beyond a simple race to show off performance; it will be a competition over who can tame this immense power more safely.

## MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

When an AI's capabilities are extraordinary, concealing them is never an easy decision for a company. However, the result of identifying 10,000 vulnerabilities and protecting society shows exactly how much more valuable a "safe AI" is to our society than an "unconditionally smart AI."
