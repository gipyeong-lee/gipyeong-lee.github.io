Imagine this. On a peaceful afternoon, you receive a sophisticated message on your smartphone that appears to be from your usual bank or a government agency. The message is incredibly natural, as if it perfectly understands your typical behavioral patterns. While we celebrate the convenience of AI and welcome "smart assistants" into our daily lives, someone else is using that very same AI technology to automate the "factory" of crime.

Generative AI (Artificial Intelligence that creates new content based on user requests), once just a tool for drafting text, is now transforming into an operating system for transnational crime. [What is Generative AI? | IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/generative-ai)

### Why does this matter?

Until now, we have focused on the bright side—that AI would reduce our working hours and boost our creativity. However, criminal organizations have noticed the appeal of this efficiency faster than anyone else.

The core danger is that criminals are not just stopping at asking AI to "write phishing emails for us." They are **redesigning their entire organizational operating model** around AI. It is akin to a small workshop that used to make products by hand switching to a high-speed automated factory powered by AI. Consequently, cross-border fraud is expanding to an unprecedented scale. [GenAI CEO: How organized crime is scaling transnational fraud...](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/genai-ceo-how-organized-crime-scaling-transnational-fraud-almási-skmve)

### In simple terms: From 'Handcrafted Workshop' to 'AI Automated Factory'

You can think of the operating principle of generative AI as a "digital writer" or "digital painter" that has learned vast amounts of data and can create new patterns. For example, image generation AI starts with random noise and gradually adds fine details to build the structure we desire. [AI Image Generator — Create amazing images... | Nana Banana Pro](https://nanabanana.pro/ai-image)

Whereas past criminal organizations hired hundreds of people to send messages and make calls manually to commit fraud, now an AI "digital worker" takes over that role. Criminal organizations use AI to perform the following:
1. **Automated Personalization**: Real-time analysis of victim information to automatically generate customized fraud scenarios.
2. **Removing Language Barriers**: Instantly sending large volumes of fraud messages in perfect grammar in the language of any country in the world.
3. **Improving Operating Systems**: AI optimizes existing inefficient criminal pathways in real-time, reducing the chances of detection.

It is like hiring tens of thousands of highly skilled assistants for pennies. This efficiency has caused crime to evolve from small-scale individual fraud to giant corporate-style networks. [GenAI CEO: How organized crime is scaling transnational fraud...](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/genai-ceo-how-organized-crime-scaling-transnational-fraud-almási-skmve)

### Current Situation: The Digital Information Network has become a Battlefield

Generative AI is casting long shadows not only in crime but also in political and social spheres. According to recent research conducted in the Mali region of Africa, states, non-state actors, and even external forces are mobilizing AI to systematically disseminate propaganda and manipulate information. [Generative AI alters the battlefield in Mali - Africa Defense Forum](https://adf-magazine.com/2026/10/generative-ai-alters-the-battlefield-in-mali/)

Fake news or propaganda created by AI is so realistic that it is difficult for the average person to distinguish whether it was written by a human or created by an AI. In an era where it is hard to discern what is true amidst a flood of information, this is emerging as a serious problem that undermines trust in our society.

### Are there countermeasures?

Technical responses to these threats are also developing rapidly. A representative method is the **Digital Watermark**. Technologies like Google's SynthID insert markers into images, text, or audio that are invisible to the human eye. This allows for the verification of whether the content was generated or altered by AI later on. It acts as a kind of "certificate of authenticity" that increases transparency in the digital world and tells us what we should believe. [SynthID — Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/synthid/)

### What will happen in the future?

The future world will be a complex form where "AI-generated content" and "human creations" coexist. Criminal organizations will develop more sophisticated tricks, and technology will put up another defense to catch those tricks.

We must maintain a critical perspective on all information we encounter online. Furthermore, until authentication technologies like digital watermarking become a standard social norm, it is essential to have a habit of double-checking suspicious messages or news that is overly provocative. Remember, as technology becomes smarter, our skepticism must also become a bit smarter.

Generative AI is clearly a blessing that will dramatically increase human productivity. However, while we study the bright side of this technology, criminals are optimizing its dark efficiency to the extreme. More important than the development of technology is raising our "digital literacy" (the ability to understand and critically accept digital content) as we encounter it.
