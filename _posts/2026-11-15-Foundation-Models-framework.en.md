Imagine waking up in the morning, unlocking your smartphone, and having your usual workout tracking app or diary app naturally understand your tone and preferences, then smoothly suggesting a routine for the day. An app offering advice like, "You pushed hard on squats yesterday, so how about focusing on light stretching today?" is no longer just a story in a movie. This is because Apple has thrown open the doors to powerful artificial intelligence for developers.

Let's chat comfortably over a cup of coffee about how the apps we use every day are suddenly becoming smart, focusing on the core technology behind it: the **Foundation Models framework (a programming tool that helps easily handle large language models on Apple devices)**.

---

## Why Does This Matter?

Until now, implementing AI features on a smartphone required developers to build complex external servers or stuff massive amounts of AI code directly into their apps. This process raised concerns about privacy leaks, and whenever internet connections were unstable, the AI would fail completely. To use a metaphor, it was like trying to build an entire massive power plant just to run a tiny intelligence inside a smartphone.

However, Apple's **Foundation Models framework** chose a completely different path. It combined the iPhone's own brain (on-device) and Apple's secure cloud (Private Cloud Compute) into a single, seamless programming language called **Swift (Apple's dedicated programming language for creating apps on Apple devices)** [[Source 1], [Source 8]].

This means countless app developers worldwide can now easily and safely build their own unique AI apps without having to construct massive, complex AI infrastructure themselves—driving as if on a highway well-paved by Apple. The foundation has been established for custom apps that deeply understand users' daily lives to emerge explosively while strictly preserving personal privacy.

---

## Understanding It Easily: What Is a Foundation Model?

Experts often compare foundation models to architecture. Just like how you need a basic building constructed with sturdy steel frames and concrete before decorating the interior for a hospital, school, or shopping center, you need a solid base. Simply put, think of it as a "massive universal intelligence skeleton" that all smart apps share and use.

Technically, a **Foundation Model** is an artificial intelligence model trained on massive, massive datasets that serves as a giant baseline building capable of performing a wide range of general tasks such as writing, summarizing, translation, and conversation [[Source 12], [Source 13]]. In other words, this model is the core "building block" for constructing all specialized AI applications [[Source 12]].

### Shall We Compare It to a Chef?
Imagine a master chef with 30 years of culinary experience stepping into your kitchen. This chef (the foundation model) has mastered all the basics, from Korean, Chinese, and Western cuisine to baking and pastry. If you ask, "Make me a healthy meal for tonight using leftovers," the master chef immediately whips up a wonderful recipe.

Apple's framework is like a "premium kitchen tool set" connecting this master chef with developers. Developers don't need to study complex recipes from scratch; using this toolset, they can effortlessly open their own specialized restaurants (apps) such as cooking apps, fitness apps, or diary apps.

In particular, the on-device language model forming the core of Apple Intelligence (Apple's integrated AI system) has **approximately 3 billion adjustable numeric values (parameters)** [[Source 2]]. These minute adjustment screws—amounting to roughly 60 times the entire population of South Korea—mesh together inside the smartphone, striking an optimal balance that minimizes power consumption while perfectly handling daily conversation and summarization.

---

## Current Status: How Far Has It Come?

Since Apple first unveiled this framework to the world at WWDC 2025 (Apple's Annual Worldwide Developers Conference), it has rapidly permeated the developer ecosystem [[Source 2], [Source 5]]. Countless famous apps have already adopted this technology to transform the user's smartphone experience [[Source 7]].

Representative innovative services such as the fitness tracking app **SmartGym**, the mental care app **Stoic**, and the video editing app **VLLO** have leveraged Apple's Foundation Models framework to introduce new intelligent features [[Source 7]].

Powerful features for developers are already built into this framework by default:
- **Guided Generation**: Guides the AI to output responses accurately in a specific format desired by the developer (e.g., JSON data) without straying or giving odd answers [[Source 1]].
- **Tool Calling**: Allows the AI to intelligently invoke external tools or functions, such as a calculator or weather app, on its own to resolve user requests [[Source 1], [Source 6]].
- **Image Understanding**: Can comprehend and explain the meaning of photos uploaded by the user, not just text [[Source 1]].
- **Dynamic Profiles**: Flexibly adjusts the AI's personality or response style to match the user's usage patterns and context [[Source 1]].

Additionally, developer communities like GitHub are sharing additional utility packages such as custom skills and chat completion clients, further enhancing development convenience [[Source 5]].

---

## What Lies Ahead?

The App Store we will encounter in the near future will be completely different from the past. Users will no longer need to complexly tap through menus to find features.

Going forward, apps themselves will become **Dynamic Agents (AI assistants that judge and act on their own)**, operating as if conversing with the user. For instance, entering a video editing app and saying, "Pick out only the ocean scenes from last week's travel video and adjust the brightness," will cause the in-app AI agent to use the framework's tool-calling feature to finish the editing task in an instant.

Combined with Apple's powerful software architecture, the speed and privacy protection of on-device AI are evolving our smartphones from simple information-consumption devices into "true assistants that know us best."
