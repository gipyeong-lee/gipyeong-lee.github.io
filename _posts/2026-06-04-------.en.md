## Why It Matters

Words like macroeconomic indicators or international oil price trends pointed out with serious expressions by experts on economic news programs can easily sound like complicated stories that have nothing to do with me. However, simply put, these cold and hard indicators go round and round the giant cogwheel of the global economy and ultimately trigger a terrifying butterfly effect that translates 100% directly into the 'weekend grocery prices at our neighborhood supermarket' and the 'living expenses draining from my wallet'.

As the hair-trigger geopolitical crisis currently unfolding mainly in the Middle East prolongs, the price of international crude oil (WTI, West Texas Intermediate), the core blood of the global economy, shows no signs of stopping its high-flying march, surpassing $96 per barrel [Exchange Rate Surges Due to Rumors of Energy Crisis and Concerns over Additional US Tariffs... Excessive Concentration](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026060481851). Sadly, our country does not produce a single drop of oil, so we have to rely 100% on overseas imports for our energy. A situation where oil prices rise and the dollar exchange rate we have to pay to buy that oil simultaneously surges to the 1,540 won level is truly the 'worst combo'.

It is exactly like a situation where you went on a trip overseas and tried to buy a meal with dollars you exchanged at a high rate, only to find that the prices on the local restaurant's menu had also doubled in just a few days. We have to obtain much more expensive dollar bills to buy oil that has jumped from its original price, so the entire country and its companies are taking an enormous cost bill head-on.

Economic experts analyze that even if these waves of rising international oil prices and surging exchange rates do not surface tomorrow morning, they will inevitably weigh heavily on the price tags of the numerous products and services we consume after a certain time lag. Because of this, the pressure of inflation (a phenomenon where prices rise continuously and broadly), which seemed to have quieted down for a while, is fiercely boiling up again across our economy [OECD: If Gulf Energy Crisis Prolongs, Global Growth Rate Plummets to 1% Range](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202606046393i). This is the fundamental reason for the bitter phenomenon where the salary deposited in my bank account has remained stagnant for years, while everything from restaurant meals and commuting costs to upcoming winter heating bills is creeping up.

## The Explainer

Then, shall we dig a little easier into the 'US 25% additional semiconductor tariff', which is another main culprit that brought about the exchange rate surge and has turned the global technology market upside down? Recently, US President Trump signed an executive proclamation to impose a whopping 25% punitive tariff on all semiconductors and derivative products produced in overseas factories, including China, and imported into the US [US Semiconductor Tariffs Aiming at 'AI Accelerators' Exported from China... Industry Says "Low Feasibility"](https://www.asiae.co.kr/article/2026011515432901143). A tariff is a kind of toll tax levied on goods crossing a border. If this huge tax is attached, naturally, the final consumer price sold in US supermarkets has no choice but to jump significantly.

Comparing this complex situation to daily life goes like this. There is a restaurant owner (the US government) with the biggest and most money in a neighborhood food alley. This owner recently comes up with a clever move to keep other neighboring restaurants from growing. They made an unreasonable threat to the supplier owner (semiconductor exporting countries like Korea, Taiwan, etc.) who exclusively supplies fresh core ingredients to the entire neighborhood.

The owner shouts like this. "From now on, every time you supply the highest-grade cabbage (advanced semiconductor chips), the core ingredient of all dishes, to our restaurant's kitchen, pay an additional 25% penalty on top of the original price!"

Just looking at the news headlines, it seems as if the cabbage suppliers are about to go bankrupt right away and a huge disaster has occurred. However, an interesting twist is that high-ranking officials in the Korean semiconductor industry, the very parties who grow and supply those highest-grade cabbages from their fields, are showing a surprisingly very calm reaction.

Our country's industry experts evaluate this high-tariff measure as being "close to a bluff" intended to seize the upper hand at the future trade negotiation table. They even diagnose that this regulation has "low feasibility" of being executed 100% strictly in the real world [US Semiconductor Tariffs Aiming at 'AI Accelerators' Exported from China... Industry Says "Low Feasibility"](https://www.asiae.co.kr/article/2026011515432901143).

Why does such a relaxed analysis emerge? Let's assume the restaurant owner's unreasonable 25% cabbage tax is pushed through. Ultimately, who is the victim who is put in the most difficult position?

The answer, frustratingly, is 'the restaurant owner themselves'. Because the people who have to buy the more expensive cabbage, paying the tax, and make kimchi to sell every day right away are none other than the chefs in the owner's own restaurant kitchen. Translated back into the real world, even US 'Big Tech' companies like Apple, Nvidia, and Microsoft, which rank first in global market capitalization, cannot run their servers right now without semiconductors made by Korea or Taiwan. Since they have to pay the enormous taxes in full and buy the chips at a high price to build data centers or smartphones, it ultimately amounts to an act of self-harm that swells the cost burden of US companies themselves by tens of trillions of won before dampening the spirits of foreign companies.

Because it is a measure that puts sandbags on their core domestic companies and eats away at their profitability, experts' cool-headed analysis is that it is highly likely to fizzle out since it ultimately does not align with the interests of anyone, US consumers or companies [US Semiconductor Tariffs Aiming at 'AI Accelerators' Exported from China... Industry Says "Low Feasibility"](https://www.asiae.co.kr/article/2026011515432901143).

## Where We Stand

However, separate from the logical sense of relief felt by the working-level professionals in the semiconductor field, the anxiety of the financial market, which reacts extremely sensitively to the bad news pouring out every day, is clearly stamped in red letters on monitor screens. Some foreign exchange market experts warn that if the global situation worsens, the won against the dollar could rise to a maximum of 1,550 won in the short term, which is the line of psychological collapse, and they cannot let go of their tension [Exchange Rate Surges Due to Rumors of Energy Crisis and Concerns over Additional US Tariffs... Excessive Concentration](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026060481851).

Moreover, the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), an international organization that diagnoses the global economy as a whole, has issued a chilling warning about the currently unstable energy situation. According to an OECD simulation, if armed conflict in the Middle East spirals out of control and the 'Strait of Hormuz', the main artery of global oil transport, gets blocked—the worst-case scenario—the global economic growth rate could plummet to a dismal 1% level, which falls far below previous forecasts [OECD Warns: If Hormuz is Blocked, Growth Rate Plummets to 1% Range](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202606032111511390).

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow alleyway through which oil from the Middle East goes out to the whole world. If this place is blockaded for even a few days, the basic energy costs to run global factories and move logistics trucks will instantly soar to the roof. It won't end at the level of gasoline prices rising at your neighborhood gas station. We will be hit by an out-of-control 'secondary inflation' tsunami where all prices, including the motorcycle delivery fee for the pizza we order, office workers' lunch costs, taxi fares, and production unit costs of daily necessities, will trigger a massive chain explosion.

To prevent such a massive price explosion, the OECD foresaw that central banks like the US Federal Reserve (Fed) or the Bank of Korea might have to take the painful, drastic measure of raising their benchmark interest rates by a massive 0.5 to 0.75 percentage points at once, even at the risk of inevitably gnawing away at economic growth [OECD Warns: If Hormuz is Blocked, Growth Rate Plummets to 1% Range](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202606032111511390). This is undoubtedly a harsh, suffocating scenario for office workers or self-employed people struggling with heavy bank loan interests, or small and medium-sized enterprises whose investment funds have dried up.

## What's Next

Looking at the provocative news pouring out every day makes our hearts heavy, as if the world might collapse tomorrow. However, the fortunate thing is that there clearly exist calm and rational voices within the economic community stating that we do not need to be swept away by the media's extreme fear and panic sentiment.

Even if energy prices or exchange rates show abnormal surges in the short term due to distant geopolitical factors, a 'monetary policy prudence theory' is gaining heavy traction, arguing that central bank policymakers who protect the national economy should not overreact to the market by hastily raising benchmark interest rates significantly or tightening the money supply [Economic News](https://www.hankyung.com/economy). This is because if monetary policy shows a see-saw reaction rejoicing and sorrowing over short-term shocks, it can instead produce the fatal side effect of choking the breath out of the real economy that was functioning perfectly fine.

The terrifying threat of a 25% US semiconductor tariff must be approached similarly. When reviewed cool-headedly, there is a very high probability that this is a precisely calculated 'leverage card for negotiation' thrown by President Trump to solidify his domestic working-class support base or to extract more concessions in future trade negotiations.

If a destructive tariff of 25% is actually applied, the retail price tags of iPhones or work computers that ordinary American consumers have to buy at supermarkets right away will inevitably skyrocket by at least 25% or more. This will obviously strongly stimulate domestic consumer prices in the US. In the end, it is obvious that it will provoke a strong backlash from the public exhausted by inflation and return as the most painful political boomerang for US politicians who have an upcoming election to face.

It is an undeniable fact that for the time being, our hearts will fluctuate like a roller coaster over real-time exchange rate graphs or provocative breaking news. However, for the 'worst-case scenario' in the news—where all economies in the world perish together—to come true 100%, today's 21st-century global economy and technology supply chain ecosystem are too densely, absolutely necessarily, and deeply intertwined with one another.
