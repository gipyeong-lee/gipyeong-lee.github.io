Imagine this: You wake up this morning and think of your day as a single 'chess match.' In a chess game, players must make their moves within a set amount of time. Just as one side's clock stops when the other starts, what if we could allocate our day's time to only one focus area at a time? Are you falling into the trap of multitasking, jumping between tasks and missing what's truly important? The recently trending productivity app 'OwnTime' has brought this chess clock idea into reality.

### Why does this matter?

Modern society is a continuous barrage of notifications and deadlines. An [article on Chess.com](https://www.chess.com/article/view/the-art-of-time-management) points out that we live lives chased by time, obsessed with alarm clocks, digital calendars, and various management tools. We often believe that 'securing more time' is the way to increase productivity, but in reality, what's truly important is 'how efficiently we split and use our already defined time budget.' OwnTime helps us take a proactive approach to managing time, rather than being dragged around by it, through a powerful constraint strategy that forces us to immerse ourselves in only one most important task at a time.

### Understanding it simply

In short, OwnTime is a 'digital chess clock' for your day. According to the [official OwnTime introduction](https://owntime.app/), this app allows you to set a 'daily time budget' for several important priority tasks.

The most unique aspect is how it operates. Just like in a chess match where your clock stops the moment you press the button for your opponent's move after your turn, OwnTime runs only one timer at a time. Looking at [related posts on the news sharing platform Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49528506) and [related service stories](https://zeli.app/story/49528506), the app's operating principle becomes clear. The moment you start the timer for a specific task, all other task clocks are automatically paused.

For example, let's say you allocated a 2-hour budget to 'morning work.' The moment you turn on the timer, a 2-hour countdown begins. If you suddenly need to do something else and switch tasks, the 'morning work' clock immediately stops, and the new task's clock starts running. It creates an environment where you are forced to focus on only one thing at a time. When the time budget for each priority set by the user is exhausted, the app sounds an alarm to notify the user. As [various sources](https://owntime.app/, [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49528506), [Zeli](https://zeli.app/story/49528506)) explain in common, this serves to balance the time so the user doesn't spend too much time on a specific role or priority, or encourages the user to finish work within the set time.

### Current Status

Currently, OwnTime is a productivity tool available on iPhone and Apple Watch. It has minimized complex procedures for user convenience. According to [official guidelines](https://owntime.app/), the app does not require users to create accounts or connect to a central server. It is designed to operate as a standalone application without a server, prioritizing user privacy above all else. One of the app's biggest advantages is that you can start managing your time immediately on your wrist and smartphone without complex cloud setups.

### What's next?

Beyond simply listing timers, this 'chess clock' approach to time management is expected to spread among those who want to structure their day. As confirmed in [Hacker News community discussions](https://thefrontpage.dev/), such tools offer a distinct usability that differentiates them from existing complex, feature-rich tools in the realm of practical productivity competition. It will be an interesting point to watch how much more precisely users will design their own 'time budgets' and find balance in their daily lives through this.
