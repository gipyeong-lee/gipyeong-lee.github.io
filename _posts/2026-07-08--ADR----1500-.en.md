Imagine this: You go to a currency exchange booth to prepare for an overseas trip, and you find that while you had to pay 1,500 won to buy 1 dollar just yesterday, it has dropped to the 1,400 won range today. Recent news that the 'exchange rate has fallen below 1,500 won' is causing many to breathe a sigh of relief. At the center of this change is a major event: the American Depositary Receipt (ADR) listing that SK Hynix is pursuing in the US.

### Why is this important?

Exchange rates do not simply affect the cost of overseas travel. In a country like Korea, which has a high proportion of exports and imports, if the exchange rate is too high, the costs for companies to import raw materials increase. Consequently, this leads to pressure for price hikes, such as increases in the prices of the goods we use. As the high exchange rate has persisted for a long time, the entire Korean economy has felt significant burden. In this situation, SK Hynix's ADR listing sent a strong signal to the market that 'a large amount of dollars will soon be coming in.' This means that the supply of dollars in the foreign exchange market will increase, which is why many are paying attention to whether it will become a 'relief pitcher' to help us exit the long tunnel of a high exchange rate. [Source 10, Source 14]

### Understanding it easily: The relationship between ADRs and exchange rates

The term 'ADR' might feel a bit unfamiliar. It stands for **American Depositary Receipt, a certificate created to allow a Korean company to issue and trade stocks in the US market.**

Metaphorically, it is like SK Hynix, a popular Korean restaurant, selling 'meal kits (ADRs)' in the US market so that Americans can easily enjoy their food. Naturally, Americans would have to pay in dollars to buy these meal kits. Through this listing, SK Hynix is expected to raise up to $30 billion (approximately 43 trillion won). [Source 5, Source 8, Source 10, Source 13] When this massive amount of dollars enters Korea, the company will convert them into won for factory expansion or facility investment. In other words, a large-scale process of 'selling dollars and buying won' occurs, which leads to more dollars being released into the market. The principle is that as dollars become more common, the value of the dollar relatively decreases, the value of the won increases, and the exchange rate falls. [Source 12]

### Current Situation: The market is already expecting it

Although the actual listing date is July 10, the market is already moving quickly. [Source 2, Source 12] Investors have started 'forward exchange selling (the act of selling dollars that will come in the future in advance)' in anticipation of the dollar funds that will come in after the listing, and this is acting as downward pressure on the exchange rate. [Source 4, Source 8] As a result, the KRW/USD exchange rate fell below 1,500 won in the Seoul foreign exchange market on the 8th for the first time in two months. [Source 1, Source 8] In particular, market expectations and reactions were very intense, with the exchange rate fluctuation range during the day exceeding 30 won, the highest level of the year. [Source 7, Source 9]

### What will happen in the future?

Market experts evaluate that the $30 billion in funds to be raised this time is a significant amount, equivalent to the National Pension Service's annual dollar demand. [Source 10] It is forecasted that this volume will improve supply and demand in the foreign exchange market and have the effect of lowering the exchange rate by about 40 won. [Source 10] However, exchange rates are like complex living organisms intertwined with many factors, not just corporate issues, but also international situations and global economic trends. Therefore, the key is to calmly observe how quickly dollars actually flow in after the listing and how much continuous stability this will bring to our economy. [Source 6, Source 14]
