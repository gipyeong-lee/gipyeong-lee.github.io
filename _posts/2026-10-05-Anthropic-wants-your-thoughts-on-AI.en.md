Imagine this: You wake up in the morning, and instead of AI organizing the work emails that piled up overnight, it says, "Why don't you go for a walk in the nearby woods with your family this afternoon? I've already adjusted your work schedule." How would that make you feel?

Until now, AI has just been a "competent assistant" to us. It was a productivity tool that summarized long research papers, helped with complex coding, and created Excel sheets in an instant. However, the results of a large-scale study recently released by Anthropic suggest that what we truly want from AI lies far beyond simple productivity.

### Why It Matters

We have evaluated AI with the cold yardstick of "how much more work can it help us handle and how fast." But this study raises a fundamental question about how technology will change our quality of life. Anthropic asked over 80,000 people deeply about why they use AI and their fears regarding it. As a result, it became clear that people now want to enjoy a "better life" rather than handle "more work." [Source: Anthropic's 81,000-person interview analysis](https://escape-engineering.tistory.com/entry/81000명의-목소리-인류는-AI에게-진정-무엇을-바라는가-Anthropic-81k-Interviews-분석)

### The Explainer

Instead of humans asking questions directly, the "Anthropic Interviewer" that led this study used a method where a customized AI led the conversation. Just like when we solve a puzzle and have to find the next piece once we fit one in, the AI analyzed the respondent's answers in real-time and posed follow-up questions one after another. Through this, it captured both the advantages of in-depth qualitative interviews and the statistical power of a massive survey. [Source: What 81,000 people want from AI](https://www.anthropic.com/features/81k-interviews)

To use a metaphor, if existing surveys were like stiff written exams asking, "Do you like apples?", this study was like a friendly café conversation asking, "You like apples? Then what memories did you have while eating that apple?" This conversation, conducted in 70 languages across 159 countries, contained the voices of 80,508 people. [Source: Anthropic's AI survey results](https://roboaidigest.com/posts/2026-03-20-anthropic-81k-ai-desires/)

### Where We Stand

The core message of the study results is clear. Humanity wants to get back the "time" they have lost through AI. [Source: Human sincerity revealed by AI survey](https://thenextweb.com/news/the-largest-ai-survey-ever-reveals-what-humans-actually-want) People value the "free time" and "new life experiences" that AI will bring more than simple efficiency improvements. Anthropic is seriously exploring the path technology should take through large-scale research that encompasses user experiences, hopes, and even concerns. [Source: Anthropic's AI research direction](https://www.anthropic.com/research/your-thoughts-on-ai)

### What's Next

In the future, we will face an era where we compete not on "what AI can do better," but on "how much AI helps us live more like humans." Through this study, Anthropic accurately grasped the fears and expectations of people that we must consider when building technology. [Source: Anthropic AI preference study](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/anthropic-whos-most-optimistic-about-ai-and-who-isnt.html) Now, technology developers must think about how to ensure AI is not a tool that steals human time, but one that helps us spend precious time with our loved ones or care for ourselves.

### MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

It is very impressive that technology is moving not simply toward replacing humans, but toward giving humanity the precious gift of "time" so that we can live like humans. Isn't the future we truly want not an AI faster than a calculator, but a warm assistant that makes our lives less complicated?
