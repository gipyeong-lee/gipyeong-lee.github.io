Imagine your everyday AI assistant suddenly causes a major global error. Can the government stop this AI immediately? Until now, the criteria have been ambiguous. However, with the recent leak of a new draft bill reportedly supported by Anthropic (an AI safety and research company), the debate over these criteria is heating up.

### Why It Matters

As AI technology advances, concerns about its potential risks are also growing. Government authority to regulate or halt AI technology is incredibly powerful. However, indiscriminate regulation without clear criteria could stifle technological innovation. This bill attempts to provide concrete figures to the difficult question of "at what level of damage should the state intervene?" It is also an important social issue for us to decide where to draw the safe boundary between AI companies and the government.

### The Explainer

Think of it like this: when we drive on a highway and an accident occurs, the insurance company intervenes. However, the government cannot shut down the entire highway simply because it "looks dangerous." Operational stability requires specific criteria, such as the scale of the accident or the amount of damage.

The leaked bill proposes that for the federal government to use its powerful authority to halt the development of "frontier AI models" (the most sophisticated AI systems developed by humanity), there must be at least **$1 billion** in damages [Source 1]. A billion dollars is an unimaginably huge sum—enough to give every single person in South Korea about 20,000 won. In other words, this bill is both a "safety device" and an "intervention criterion" designed to prevent the government from arbitrarily halting AI development [Source 1].

### Where We Stand

Anthropic is currently working to build reliable, interpretable, and steerable AI systems [Source 2, Source 14]. However, concerns are consistently being raised that the speed of technological advancement is too fast for safety guidelines to keep up [Source 11]. This leaked bill appears intended to clearly set the scope of federal government intervention, allowing companies to develop technology with peace of mind while simultaneously promoting social safety.

### What's Next

Moving forward, the government and Congress will discuss whether these criteria are appropriate. The debate over whether the $1 billion damage threshold is too high or if it represents the best line of defense for technological innovation is expected to continue. Readers should keep an eye on how much damage becomes the benchmark in future policy news related to AI. Anthropic is already leading various discussions for AI safety, such as proposing "Responsible Scaling Policies (RSP)" across the industry [Source 3, Source 15].

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

The process of social consensus to achieve both safety and innovation has just begun.
