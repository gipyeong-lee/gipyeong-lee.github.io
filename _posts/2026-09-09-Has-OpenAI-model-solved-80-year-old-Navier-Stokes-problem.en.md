Imagine a problem that has eluded the world's greatest genius mathematicians for decades. It is like a highly complex maze where everyone who enters the gate ends up lost. If one day, an artificial intelligence appeared like a comet and claimed to have completed the map in just a few days, would you believe it?

Recently, OpenAI announced that its AI model had solved the "Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem," a mathematical challenge that humanity has failed to resolve for over 80 years [Source 6, Source 8, Source 12]. This event goes beyond a simple calculation; it touches upon one of the "Millennium Problems," considered among the most difficult and important in the field of mathematics [Source 10, Source 12].

### Why is this important?

The name "Navier-Stokes equation" might sound a bit intimidating. Simply put, this equation is a mathematical law that describes how "fluids" (substances with the property of flowing), such as air or water, move. It contains the core principles of all physical phenomena in the world we live in—why airplanes can fly, why weather forecasts sometimes miss the mark, and even how blood flows through the blood vessels in our bodies.

What if this problem were solved perfectly? We would be able to make much more accurate weather forecasts than we do today and design aircraft or engines with maximized fuel efficiency, taking humanity's physical understanding to a new level. However, this problem is so complex that human intelligence alone has not been able to find a clear answer over the last 80 years [Source 4, Source 10]. If OpenAI's announcement is true, it means that AI can become a powerful partner that transcends human intellectual limits, moving beyond a simple chatbot.

### Simplified: What if 10,000 geniuses solved a puzzle together?

How did the AI solve this difficult challenge? OpenAI stated that it utilized an internal AI model with significantly higher performance than the publicly available "GPT-6 Astra" model [Source 4, Source 10].

Metaphorically speaking, it is like deploying 10,000 experts to complete a giant puzzle that could never be solved alone. OpenAI operated approximately 10,000 AI agents (AI systems that perform tasks autonomously) simultaneously [Source 4, Source 10]. These agents acted as if 10,000 mathematicians had divided into teams to share information, cooperate, and explore a vast number of possibilities. Surprisingly, it took them only 88 hours to find the answer [Source 4, Source 6].

### Current Situation: Why it is too early to celebrate

However, the academic community's response is cautious and cold. While OpenAI confidently claims to have solved it, mathematicians agree that there is still a long way to go before it is officially recognized [Source 1]. This is because in mathematics, a "proof" does not end with just providing an answer. It requires a tedious and rigorous verification process to prove that the logic is perfect without a single error.

Moreover, the announcement recently hit an unexpected reef. A researcher raised the possibility that OpenAI used their research data without authorization in the process of achieving these results [Source 12]. As AI has solved the challenge by learning human knowledge, the legitimacy of that learning process has been brought into question, fueling controversy [Source 6].

### What happens next?

There are two main points we need to watch. First, whether the solution presented by OpenAI can pass the rigorous verification of mathematicians around the world and be recognized as the official answer [Source 1]. Second, how social consensus will be reached regarding the copyright issues and transparency of the data AI learns.

If these results are verified, AI will open a new era of mathematics and science as a "creative problem solver." If not, the incident will be difficult to escape criticism regarding "AI data bias and exaggerated advertising."
