Imagine this: You have a screenplay you spent your whole life perfecting or a song you composed, and suddenly, one day, someone takes it without your permission and uses it as a 'textbook' to teach Artificial Intelligence (AI). What if that AI even starts to imitate your style exactly? Recently, with the growth of the AI industry, the source of 'training data,' which can be called the most fundamental asset of this technology, has emerged as a hot potato. This time, the legacy of the 'King of Rock and Roll,' Elvis Presley, is at the center of this debate.

## Why is this important?

This lawsuit goes beyond a simple dispute between companies; it raises important questions about how AI is absorbing our culture and how the rights of original creators should be protected in that process. [Anthropic](https://emergent.sh/news/anthropic-sued-over-elvis-presley-music-ai) is the company that developed 'Claude,' currently one of the smartest AI models. If AI can freely learn from someone's creative work to produce results without permission, can the jobs of future composers, writers, and artists be safe? Consumers also have the right to know what processes were used to create the music they listen to or the writing they read.

## Understanding it easily

You can easily liken AI training to a 'student studying in a giant library.' For a student (AI) to become smart, they must read all the books and songs in the world. However, the problem is that a significant portion of the books and songs in this library are precious treasures locked with a key called 'copyright.'

Anthropic is currently being sued one after another by giants in the music industry. [Authentic Brands Group, which manages Elvis Presley's music copyrights](https://lumienai.com/news/anthropic-elvis-presley-lawsuit-ai-claude-copyright), alleged that Anthropic used the lyrics of 274 songs, including 'Can't Help Falling in Love' and 'Don't Be Cruel,' to train its Claude model without authorization. [The Elvis Presley side is demanding damages of $150,000 per infringed song](https://lumienai.com/news/anthropic-elvis-presley-lawsuit-ai-claude-copyright).

Simply put, this is like a situation where students did not legitimately buy the reference books they needed to study, but instead sneaked into a library, tore out pages, or made illegal copies to study before taking an exam. Can an 'intelligent AI' created this way be considered to have gone through a fair training process? Metaphorically speaking, it's like opening a high-end restaurant with food made by secretly stealing someone else's recipe, so the anger of the creators might be justified.

## Current situation

This is not the first time Anthropic has faced a copyright lawsuit. Major music companies like [Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell](https://azat.tv/en/anthropic-sued-over-alleged-theft-of-songs-for-ai-training/) have already filed lawsuits claiming that tens of thousands of songs were used without authorization.

It is not just music. [The publishing industry is also conducting a lawsuit claiming that Anthropic used a dataset called 'Books3' to train on 196,640 books without permission](https://www.xatakaon.com/robotics-and-ai/a-group-of-authors-has-sued-anthropic-the-reason-it-trained-its-ai-with-copies-of-their-books). In another case, [Concord Music Group raised suspicions that Anthropic used illegally copied music data of over 20,000 songs for training](https://www.zerohedge.com/ai/music-publishers-sue-anthropic-copyright-infringement). The courts are currently closely examining whether their data usage methods violated copyright laws.

## What will happen in the future?

These lawsuits are highly likely to become 'precedents' that set the standards for how AI handles data. AI companies are expected to go through stricter data acquisition procedures (license agreements) in the future. This could increase AI development costs, but it could also create a structure where fair compensation goes to the creators. Technology is sweeping in like a giant wave, but the way we ride that wave must not become 'looting.' When you see the results produced by AI in the future, you will likely think at least once about 'whose efforts were used to study this.'

## MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

As important as the speed of technological development is the question, 'whose sacrifice is it based on?' Elvis Presley's songs may have made Claude, the AI, smarter, but if that data was used without a fair process, it will be an ethical homework assignment, not a technological achievement. For a fairer AI era, we must create new rules between technology and creation.
