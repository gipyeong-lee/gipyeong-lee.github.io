Imagine this. You are a farmer cultivating vast fields. Right in the middle of the busy planting season, a state-of-the-art tractor you bought for hundreds of thousands of dollars suddenly stops working. It turns out to be a software issue inside, but calling an engineer designated by the manufacturer requires waiting for days, and repair costs are whatever they charge. For a farmer who needs to farm right away, this is a bolt out of the blue.

Recently, farmers around the world have been growing tired of this exact situation. However, in a small town in Alberta, Canada, a 'special tractor' that solves this problem in one shot has appeared and is garnering global attention.

### Why Are Farmers Crazy About Simplicity?

The agricultural machinery market has rapidly become 'sophisticated' over the past few years. Now, tractors are no longer simple machines; they have become massive smart devices packed with complex computer circuits, GPS (Global Positioning System), and autonomous driving sensors. While convenient in many cases, the biggest issue is that farmers can hardly touch anything themselves when a problem arises.

In particular, strict 'computer servicing contracts' demanded by manufacturers pose a heavy financial burden and inconvenience to farmers. [[Source: Alberta-made low-tech tractor getting attention worldwide](https://www.newslocker.com/en-ca/region/calgary/alberta-made-low-tech-tractor-getting-attention-worldwide/)] Under these circumstances, 'simplicity'—where a farmer can easily fix a breakdown with a single wrench—is not a step backward in technology, but a 'matter of survival' for farmers.

### Simply Put, What Kind of Tractor Is It?

The core of the tractor built by 'Ursa Ag', a small farm equipment repair shop located in Bowden, Alberta, Canada, is that it is 'all-mechanical'. [[Source: Alberta-made low-tech tractor getting attention worldwide](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/ursa-ag-bowden-tractors-9.7399940)]

To make an analogy, it is similar to the difference between modern smartphones and the sturdy walkie-talkies we used in the past. Smartphones (modern tractors) can do many things like cameras, internet, and apps, but if the screen cracks or the software glitches, you have to visit a service center. On the other hand, walkie-talkies (Ursa Ag tractors) only function for calls, but they are sturdy even if dropped, and if a problem occurs, you can open the internal circuit to fix it easily.

Instead of complex computer chips, this tractor chose a method to maximize the power of physical gears and the engine. It has returned to an 'intuitive tool' that farmers can understand and repair on their own. [[Source: Alberta-made low-tech tractor getting attention worldwide](https://www.msn.com/en-ca/money/general/alberta-s-ursa-ag-tractors-draw-worldwide-attention-for-simple-design/ss-AA2gkrFa)]

### What Is the Current Situation?

This small repair shop's experiment is creating a massive splash. While it started with small local responses in Canada, it has now become a major topic of discussion in the global agricultural community. To date, over 1,000 farmers from 30 countries worldwide, including North America, have directly contacted Ursa Ag expressing their desire to purchase this tractor. [[Source: Alberta-made low-tech tractor getting attention worldwide](https://www.msn.com/en-ca/money/general/alberta-s-ursa-ag-tractors-draw-worldwide-attention-for-simple-design/ss-AA2gkrFa)]

Currently, they are focusing on building 'reliable and affordable-to-maintain' machinery that farmers need. Paradoxically, in agricultural fields swept by cutting-edge technology, the voices of farmers desiring the 'power of basic machinery' have been confirmed. [[Source: Alberta-made low-tech tractor getting attention worldwide](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/ursa-ag-bowden-tractors-9.7399940)]

### What Will Happen in the Future?

Ursa Ag's success sends a strong message to other major farm equipment manufacturers: "Technology cannot replace everything." In the future, the agricultural machinery market is likely to split into highly automated equipment and practical, easy-to-repair equipment.

While we will see even smarter AI technology in the future, the value of transparent and honest machinery that anyone can understand will also be highlighted again. What farmers want is not more complex code, but a 'reliable partner' that can hit the field tomorrow morning.
