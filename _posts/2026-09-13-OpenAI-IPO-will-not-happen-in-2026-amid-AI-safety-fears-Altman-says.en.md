Imagine you have a very smart pet dog. It’s clever enough to open the refrigerator and get snacks on its own. But one day, this dog decides it wants to rearrange the furniture in your house. You are worried the dog might get hurt, but it’s already downloading and running a furniture arrangement app. The situation OpenAI currently faces is similar. AI technology is advancing so rapidly that the task of controlling it safely has become far more important than the speed of the technology itself.

OpenAI, the artificial intelligence company that has received intense interest from the global tech industry and Wall Street, has finally decided not to pursue a stock market listing, or an initial public offering (IPO), in 2026 [Source 1](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/open-ai-ipo-sam-altman_n_6aa5bb7ae4b0781f9692bf70), [Source 11](https://www.techmeme.com/260912/p16). While rumors of an imminent IPO have been rampant in the market for the past few months, and it was known that they had been hiring a large number of relevant experts to prepare for the listing [Source 10](https://wageyman.com/openai-ceo-sam-altman-confirms-ipo-delay-amidst-heightened-ai-safety-concerns-and-market-volatility/), [Source 12](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/openais-sam-altman-says-it-would-be-ill-advised-to-go-public-in-2026/), CEO Sam Altman has boldly hit the brakes.

### Why is this important?

Generally, when successful startups grow, they list on the stock market to raise more capital and reward their investors. The voices of shareholders also become that much louder. However, OpenAI has turned its back on the sweet temptation of "capital raising."

CEO Sam Altman cited concerns about the safety and security of current AI technology as the primary reason for the delay [Source 1](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/open-ai-ipo-sam-altman_n_6aa5bb7ae4b0781f9692bf70), [Source 9](https://wageyman.com/openai-ceo-sam-altman-confirms-ipo-delay-amidst-heightened-ai-safety-concerns-and-market-volatility/). When a company goes public, it must report its performance every quarter, and the market's pressure to maximize profit intensifies. This is because it becomes difficult to ignore shareholders who want products that make money immediately, even if the cost of building "safe AI" is very high. Altman judged that it would become more difficult for the company to maintain safety as its top priority under such pressure [Source 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v8rL2Pu_2VY).

### Simply put

Let's compare this situation to "driving." You are in a very fast and powerful supercar that is about to enter the highway. The engine performance is incredible, but you aren't 100% sure if the brakes are working properly or if the seatbelts are sturdy.

Here, an IPO is like entering a "car racing tournament." If you enter the tournament, you have to drive fast to improve your ranking. But OpenAI has essentially declared, "Now is not the time to drive fast and improve rankings, but the time to ensure the car's safety checks are more thorough" [Source 2](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/sam-altman-openai-ipo-delay-ill-advised-moment-safety-concerns/), [Source 11](https://www.techmeme.com/260912/p16).

Let's use another analogy. Suppose you are building a very complex Lego castle. The one we are building now is the most sophisticated one ever. However, if someone keeps urging you to "build it faster" while you're building, or demands that you "design it more flashily to show off to others," the foundation of the castle, which is the most important part, might shake. An IPO is the process of accepting the demands of shareholders, but OpenAI believes that strengthening the "foundation of the castle (AI)" is far more important right now.

### What is the current situation?

OpenAI has reportedly already been secretly taking steps to prepare for an IPO [Source 10](https://wageyman.com/openai-ceo-sam-altman-confirms-ipo-delay-amidst-heightened-ai-safety-concerns-and-market-volatility/), [Source 12](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/openais-sam-altman-says-it-would-be-ill-advised-to-go-public-in-2026/). They even brought in financial experts with the goal of going public around September 2026 [Source 8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v8rL2Pu_2VY), [Source 12](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/openais-sam-altman-says-it-would-be-ill-advised-to-go-public-in-2026/). However, the situation has changed rapidly recently as concerns about AI risks, unpredictable behavior, and security threats—essentially "AI doomsday theories"—have grown globally [Source 9](https://wageyman.com/openai-ceo-sam-altman-confirms-ipo-delay-amidst-heightened-ai-safety-concerns-and-market-volatility/), [Source 11](https://www.techmeme.com/260912/p16).

In a recent interview, Sam Altman was firm about the possibility of an IPO within 2026, saying, "It won't be 2026" [Source 3](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/12/openai-delays-ipo-sam-altman-ai-safety-concerns), [Source 14](https://www.techmeme.com/260912/p16). He clearly expressed that the current moment is "ill-advised" for pursuing an IPO [Source 2](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/sam-altman-openai-ipo-delay-ill-advised-moment-safety-concerns/), [Source 10](https://wageyman.com/openai-ceo-sam-altman-confirms-ipo-delay-amidst-heightened-ai-safety-concerns-and-market-volatility/).

### Where do we go from here?

As a result of this decision, investors will be unable to purchase OpenAI stock directly for the time being [Source 2](https://fortune.com/2026/09/12/sam-altman-openai-ipo-delay-ill-advised-moment-safety-concerns/). While this may be disappointing news for investors, it is being evaluated as a very cautious and responsible move by those who think about the ethics and safety of AI technology.

We will have to wait and see how OpenAI proves the value of "safe AI" alongside technical perfection in the future. By the next time they pursue an IPO, whether it is in 2027 or later, a much safer and more predictable AI might be changing our lives.
