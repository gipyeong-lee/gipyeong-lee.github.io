Imagine you log into Minecraft, and 50 AI colleagues have stepped forward to build a perfect civilization for you. They’ll construct buildings, farm, and manage the community. But what happens if, shortly after, they spend all their time in 'meetings' deciding who does what instead of actually doing the work?

This is exactly what happened in a recent experiment where researchers unleashed AI agents—AI software that autonomously carries out goals—into the virtual world of Minecraft to build a civilization. The efficient AI workers we expected were nowhere to be found; instead, they were drowning in a swamp of massive bureaucracy.

## Why does this matter?

AI technology is moving beyond simple calculation or writing into an era of 'agents' that judge and act on their own. What would happen if a company entrusted tasks to dozens of AI agents to maximize efficiency? This experiment suggests that no matter how high the individual intelligence of an AI, getting them to 'collaborate' is never easy. The fact that the inefficient bureaucracy common in human society can occur in an AI society is a task we must seriously consider as we introduce AI into actual industrial settings.

## In simple terms: What is AI 'bureaucracy'?

Transformer-based AI is excellent at solving problems alone. For example, a small team of about 4 AI agents succeeded in cooperating to defeat one of Minecraft's most difficult boss monsters, the 'Ender Dragon' [Source: AIAgentSwarmsvs. a SingleAgent](https://dev.to/jamilxt/ai-agent-swarms-vs-a-single-agent-build-a-fair-coordination-benchmark-553c).

However, when the number of agents increases to 50, the situation changes completely. You can compare this to the situation where 'too many cooks in the kitchen spoil the broth.'

When there are 2 or 3 cooks, everyone does their assigned job, but when 50 people enter a kitchen, they spend all their time debating who should pick up the knife or who should season the pot, and eventually, no one gets to eat. AI agents also hit a wall called 'Coordination Overhead.' The energy spent exchanging information on who will do what, making decisions, and aligning each other's actions became greater than the actual activity within the game [Source: AIAgentSwarmsvs. a SingleAgent](https://dev.to/jamilxt/ai-agent-swarms-vs-a-single-agent-build-a-fair-coordination-benchmark-553c). This is the reality of the 'bureaucracy' that appeared in the AI society.

## Current situation: Heroes exist, but society is hard

Current AI agents show amazing potential in achieving single goals in complex environments like Minecraft. The case where a Gemini 4 agent completed the Minecraft game proves this. Of course, it had to endure the 'trial and error' of dying a staggering 605 times in the process [Source: GPT and ClaudeAgentsJust Started anAICivilizationWar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l1L9sezAcxc).

On the other hand, the attempt by 50 GPT agents to build a civilization faced severe resource shortages and coordination problems, failing to achieve the grand goal of building a civilization [Source: GPT and ClaudeAgentsJust Started anAICivilizationWar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l1L9sezAcxc), [Source: AIAgentSwarmsvs. a SingleAgent](https://dev.to/jamilxt/ai-agent-swarms-vs-a-single-agent-build-a-fair-coordination-benchmark-553c). We have created 'genius individuals,' but we haven't yet taught AI how to build an 'organized society.'

## What will happen in the future?

AI agent orchestration—coordinating multiple agents—is currently the hottest research field. Frameworks like 'Agency Swarm' have already emerged, with attempts underway to manage AI agents more efficiently and assign them different roles [Source: GitHub - VRSEN/agency-swarm](https://github.com/VRSEN/agency-swarm).

In the future, the key will not simply be increasing the number of AIs, but how to ensure they do not interfere with each other and fulfill their individual roles like an ant colony (swarm) while achieving the overall goal. The day might come when the AIs that were lost in bureaucracy in Minecraft eventually find an efficient system themselves and build a massive city.

## AI's Perspective: MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective

The news that 50 AIs gave up on building a civilization after holding meetings with each other is somewhat absurd, but at the same time, it paradoxically shows why organizations created by humans are so complex and difficult. Beyond the process of AI resembling human 'intelligence,' we have now entered a stage where we must teach AI the higher-order wisdom of human 'collaboration.'
