Imagine this: you walk past a university promotional banner and see your own face, but one day, it has suddenly been changed into someone of a different race. Your own existence has been erased, and a virtual person created by artificial intelligence (AI) has taken your place. Recently, at Stanford University, one of the world's most prestigious institutions, exactly this bizarre and shocking incident occurred.

## Why does this matter?

This incident is not merely an editorial error in university promotional material. It is a case that starkly demonstrates how easily technology can 'modify' and 'replace' a person's identity. [Source 1](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/stanford-violated-ai-policy-turning-221921805.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall), [Source 3](https://abcnews.com/US/students-outraged-after-stanford-university-ai-alter-race/story?id=136718665) It poses a heavy question about where the line lies between 'touching up' and 'distortion' when the AI image editing tools we commonly use are deployed in the public sphere. [Source 6](https://nypost.com/2026/09/24/us-news/stanford-admits-to-ai-race-and-gender-swap-in-doctored-student-ad/) The fact that an educational institution like a university broke its own established principles to 'edit' reality through AI has brought great disappointment to the public. ## Easy to Understand: Is it Photoshop for 'Identity'?

It is easy to explain this with an analogy. While editing in the past meant taking a photo and using Photoshop to remove blemishes, today's Generative AI can reach a level of 'personality editing' that makes it possible to replace a person in a photo with someone entirely different or change their form. [Source 1](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/stanford-violated-ai-policy-turning-221921805.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall), [Source 2](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/stanford-ai-policy-student-photo-race-swapping-22444142.php)

In simple terms, just as we often apply filters in smartphone photo apps, the university mobilized AI to arbitrarily change a student's race, gender, and facial shape to boost promotional effectiveness. [Source 5](https://san.com/cc/stanford-university-admits-ai-was-used-to-race-swap-student-photo/), [Source 10](https://aicoverage.org/story/stanford-altered-student-s-race-in-promotional-photo-using-ai) According to reports, Billy Ramirez, the Hispanic male student on the promotional banner, was replaced by a non-existent Black female. [Source 2](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/stanford-ai-policy-student-photo-race-swapping-22444142.php), [Source 7](https://nypost.com/2026/09/22/us-news/stanford-busted-using-ai-to-race-swap-white-male-student-for-black-female-in-ad-silenced-and-erased/), [Source 12](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/stanford-university-apology-ai-photo) Additionally, the faces of other students on the banner were also edited using AI tools to appear slimmer than they actually were. [Source 4](https://sfstandard.com/2026/09/22/stanford-student-ai-photo-replaces-latino-man-black-woman/), [Source 5](https://san.com/cc/stanford-university-admits-ai-was-used-to-race-swap-student-photo/), [Source 10](https://aicoverage.org/story/stanford-altered-student-s-race-in-promotional-photo-using-ai)

## Current Status: Admission and Apology

Stanford University has admitted to these facts. [Source 5](https://san.com/cc/stanford-university-admits-ai-was-used-to-race-swap-student-photo/), [Source 17](https://stanfordreview.org/stanford-r-de-uses-ai-to-race-swap-students-for-advertising/) The promotional material in question was a banner for the campus Dining Hall, and upon the controversy arising, the university immediately removed the banner and issued an official apology. [Source 2](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/stanford-ai-policy-student-photo-race-swapping-22444142.php), [Source 17](https://stanfordreview.org/stanford-r-de-uses-ai-to-race-swap-students-for-advertising/) Most painfully, the university directly violated the 'AI Usage Policy' it had established for itself. [Source 6](https://nypost.com/2026/09/24/us-news/stanford-admits-to-ai-race-and-gender-swap-in-doctored-student-ad/), [Source 12](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/stanford-university-apology-ai-photo) This incident became public when the campus student newspaper, 'The Stanford Review,' first raised suspicions. [Source 16](https://stanfordreview.org/stanford-r-de-uses-ai-to-race-swap-students-for-advertising/), [Source 17](https://stanfordreview.org/stanford-r-de-uses-ai-to-race-swap-students-for-advertising/) Billy Ramirez, the victim in this matter, expressed strong regret regarding the university's actions, stating that he felt "erased and silenced." [Source 12](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/stanford-university-apology-ai-photo)

## What Happens Next?

This controversy is expected to serve as a warning to universities and companies around the world regarding 'AI guidelines.' This is because behind the convenience provided by generative AI always lurks the 'danger of distorting reality.' Going forward, schools and organizations will likely struggle more deeply with how to rigorously apply 'ethical principles' alongside technical perfection when introducing AI into promotional materials.

Technological progress changes the way we communicate with the world, but treating the 'human' beings who are the subjects of that communication as mere data to be swapped at will is by no means an advancement of technology. Stanford's case reminds us once again of how heavy the responsibility of humans who wield AI is, equal to the power that AI possesses.
