Imagine you are trapped in a very complex maze, but you don't have a map, so you don't know which way to go. For scientists studying artificial intelligence (AI), 'neural networks'—the core design of AI modeled after the structure of the human brain—have long been exactly that kind of existence. It has been difficult to perfectly understand why AI provides certain answers or what happens inside, so they have often been called 'black boxes.'

However, hopeful news has recently emerged suggesting that we might be able to draw a map of this maze. The essay 'Neural Networks Are Already Clean,' published on October 15, 2026, by AI researcher Nick Cammarata, is creating a ripple effect in the AI industry [Source 1].

### Why is this important?

Until now, AI has been an 'intelligent but inscrutable existence' to us. When an AI generates fake news or provides biased information, it has been very difficult to find the cause at the level of internal neurons (the basic units that make up AI) and fix it. But things change if the inside of a neural network is actually much more orderly and easier to understand than we thought, as this study suggests. This will be an important step toward an era where we can increase AI safety and control AI as we desire.

### In simple terms: The misunderstanding of superposition

Scientists have long explained the reason why AI becomes complex using the theory of 'superposition.' To put it in an easy metaphor, it is a phenomenon where, because an AI's 'chest of drawers' is insufficient, it is forced to cram completely different socks and underwear into a single compartment. Thus, it was believed that 'polysemanticity,' where a single neuron expresses multiple concepts simultaneously, occurs, making understanding difficult.

However, researcher Cammarata points out that this claim stemmed from a misinterpretation of AI models in the early stages [Source 3, Source 5]. He argues that the cause of polysemanticity is not due to a lack of AI capacity, but is actually a set of mundane technical problems that are sufficiently fixable [Source 1, Source 7]. It means that AI hasn't been messily mixing up information, but rather we just haven't been reading AI's language well yet.

Metaphorically speaking, whereas previously it was thought that the concepts of a 'cat' and a 'car' in a photo were mixed in one neuron, they have now discovered a 'clean' structure that can clearly separate and interpret them [Source 4, Source 7].

### Current situation: How deep can we see?

The researcher is confident that most neurons can be clearly understood even in massive models with trillion-scale parameters (Parameter: numerical values that AI adjusts itself while learning) [Source 1, Source 7]. This is a major breakthrough in the field of 'mechanistic interpretability,' which studies the inside of AI directly, at a time when AI is rapidly advancing by mimicking the human brain [Source 15, Source 4].

Of course, we cannot say we know everything yet. However, it is very encouraging that it has been revealed, at the very least, that the internal structure of AI is not being left in a chaotic state as we thought.

### What will happen in the future?

In the future, 'AI surgery' may become possible, where we peer more clearly into the 'insides' of AI and precisely treat problems at the neuron level. This means that making an explainable AI that can explain why it made such a judgment may no longer be a dream of the distant future. We are moving beyond the stage of simply trusting and using AI, and now into the stage of surely understanding and managing AI.
