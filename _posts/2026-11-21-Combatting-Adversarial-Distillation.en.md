Imagine you have an original educational curriculum and know-how built through decades of research at a world-class prestigious university. Enormous funds, astronomical time, and the efforts of numerous talented individuals were invested to establish the school. However, someone secretly climbs over the wall, illicitly copies all textbooks and exam questions, and sets up an identical private academy without incurring costs to recruit students. The reality currently faced by the global artificial intelligence (AI) industry is very similar to this.

Recently, as the performance of cutting-edge AI models has improved dramatically, security and intellectual property protection surrounding them have emerged as global hot topics. At the center of this concept is **'Adversarial Distillation'**. Metaphorically speaking, it is digital theft that smartly intercepts core know-how forged through others' blood and sweat. Let's examine one by one why this phenomenon is attracting intense interest from global big tech and government agencies.

---

### What is Knowledge Distillation? (And Why Is It a Problem?)

First, let's go over the basic concepts step by step. In the field of artificial intelligence, **'Knowledge Distillation (a training technique where a compact model learns and replicates the knowledge and capabilities of a larger, more powerful source model)'** has long been a widely used, legitimate, and useful technique [Source: OpenAI Anthropic Google vs China: AI distillation war explained | Idlen](https://www.idlen.io/news/openai-anthropic-google-alliance-china-distillation-frontier-model-forum/), [Source: Adversarial Distillation - Frontier Model Forum](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/issue-briefs/issue-brief-adversarial-distillation/), [Source: Detecting and preventing distillation attacks – Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks). 

Put simply, it is the process where a genius professor (giant AI model) efficiently delivers core summaries realized after reading tens of thousands of books to a disciple (small AI model). It is like a junior student quickly inheriting a senior's know-how to build up their skills. Because giant AI models cost an immense amount to operate, distillation technology is essential for creating compact models scaled down to run lightly on smartphones or general servers [Source: [2505.24842] Cascading Adversarial Bias from Injection to China-Based Artificial Intelligence Companies Conducting ...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24842]). In fact, leading AI labs such as OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google also regularly utilize distillation methods to lightweight their own models [Source: [Detecting and preventing distillation attacks – Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks).

However, knowledge distillation technology is not always used for positive purposes. The problem arises when it is **exploited illicitly and on an industrial scale**. This is called **'Adversarial distillation'**, which refers to attackers sending massive queries to a giant AI model without permission and collecting output results to replicate the giant model's core functions without investing massive computing costs [Source: The United States Needs a Strategic Response to Adversarial AI... | ITIF](https://itif.org/publications/2026/06/26/the-united-states-needs-a-strategic-response-to-adversarial-ai-distillation/), [Source: OpenAI Anthropic Google vs China: AI distillation war explained | Idlen](https://www.idlen.io/news/openai-anthropic-google-alliance-china-distillation-frontier-model-forum/). Attackers sometimes deploy deliberate workarounds to bypass service access controls [Source: The United States Needs a Strategic Response to Adversarial AI... | ITIF](https://itif.org/publications/2026/06/26/the-united-states-needs-a-strategic-response-to-adversarial-ai-distillation/). For example, developing a state-of-the-art language model directly requires hundreds of millions of dollars and massive power, but using adversarial distillation allows stealing those capabilities at a much lower cost [Source: OpenAI Anthropic Google vs China: AI distillation war explained | Idlen](https://www.idlen.io/news/openai-anthropic-google-alliance-china-distillation-frontier-model-forum/).

### Adversarial Distillation in Image Generation and Diffusion Models

Knowledge distillation is not limited to language models. Distillation techniques to enhance efficiency are also actively researched in the field of image generation. For example, the **Adversarial Diffusion Distillation (ADD)** technique researched by Stability AI and others transforms the way large-scale foundational image diffusion models traditionally generate images across dozens of steps into an efficient sampling process requiring only 1 to 4 very brief inference steps for high-quality images [Source: Adversarial Diffusion Distillation — Stability AI](https://stability.ai/research/adversarial-diffusion-distillation), [Source: [2403.12015] Fast High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12015]). 

While such technology dramatically speeds up computation when used legitimately, it can pose a significant security threat when used for malicious attempts to undermine or illicitly extract the value of the source model. The double-edged sword nature of the technology itself is what makes this security battle even more complex.

---

### Why Does This Matter?

From a general user's perspective, one might think, "Isn't it a good thing if we can use high-capability AI models more cheaply?" However, this issue goes beyond simple copyright disputes between companies—it is directly tied to national security and the health of the AI ecosystem.

1. **Loss of AI Innovation Drivers**: Companies developing cutting-edge AI models pour massive capital and research and development (R&D) capabilities over years. If someone can effortlessly replicate those results without effort, companies lose the incentive to take risks and develop innovative models.
2. **Security and Reliability Threats**: When illicitly extracted models are exposed to malicious purposes or biased data injection (data poisoning), the risk of producing socially harmful outcomes increases. In fact, research has confirmed a phenomenon (Cascading Adversarial Bias) where subtle biases injected into a teacher model through minimal data manipulation are propagated as-is to the distilled student model [Source: [2505.24842] Cascading Adversarial Bias from Injection to China-Based Artificial Intelligence Companies Conducting ...](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24842]).

---

### Current Status: Big Tech and Governments Establishing Defensive Lines

As these threats intensify, the U.S. government and global big tech companies are moving toward comprehensive responses.

#### 1. Strong Policy Response from the White House and Government
At the government level, this has begun to be treated as a national security issue. On April 23, 2026, White House Memorandum NSTM-4 signed by Michael Kratsios officially addresses the issue of unauthorized and industrial-scale distillation of American AI models under the title **"Adversarial Distillation of American AI Models"** [Source: Distillation attack - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distillation_attack). 

In addition, according to joint U.S. government advisories and CISA (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency) reports released in September 2026, China-based AI companies are conducting industrial-scale knowledge distillation campaigns systematically extracting proprietary features and capabilities of U.S. AI models, which form the core of their AI development strategy [Source: China-Based Artificial Intelligence Companies Conducting ... | CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-251a). In Congress, legislative measures (e.g., the BLADE Act) are also being pursued to block large-scale adversarial distillation efforts, raising institutional defense barriers [Source: CRI2026 - BLOCKING LARGE-SCALE ADVERSARIAL... | GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CRI-2026/CRI-2026-CRI-2026-BLOCKING-LARGE-SCALE-ADVERSARIAL-D-24136C).

#### 2. Technological Defense Strategies by Big Tech Companies
Leading companies, including OpenAI, are going beyond mere legal responses to build technological defense systems. For example, OpenAI is actively defending against unauthorized capability extraction through a **layered approach combining model training, safeguards, detection, and enforcement** [Source: Combatting Adversarial Distillation](https://openai.com/index/combatting-adversarial-distillation/). Such technological measures include monitoring API usage patterns of AI models in real time to detect patterns where abnormally large amounts of knowledge are systematically collected, and applying strict access controls.

---

### What Lies Ahead?

The battle surrounding adversarial distillation is expected to intensify further. As AI models become more advanced, the "spear and shield battle" between defensive technologies protecting them and extraction technologies bypassing them will accelerate. 

However, one thing is clear: for AI technology to grow sustainably on public trust, clear rules and technical safety devices protecting intellectual property rights and security are essential. Regulatory legislation by governments, threat intelligence from security agencies, and multi-layered security defenses by corporations will interlock to act as a robust breakwater protecting the health of the AI ecosystem.

Only when companies' legitimate investments and research achievements are securely protected can even greater innovations beneficial to human society blossom.
