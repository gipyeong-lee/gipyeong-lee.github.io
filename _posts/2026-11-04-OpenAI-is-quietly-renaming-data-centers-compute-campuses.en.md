Imagine hearing news that a massive 'data center' is coming to your neighborhood. Doesn't an image like a factory, where countless servers run constantly consuming power and creating massive noise, pop into your head first? In fact, many local residents recently feel reluctant about the construction of these AI (artificial intelligence) infrastructure facilities and are voicing their opposition. [References 9](https://www.livemint.com/ai/openai-wants-to-rename-data-centres-as-ai-compute-campuses-amid-growing-public-backlash-11793596707984.html)

But what if we called this facility a 'Compute Campus' instead of a 'data center'? You might ask what difference a name change makes, but OpenAI, a giant in the artificial intelligence industry, is preparing just such a strategy.

## Why does this matter?

It may seem like just a name change, but there are complex political and social calculations surrounding the AI industry. Currently, large-scale infrastructure facilities to power artificial intelligence are facing opposition from residents across the United States, becoming the center of social controversy. [References 4](https://gizmodo.com/openai-would-reportedly-like-you-to-use-its-pleasant-new-term-for-data-centers-2000833570), [References 5](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025242/openai-quietly-renames-data-centers-as-compute-campuses)

OpenAI's attempt to change the terminology at this time is more than just a marketing move; it is intended to prepare for upcoming political shifts and regulatory environments. It is interpreted as part of a 'PR strategy' to build a more favorable image amidst a regulatory environment that may become even more demanding after the election. [References 6](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/01/openai-might-rebrand-its-data-centers-as-ai-compute-campuses/), [References 7](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/openai-wants-rebrand-data-centers-190500300.html)

## Easy to understand: What is a 'Compute Campus'?

To put it simply, it's like trying to transform a very old, dilapidated factory into a modern 'research complex.'

The term 'data center,' which we commonly use, strongly carries the image of a cold, stiff machine room that simply stores and processes data, like a 20th-century factory. On the other hand, the term 'Compute Campus' gives the impression of a space where intellectual and creative activities take place, much like a university campus or a research institute. [References 3](https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-ai-data-centers-rebrand-compute-campuses-midterms-2026-11), [References 8](https://true-tech.net/openai-rename-ai-data-centers-as-compute-campuses/)

As an analogy, just as a photo editing app has many filters, thousands of complex calculations are required behind artificial intelligence. A 'Compute Campus' is a cutting-edge complex where tens of thousands of computers are gathered for this calculation (compute), and OpenAI wants to emphasize that this space is not just a server room, but infrastructure that designs the future of humanity.

## Where do we stand?

OpenAI is currently preparing for this strategic name change and is showing moves to communicate more closely with Congress. In addition, the company is planning to expand consultations with residents to coexist with local communities where facilities are located, and intends to sign special agreements for the local community (Community Compacts). [References 1](https://forklog.com/en/openai-proposes-renaming-data-centers-to-ai-compute-campuses/), [References 10](https://aistockwire.com/blog/openai-ai-compute-campus-data-center-rebrand-midterms-november-2026)

Of course, there is also high criticism. Some point out that "instead of changing terms, they should first resolve residents' practical concerns, such as environmental problems or power consumption that occur during facility operations." [References 5](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025242/openai-quietly-renames-data-centers-as-compute-campuses)

## What is the next step?

After the election, it appears OpenAI will launch a full-scale public relations strategy. It won't be long before we hear the term 'AI Compute Campus' more often instead of the word 'data center.'

Whether this will end as a simple name change or truly settle as a new form of infrastructure that harmonizes with the local community remains to be seen. What do you, the readers, think? As the name changes, will it also bring positive changes to our lives?
