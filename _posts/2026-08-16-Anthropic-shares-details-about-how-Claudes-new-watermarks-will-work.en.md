## Now, 'invisible fingerprints' will be left on Claude's writing

Imagine this: one morning, you are reading a report sent by a colleague and a thought suddenly occurs to you. 'Did my colleague really write this, or did an AI write it?' Right now, there isn't a reliable way to verify such questions, but things are set to change in the future.

Anthropic has decided to leave 'invisible fingerprints' on all text and files generated by its AI model, Claude. Starting with Claude models released after August 2, 2026, special markers will be included in the results processed or created by the artificial intelligence. [Source: Anthropic shares more details about how Claude’s new watermarks will work](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/15/anthropic-shares-more-details-about-how-claudes-new-watermarks-will-work/)

## Why is this important?

The biggest reasons are 'trust' and 'accountability.' As AI technology has advanced rapidly, cases where AI-generated text or video is passed off as something made by a real human have been increasing. Especially with growing concerns over the misuse of AI in academic and professional settings, the European Union (EU) has established regulations under Article 50 of the EU AI Act to ensure that AI-generated content can be identified. [Source: Claude will begin digitally watermarking marking AI-generated text and images](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/claude-will-begin-digitally-watermarking-marking-ai-generated-text-and-images-anthropic-details-how-itll-comply-with-the-eus-artificial-intelligence-act)

Anthropic's latest move goes beyond simply complying with European rules; it embodies a commitment to transparently disclose the origin of AI content worldwide. This is expected to bring significant changes to fields like journalism, education, and content production, where the authenticity of information must be verified.

## Simple explanation: How do the 'invisible fingerprints' work?

The way Claude watermarks text is divided into two main categories.

First is the **text watermark**. Anthropic uses a method called 'SynthID-Text,' which was proposed by the Google DeepMind team in 2024. [Source: Anthropic shares more details about how Claude’s new watermarks will work](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/15/anthropic-shares-more-details-about-how-claudes-new-watermarks-will-work/)

It is easy to understand with an analogy. Just as we insert invisible digital watermarks into photos, this method 'weaves' extremely subtle variations directly into the text itself. This is different from metadata (supplementary information about a file). Metadata can be erased if you copy the file or rename it, but a watermark woven directly into the text remains largely intact even if you copy-paste the text or edit parts of it. [Source: Claude Invisible Watermarks — What They Detect (And Miss)](https://explainx.ai/blog/anthropic-claude-invisible-watermarks-c2pa-august-2026)

Second is the **file watermark**. For files like documents or images, it uses an international open standard called 'C2PA.' This method embeds a digital signature (provenance metadata) inside the file, documenting who created it and which tools were used. [Source: Anthropic says it will watermark text generated by its AI models](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-says-it-will-watermark-text-generated-by-its-ai-models/) Think of it as similar to stamping an official document.

## Current situation: How much can be verified?

Currently, Claude's watermarks are not visible to the naked eye of an average user. Anthropic explains that "an invisible watermark is woven into the text." [Source: Anthropic’s Claude will watermark AI-generated text. Here’s how it works](https://globalnews.ca/news/12018450/ai-anthropic-claude-watermark-text/)

However, there are points to note. Anthropic has clearly drawn a line, stating that this watermark does not 'definitively' prove AI authorship. In other words, if a watermark is found, it is accurate to understand it as a signal that it is highly likely Claude was involved in writing the text. [Source: Anthropic Will Watermark Claude-Generated Text and Files](https://www.eweek.com/news/anthropic-claude-watermarks-eu-ai-rules-emea/)

Additionally, Anthropic plans to release a 'watermark detection API' in the future so that anyone can check whether content was generated by AI. [Source: Anthropic shares more details about how Claude’s new watermarks will work](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/15/anthropic-shares-more-details-about-how-claudes-new-watermarks-will-work/)

## What will happen in the future?

This policy will be applied globally, and the feature cannot be turned off at the user's discretion. [Source: Anthropic puts hidden watermarks on Claude text under new EU rules](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/anthropic-claude-text-invisible-watermarks)

While there are voices of concern among some users regarding the fact that records will be left on AI-generated text, this can be viewed as a 'digital civilization' process for using AI more safely and responsibly in the long run. In the future, checking the 'AI source' of news, reports, or papers you read might become as natural a part of daily life as checking the ingredients label on food.
