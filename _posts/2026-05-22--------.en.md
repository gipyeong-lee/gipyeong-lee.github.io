Imagine this: You are just a few days away from paying for a family trip you've been saving for for months, or you have high-end electronics in your cart for a direct overseas purchase you've been eyeing. The difference in the financial burden we feel when the exchange rate is 1,300 KRW versus 1,500 KRW is literally like heaven and earth. This is because the price of a $1,000 item jumps from 1.3 million KRW to 1.5 million KRW in an instant. This terrifying exchange rate, which has been thinning our wallets and making us feel suffocated, is finally showing signs of retreating. What on earth is happening on the other side of the globe that is making our bank accounts sigh in relief?

Yesterday, on May 21, the USD/KRW exchange rate closed at 1506.1 KRW in the Seoul foreign exchange market, down 0.7 KRW from the previous day [[USD-KRW Exchange Rate Drops for Second Day on Peace Expectations… 1506.1 KRW - SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/article/20000311972)]. While it may seem like a small number—less than the price of a cup of coffee—it carries significant weight. It marks the second consecutive day of decline despite still being at a high level. Although it has held onto the excessively high "roof" of the 1,500 KRW range for five consecutive trading days based on the closing price, the endless upward trend has been halted, and clear signs of a mood shift have been detected [[USD-KRW Exchange Rate Drops for Second Day on Peace Expectations… 1506.1 KRW - SBS Biz](https://biz.sbs.co.kr/article/20000311972)].

The following day, May 22, the KRW/USD exchange rate continued its welcome downward trend, starting trade at 1504.7 KRW, down 1.4 KRW from the previous close [[KRW/USD Exchange Rate Starts at 1504.7 KRW, Down 1.4 KRW - Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/economy/2026/05/22/2026052209035725550), [KRW/USD Exchange Rate Starts at 1504.7 KRW, Down 1.4 KRW on Peace Expectations](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260522_0003640511)]. What exactly broke the terrifying momentum of the soaring exchange rate? At the core of this shift lies a powerful expectation for peace—the hope that a long-standing conflict might finally end and the war might stop.

## Why It Matters

The value of the money we use daily and the price of the food on our tables are not determined solely by our internal economic situation. Today's global economic system is tightly interwoven like a giant spiderweb, meaning a single line of news from across the world can deal an immediate and painful blow to our grocery bills and apartment loan interest rates.

The darkest cloud weighing down the global economy recently was the fear of physical conflict and escalation between the US and Iran. As tensions between the two nations reached a peak, the anxiety of global investors also hit a boiling point, like a pressure cooker about to explode [[[News] KRW/USD Exchange Rate Starts Down on Peace Expectations… UK Political Risk is a Variable...](https://blog.ai.dmomo.co.kr/news/26395)]. In a grim atmosphere where bombs could go off anytime, anywhere, the flow of money was bound to freeze up. However, a ray of light has recently emerged, signaling the end of this long, dark tunnel: news of concrete progress in peace negotiations between the US and Iran [[Pre-market News (05.19): Naver Blog](https://blog.naver.com/nottoday1111/224289833224)].

Just the news that dialogue is happening has triggered a remarkable chain reaction (domino effect) in the global economy. As investors' extreme anxiety thawed significantly, the phenomenon of everyone rushing to the safe-haven dollar decreased, strengthening downward pressure on the dollar. Consequently, the foreign exchange market is gradually finding stability, showing positive movement as the KRW/USD exchange rate turns downward from the low 1,500s [[KRW/USD Exchange Rate Starts Down on Peace Expectations… UK Political Risk is a Variable | Korea Economic Daily](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202605223466i), [Pre-market News (05.19): Naver Blog](https://blog.naver.com/nottoday1111/224289833224)].

The perspective of experts who analyze massive capital flows daily also clearly supports this. Min Kyung-won, a senior researcher at Woori Bank, diagnosed the cause of the intraday decline, stating, "Expectations for the conclusion of a Middle East peace agreement are expected to lead to a decline as financial market instability stemming from oil prices and interest rates calms down" [['US-Iran Peace' Truly Imminent? "Fluctuating in the Low 1,500s" [Exchange Rate Outlook]: Nate News](https://news.nate.com/view/20260522n04774)]. In simple terms, the peaceful news that dialogue, not weapons, is being exchanged is shifting the massive flow of funds in the real economy in a direction favorable to us.

## The Explainer

I will break down why complex numbers and dry foreign news matter to your life and why exchange rates and stock prices dance to the tune of Middle East peace news through three simple metaphors.

**First Metaphor: The Dollar is the Global Economy's 'Fortified Shelter.'**
When war or a major economic crisis strikes, people and giant corporations worldwide instinctively look for safe-haven assets that won't lose value. Currently, the currency of the world's greatest superpower, the US dollar, plays the role of this giant 'bunker.' When the risk of war was high due to the US-Iran conflict, investors scrambled to convert their assets into dollars. With too many people trying to enter the shelter and limited space, the 'entry fee'—the value of the dollar (exchange rate)—skyrocketed.
However, the situation flipped 180 degrees when news of progress in peace talks arrived. People began selling off their dollars and coming out, thinking, "There's no longer a need to hide in this cramped, expensive shelter (the dollar)." Since everyone is putting dollars back onto the market, its value (exchange rate) is naturally falling.

**Second Metaphor: Oil Prices are the 'Blood Pressure' of the Economic Ecosystem.**
The Middle East is the heart of the world economy, where the oil that keeps global industries running gushes out. When an atmosphere of unrest like war looms over this region, there are fears that oil transport routes will be blocked, causing oil prices to skyrocket. When oil prices rise, factory operating costs and truck delivery costs soar, which is directly passed on to consumers as higher prices on supermarket shelves (inflation). It's as if our entire economy's blood pressure has jumped to a level that risks a stroke.
However, as peace expectations spread, international oil prices in global markets dropped by 1.94% overnight in a sign of relief [[[2026.05.22] Dow Hits Record High — KOSPI Additional Rise vs. Profit Taking... (coinfeed)](https://www.coinfeed.co.kr/board/dailyissue/1628839)]. To use the metaphor, this is due to the certainty that if peace comes to the Middle East, oil will be supplied cheaply and without interruption. As oil prices fall, the grueling fear of inflation also diminishes [[[2026.05.22] Dow Hits Record High — KOSPI Additional Rise vs. Profit Taking... (coinfeed)](https://www.coinfeed.co.kr/board/dailyissue/1628839)], which ultimately serves as a positive signal that central banks may not need to raise interest rates further, bringing our economy's blood pressure back to normal.

**Third Metaphor: The Stock Market is a 'Spring' Pressed Down by a Heavy Rock.**
An uncertain and fearful future is like a giant rock sitting on the spring of the investment market. The moment the rock (fear of war) is removed, the spring (stock prices) releases all its pent-up elasticity and bounces high into the sky. As hopes for progress in peace negotiations significantly improved frozen investment sentiment [[[2026.05.22] Dow Hits Record High — KOSPI Additional Rise vs. Profit Taking... (coinfeed)](https://www.coinfeed.co.kr/board/dailyissue/1628839)], global stock markets entered a festive mood.
As a result, the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which tracks blue-chip companies, rose 0.55% in New York overnight to reach a new record high [[[2026.05.22] Dow Hits Record High — KOSPI Additional Rise vs. Profit Taking... (dogdrip)](https://www.dogdrip.net/703148172), [[2026.05.22] Dow Hits Record High — KOSPI Additional Rise vs. Profit Taking... (coinfeed)](https://www.coinfeed.co.kr/board/dailyissue/1628839)]. The reaction in the Korean stock market was even more explosive. As sentiment to willingly invest in risky but high-yield assets like stocks recovered quickly (recovery of risk-on sentiment) along with the falling exchange rate, the early market mood shifted dramatically [[KRW/USD Exchange Rate Drops Slightly on Peace Expectations, Remains in '1500s'... KOSPI Rebounds Sharply by 8%](https://www.betanews.net/article/view/beta202605210086)], and Korea's representative stock index, the KOSPI, even staged an incredible intraday rebound of over 8% [[KRW/USD Exchange Rate Drops Slightly on Peace Expectations, Remains in '1500s'... KOSPI Rebounds Sharply by 8%](https://www.betanews.net/article/view/beta202605210086)]. In fact, the previous day, it recorded its largest-ever gain of 8.42% [[[2026.05.22] Dow Hits Record High — KOSPI Additional Rise vs. Profit Taking... (coinfeed)](https://www.coinfeed.co.kr/board/dailyissue/1628839)]. It vividly demonstrated the amazing energy of a long-suppressed spring bouncing back all at once.

## Where We Stand

So, can we now fully breathe a sigh of relief and open our wallets? Experts cautiously warn that it is too early to pop the champagne. As is always the case in the world, variables are always lurking.

The main reason is that while the exchange rate has started to fall, it has retraced some of its intraday losses and remains at a very burdensome level in the 1,500 KRW range [[KRW/USD Exchange Rate Drops Slightly on Peace Expectations, Remains in '1500s'... KOSPI Rebounds Sharply by 8%](https://www.betanews.net/article/view/beta202605210086), ['US-Iran Peace' Truly Imminent? "Fluctuating in the Low 1,500s" [Exchange Rate Outlook]: Nate News](https://news.nate.com/view/20260522n04774)]. This means that while the initial suppression of the fiercest forest fire was successful, the embers remaining underground have not been completely extinguished.

On top of this, there is another unexpected 'hidden ambush' we must watch closely: 'UK political risk' coming from Europe [[KRW/USD Exchange Rate Starts Down on Peace Expectations… UK Political Risk is a Variable | Korea Economic Daily](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202605223466i)]. Despite the peaceful mood building in the Middle East, the political situation in the UK—one of the central pillars of the European economy—is very chaotic. Political instability in the UK can lower the value of the British Pound or the Euro, which in turn can create a 'butterfly effect' that drives global capital back toward the US dollar, with people thinking, 'At least the US dollar is safe.' In other words, as the big fire between the US and Iran subsides, the UK remains a new variable that could shake the foreign exchange market [[[News] KRW/USD Exchange Rate Starts Down on Peace Expectations… UK Political Risk is a Variable...](https://blog.ai.dmomo.co.kr/news/26395)].

Furthermore, a major schedule for the change of US economic leadership is attracting market attention. According to reports, Kevin Warsh, the nominee for Chair of the Federal Reserve (the US central bank), is expected to officially take office on May 22 local time [[Pre-market News (05.19): Naver Blog](https://blog.naver.com/nottoday1111/224289833224)]. What kind of interest rate policy the person often called the 'President of the World Economy' will pursue will be the most important milestone in determining where the world's money flows in the future.

## What's Next

From the perspective of a layperson, the two most important points to watch when opening your news feed every morning are:

First, we must watch whether the US-Iran peace negotiations go beyond mere 'expectations' and actually lead to a firm signature on a peace treaty. If a peace treaty is formalized and the war completely ends, the oil price instability that has plagued global prices will be fundamentally resolved, providing the powerful momentum needed to tear down the iron wall of the 1,500 KRW exchange rate.

Second, attention should be paid to when the UK political situation stabilizes and what statements the newly inaugurated US Fed Chair Kevin Warsh makes regarding interest rates [[KRW/USD Exchange Rate Starts Down on Peace Expectations… UK Political Risk is a Variable | Korea Economic Daily](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202605223466i), [Pre-market News (05.19): Naver Blog](https://blog.naver.com/nottoday1111/224289833224)]. This is because the price of the dollar could fluctuate again depending on how much the UK risk shakes the currency values of other countries and how the US Fed manipulates the strength of the dollar.

In conclusion, it is unlikely that a miracle will happen where the exchange rate magically drops back to the 1,100 KRW range in just a few days. However, the fact that the economic fear that seemed to only rise endlessly has clearly buckled in the face of the word 'peace' is undoubtedly a welcome and fortunate change for us.
