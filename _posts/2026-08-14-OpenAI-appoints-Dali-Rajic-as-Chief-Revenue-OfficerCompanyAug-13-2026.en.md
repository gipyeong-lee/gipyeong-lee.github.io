Imagine a landscape where the artificial intelligence (AI) services we use daily evolve beyond mere convenient tools to become the core business engines for countless companies worldwide. OpenAI has recently made a pivotal decision to turn this future into reality.

On August 13, 2026, OpenAI announced the appointment of Dali Rajic as its new Chief Revenue Officer (CRO) [Source 1, Source 6]. This is not merely a change in executive leadership; it is a signal that OpenAI is fundamentally transforming its constitution from a research-focused organization into a "revenue-generating corporation" that extends beyond technical growth.

### Why does this matter?

For the average user, the title of CRO might feel somewhat unfamiliar. Simply put, a CRO is the individual responsible for overseeing every process by which a company earns money. This appointment could significantly impact the form, pricing, and usage patterns of the AI services we will encounter in the future.

While OpenAI began as a research lab, it has since grown into a platform providing AI infrastructure to companies globally. When enterprises adopt technology, "cost-effectiveness" and "reliability" are paramount. The recruitment of Dali Rajic demonstrates OpenAI's intent to communicate in the "language of business" so that companies can confidently invest and integrate AI beyond mere technical explanations.

In short, OpenAI has entered a phase where, beyond simply creating intelligent AI models, it is perfecting the "revenue formula" to determine how efficiently it can monetize these models [Source 1, Source 6].

### A Metaphor: From 'Genius Chef' to 'Competent Manager'

Let’s compare this process to cooking. If OpenAI has been the "genius chef" focused on creating "secret recipes (technical development)" that produce the best flavor, it is now in a situation where the role of a "competent manager"—who worries about how to efficiently deliver this cuisine to people worldwide, manage the menu consistently, and increase the number of store locations—has become increasingly important.

The new commander, Dali Rajic, is an expert who previously led commercial operations and revenue growth as President and Chief Operating Officer (COO) at the cybersecurity firm Wiz [Source 2, Source 3]. Recruiting him is akin to inviting an expert gardener to ensure the well-grown trees (technology) bear more fruit (revenue). Notably, Rajic is a veteran who has long accumulated experience in market expansion within the cloud computing and enterprise software sectors [Source 3].

### Current Situation: OpenAI’s Rapidly Changing Leadership

This recruitment accompanies significant changes within OpenAI. Dali Rajic's predecessor, Denise Dresser, is leaving to pursue new opportunities after an approximately eight-month tenure [Source 6, Source 7]. Consequently, Rajic becomes OpenAI's second revenue chief in less than a year [Source 7].

These frequent changes highlight how rapidly OpenAI is currently revising its strategy. Industry insiders predict that OpenAI will soon debut on Wall Street (the stock market), and maximizing enterprise revenue appears to be the most pressing task in preparation for this [Source 6].

### What happens next?

Moving forward, we will likely see OpenAI's enterprise services become significantly more aggressive. Beyond just updating features, it is highly probable that optimized packages or new pricing policies tailored to corporate client requirements will be introduced.

Furthermore, the appointment of Dali Rajic indicates how seriously OpenAI is considering global market expansion. A key point to watch will be the changes this brings to the AI service environment that we, as individuals, use once the market expansion strategies he previously demonstrated are combined with OpenAI’s product suite [Source 1, Source 3].
