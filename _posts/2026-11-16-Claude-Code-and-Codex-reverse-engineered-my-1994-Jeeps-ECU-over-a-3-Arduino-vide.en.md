Imagine this: Your cherished car, which has been with you for a long time, repeatedly fails its emissions inspection. Even when you visit a repair shop, you only receive a cold response that diagnostic tools cannot even be plugged in because it's an older model over 30 years old. What would you do if you were faced with scrapping your car with no way to fix it?

Recently, a developer found an astonishing solution in this seemingly impossible situation [Source 8, Source 12]. To fix a 32-year-old vintage Jeep with no dedicated scan tool and no standard diagnostic port, they mobilized a tiny $3 computer and cutting-edge artificial intelligence (AI) coding tools to completely decode the vehicle's brain [Source 1, Source 9]. Let's explore the fascinating story of how AI, once considered merely a resident of the software world, deeply penetrated the realm of physical machinery.

## Why Does This Matter?

We are commonly accustomed to AI writing texts in chatbot windows or generating website code. However, the reason this case shocked the global tech community is that it proved AI's capabilities have expanded beyond monitor screens into the "physical world and legacy hardware" (older existing systems).

Today, countless aging mechanical devices, factory equipment, and vintage cars are left stranded without official support when they break down, making repairs daunting. If AI can independently analyze and communicate with proprietary communication standards or old computer codes that humans struggle to understand, countless obsolete devices in the world could gain new life. This project is a significant 21st-century event showing that AI is evolving beyond a simple writing assistant into a powerful problem solver tackling complex physical problems in real life.

## Easy to Understand: When a $3 Chip Meets AI

To help you understand, let's use an everyday analogy. The engine computer of a 1994 Jeep Cherokee is like **"an ancient ruin's stone tablet that communicates only in old ciphers"** [Source 1, Source 9]. Modern repair shop scan tools can only speak the latest languages and thus cannot read this tablet. 

Here, developer Muhammad Zulali used a **$3 Arduino (a small microcontroller board)** [Source 1, Source 9]. The Arduino acted like **"a translator earpiece connecting the ancient stone tablet to a modern laptop."** However, having an earpiece alone doesn't mean you instantly understand an ancient language. A genius linguist was needed to interpret the ciphers inscribed on the tablet. 

That exact role was played by Claude Code (an AI agent that analyzes and writes code in a terminal environment) and OpenAI's Codex [Source 1, Source 9]. The developer wired the Arduino directly to the vehicle's engine computer (ECU, Engine Control Unit) and fed real-time data streams collected from it to the AI coding agents [Source 1, Source 9]. Like a cryptographer instantly reading tens of thousands of decryption books, the AI traced back the rules of the signals emitted by the vehicle and figured out the program structure.

This process is akin to **"perfectly mastering the grammar of an entirely unknown extraterrestrial script in just a few days through massive data analysis."**

## Current Status: A 32-Year-Old Jeep and a Week-Long Challenge

The protagonist Muhammad Zulali's vehicle was a 1994 Jeep Cherokee 4.0L model, which lacked any standardized OBD2 (on-board diagnostics standard port) used today [Source 1, Source 9]. Furthermore, even older diagnostic equipment capable of communicating with the car failed to work, creating a frustrating situation where it repeatedly failed California smog inspections [Source 1, Source 9, Source 12].

To solve the problem, he embarked on a week-long journey [Source 9, Source 12]. The specific process was as follows:
1. **Hardware Connection**: Physically connected a $3 Arduino microcontroller directly to the vehicle's engine computer, accessing the communication lines (OBD1 and SCI serial communication methods) [Source 1, Source 9].
2. **Data Collection and Logging**: Built a drive log that reads and records real-time sensor data while driving the vehicle [Source 9].
3. **AI-Based Analysis**: Deployed Claude Code and Codex agents to analyze the collected data and the internal program of the engine computer, executing code directly to reverse-engineer (the process of dismantling a finished product to inversely understand its design principles and structure) the vehicle's communication protocol [Source 1, Source 9].

This dramatic week-long journey was revealed in a detailed technical video on YouTube on November 10, 2026, generating massive buzz [Source 9].
