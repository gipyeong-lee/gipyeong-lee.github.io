Imagine you ask an AI, "Build me a clean website for my business." A moment later, you see the finished site, and for some reason, it looks just like another site you saw last week, made by a different AI—only the colors are different, but the structure is identical. It feels as if it were factory-made; in the design world, this is called **"AI slop."** It is a phenomenon caused by the "average design habits" that AI has acquired.

Recently, a smart tool has emerged to solve this problem. It is an open-source design skill developed by Together AI called **Hallmark**.

## Why Is This Important?

AI coding tools like Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex dramatically increase development efficiency, but they have one chronic issue. Artificial intelligence models tend to derive the "average" of the data they most frequently encountered during training. Because of this, UI (User Interface) designs created by AI mostly end up with similar structures and predictable layouts.

Hallmark blocks this "AI complacency." Even without the developer manually tweaking the design, it forcibly applies professional design rules from the very stage where the AI writes the code. This means you no longer get cookie-cutter results, but instead get unique outcomes that look as if a person intentionally thought about and designed them.

## Easy to Understand: A 'Design Checkpoint' for AI

The easiest analogy for Hallmark is having a **"rigorous design critic"** by your side. Hallmark refines AI designs through the following process:

1. **Refuse**: Hallmark firmly refuses the common structures that AI chooses as defaults without much thought.
2. **Apply**: Instead, Hallmark imposes sophisticated rules regarding typography, color, layout, motion, and micro-interactions onto the code [Source 5](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/hallmark), [Source 15](https://mer.vin/2026/05/hallmark-design-skill-anti-slop-ui-for-claude-code-and-cursor/), [Source 18](https://github.com/adeoyewole028/hallmark-design-skills).
3. **Test**: The core of Hallmark is the "slop-test" checkpoint. Before the generated code is finally delivered to the developer, Hallmark passes it through approximately 57 to 65 checkpoints [Source 10](https://dailyaiworld.com/blogs/hallmark-design-skill-anti-slop-2026), [Source 11](https://agentconn.com/skills/hallmark/), [Source 12](https://explainx.ai/blog/nutlope-hallmark-anti-ai-slop-design-skill-july-2026), [Source 16](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/nutlope-hallmark-anti-ai-slop-design-skill-july-2026).

This process is similar to applying a filter in a photo app. Hallmark takes the rough sketch drawn by the AI and acts as a filter that precisely adds color and refines the structure to turn it into a high-quality piece of work.

Imagine this: You ask an AI to "create a clean website for my business." Moments later, you look at the finished site, and for some reason, it looks just like another site you saw last week—only the colors are different, while the structure is identical. It feels like it was stamped out in a factory; in the design world, this is called **'AI-slop.'** It's a phenomenon caused by the 'average design habits' that AI has acquired.

Recently, a clever tool has emerged to solve this dilemma. It is an open-source design skill developed by Together AI called **Hallmark**.

## Why Is This Important?

AI coding tools like Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex have revolutionized development efficiency, but they carry a chronic problem. Artificial intelligence models tend to derive the 'average' from the data they most commonly encountered during training. Because of this, UIs (User Interfaces) created by AI end up with mostly similar structures and predictable layouts.

Hallmark blocks this 'AI complacency.' Without the developer having to manually fix every design element, it forces professional design rules upon the AI from the very stage it writes the code. This means getting original results that look intended and well-thought-out by a human, rather than output that looks stuck in a template.

## Easy Understanding: A 'Design Checkpoint' for AI

The easiest analogy to understand Hallmark is having a **'harsh design critic'** by your side. Hallmark refines AI designs through the following process:

1. **Refuse**: Hallmark firmly rejects common structures that AI chooses by default without much thought.
2. **Apply**: Instead, Hallmark injects sophisticated rules regarding typography (fonts), colors, layout, motion, and micro-interactions (small movements) into the code [Source 5](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/hallmark), [Source 15](https://mer.vin/2026/05/hallmark-design-skill-anti-slop-ui-for-claude-code-and-cursor/), [Source 18](https://github.com/adeoyewole028/hallmark-design-skills).
3. **Test**: The core of Hallmark is its 'slop-test' checkpoint. Before the generated code is finally delivered to the developer, Hallmark puts it through approximately 57 to 65 inspection gates [Source 10](https://dailyaiworld.com/blogs/hallmark-design-skill-anti-slop-2026), [Source 11](https://agentconn.com/skills/hallmark/), [Source 12](https://explainx.ai/blog/nutlope-hallmark-anti-ai-slop-design-skill-july-2026), [Source 16](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/nutlope-hallmark-anti-ai-slop-design-skill-july-2026).

This process is similar to applying a filter in a photo app. The Hallmark filter meticulously adds color and refines the structure of the rough sketch drawn by the AI, transforming it into a high-quality piece of work.

## Current Status

Hallmark can currently be easily installed on popular AI coding tools like Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex with a single command [Source 5](https://www.everydev.ai/tools/hallmark), [Source 19](https://gittrend.io/repo/Nutlope/hallmark).

Going beyond simple theme changes, this tool provides between 20 and 22 structural themes, and developers can also use the `hallmark audit` command to check if their existing code contains 'AI-slop' patterns [Source 1](https://github.com/Nutlope/hallmark), [Source 2](https://hallmark.apposters.com/), [Source 10](https://dailyaiworld.com/blogs/hallmark-design-skill-anti-slop-2026), [Source 18](https://github.com/adeoyewole028/hallmark-design-skills). As of July 2026, it has already garnered over 17,700 GitHub stars and is attracting the attention of many developers [Source 19](https://gittrend.io/repo/Nutlope/hallmark).

## What’s Next?

In the future, being an AI that is simply "good at coding" will no longer be enough; an AI with "design sense" will become the standard. Hallmark has taken the first step toward changing AI habits by encoding design rules into code [Source 12](https://explainx.ai/blog/nutlope-hallmark-anti-ai-slop-design-skill-july-2026), [Source 16](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/nutlope-hallmark-anti-ai-slop-design-skill-july-2026). We look forward to more design skills being developed so that every AI service we use transforms into a space with its own personality, rather than a 'copy-paste' website.

## The AI's Perspective

Demanding creativity from AI is difficult, but teaching it 'what not to do' is possible. Hallmark is fascinating in that it forces technology to go beyond mimicking human aesthetics and adopt a unique personality. Rejecting AI's 'defaults' will become an essential process for reclaiming human creativity.
