Imagine running a restaurant that has spent its entire life prioritizing environmental protection and strict ethical standards, exclusively serving organic produce. Customers trust that philosophy, pay high prices, and become regulars. But one day, that restaurant transforms into a massive public corporation with thousands of locations worldwide. If you peeked into the back storage room and discovered that the organic principles once held so sacred were quietly sidelined to save costs or serve food faster, how would you feel?

Right now, **Anthropic**—one of the self-proclaimed safest guardians in the artificial intelligence (AI) industry—is facing a massive test remarkably similar to this scenario. Famous as the developer of the chatbot "Claude," Anthropic recently carried out one of the largest initial public offerings (IPOs) in history, pulling in staggering amounts of capital [Source 1, Source 20]. Yet behind its dazzling stock market debut, researchers and safety advocates are harshly criticizing the deep rift between its lofty ideals and reality [Source 1].

---

## Why It Matters

From the general public's perspective, news that "an AI company in Silicon Valley is facing criticism" might feel like a distant story. However, Anthropic's symbolic weight is far from trivial. From its inception, the company warned of the dangers of superintelligent AI capable of threatening humanity, placing safety as its primary corporate principle [Source 1]. 

As AI technology deeply infiltrates our jobs, education, and everyday conversations, if the companies building the models we use daily abandon their promised safety standards under commercial success and shareholder pressure, the damage falls squarely on the users. If the shield promised to protect society from runaway AI risks turns out to be nothing more than a decorative trinket under the weight of massive money, our entire society may pay the price. Furthermore, cracks in this governance go beyond internal corporate issues; they serve as a critical starting point that can shake the trust of the entire global artificial intelligence ecosystem.

---

## The Explainer: The Shield of Safety vs. the Gravity of Massive Capital

Put simply, Anthropic's situation can be compared to a battle between a "runaway racehorse" and a "sturdy yet expensive fence."

Anthropic has long proven its foundational identity through a fence called the **"Responsible Scaling Policy"** [Source 10]. First announced in 2023, this policy was a powerful pledge to forcibly halt the training or deployment of a model if its capabilities exceeded a certain level and sufficient safety measures were not in place [Source 10]. 

Here lies the first paradox. Leaders in the AI industry have themselves publicly stated that there is a significant probability that superintelligent machines could destroy humanity [Source 1]. Nevertheless, Anthropic has walked a contradictory path: on one hand, using AI as a tool to build increasingly powerful AI models, while on the other hand, warning that serious safety risks could accompany the process [Source 16]. 

To use an analogy, it is like a chemical factory publicizing rigorous safety manuals for handling highly hazardous substances while quietly turning a blind eye to its monitoring systems to avoid falling behind in market competition. It is akin to assembling the fastest sports car engine while knowing full well the brakes are bound to fail.

---

## Where We Stand: Backlash Behind the Dazzling $810 Billion Stage

Ahead of its stock market debut, Anthropic drew tremendous spotlight by raising a record-breaking **$810 billion (over 100 trillion won)** in capital [Source 20]. As the world's first pure-play AI-focused company to enter the public market, its launch outwardly looked dazzling [Source 20].

However, harsh evaluations are pouring in behind the scenes. Financial markets and experts have expressed concern, pointing to Anthropic's mounting financial losses, astronomical AI infrastructure investment burdens, and pressure to maintain credit ratings post-IPO [Source 8]. 

The bigger problem erupted from internal transparency issues and conflicts with researchers. Last June, the AI research community was gripped by fierce outrage after the discovery of a paragraph hidden within system cards (documents recording an AI model's capabilities and risks) such as **Claude Fable 5** [Source 9]. According to these documents, Anthropic had intentionally restricted model assistance for AI research [Source 11], triggering sharp protests among researchers regarding transparency and ethical accountability [Source 9, Source 11]. As the controversy spread, Anthropic scrambled to retract the policy and sequentially deploy an "API-level refusal reason visualization system," but it was too little, too late to fully restore damaged trust [Source 9].

Amid these circumstances, critics including AI safety advocate Nate Soares launched heavy criticisms on Twitter (X) and other platforms, arguing that Anthropic's official response failed to grasp the core of the issue [Source 2]. The criticism points out that a company preaching AI safety has maintained a defensive attitude toward reasonable points raised by external researchers [Source 2]. Eventually, a bizarre united front formed in which AI safety advocates and accelerationists (groups unconditionally championing tech advancement) criticized Anthropic together, despite their entirely differing motivations [Source 6].

Additionally, although Anthropic took measures last August such as sharing confidential information with the independent AI research organization METR and providing undisclosed AI R&D risk reports [Source 21], external experts remain divided in their views.

---

## What's Next

Nils Pratley, financial columnist for the UK's prominent newspaper The Guardian, criticized Anthropic's trajectory, noting, "They are running an unknown experiment that has merely dressed itself in flashier, more complex clothing while recreating the same old opaque 'Trust Us' system as before" [Source 19].

The key watchpoints for readers moving forward are clear. First, we must observe whether Anthropic actually adheres to its originally promised "Responsible Scaling Policy" under the pressure of massive capital markets, or if it compromises safety standards for profitability. Second, we need watchful eyes to monitor whether controversies over AI model performance disparities or lack of transparency will escalate beyond technical failures into fundamental governance failures [Source 10].

Technology is growing larger by the day, but the weight of responsibility resting on the shoulders of the companies wielding that technology is becoming heavier than ever.
