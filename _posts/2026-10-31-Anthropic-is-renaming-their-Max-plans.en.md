Imagine this: You sign up for a 'premium unlimited' membership, paying a hefty subscription fee of over 200,000 KRW (approx. $150) per month. But every time you try to use a core feature, you get a message saying, "You have exceeded your usage limit for today." How would you feel? Recently, user frustration surrounding the AI company Anthropic has been mounting. Beyond the simple question of service quality, a deeper doubt—whether we are actually receiving value commensurate with the money we pay—is now being taken to court.

### Why does this matter?

AI technology has moved beyond the lab to become an essential partner in our work. In this context, AI service pricing is akin to the ticket price for the 'transportation' we use daily. If you buy a specific ticket only to find you can travel far less than expected, you are deprived of the ability to make a rational choice. This incident serves as a significant case study on what value premium plans should actually provide to users and how transparent companies must be when promoting and operating their services.

### In simple terms: Shall we compare it to a 'buffet'?

Let’s compare this situation to a local buffet. Think of the 'Max Plan' as a 'premium unlimited buffet' that charges a very high admission fee per person. But once you get inside, you find that despite paying a premium price, you are restricted to only one plate a day for the most popular menu items. How ridiculous would that be?

Users are expressing their frustration, saying, "I came here because it was marketed as premium and unlimited, but in reality, there’s not much difference from the basic service." Experts analyze that it is highly likely this 'Max Plan' was not a sustainable model for Anthropic, either technically or economically [Source: [Is Anthropic Killing Max Plan? Here's What I Found | Nidzam](https://nidzam.com/blog/is-anthropic-killing-max-plan-here-s-what-i-found-2)]. Operating AI models incurs massive computing costs; if too many users try to enjoy premium benefits, the structure could actually result in losses for the company.

### Current situation: What is the problem?

Anthropic currently operates various subscription tiers, including Free, Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, and Enterprise [Source: [Plans & pricing | Claude by Anthropic](https://claude.com/pricing)]. Recently, a class-action lawsuit was filed in federal court regarding the Max plan, one of the top-tier individual subscriptions [Source: [BREAKING: Anthropic is facing a federal class-action lawsuit...](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/guillermoflor_breaking-anthropic-is-facing-a-federal-class-action-activity-7472336453574803456-x3JA)].

The core of the lawsuit is that plans like 'Max 5x' and 'Max 20x' were marketed in a way that overstated actual usage capacity. Users argue that despite paying fees reaching up to $200 (approx. 270,000 KRW) per month, overly tight usage limits are still being applied [Source: [Anthropic Hit With Lawsuit Over Its Claude Max Usage Limits](https://www.engadget.com/2194626/anthropic-hit-with-lawsuit-over-its-claude-max-usage-limits/)].

Of course, Anthropic hasn't been standing still. Last July, they made minor adjustments, such as doubling the usage limits for Pro and Max plan users over a 5-hour window and easing rate limits during peak hours [Source: [Anthropic is not Raising Useage Limits for Most Users](https://maganda.tech/blog/anthropic-is-not-raising-useage-limits-for-most-users/)]. However, the prevailing sentiment is that these measures were insufficient to appease angry users.

### What happens next?

Anthropic is currently preparing for an Initial Public Offering (IPO) and is eyeing a larger market [Source: [AI giant Anthropic files for IPO, plans Nasdaq listing... - India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/business/story/ai-giant-anthropic-files-for-ipo-to-list-its-stock-on-nasdaq-under-ant-symbol-3025483-2026-10-26)]. Transparency and the establishment of a stable revenue model are paramount during the listing process. Therefore, it is highly likely that this Max plan controversy will be resolved by Anthropic either completely overhauling its pricing structure or rebranding and transitioning to new service offerings.

We must keep a close watch on whether AI companies will more clearly announce actual usage caps hidden behind marketing buzzwords like 'unlimited.' From a user's perspective, choosing the ticket (pricing plan) that fits me perfectly is one of the most important survival strategies in the AI era.
