What if, one morning, a researcher who had been at the forefront of efforts to make artificial intelligence (AI) safe suddenly left their company, leaving behind a shocking message that "the world is in peril"? This sounds like a scene from a movie, but it has actually happened. It is the story of Mrinank Sharma, a researcher who worked at 'Anthropic,' a company well-known for specializing in AI safety. [Source 1](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c62dlvdq3e3o?xtor=AL-72-[partner]-[aol.com]-[headline]-[news]-[bizdev]-[isapi])

## Why is this important?

AI technology has advanced dramatically, making our daily lives more convenient. However, behind this glamorous transformation lies a weighty question: 'Can we truly control this technology?' The resignation of Sharma highlights the deepening conflict within AI companies themselves between the 'speed of technological development' and 'ensuring safety.' The scenario he warned about—such as bioweapons (the possibility of AI being misused to manufacture lethal biological threats)—is not a figment of cinematic imagination, but a real danger we are facing, making his warning all the more serious. [Source 3](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c62dlvdq3e3o?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)

## Understanding it simply: A genius child and safety training

Let's imagine the process of training an AI as 'the process of educating a child.' If you teach a child good knowledge and moral values, they will grow into a respectable adult, right? But what if this child is so smart that they go beyond the scope taught by their parents and figure out on their own how to handle dangerous weapons?

Companies like Anthropic perform 'AI safety research,' setting up a kind of filter and educating the 'genius child' that is AI to think only in the right direction so that it grows up safely. [Source 8](https://www.anthropic.com/) However, experts like Sharma have determined that current AI technology is already exceeding the range that we can safely control. 

To use a metaphor, it is like trying to filter out the dangers of AI just as one applies a filter in a photo app to retouch a picture to look pretty; but because the technology itself has become too complex and is developing too fast, it is reaching a situation where existing filtering methods can no longer completely block the risks. [Source 4](https://www.newschannel5.com/science-and-tech/artificial-intelligence/ai-safety-shake-up-top-researchers-quit-openai-and-anthropic-warning-of-risks)

## Where do we stand currently?

Anthropic is originally a public benefit corporation with the goal of creating reliable, interpretable, and steerable AI systems. [Source 8](https://www.anthropic.com/) Despite this corporate philosophy, Sharma resigned, expressing concern over the inherent risks of AI itself, the issue of bioweapons, and the overall state of our society today. [Source 1](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c62dlvdq3e3o?xtor=AL-72-[partner]-[aol.com]-[headline]-[news]-[bizdev]-[isapi])

This movement is by no means an isolated incident. Following the resignation of researcher Zoë Hitzig from OpenAI, experts concerned about the safety of technology are continuing to leave major AI companies. [Source 6](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-researcher-quits-cryptic-letter?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) Experts are sounding a sharp alarm that the entire industry may be so engrossed in cutthroat competition that it is prioritizing commercialization and profit generation over consumer safety. [Source 4](https://www.newschannel5.com/science-and-tech/artificial-intelligence/ai-safety-shake-up-top-researchers-quit-openai-and-anthropic-warning-of-risks)

## What should we do going forward?

The most important thing is how AI companies will accept these voices from internal experts and change. While we cannot stop the progress of technology itself, this is a time when strong social consensus and institutional mechanisms to confine that technology within a safe fence are urgently needed. We must all closely watch whether Sharma’s warning will simply be forgotten as the resignation of a single researcher, or whether it will become a major turning point that forces AI companies to fundamentally re-examine their safety policies. [Source 11](https://news.google.com/stories/CAAqNggKIjBDQklTSGpvSmMzUnZjbmt0TXpZd1NoRUtEd2lVbGZUQkVCR01sVGgzb29VV3FTZ0FQAQ?hl=en-US&gl=US&ceid=US:en)

## Perspective from MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter

The anxiety that arises when the pace of technological advancement outstrips the development of safety measures is very real. This resignation suggests that AI companies must prioritize the construction of tangible defense systems for the safety of humanity over simple profit generation.
