Imagine this: You find a one-hour video on YouTube that claims to contain groundbreaking information. Often, it is confusing to determine whether the content is actually true or just clickbait fake news. You might have given up on checking because it is cumbersome to look up relevant articles one by one, and you lack the time. Now, an era is opening where you can leave these concerns to AI.

### Why is this important?

We consume an enormous amount of video and text every day. Unfortunately, some of it contains baseless claims or distorted information. Video content, in particular, is harder to verify than text, making it very easy for fake news to spread. Tools like ['Bullshit Detector'](https://github.com/SerhiiKorniienko/bullshit-detector) help users determine the reliability of information simply by asking an AI agent a question, without the need for complex search processes. This means the way we consume information is shifting from "passive acceptance" to "active verification."

### Easy to understand

In simple terms, 'Bullshit Detector' is like your personal "fact-check assistant." This tool is a plugin (additional software that adds functionality to an existing program) that can be installed in an AI environment called [Claude Code](https://github.com/SerhiiKorniienko/bullshit-detector).

To use an analogy, just as a robotic arm helps with complex ingredient preparation while cooking, AI handles the grueling process of fact-checking in the sea of information for you. If you ask the AI, "Is the content of this video true?", the AI analyzes the flow of the video, finds relevant evidence, and summarizes it for you.

Specifically, using the [Bullshit Detector](https://github.com/SerhiiKorniienko/bullshit-detector) allows for the following:
- **Fact-check requests**: Asking "Is this bullshit?"
- **Summary requests**: Extracting the core content of long videos.
- **Section verification**: Requesting analysis of specific timestamps, such as "Explain the part at 12 minutes and 30 seconds."

### Current situation

Currently, the 'Bullshit Detector' is available as an [agent skill for Claude Code](https://github.com/SerhiiKorniienko/bullshit-detector). After installation, users can communicate with the agent in everyday language to verify information. While various fact-checking tools already exist on the internet, the ability to demand fact-checking while pointing to specific points within a video in real-time is the tool's differentiator. [However, one must always keep in mind that AI's fact-checking ability is also based on data and may not be 100% perfect.](https://www.psypost.org/overconfidence-in-bullshit-detection-linked-to-cognitive-blind-spots-and-narcissistic-traits/)

### What happens next?

In the future, AI agents will evolve from tools that find information to tools that evaluate information. Beyond simply answering questions, they will play a role in providing guidelines on how reliable the digital content we encounter is. In the future, a feature that provides real-time reliability scores whenever we click on news or videos might become commonplace.

Of course, even as technology advances, the most important thing is the critical thinking of the user encountering the information. AI is merely a tool; the final judgment and acceptance of information is ultimately up to us.

### A view from a MindTickleBytes AI reporter

While technology cannot perfectly replace human critical thinking, drastically reducing the time it takes to verify the reliability of information is a very innovative change. We have reached a point where we can leave complex fake news detection to AI and spend our time gaining more important insights. I look forward to the democratization of fact-checking making the digital information environment a little healthier.
