## Why It Matters

When we tell AI, "Change me in this photo to be standing in front of the Eiffel Tower in Paris," it's hard to call it true editing if the AI draws 'someone else' who looks like me. The reason this update is revolutionary is because of its ability to maintain **likeness** (original appearance and characteristics). Simply put, it's now possible to change only the background while recognizing 'me' as 'me'.

## The Explainer: The Magic of 'Nano Banana'

The star of this update is the **'Gemini 2.5 Flash Image'** model developed by Google DeepMind. Among developers, it is known by the fun nickname **'Nano Banana'**. [Gemini 2.5 Flash Image: The Big AI Image Editing Leap (2025)](https://tech-now.io/en/blogs/gemini-photo-editor-gemini-2-5-flash-image-the-big-ai-image-editing-leap-august-2025)

To use an analogy, this model is like a 'genius painter' who observes the subject in the photo very precisely and perfectly remembers those characteristics as data. 

While existing AI was at the level of taking rough notes of a person's features and drawing a similar picture, 'Nano Banana' understands the facial structure, eye shape, and nose angle of that person in three dimensions. It's not like peeling off a transparent character sticker and sticking it on another background; it's like changing the clothes and moving the location while keeping the person's bone structure and skin tone. So, when we order "Put this person in a spacesuit," it perfectly synthesizes the outfit and background while leaving the person's unique impression intact. [Introducing Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, our state-of-the-art image model - Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-gemini-2-5-flash-image/)

Google confidently explained that Gemini "will keep their appearance the same across every image, even if the person is placed in a new scenario." [Image editing in Gemini just got a major upgrade - Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/image-editing-in-gemini-just-got-a-major-upgrade/) This is the same principle as an actor on a movie set changing clothes and moving to a different set while keeping their facial makeup the same.
