Therefore, it is perfectly normal for hallucinatory phenomena to appear completely different for each person. One person might see a giant dragon flying in the sky, another might recall their old childhood home, and someone else might see geometric patterns. 

However, this specific mushroom goes beyond simply making people experience hallucinations; it forcibly unifies the content of those hallucinations into "little people." The very fact that a mushroom, a type of fungus, makes people see a distinct "human figure" is shocking in itself, but scientists completely fail to understand the fundamental mechanism of exactly "why" our brains exhibit the exact same reaction only to this particular substance [The Strange Mushroom That Makes You See Tiny People](https://www.sciencing.com/2087951/mushroom-makes-you-see-tiny-people/). 

Furthermore, people from different cultures inevitably imagine fairies or forms of fantasy differently. But this mushroom easily leaps over even such massive cultural barriers. ## The Explainer

According to persistent investigative research by scientists, the culprit behind these fairy-tale-like hallucinations is a red-hued mushroom with the scientific name **"Lanmaoa asiatica"** [Colin Domnauer – The mushroom making people hallucinate dozens of tiny humans - School of Biological Sciences](https://www.biology.utah.edu/news/colin-domnauer-the-mushroom-making-people-hallucinate-dozens-of-tiny-humans/). This unique mushroom does not grow lonely on its own; it thrives by forming a symbiotic relationship with the majestic pine trees of the nearby forest, exchanging nutrients with them in the soil. 

Moreover, this mushroom is quite excellent from a gastronomic perspective. Locally, it has long been loved as a popular, high-quality ingredient rich in a deep umami flavor and a chewy, meat-like texture [Colin Domnauer – The mushroom making people hallucinate dozens of tiny humans - School of Biological Sciences](https://www.biology.utah.edu/news/colin-domnauer-the-mushroom-making-people-hallucinate-dozens-of-tiny-humans/).

The scientific and medical communities formally refer to the symptoms experienced by people who consume this mushroom as **"Lilliputian hallucinations."** This unique name is taken directly from the Lilliputians, the tiny people featured in the famous classic novel "Gulliver's Travels" [Little people hallucinations: 5 Questions with mushroom ...](https://themicrodose.substack.com/p/little-people-hallucinations-5-questions). Interestingly, locals in China also call this phenomenon by a very intuitive name. Although the mushroom itself is called "jian shou qing" due to its unique toxic reaction [Little people hallucinations: 5 Questions with mushroom ...](https://themicrodose.substack.com/p/little-people-hallucinations-5-questions), the little people that this mushroom summons before people's eyes are called **"xiao ren ren (小人人),"** a Chinese term that literally means "little people" [Hallucinogenic bolete mushroom - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinogenic_bolete_mushroom). 

At this point, you might reasonably wonder: "Isn't this just a common magic mushroom that often appears in the news?" The answer is "No." The most intriguing fact that excites scientists is that the active compound causing hallucinations in this mushroom is **absolutely not "psilocybin,"** the hallucinogenic ingredient typical of magic mushrooms [Meet The Mushroom That Makes People Have The Exact Same Hallucination](https://www.vice.com/en/article/meet-the-mushroom-that-make-people-have-the-exact-same-hallucination/). Magic mushrooms containing psilocybin and this fairy mushroom have fundamentally different mechanisms for how their switches tap into the brain's neural networks.

Experts clearly distinguish this dramatic difference as the distinction between psychedelics and deliriants. Typically, when taking a common psychedelic like psilocybin, the user can rationally grasp that the bizarre sights or distorted world before their eyes are not actual reality, or at least recognize that the current situation is somehow abnormal [A mushroom that makes people have the exact same hallucination | Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47918657). On the other hand, when ingesting a substance classified as a "deliriant," like this fairy mushroom, even if the user clearly remembers just having eaten some strange mushroom, they will firmly believe the tiny people before their eyes to be a "perfect, unquestionable reality" [A mushroom that makes people have the exact same hallucination | Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47918657). 

To put it metaphorically: **What if typical psychedelics were like "wearing a state-of-the-art 3D virtual reality (VR) headset and watching a thrilling movie"?** Even if a terrifying dinosaur suddenly appeared right in front of you, a corner of your brain would know that you are safely sitting on your living room sofa wearing a headset, so you would remain still. In contrast, **the state of delirium caused by this mushroom is like "a state of sleepwalking deep within a dream, completely unaware that you are even dreaming."** Our brains accept the illusion as 100% truth and act on it—to the extent that one might earnestly talk to the fairies appearing on the real-world dining table or try to tear off pieces of bread to share with them.

## The Web of Mystery Spreading Across the World

For a long time, this bizarre phenomenon was merely considered a local strange tale occurring only in the rugged mountain ranges of China's Yunnan Province, or just old stories told by grandmothers. It was only relatively recently that its true nature was officially reported to the Western scientific community. However, as persistent biological research progressed, this mysterious mushroom surprisingly began to emerge in vastly different environments and continents around the world. And it was newly revealed that, no matter what region it is found in, it bestows the exact same "fairy illusion" upon those who eat it ['They saw them on their dishes when eating': The mushroom ...](https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20260121-the-mysterious-mushroom-that-makes-you-see-tiny-people).

The person who made a decisive contribution to fitting together the key pieces of this complex puzzle is a tenacious researcher named Colin Domnauer [Colin Domnauer – The mushroom making people hallucinate dozens of tiny humans - School of Biological Sciences](https://www.biology.utah.edu/news/colin-domnauer-the-mushroom-making-people-hallucinate-dozens-of-tiny-humans/). One day, while conducting biological research in the Philippines, he discovered a peculiar mushroom called "Sedesdem" among the locals. Although its appearance was completely different from the red mushroom growing in China, he shockingly heard bizarre rumors that this Sedesdem mushroom also caused vivid hallucinations of seeing "little fairies" for the people in the Philippines [Mushroom causes fairytale-like hallucinations – @theU](https://attheu.utah.edu/science-technology/mushroom-causes-fairytale-like-hallucinations/).

How on earth could a tropical mushroom in the Philippines with a completely different appearance work the exact same magic as a mushroom from the high-altitude regions of China? Domnauer uncovered an unbelievable fact through the latest genetic (DNA) analysis technology. He recalled the shock of that moment as follows: 

> "What surprised me the most was when I unexpectedly discovered the genetic identity of this Sedesdem mushroom. The DNA test results revealed that this mushroom was none other than **'Lanmaoa asiatica,'** a species biologically perfectly identical to the one found in China's Yunnan Province thousands of kilometers away. It was the exhilarating moment when the puzzle pieces of a mystery scattered across the globe finally fit together." [Experts Explore New Mushroom Which Causes Fairytale-Like Hallucinations | Natural History Museum of Utah](https://nhmu.utah.edu/articles/experts-explore-new-mushroom-which-causes-fairytale-hallucinations).

Although its appearance had slightly changed depending on the climatic environment, the fact remained unchanged that its essence was Lanmaoa asiatica. The mysterious power of this mushroom was spread much more broadly and globally than scientists had initially thought [Meet The Mushroom That Makes People Have The Exact Same Hallucination](https://www.vice.com/en/article/meet-the-mushroom-that-make-people-have-the-exact-same-hallucination/). The fact that, regardless of nationality, race, or upbringing, the moment this specific chemical substance crosses the blood-brain barrier of humans, it makes them see "little people" without exception, sent massive shockwaves not only through the biological community but across the entire field of neuroscience [Hallucinogenic bolete mushroom - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinogenic_bolete_mushroom).

## Where We Stand

Following this astonishing discovery, numerous chemists and neuroscientists around the world are currently engaged in a fierce research competition to isolate exactly which specific molecular compound inside this rare mushroom induces such detailed fairy-tale illusions in humans [“There Could be a Mushroom Out There Causing Fairytale-Like ...”](https://thedebrief.org/there-could-be-a-mushroom-out-there-causing-fairytale-like-visions-people-eating-these-mushrooms-claim-to-have-the-same-hallucinations/). 

During this research process, one very fortunate and interesting point was discovered: the effects of this mushroom vary completely depending on the "cooking method" used in the kitchen. When thoroughly cooked in boiling water or hot oil, the hallucinogenic components are destroyed, turning it into an excellent, umami-rich ingredient for dinner. However, if this mushroom is consumed "raw" like a salad or undercooked, the dormant hallucinogenic components are activated, leading to an astonishing hallucination where countless tiny people suddenly pop out from all directions [“There Could be a Mushroom Out There Causing Fairytale-Like ...”](https://thedebrief.org/there-could-be-a-mushroom-out-there-causing-fairytale-like-visions-people-eating-these-mushrooms-claim-to-have-the-same-hallucinations/). 

For decades, countless people who accidentally consumed this mushroom raw have commonly and vividly testified that "fairies dressed in colorful clothes jumped around on my food." Yet, as repeatedly mentioned earlier, even now in the era of cutting-edge science, we have yet to clearly unravel the neuroscientific cause of "why our brains consistently produce only little people out of tens of thousands of hallucinatory forms" [The Strange Mushroom That Makes You See Tiny People](https://www.sciencing.com/2087951/mushroom-makes-you-see-tiny-people/).
