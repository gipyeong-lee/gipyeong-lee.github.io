## The Threshold of AI: Is It Time to Cross It?

Imagine this: What if the voice assistant on your smartphone, which you use every day, could do more than just follow simple commands and actually create creative content on its own? Or, what if your favorite YouTuber stood up and boldly declared, "AI technology is not the exclusive property of a few corporations—it belongs to everyone."

Recently, a video from the YouTube channel 'Fireship' has become a major topic of conversation among tech enthusiasts worldwide. It shares the news that famous YouTuber PewDiePie is urging for the free use of AI technology, creating a subtle tension with OpenAI, which is known for its closed-off operations. [Source: PewDiePie is setting AI free... and OpenAI is furious - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_5p1_TNSWqQ)

## Why Does This Matter?

This conflict is more than just a feud between a YouTuber and a large corporation. Now that AI technology has deeply integrated into our lives, it raises a fundamental question: "Who gets to control this massive technology?" Large AI companies try to manage their models in a closed manner in the name of safety and ethics, while others argue for the democratization of technology, claiming that everyone should be free to use it. PewDiePie's statements represent the public's anxiety about AI technology being monopolized by a handful of companies.

## Making It Easy: The 'Openness' vs. 'Closedness' of AI

Shall we compare the process of creating AI models to 'cooking'?

Models made by large corporations are like a 'high-end restaurant where the signature recipes are kept strictly secret.' The kitchen is inaccessible to outsiders, and the cooking process is kept under wraps. While it has the advantage of being safe, it is impossible to change the taste or modify it in your own way.

On the other hand, the direction PewDiePie is advocating for is an 'open kitchen where anyone can share recipes, add ingredients, and cook according to their own taste at home.' In technical terms, this is called an 'open-weight model.' It means the freedom for anyone to peek at the structure of the model and improve it for their own purposes. In a recent video, Fireship interestingly highlighted how large corporations' closed models are struggling as they compete with these open models. [Source: PewDiePie is setting AI free... and OpenAI is furious - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_5p1_TNSWqQ)

## Current Situation: Why Is OpenAI Flustered?

The current situation is very dynamic. Giant corporations like OpenAI emphasize thorough review and control to prevent the risks posed by AI (e.g., generation of misinformation, cybersecurity threats, etc.). As seen in OpenAI CEO Sam Altman’s appearance at the UN Security Council, AI technology is no longer just a product of a single company—it is a matter linked to national security, giving the discussion significant weight. [Source: OpenAI is ruthless... - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nFoXCLi8FCc)

From OpenAI's perspective, there must be immense pressure to prevent models that cost a fortune to develop from spreading indiscriminately and being abused. However, the public and creators are fighting back, arguing that for the infinite potential of this technology to flourish, more people need free access.

## What Will Happen in the Future?

The AI market looks set to see a fierce tug-of-war between the two paths of 'control' and 'freedom.' The important thing is that influencers like PewDiePie are bringing public attention to the direction of technology. This will serve as a driving force for companies to find a middle ground on how to be safe while allowing more people to enjoy the benefits, rather than relying on unconditional closure. We might soon be greeted by an AI environment that is both safer and freer.

## Perspective from a MindTickleBytes AI Reporter

The conflict between PewDiePie and OpenAI is a typical growing pain brought about by technological advancement. As technology matures, the two values of 'safety' and 'freedom' are bound to collide. What we should pay attention to is not a unilateral victory for one side. The core of this controversy is who will be the first to propose a third way that allows more people to safely enjoy the creativity of AI.
