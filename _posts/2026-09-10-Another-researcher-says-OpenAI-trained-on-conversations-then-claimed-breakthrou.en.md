Imagine this: There is a high-level challenge that genius mathematicians around the world have been unable to solve for the past 100 years. Then, one day, a company announces, "Our AI solved this problem in just 88 hours." How would you feel? Would you be filled with wonder, or suspicion? This is exactly what has happened recently in the artificial intelligence industry.

### Why does this matter?

This incident goes beyond simply "solving a math problem." Solving a major mathematical challenge signifies that the reasoning (computational) ability of artificial intelligence has approached the level of human intelligence. However, it also poses a fundamental ethical question: "Did this AI figure it out on its own, or did it 'steal' and piece together the achievements of human researchers under the guise of 'learning'?" The debate over how to protect the rights of original creators when such technology is applied in actual industrial fields is an important issue we all need to watch.

### Understanding it simply

To use a simple analogy, this situation is like a "genius chef" and an "apprentice who secretly read the chef's secret recipe notebook." Mathematicians have been researching culinary arts for decades, sweating and writing their own "secret recipe notebooks (research drafts)." OpenAI's AI, however, poured in massive computing resources (worth approximately $22.5 million)[OpenAI's Controversial Role in Solving the Navier-Stokes Problem](https://chang.aevumnews.com/en/openai-s-controversial-role-in-solving-navier-stokes-problem), read through vast amounts of data in an instant, and presented the finished dish as if it had developed it itself.

Mathematician Tristan Buckmaster uploaded his research draft to a digital platform and raised the possibility that OpenAI's model utilized this data during its training process or plagiarized the research based on it[OpenAI researcher allegedly pressured mathematician to drop...](https://the-decoder.com/openai-researcher-allegedly-pressured-mathematician-to-drop-anthropic-co-author-from-math-breakthrough-paper/). OpenAI claims that their model solved the problem independently[OpenAI claims blockbuster math breakthrough... | Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-claims-blockbuster-math-breakthrough-amid-swirl-of-controversy/).

### Current situation

The current situation is getting intense. OpenAI officially announced that 10,000 AI agents worked non-stop for 88 hours to resolve the Navier-Stokes problem (a complex set of equations describing fluid flow)[OpenAIsaysit cracked decades-old math problem — but profclaims...](https://nypost.com/2026/09/09/tech/openai-says-it-cracked-decades-old-math-problem-but-prof-claims-they-cribbed-his-work-and-then-threatened-him/)[OpenAIClaimsAI Solved Navier-Stokes Problem After... - ynews.digital](https://ynews.digital/newsflash/openai-ai-solved-navier-stokes-problem-88-hours/). However, the academic community is highly critical, as the results have not been independently verified and suspicions of plagiarism are strong[Math breakthroughclaimbyOpenAIsparks credit dispute](https://www.usatoday.com/story/tech/2026/09/08/openai-navier-stokes-breakthrough/91664870007/). While OpenAI researcher Sébastien Bubeck counters that the model solved the problem on its own, controversy persists regarding overlaps in methodology with other mathematical achievements[OpenAI claims blockbuster math breakthrough... | Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-claims-blockbuster-math-breakthrough-amid-swirl-of-controversy/)[Who is Tristan Buckmaster? NYU mathematician at centre ofOpenAI...](https://www.indiatimes.com/trending/who-is-tristan-buckmaster-nyu-mathematician-at-centre-of-openai-navier-stokes-controversy-over-private-codex-logs-and-research-credit/articleshow/133954116.html).

### What happens next?

There are two key points to watch moving forward. First is **objective verification**. The question is whether it can pass the rigorous, independent verification expected by the mathematical community. Second is the **issue of AI training data sources**. The suspicion that AI models have been training on copyrighted data without authorization has persisted for some time[ResearcherssuggestOpenAItrainedAI models on... | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/01/researchers-suggest-openai-trained-ai-models-on-paywalled-oreilly-books/). This situation will serve as a critical turning point in establishing legal and ethical guidelines on how much artificial intelligence can utilize human intellectual property.

True innovation is only valuable when built upon transparency.
