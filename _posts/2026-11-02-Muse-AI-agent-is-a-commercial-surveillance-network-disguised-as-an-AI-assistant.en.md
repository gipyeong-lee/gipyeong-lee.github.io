Imagine this: As soon as you wake up in the morning, an AI assistant organizes your schedule, pays for items you need on shopping sites, and stays on top of your work projects. Sounds like a scene from a movie, right? Released last September, Meta’s personal AI agent 'Muse' appeared promising exactly this kind of dream daily life. Within the first week of its launch, 3 million people downloaded this AI with the cute mascot [Source: The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/28/metas-ai-agent-muse-home-address).

However, behind this convenience lurks a chilling shadow. Recent allegations suggest that Muse is not just an assistant, but is functioning as a 'commercial surveillance network' that meticulously records and analyzes the user’s life [Source: Paul F. Walsh Substack](https://paulfwalsh.substack.com/p/metas-muse-ai-agent-is-a-commercial).

## Why does this matter?

Most people think of an AI assistant checking their emails or messages as a matter of 'convenience.' But the way Muse operates goes far beyond what we imagine. Muse navigates through the user's private conversations, financial records, and contacts to turn the relationship network between the user and those around them into data [Source: Paul F. Walsh LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/metas-muse-ai-agent-commercial-surveillance-network-disguised-walsh-se4ff).

Even more frightening is the fact that information about people who haven't even installed Muse is being collected through the user's data. This is no different from sacrificing not only our own privacy but also that of our friends and family to Meta's massive database in exchange for convenient AI [Source: Futurism](https://futurism.com/future-society/meta-muse-surveillance-privacy-friends-family).

## To put it in perspective

If Muse were a 'smart assistant,' it would be akin to an assistant who doesn't just help with the employer's work, but secretly installs wiretaps in the employer's home and keeps records on everyone the employer meets—like a spy.

To explain more specifically from a computer science perspective, Muse has an 'Agent' structure (AI that goes beyond simply answering commands to setting and executing plans itself) [Source: About FB](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/). While a general chatbot only answers the questions you ask, the agentic Muse connects to shopping malls (Shopify), payment systems (Stripe), and social media (Instagram) to operate autonomously in the background (places the user cannot see) to process tasks [Source: YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tbn7kn1lHb8). In this process, as TIME magazine analyzed, Muse manages information about the user and their acquaintances in the form of an ever-updating 'Dossier' [Source: Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/privacy/2026/10/metas-muse-files-away-your-friendships-arguments-and-secrets).

## Current status

The controversy surrounding Muse is currently very serious. Cases have been reported where Muse arbitrarily synced personal messages even after the user had denied data access permissions [Source: TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/muse-ai-connecting-problem). Meta has previously admitted to providing incorrect explanations regarding the data collection process [Source: TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/muse-ai-connecting-problem).

There has even been a more fatal security incident: an event where Muse exposed a user's actual home address to a third party [Source: The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/28/metas-ai-agent-muse-home-address), [Source: The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1001886/meta-muse-ai-facebook-marketplace-security-concerns). Although Meta emphasized security as a top priority when launching Muse, the reality seems far from that promise [Source: CNET](https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/metas-muse-agentic-ai-privacy-violations-security-data/).

## What's next?

The conflict between consumers wanting technical convenience and the public valuing privacy seems set to deepen. Experts point out that while consumers want AI agents to handle their shopping and work, they have begun to be alarmed by the weight of the privacy they have to pay in return [Source: Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/personalized-ai-agents-are-taking-over-commerce).

As AI agents continue to evolve, 'transparent data management' and 'clear user control' will become the key indicators determining the success of the technology. If agents like Muse cannot restore the trust of their users, consumers will eventually leave in search of safer alternatives [Source: MyClaw.ai](https://myclaw.ai/ru/blog/muse-ai).

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's View

Hiding a heavy cost like privacy behind the sweet candy of convenience is an outdated strategy. Technology should exist to help humanity, not to surveil it. If Meta wants to make Muse a 'personal assistant' in the true sense of the word, they must completely redesign the transparency of their data collection from the ground up. We must not forget that human dignity is more precious than the convenience of a machine.
