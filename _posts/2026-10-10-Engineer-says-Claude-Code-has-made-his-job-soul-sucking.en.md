Imagine this: You arrive at work in the morning and turn on your computer. There was a time when you would rack your brain, agonizing over "how to write a more efficient algorithm." But now, you simply give commands to Artificial Intelligence (AI), and it instantly churns out everything from product specifications to test code and reports. All you have to do is check the results and hit the 'Enter' key. Twelve hours a day, every day.

Is this a dream job, or a nightmare? A debate sparked by a software engineer's recent experience has set the developer community ablaze.

## Why Does This Matter?

We have long believed that AI would liberate humans from mundane, repetitive tasks. We thought that once productivity skyrocketed, people would be able to immerse themselves in more creative work. However, reality seems to be unfolding a bit differently.

This incident demonstrates that the adoption of AI does not just increase work speed; it also highlights how it affects the way humans work and their subsequent psychological satisfaction. It forces us to move beyond the simple logic of "it's good because it's easier" and question whether we are "masters of technology" or have become "mechanical components" that merely verify AI-generated data.

## Simplified: People Who Have Become 'Robots That Hit the Enter Key'

A software engineer using the anonymous handle 'v0xium' described their new work environment as "soul-sucking." [[Source 1](https://wonderfulengineering.com/engineer-says-claude-code-has-made-his-job-soul-sucking-as-they-spend-12-hour-days-pressing-enter/), [Source 5](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/claude/articles/engineers-viral-post-captures-soul-145453359.html)]

The tool this engineer uses is 'Claude Code' by Anthropic. This tool handles a significant portion of core development processes, including writing product specifications, generating software code, issuing various tickets, and writing reports. [[Source 1](https://wonderfulengineering.com/engineer-says-claude-code-has-made-his-job-soul-sucking-as-they-spend-12-hour-days-pressing-enter/), [Source 6](https://www.thecooldown.com/green-tech/ai-coding-impact-on-developers/)]

To put it simply: if a developer of the past was a 'chef' who carefully selected ingredients and studied recipes to set a table with care, the current way of working is more like being an 'inspector' who just picks up a spoon and checks, "Is everything okay?" with a meal already prepared by AI.

These Transformer-based tools (an AI architecture that understands context by identifying complex relationships between words in a sentence) are like having a skilled assistant writing documents at lightning speed. It is convenient, but when the assistant does too much, there is no time for the master to think about *why* those decisions were made. Instead of agonizing over why the code was written this way or if there is a better approach, engineers are racing through 12 to 13-hour days, hitting 'Enter' to process the flood of AI-generated output. [[Source 2](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/claude-code-ai-engineering-work-pressure-review-1808763), [Source 3](https://www.newsbreak.com/techspot-510046/4900988969910-engineer-says-claude-code-has-made-his-job-soul-sucking-as-workers-spend-12-hour-days-pressing-enter)]

## Where Do We Stand?

The engineer's post instantly gained nearly 8 million views, creating quite a stir. [[Source 8](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3996624370421635)] Many fellow developers have empathized with the situation, pointing out the paradoxical reality that as reliance on AI increases, code quality actually drops, leading to an endless cycle of bug fixes. [[Source 4](https://www.teamblind.com/post/engineer-says-claude-code-has-made-his-job-soul-sucking-as-workers-spend-12-hour-days-pressing-enter-pop7i2ms)]

The speed at which AI writes code is phenomenal. However, the speed at which humans can understand and verify that code is limited by human cognitive ability. Ultimately, under the pressure to "build more, faster," time for quality review is reduced, creating a vicious cycle that returns as technical debt and bugs.

## What Comes Next?

Technological progress is inevitable. The ability to utilize AI has now become an essential skill for developers. But the important thing is the question of how to control technology as a 'tool.'

We must now find ways to maintain 'depth of thought' by working alongside AI, rather than simply accepting the results it generates. The developer role of the future is expected to require the competence of a 'supervisor' who critically evaluates AI-generated results and designs overall architecture, rather than just technical coding skills. Otherwise, we might all become machines that just press 'Enter' for someone else.

## The AI's Perspective

While we expected technology to liberate humans from mundane labor, reality shows the risk of creating a new form of 'digital factory labor.' This case proves that as AI becomes smarter, what humans need is not the 'ability to find answers' but the 'attitude to ponder what the right questions are.'
