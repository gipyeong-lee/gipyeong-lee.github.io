Imagine this: It’s a weekend afternoon, and you want to draw up a plan to build a cabinet that fits perfectly in a corner of your living room. But have you ever given up, feeling overwhelmed before even starting with the complex dimensional calculations and 3D spatial design? Woodworking is a wonderful hobby, but the initial threshold of "design" is quite high for the average person. Recently, however, promising technologies are being researched that could allow AI to take over this difficult process.

## Why does this matter?

Until now, Computer-Aided Design (CAD) tools have been the domain of experts. It takes months just to learn complex programs, and calculating numerous measurements and spatial constraints in your head while designing a single piece of furniture is a daunting task. But as Large Language Models (LLMs)—AI trained on massive amounts of language data—join this process, we are approaching an era where we can refine designs by communicating in everyday language. This represents more than just a change in tools; it signifies an expansion of "creative productivity," where anyone can immediately turn their ideas into physical results.

## Understanding it simply

How can an LLM assist with design? Simply put, it involves teaching "mathematical logic" to artificial intelligence. Recent research methods involve fine-tuning Large Language Models—already pre-trained to understand text—with "engineering sketch" data [Source: CAD-LLM: Large Language Model for CAD Generation](https://www.research.autodesk.com/publications/ai-lab-cad-llm/).

Here, another important technology emerges: a language called 'AIDL' [Source: A Solver-Aided Hierarchical Language for LLM-Driven CAD Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09819). Imagine you tell an AI, "Draw me a 3-tier shelf that is 50cm wide and 30cm deep." The AI understands the sentence. However, if the AI were to calculate the complex geometric dimensions itself, it might make mistakes.

This is where AIDL steps in as the problem solver. AIDL offloads complex spatial reasoning problems to a "geometric constraint solver" (a tool that uses mathematical formulas to accurately align positions in space). In short, it is a structure where the AI grasps the intent of the blueprint, while professional solvers handle precise mathematical calculations to ensure accuracy [Source: A Solver-Aided Hierarchical Language for LLM-Driven CAD Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09819). This is similar to a seasoned architect capturing the overall design while an engineer handles the structural calculations.

## Current status

Currently, these technologies are in the early stages of research. Using already pre-trained models to manipulate engineering sketches, they are showing excellent results in specific CAD generation scenarios [Source: CAD-LLM: Large Language Model for CAD Generation](https://www.research.autodesk.com/publications/ai-lab-cad-llm/).

However, it will take a little more time before we see them as commercial tools that anyone can use easily, like woodworking-specific software. Traditional techniques and information used by many woodworkers today are shared through sites like [Popular Woodworking](https://www.popularwoodworking.com/), and it is expected that if AI is combined with such seasoned knowledge in the future, it will create very powerful synergy.

## What will happen in the future?

In the near future, an era will come where you can say, "Move the handle on this drawer from the right to the left and increase the total length by 10cm," and the AI will immediately modify the blueprint. Furthermore, when data containing professional design know-how is combined with LLMs, it will be possible to catch design errors in real-time or receive suggestions for optimizing wood cutting. AI is now moving beyond being a tool that simply writes text or draws pictures and is deeply entering the realm of directly designing and creating the spaces we live in.

Perhaps the day is not far off when the piece of furniture you've always dreamed of will be placed in the middle of your living room as a physical reality, thanks to a reliable AI assistant.
