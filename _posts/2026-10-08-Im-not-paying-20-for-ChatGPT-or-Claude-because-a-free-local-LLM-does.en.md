Imagine this: a payment notification arrives for a $20 AI subscription service, part of your monthly fixed expenses. A question suddenly crosses your mind: "Did I really use $20 worth of AI this month?"

Many people are happy to pay $20 a month for powerful AI services like ChatGPT or Claude. However, in reality, a large portion of the tasks we perform daily can be sufficiently handled without highly advanced artificial intelligence. Recently, more people are choosing "Local LLMs" (Local Large Language Models)—models installed directly on your computer that work without the internet—instead of paying a monthly subscription fee. We take a closer look at whether local AI is truly enough and in what cases it is more advantageous.

## Why is this important?

AI has now become a part of our daily lives. However, sending all your data to a cloud service can be somewhat unsettling in terms of privacy. This is especially true for sensitive financial records, medical information, or your own precious personal documents. Furthermore, a $20 monthly subscription fee is not just a single month's cost. Over a year, that adds up to $240, a significant amount of money. Local AI is attracting attention as a new alternative that not only reduces this cost burden but also enhances security by ensuring your data does not pass through external servers [[Source 5], [Source 6]].

## In simple terms: The difference between a 'public kitchen' and 'my own refrigerator'

To use a simple analogy for how we use AI: cloud services like ChatGPT or Claude are like a "famous restaurant" with an excellent chef on staff. The prices are high, but you can order the best dishes at any time. On the other hand, local AI is like "my own refrigerator." There is the hassle of having to prepare ingredients and cook for yourself, but all the food in the refrigerator is accessible only to you, and there is no risk of others peeking at what the chef is making.

Local AI works without an internet connection, and the prompts (commands) and results entered by the user never leave the user's computer [[Source 3], [Source 5]]. Services like ChatGPT send all inputs to the company's servers for processing, but local models operate only within the safe fence of your own computer [[Source 3]].

## Current state: What can it do?

Of course, local AI is not a panacea. For tasks requiring complex reasoning capabilities at the level of the "frontier intelligence" that boasts the most powerful performance, paid cloud models still lead. However, for practical tasks like daily writing, simple data analysis, or coding assistance, local models also demonstrate significant performance [[Source 2], [Source 9]].

Currently, many users are adopting a smart strategy:
1. **Leveraging Local AI**: Actively use it for personal tasks where security is important, writing code that requires confidentiality, or consulting on personal matters that are difficult to share with others [[Source 2]]. 
2. **Using Cloud AI in Parallel**: Selectively use paid services only when highly complex and sophisticated problems arise that the local model cannot resolve [[Source 2]].

By handling daily tasks where privacy is important with local AI and using paid subscriptions only for special purposes that require powerful intelligence, users are finding a balance between cost and security [[Source 2], [Source 5]].

## What does the future hold?

As hardware technology advances, the performance of local models that can run on your own computer will continue to improve. While installation and setup may feel a bit complicated right now, the era where anyone can easily install their own AI on their computer is approaching rapidly as the technology gradually becomes simplified [[Source 5]]. Depending on your computer's specifications and your usual AI usage, the pleasant choice of saving $20 every month is expected to become much wider in the future.

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's View
Not everyone needs to use top-tier paid models. The democratization of technology starts with the equalization of performance. Taking control of your own data and choosing the tools that fit your situation yourself—achieving "AI sovereignty"—will be the core of a smart digital life in the future.
