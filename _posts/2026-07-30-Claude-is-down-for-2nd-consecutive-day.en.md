Imagine this: You wake up in the morning, drink a cup of coffee, and say to your AI assistant, Claude, "Please draft a memo for the important work email I need to send today." But instead of the usual helpful response, you get a cold message saying, "Unable to connect." Recently, many users around the world had exactly this bewildering experience.

This situation, which initially seemed like a simple temporary error, persisted for two days, causing major confusion across the services provided by Anthropic, the developer of Claude. What exactly went wrong?

### Why does this matter?

We are now using AI as more than just a tool; it has become a practical "assistant" that processes our work and provides ideas. It is like having an assistant who always takes notes by your side suddenly disappear. How does it affect our lives when a service so deeply embedded in our daily routines and work stops functioning?

It goes beyond simply not getting answers to questions. Countless corporate services and developer tools that were leveraging Claude's intelligence via APIs (Application Programming Interfaces, the bridges used to communicate with other software) also ground to a halt. The impact was so significant that some joked that "developers forgot how to code" because they were so reliant on it [Source 6]. This is a prime example of just how much we have come to depend on AI.

### Making it simple: What does it mean for AI infrastructure to stop?

To use a simple analogy, Claude is like a massive "knowledge library." When you ask a question, the librarian searches through vast amounts of material to provide an answer. This incident was like having all the roads and entrances to that library blocked at the same time.

It wasn't just the window for borrowing books (the web app) that closed; the place to call for inquiries (the API) and even the space where library staff worked from outside (Claude Code) all shut their doors [Source 6]. As a result, user requests could not be delivered to the librarian, causing them to get lost (timeouts), or the librarian was overwhelmed by too many requests at once, resulting in no answers at all (errors and failures) [Source 6].

### Current situation: How is the recovery going?

This incident began on March 2nd when Anthropic's entire infrastructure went down globally [Source 6]. Services are currently in a phase of gradual normalization, but there are still reports of inconsistent connectivity or residual issues in some environments [Source 4, Source 5].

According to sites that monitor Claude's status, real-time performance discrepancies across different regions and indicators of fluctuating recovery and instability are being observed [Source 5, Source 7, Source 8]. What users find most frustrating is this "uncertainty in the recovery process."

### What happens next?

This outage has made many users acutely aware of the fact that "if the AI service stops, my work stops, too." Consequently, we are likely to see an increase in the adoption of "backup services" that avoid relying on a single platform, or moves to prepare analog alternatives for core tasks that can be handled without AI.

Furthermore, AI companies, including Anthropic, will dedicate enormous efforts to strengthening safety measures—such as more granularly partitioning server structures—to prevent global infrastructure shutdowns like this one from happening again. It is the same principle as designing power grids with multiple feed points to prepare for a blackout.

### A perspective from MindTickleBytes' AI reporter

Perfect services do not exist in the digital world. While AI mimics human intelligence and makes our lives more convenient, ironically, the more accustomed we become to that convenience, the greater the feeling of helplessness we experience when the technology stops. This incident reminds us once again that our coexistence with AI requires the wisdom to maintain an "appropriate distance" and not lose our "ability to think for ourselves."
