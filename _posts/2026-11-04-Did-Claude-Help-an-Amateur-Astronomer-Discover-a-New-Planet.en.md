Imagine this: amateur astronomers gazing at the dark night sky, dreaming of finding their own planet. Until recently, this was a nearly impossible challenge without a Ph.D. or expensive equipment. However, surprising news recently broke that an amateur astronomer uncovered hidden secrets of the universe with the help of AI. How did the AI assistants we use in our daily lives become "space detectives" capable of finding planets orbiting stars tens of thousands of light-years away?

### Why does this matter?

Until now, scientific discovery was largely the exclusive domain of giant research labs or government agencies. Analyzing vast amounts of cosmic data required immense time and highly specialized personnel. But now, an era is opening where anyone can easily access and work with public data. As tools like [Claude (an AI assistant developed by Anthropic)](https://claude.com/) become mainstream, the barrier to entry for scientific research is drastically lowering. Beyond mere technical achievement, this signals a democratic age of science where [people without specialized knowledge can contribute to massive research efforts like space exploration](https://gizmodo.com/did-claude-help-an-amateur-astronomer-discover-a-new-planet-2000824393).

### Simplified: The birth of a high-speed research assistant

Amateur astronomer Pavel Rabtsevich identified a new exoplanet candidate called 'TIC 402710280' by [analyzing vast amounts of data released by NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS)](https://emergent.sh/news/researcher-discovers-exoplanet-using-claude).

How was this possible? [Think of it like this.](https://emergent.sh/news/researcher-discovers-exoplanet-using-claude) Imagine you have to read tens of thousands of thick encyclopedias by yourself to find a specific word. A human might not be able to finish reading them even in a year, but AI can scan the entire content and find the desired information in just a few minutes.

Pavel used a powerful AI assistant named Claude as a kind of "high-speed research assistant." He input the data into the AI and instructed it to find the subtle patterns that appear when a planet orbits a star and blocks its light. [Claude demonstrated remarkable ability in assisting with these complex scientific data analysis tasks](https://claude.com/), capturing tiny traces of the universe that had previously eluded human eyes.

### Current status: An exploration requiring verification

Of course, this planet is [still in the candidate stage, awaiting official verification procedures](https://www.xda-developers.com/dont-get-too-excited-but-claude-code-may-have-just-discovered-a-new-planet/). Astronomers have already cataloged over 6,400 exoplanets, but the universe remains full of unknown territories.

Currently, [tools like Claude Code or Codex](https://gizmodo.com/did-claude-help-an-amateur-astronomer-discover-a-new-planet-2000824393) are accessible to anyone, and countless amateur astronomers around the world are trying out these AI tools for their own discoveries. AI is evolving beyond a simple text-writing tool into a reliable research partner that helps solve humanity's scientific challenges.

### What lies ahead?

In the future, it will become even more common for individual researchers to use AI to read professional papers, analyze data, and formulate new hypotheses. [AI assistants empower us to learn, analyze, and solve difficult problems on our own, even in areas where we lack specialized knowledge.](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.anthropic.claude&hl=en)

In the near future, a groundbreaking cosmic discovery worthy of a textbook might not be made by a famous university astronomer, but by your neighbor who observes celestial bodies as a hobby or an ordinary student. The universe is no longer the exclusive property of experts with massive telescopes; it is becoming a wide playground for anyone with curiosity.
