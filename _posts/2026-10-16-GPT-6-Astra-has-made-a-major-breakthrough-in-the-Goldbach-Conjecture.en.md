Imagine this: back in 1742, someone posed a math problem. "Can every even number be expressed as the sum of two primes (numbers divisible only by 1 and themselves)?" This seemingly simple question, known as "Goldbach's Conjecture," has plagued countless genius mathematicians around the world for nearly 300 years.

However, news recently emerged that an AI model called "GPT-6 Astra" has achieved remarkable results regarding this ancient riddle. Has AI truly started to break down the mathematical walls that humans have been unable to scale for centuries?

## Why Does This Matter?

Mathematics is the language of all science and technology. Solving a mathematical riddle means more than just "solving a problem"; it signifies that the tools we use to understand the universe and logic have evolved to the next level.

The fact that AI has begun to intervene in such high-level reasoning is a major turning point. Mathematical proof has a very precise structure where if even one logical step is wrong, it all collapses. If AI can complete complex proof processes without errors, it means we have gained a "powerful colleague" that can help us solve challenges in drug development, securing cryptographic systems, and even uncovering the fundamental principles of the universe—problems we have struggled with for centuries—much faster.

## Easy Explanation: How Does AI Prove Math?

Mathematical proof is similar to assembling an intricate puzzle. The AIs we commonly use, like GPT, are adept at handling language, but achieving mathematical rigor is a different dimension. An important tool here is "Lean 4," a programming language designed to verify mathematical proofs.

Simply put, Lean acts as a "strict judge" that mechanically checks whether the logical proof constructed by an AI "properly follows mathematical rules." GPT-6 Astra did not falter before this judge and proved variants of Goldbach's Conjecture.

Specifically, the AI converted Goldbach's Conjecture into a special form using the "Liouville function" (a function defining mathematical properties) and proved that all even numbers can be expressed as the sum of two positive integers where the value of the Liouville function is always -1 ([Source: GPT-6 Astra is credited with a Liouville–Goldbach proof...](https://www.aidailyinsights.cn/en/2026-09-22/11/)). It also proved "Linnik's variant," another riddle in the mathematical community, and this result is known to have passed the Lean language verification process cleanly, without any "sorry" syntax—which indicates an incomplete proof ([Source: GPT-6 Astra proves Linnik's variant of the Goldbach conjecture](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/gpt-6-astra-proves-linnik-s-variant-of-goldbach-conjecture)).

## Current Situation: How Far Have We Come?

Of course, we need to look at this cool-headedly. While it is clearly an amazing achievement that GPT-6 Astra has solved important variants related to Goldbach's Conjecture, the classical sense of "Goldbach's Conjecture" itself—the fundamental proposition that every even number is the sum of two primes—remains an unsolved riddle in the mathematical community ([Source: GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, and the Open Model War](https://www.swiftinference.ai/blog/gpt-6-astra-claude-opus-55-and-the-open-model-war-2026-09-23)).

However, the mere fact that AI constructed complex logic on its own, formalized it into mathematical language, and passed perfect verification shows that the reasoning ability of AI has developed dramatically ([Source: GPT-6 Astra Unveils Major Breakthrough in Proving the ...](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3992525310802946)).
