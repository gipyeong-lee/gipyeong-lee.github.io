Imagine this: one morning, you wake up and check your smartphone, only to find that artificial intelligence (AI) is controlling all sorts of information on the internet as it pleases without your permission. It sounds like something out of a science fiction movie, but one of the most authoritative leaders in the AI industry has issued a warning that this could actually become a reality.

Dario Amodei, CEO of Anthropic, the company that developed the popular AI model "Claude," recently made a groundbreaking proposal to the AI industry: "Let's slow down for a moment." This is not simply a call to stop technological competition, but a message that we must first build a "safety net" to ensure this powerful tool we are handling does not threaten our lives.

### Why is this important?

We live in an era where new AI models appear every day, and machines that are smarter than ever before are assisting with our work and daily lives. However, CEO Amodei diagnoses that the current pace of technological advancement has already outpaced the development of "safeguards" that prevent the side effects of AI [Source: Anthropic CEO calls for slowing the AI race as safety ...](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-ai-development-rcna597383).

What would happen if AI learns in an unintended direction or takes dangerous actions based on its own judgment, and there is no technology to stop it immediately? Amodei describes these risks as "serious," emphasizing that governments and companies must join forces to monitor them closely [Source: Anthropic boss Dario Amodei calls for AI development to slow down](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c14dpgm0rg4o).

### In simple terms: A sports car without brakes

How about we use this analogy? Artificial intelligence technology is like a high-performance sports car whose engine performance doubles every day. The AI industry today is obsessed with upgrading the engine every day to go faster. However, the most important "brakes" or "steering wheel" for this sports car have not yet been finely tuned.

CEO Amodei's argument is simple: "I am not saying stop making sports cars. However, before driving at top speed, let's take the time to perfect the brakes and steering wheel so we can stop and change direction reliably." This safety device is the core of the "3-stage supervision plan" he mentions [Source: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei calls on AI companies to slow down ...](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/09/12/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-calls-on-ai-companies-to-slow-down-ai-development-amid-superint).

### Current situation: A six-month warning

CEO Amodei's concerns are not vague fears. In a recent blog post, he warned that a situation where uncontrolled "autonomous AI agents" (AI that attempts to achieve goals on its own without human intervention) take over the internet could occur within just six months [Source: Anthropic CEO calls for slowdown of AI development amid ...](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-calls-slowdown-ai-development/).

Of course, he is not against AI development itself. He believes in the immense potential of AI, but is concerned that the risks it poses are approaching faster than the benefits it provides [Source: Anthropic boss Dario Amodei calls for AI development to slow down](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/claude/articles/anthropic-boss-dario-amodei-calls-153744714.html).

### AI's perspective: More important than technology is control

We dream of a world coexisting with AI, not a world controlled by AI. CEO Amodei's proposal is a "growing pain" the AI industry is experiencing as it enters a mature phase, and a necessary "safety inspection."

It is important to watch what practical changes this proposal will bring to the industry. The key will be whether other AI companies join in on this proposal and what level of guidelines the government prepares. What is clear is that the consensus that "human control" is as important as technological progress is gradually expanding. Now is the time for the AI industry to prepare to take a step back and correct its direction rather than accelerating.
