Imagine this: There is a community called 'Hacker News' where countless developers and engineers from around the world gather to engage in heated discussions, ranging from the latest technologies to the humanities. You want to discover a book that might change your life, but sifting through the thousands of comments pouring in is never an easy task.

An interesting service has emerged to solve this dilemma. **HackerBooks**, which we are introducing today, gathers books verified by the Hacker News community and presents them to you in order, much like a curator at a professional library.

## Why is this important?

We live in a flood of information. It has become a time when finding books that have already been 'verified' by numerous experts is more important than just finding a good book. HackerBooks has systematically organized the recommendation lists created by the powerful collective intelligence of Hacker News. [HackerBooks collects the best books recommended in the Hacker News community, ranks them, and shows them at a glance](https://yahnd.com/books/?t=year).

For those who pursue deep knowledge and are sensitive to trends, such as developers or engineers, a book list carefully selected and read by peers becomes a more reliable signpost for reading than anything else.

## Understanding it easily: "The book version of HackerTube"

The easiest way to understand this service is to think of a service called **'HackerTube'**. HackerTube is a platform that collects valuable YouTube videos recommended and shared among Hacker News users, allowing them to be viewed in a clean user interface similar to YouTube [HackerTube](https://hackertube.org/), [HackerTube is a platform that collects YouTube videos recommended by Hacker News members](https://karpathy.bearblog.dev/hacker-tube/).

Simply put, HackerBooks is a service that applies this concept to 'books.'
- **HackerTube**: A place that collects community-recommended videos
- **HackerBooks**: A place that collects community-recommended books

This analogy makes it clear, right? [HackerBooks can be called the 'book version of HackerTube'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d8dQ78uI5ms). Without a complex search process, you can comfortably check the best reading lists selected by the collective intelligence of the community.

## Current status: How far has it come?

Currently, HackerBooks calculates recommendation rankings and operates the service by utilizing the powerful data source that is Hacker News [HackerBooks collects books recommended on Hacker News, ranks them, and showcases them](https://yahnd.com/books/?t=year). Of course, this list is updated annually, and efforts are being made to reflect community reactions in real-time.

Just as HackerTube has established itself as a reliable content repository in the video field, HackerBooks is evolving to become a 'seal of reliability' that developers look for first when choosing books. However, as it is a project run by an individual or built on a community basis, the key to its success will be how much data is systematically accumulated and maintained in the future.

## What will happen in the future?

Moving forward, it is expected to go beyond simply listing book titles and rankings, and strengthen the context of why the books were recommended or their connectivity with related technical information. As long as the healthy activity of the community continues, the 'HackerBooks of the Year' list, updated annually, will serve as a compass providing reading direction to countless people [HackerBooks shows the best books recommended on Hacker News](https://yahnd.com/books/?t=year).
