<p>
A wise person gains two advantages from solitude. The first is the opportunity to 
<b>
be with oneself
</b>
, and the second is the opportunity 
<b>
not to be with others
</b>
. A person who is healthy and bright does not view time spent alone as a hardship. Rather, they think of it as an exciting and enjoyable date with themselves.
<br>
<br>
In any case, 
<b>
life becomes lonelier as we head toward the end
</b>
. How, then, shall we prepare for this life? The answer is already laid out too clearly.
<br>
<br>
- From "Schopenhauer's Life Lessons: How to Live Your One and Only Life" -
</p>

<p>
In the process of humans living in society, I believe relationships are an essential part of what we experience. Upon reflecting carefully, I realized that I have been a person more influenced by relationships than I had thought. I have belonged to various relationships, from those as close as my wife to those with people I occasionally exchange news with. I didn't join them because I wanted to, but they were formed as I lived in society. And it seems I was struggling unconsciously to maintain those relationships while adhering to the things one must protect within them.
</p>
<p>
Among the sentences mentioned in the book, the phrase 
<b>
gaining the opportunity to be with oneself
</b>
 was a very unfamiliar expression to me. It was also slightly frightening, but the phrase 
<b>
life becomes lonelier as we head toward the end
</b>
 made me feel as if I was already living while thinking that way; in truth, I suspect I was avoiding it while knowing about it without having prepared yet. Therefore, I will now make good use of the 
<b>
opportunity not to be with others
</b>
 and faithfully devote myself to the time spent with my own self.
</p>
