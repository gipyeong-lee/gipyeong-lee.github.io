Imagine a complex operating room. While a doctor checks a patient's condition, a robotic arm next to them makes decisions on its own and performs a delicate surgery. It sounds like something out of a sci-fi movie, but a similar remarkable experiment was recently successful in the field of life sciences. An AI agent—an artificial intelligence that makes its own judgments and acts to achieve a specific goal—actually performed brain surgery on a mouse. [AI performs brain surgery on a mouse, Oxford PhD claims](https://www.superhuman.ai/p/ai-performs-brain-surgery-on-a-mouse-claims-oxford-phd)

In the past few days, the scientific and technological community has been abuzz with news that an AI agent successfully completed a brain surgery on a live mouse. [Startup uses AI agent to perform brain surgery](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/startup-uses-ai-agent-to-perform-controversial-brain-surgery-on-a-mouse-anthropic-sonnet-controlled-motorized-surgery-rig-ai-given-free-reign-to-treat-epilepsy-with-a-range-of-gene-therapy-vectors-after-opus-5-5-refused-to-conduct-biological-experiment) The AI agent at the heart of this experiment devised its own treatment strategy and carried it out without human intervention.

## Why is this important?

Until now, AI has primarily been a tool of the 'digital world,' analyzing data, writing text, or drawing pictures. However, this experiment proved that AI can perform precise human activities in the 'physical world.' [AI agent injects drugs into the brain of a live mouse](https://www.wired.com/story/an-ai-agent-did-brain-surgery-on-live-mouse/)

Specifically, when researching intractable diseases like epilepsy, AI-automated experiments allow human researchers to verify countless treatments at a much faster rate. This means that in the future, we could dramatically accelerate the speed at which we treat diseases, providing faster medical benefits to more people. [AI agent performs brain surgery](https://www.aigpu.cloud/en/blog/ai-agent-brain-surgery-mouse-lab-automation)

## Making it easier to understand: AI as a seasoned chef

Let's compare this experiment to 'cooking.' A human chef decides which ingredients to pick and what to cook from a refrigerator filled with tens of thousands of items. In this experiment, the AI agent acted like a seasoned chef.

1. **Selection of treatment materials:** The AI was given a spreadsheet listing 19 different 'gene therapy vectors' (gene delivery vehicles for treatment). [AI agent performs mouse brain surgery](https://www.indiatoday.in/science/story/ai-agent-brain-surgery-mouse-epilepsy-gene-therapy-3028602-2026-10-30) The AI chose for itself the method most suitable for stopping the seizures. [Telegram RTNews](https://t.me/rtnews/173969)
2. **Precise cooking (surgery):** To inject the selected treatment, the AI controlled robotic surgical equipment. Just as a chef moves chopsticks to handle ingredients delicately, the AI observed the mouse's brain through a camera, calculated the precise coordinates, and manipulated the syringe. [AI agent performs brain surgery](https://www.aigpu.cloud/en/blog/ai-agent-brain-surgery-mouse-lab-automation)

In short, AI has evolved into an 'intelligent agent' that does not simply follow orders, but chooses the best tool (treatment) and executes (surgery) for a given goal (seizure reduction).

## How far have we come?

Of course, this experiment does not mean it will immediately be applied to human surgeries. We are currently at the early stages of laboratory automation. The procedure involved a human performing the initial surgery to open the mouse's skull (craniotomy), and then the AI took over to perform the injection process. [AI agent injects drugs into the brain of a live mouse](https://www.wired.com/story/an-ai-agent-did-brain-surgery-on-live-mouse/)

There is also homework to do in terms of time. A process that takes about 45 minutes for a human took over an hour in this experiment controlled by the AI. [AI agent injects drugs into the brain of a live mouse](https://www.wired.com/story/an-ai-agent-did-brain-surgery-on-live-mouse/) Further technical improvements seem necessary before the AI can judge situations and maneuver more smoothly.

## What will happen in the future?

This experiment is a starting signal for the 'AI laboratory.' In the future, researchers won't need to stay in the lab 24/7; the AI agent will be able to perform hundreds of experiments overnight and organize the results. If this technology continues to develop and its safety is verified, we can expect even more sophisticated precision medical services.
