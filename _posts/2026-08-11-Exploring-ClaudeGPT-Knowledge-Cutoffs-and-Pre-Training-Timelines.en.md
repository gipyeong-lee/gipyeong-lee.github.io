## 1. AI frozen in memory: Why does this happen?

Imagine this: You ask a very smart friend, "Did you see the news yesterday?" and they reply, "Actually, I have no idea what's happening in the world since January 2026." How baffled would you be? The artificial intelligence (AI) models we use every day sometimes act just like that. They clearly seem like cutting-edge technology, yet when you ask them about something that happened yesterday, they might say they don't know or give you completely wrong information.

This isn't because the AI is broken. In the AI field, this is called a "knowledge cutoff." Today, we want to unravel the secret behind what this term means and why AI seems to be frozen at a certain point in the past, as if it rode a time machine.

## 2. Why is this important?

For the average person using AI in daily life, the knowledge cutoff is a concept worth knowing. It allows you to distinguish whether the AI is answering based on its own "memory" (pre-trained data) or whether it is looking up "real-time information" (web search).

Simply put, the AI's internal memory is sufficient when asking about historical facts or general knowledge. However, you shouldn't rely solely on the AI's memory for questions where real-time accuracy is critical, such as the latest stock market trends or yesterday's game results. Understanding knowledge cutoffs provides a clever standard for judging when to trust this smart assistant and when to provide it with extra external materials. ## 3. Easy to understand: AI's "study period"

To make the knowledge cutoff easier to understand, let's use the analogy of a student preparing for an entrance exam.

The process of creating an AI model is similar to preparing for university entrance exams. AI models practice "auto-completion" on massive amounts of data scraped from the internet. [Source: Exploring Claude/GPT Knowledge Cutoffs- by Shrivu Shankar](https://blog.sshh.io/p/exploring-claudegpt-knowledge-cutoffs) It’s like a student memorizing thousands of textbooks and reference books to take a test. The date of the last textbook the student studied is the "knowledge cutoff." It is only logical that the student wouldn't know the content of books published after they walked into the exam hall.

AI trained based on the Transformer architecture (the core structure of AI that understands context by mathematically grasping relationships between words in a sentence) internalizes only the data included in this "study period." [Source: Exploring Claude/GPT Knowledge Cutoffs- by Shrivu Shankar](https://blog.sshh.io/p/exploring-claudegpt-knowledge-cutoffs) Therefore, checking the cutoff date is equivalent to identifying the AI's training timeline—that is, up to what point the model has grasped knowledge. ## 4. Current status: How much does Claude in 2026 know?

AI models have different study completion dates depending on the version and the developer. Looking at the cases of recently released Claude models clarifies this:

- **Claude Opus 5**: Trained on data up to May 2026. [Source: How up-to-date is Claude's training data? | Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114494-how-up-to-date-is-claude-s-training-data)
- **Claude Sonnet 5, Fable 5, Opus 4.8**: Possess knowledge up to January 2026. [Source: How up-to-date is Claude's training data? | Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114494-how-up-to-date-is-claude-s-training-data)
- **Claude Sonnet 4.6**: An slightly older model, remembering data up to August 2025. [Source: How up-to-date is Claude's training data? | Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114494-how-up-to-date-is-claude-s-training-data)

As seen, the newer the AI model, the more its cutoff date pushes toward the future. However, the crucial fact is that even the most high-performing model does not perfectly remember the news from "this morning" on its own. This is why when up-to-date information is needed, AI uses a method of calling external search tools to scrape information in real-time. ## 5. What will happen in the future?

Even if AI gets smarter in the future, the cutoff itself won't disappear. Instead, development will head toward AI being more aware of its own limitations.

For example, if you ask, "Tell me the election results that were just announced," a smarter AI will have more sophisticated capabilities to judge and act, saying, "My training data only goes up to last month, so I don't know the exact results, but I will search the web right now and let you know." [Source: AI Knowledge Cutoff Dates: Every Major LLM Updated for 2026](https://www.temso.ai/blog/ai-knowledge-cutoff-dates-every-major-llm-updated-for-2026) We are moving into an era where "how to find out what you don't know" is becoming more central to AI competitiveness than simply "knowing a lot."

When you talk to AI from now on, try thinking about the cutoff date. Understanding the "limits of memory" that AI experiences will be the guiding principle for us to use AI more wisely.
