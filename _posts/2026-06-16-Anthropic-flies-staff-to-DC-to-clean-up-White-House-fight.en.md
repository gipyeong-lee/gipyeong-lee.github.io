Imagine this: You have developed the smartest artificial intelligence (AI) assistant in the world. Not only can this assistant solve difficult math problems, but it can also spot errors in complex computer programming code in mere seconds. Just as you are about to unveil this amazing invention to the public, government authorities suddenly intervene. "This technology is too powerful and could be dangerous. Starting tomorrow, no foreign national is allowed to use this AI under any circumstances."

What would you do? In a global online environment where anyone in the world with internet access can log on, perfectly filtering out only 'non-Americans' is practically impossible technologically. Simply put, it's as unreasonable as being asked to sift out only a specific company's salt from the ocean water. Ultimately, you make a heavy decision: "In that case, I will completely shut down the service so that no one can use this AI for the time being."

This is not a sci-fi movie scenario. Just a few days ago, this unprecedented event actually unfolded between **Anthropic**, considered one of ChatGPT's strongest rivals and a top global AI company, and the **US White House** [Anthropic to meet White House officials over AI tool suspension](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9w2p7ykp8yo). 

As the incident blew up, Anthropic executives hastily boarded a plane on Monday, flying to Washington D.C. to meet with government officials and do damage control [Anthropic Is Still at Odds With the White House Over... | WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-is-still-at-odds-with-the-white-house-over-claude-fable-5/). Moving beyond a common friction between a simple information technology (IT) company and government regulators, this incident raises a massive question about how the state should control 'AI that is beginning to surpass human intelligence.' ## Why It Matters

Until now, artificial intelligence has been a 'convenient tool' and a 'friendly assistant' that helps us with our daily lives and work. It was simply software that summarized meeting minutes for us, smoothed out awkward English emails, and drew cool pictures. 

However, this Anthropic incident clearly demonstrates that the US government's perspective on artificial intelligence has fundamentally changed. The government has now begun to treat the latest AI not as a convenient chatbot, but **on par with a 'national strategic asset' or 'weapon' like nuclear arms or advanced fighter jets**. 

Behind the US government's prohibition ordering Anthropic that "no foreign national should be allowed access to this technology" lies a deep-seated fear that hostile nations or hacker groups could use this AI to attack America's security systems [Anthropic to meet White House officials over AI tool suspension](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9w2p7ykp8yo). 

During the Cold War, the export of uranium used for manufacturing nuclear weapons was blocked, and more recently, strict controls were placed on cutting-edge semiconductor chips—essential components for smartphones and computers—preventing them from reaching certain countries. But now, an invisible software program—specifically, **the very right to access an AI model—has emerged as the core issue in international hegemonic competition and security**. This also has massive repercussions for the daily lives of ordinary people. If access to 'powerful intelligence' starts being controlled and censored on the grounds of nationality or national security, the benefits of humanity's technological progress that the entire world enjoyed without borders could be cut off overnight.

## The Explainer: Just How Smart Is It?

What exactly happened to cause such a massive uproar? At the center of this incident is Anthropic's ambitious new state-of-the-art AI model, **'Claude Fable 5'**.

To illustrate just how exceptional this model is, even Anthropic itself previously evaluated this latest AI tool as **"too powerful"** [Anthropic to meet White House officials over AI tool suspension](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9w2p7ykp8yo). 

To use an analogy: if the general AI we commonly used was like a friendly 'librarian' at a local library, the newly unveiled Claude Fable 5 is like a **'genius master key maker'** who has memorized the blueprints to every safe in the world. 

If a well-intentioned company or security expert enlists the help of this master key maker, they can get excellent advice to perfectly fortify their computer networks (security systems). The problem, however, lies in the opposite scenario. What if a malicious foreign hacker commands this AI to "write code that can breach the networks of a specific bank or government agency"? This is exactly what the White House fears most: the fact that this model could be exploited as a lethal weapon for advanced **Cyber Operations (military and intelligence activities utilizing computer networks, such as hacking and cyberattacks)** [Anthropic Rushes Staff To D.C. After A National-Security... | ZeroHedge](https://www.zerohedge.com/ai/anthropic-rushes-staff-dc-after-national-security-order-yanked-fable-three-days).

As fears over cybersecurity grew, the US government immediately issued an ultra-strict mandate to Anthropic to "completely ban foreign access." However, in the internet world, accurately identifying and blocking someone's nationality 100% of the time is nonsensical. This is because users can easily disguise the country they are connecting from by using a simple tool like a VPN (Virtual Private Network, a technology that spoofs your internet connection location to another country). 

Ultimately, caught in a no-win dilemma, Anthropic made a momentous decision last Friday. Rather than risk violating the US government's strict security regulations by clumsily attempting to block foreigners, they opted for the drastic measure of taking down the service entirely, declaring they would **"block all public access to the recently released state-of-the-art AI tool"** [Anthropic to meet White House officials over AI tool suspension](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9w2p7ykp8yo).
