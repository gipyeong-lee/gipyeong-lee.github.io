Imagine this: You wake up in the morning and want to send money to a friend. You don't need to open a banking app, enter a password, and wait like you do now. It's a world where you can send money anywhere in the world as quickly and cheaply as sending a photo via a messenger app. One of the leading candidates to make this possible is the 'stablecoin.'

Recently, however, the heart of the U.S. financial system, the Federal Reserve (Fed, the U.S. central bank), has made a very significant move regarding this digital money. They have stepped forward to create rules that companies issuing stablecoins must follow. What does this change mean for our daily lives?

## Why is this important?

Until now, the virtual asset market has been like the 'Wild West.' There were many opportunities, but the risks were just as high. However, the situation changed with the passage of the 'GENIUS Act' in the U.S. This act paved the way for banks, non-bank institutions, and credit unions to issue their own stablecoins [Source: Trump signs landmark GENIUS Act, hailing "exciting new..." - CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-signs-genius-act-crypto-bill/).

The fact that the Federal Reserve has now released regulatory proposals for public comment means that this digital money is 'starting to enter the safe institutional fold.' For the general consumer, the most important point is 'trust.' Once the rules are clear, we can worry less about the digital money we use disappearing in an instant and enjoy faster financial transactions with lower fees [Source: Trump signs landmark GENIUS Act, hailing "exciting new..." - CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-signs-genius-act-crypto-bill/).

## Easy understanding: A stablecoin is a 'digital safe'

Let's look at what a stablecoin is through a metaphor. When you think of cryptocurrency, you often think of Bitcoin, where the price jumps around, right? Stablecoins are the opposite. As the name suggests, they are coins with a 'stable' value.

Simply put, whenever a company issuing a stablecoin issues a $1 coin, it safely puts $1 in a bank vault. Just as a filter in a photo app adjusts the colors of an original photo, a stablecoin acts as a 'filter' that brings the value of the dollar into the digital world.

One of the Federal Reserve's current regulatory proposals concerns this 'digital safe.' According to the proposal, stablecoins issued by companies supervised by the Fed must be 100% backed by permitted assets such as cash [Source: Fed proposes reserve and capital rules for stablecoin issuers under genius act](https://cryptobriefing.com/fed-proposes-reserve-and-capital-rules-for-stablecoin-issuers-under-genius-act/). In other words, they are putting on a strong seatbelt to ensure that the company issuing the coins cannot engage in misconduct.

## What will happen in the future?

The Federal Reserve's latest move signifies that the technology of stablecoins is moving beyond the lab and becoming a part of the global financial system. In the future, countless stablecoins will find their place inside the smartphones we use.

The points readers should pay attention to are 'speed' and 'cost.' Once faster and cheaper transactions become possible, our consumption patterns will change significantly. However, if regulations are too strict, innovation will slow down, and if they are too weak, unexpected accidents could occur. Now you see why the 'public comment' process currently being conducted by the Fed is so important, right? Just by watching the scene where the rules of finance are being written, you can catch a glimpse of the future that awaits us.
