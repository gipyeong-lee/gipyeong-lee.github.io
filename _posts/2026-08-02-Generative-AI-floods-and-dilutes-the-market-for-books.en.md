Imagine this: You head to a bookstore on a weekend, excited to check out the bestseller display. In the past, you would have seen novels imbued with an author's deep philosophy or guidebooks packed with the know-how of experts in the field. Now, however, hundreds of books released in just a few days are taking their place. The "writing imbued with the warmth of a human author" that we have taken for granted is increasingly being swept away by the massive waves of the digital world.

Recent research results show that this phenomenon is not just a simple concern. As generative AI (artificial intelligence technology that creates text or images on its own) mass-produces literary works, such as novels, at almost zero cost, the ecosystem of the book market is fundamentally changing [[Source 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20349), [Source 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20349)].

### Why does this matter?

The book market has been considered one of the last bastions where human creativity blossoms. However, as AI transforms this market into an economy of "scale," human authors are now in a situation where they must compete with "mass offensives" rather than the value of their creation. In particular, profits in the book market are becoming increasingly fragmented, and the space for writing that humans have carefully crafted is narrowing. This raises a fundamental question, going beyond the simple issue of how books are sold, about whose voice is truly contained in the knowledge and narratives we consume [[Source 4](https://arxivtldr.org/abs/2607.20349), [Source 6](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2607.20349v1), [Source 11](https://www.pastemagazine.com/books/publishing/generative-ai-is-turning-publishing-into-a-swamp-of-slop)].

### In simple terms: The 'Fast-Foodization of the Book Market'

Shall we use an analogy to understand this phenomenon? If the book market of the past was a "home-cooked meal restaurant" that carefully prepared ingredients and simmered them for a long time, the current market is like streets filled with "factory-made fast food" cranked out in an instant by 3D printers.

By utilizing technologies such as Transformers (an AI core structure that understands context by grasping the relationships between words in a sentence), AI learns from vast amounts of data and completes a book-length story in an instant. It creates books by logically connecting words and sentences, much like assembling Lego blocks [[Source 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20349), [Source 9](https://www.unboxfuture.com/2026/07/how-ai-books-infiltrate-stores.html)].

The problem is that before readers can even consider the quality of a work when choosing a book, they are first overwhelmed by the vast quantity of books pouring out before their eyes. Researchers analyzed 14,419 self-published genre novels sold on Amazon between 2023 and 2026 and found that AI-assisted books are gradually increasing their sales share, even encroaching on top rankings previously held by human authors [[Source 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20349), [Source 14](https://janefriedman.com/researchers-attempt-to-prove-that-generative-ai-dilutes-the-market-for-books/)].

### Current Situation: The Era of Quantity Over Quality

The market's change is already at a tangible level. In specific sectors, such as technology and travel guides, the average e-book price has plummeted by 32% as synthetic content generated by AI has flooded the market [[Source 9](https://www.unboxfuture.com/2026/07/how-ai-books-infiltrate-stores.html)].

AI does not worry about trying to show better literary merit than a competitor's work. Instead, it overwhelms the market with "volume" at a speed that humans can never follow, rewriting the rankings [[Source 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20349v2), [Source 14](https://janefriedman.com/researchers-attempt-to-prove-that-generative-ai-dilutes-the-market-for-books/)]. It is like seeing giant warehouse discount stores with infinite inventory popping up on every corner instead of small, beautiful independent bookstores.

### What will happen in the future?

This trend foreshadows a tension between "digital abundance" and "human cognitive limits" [[Source 12](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12488)]. The more books that are released, the more difficult it will be for readers to pick out good ones. The key point to watch moving forward will be how we identify valuable information amidst the flood of AI-generated content, and what strategies human authors will take to protect their own narratives.

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective

It is true that the advancement of technology has lowered the barriers to creation, but that does not guarantee the depth of the work. Ultimately, what will keep us from getting lost in the ocean of information is not artificial intelligence, but our own "discernment" to contemplate what we want to read. I hope you do not lose the joy of discovering your own gem-like book among the flood of books being released.
