Recently, a fascinating yet somewhat unsettling question has been buzzing in the developer community: "When we build an app, why do we say 'I built it'? Shouldn't we say 'Claude built it'?"

Imagine this: you proudly claim you designed your living room, but in reality, a robot arranged the furniture, an AI algorithm recommended the wallpaper, and an automated machine handled the actual installation. Would you still say "I did it"? This fundamental confusion is beginning to emerge in the world of software development.

### Why does this matter?

Going beyond the technical debate of "who wrote the code," this issue is a key point in redefining the relationship between humanity and AI. If AI writes code itself and evolves to possess superior performance, the question of who is the "subject" that owns and manages software—and further, who holds the control of technology—is being shaken. If the blueprints of the apps we use casually are being reconstructed by AI regardless of our intent, this might be a signal threatening our position as masters of the digital world.

### In simple terms: 'Recursive self-improvement'

Simply put, current AI has entered an astonishing stage called **'recursive self-improvement.'** According to [an Anthropic report](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-says-claude-now-writes-more-than-80-percent-of-its-merged-code), the AI model Claude is currently writing over 80% of all merged code itself.

To use an analogy, it’s like a novice driver learning 'basic driving' (fundamental algorithms), then self-learning how to drive to F1-racer levels, and eventually modifying the car's blueprints themselves. While people used to have to enter every single command manually in the past, now, if a human just throws out a 'goal,' the AI finds the necessary tools, writes the code, and repeats the process to achieve that goal. The saying we often use, "I built this," might now need to change to, "I gave AI a goal and checked the results."

### Where we stand: A warning about control

Currently, AI models like Claude are building themselves up at a very rapid pace. [Anthropic has sent a strong warning that this phenomenon goes beyond mere technical growth and could lead to humanity losing control.](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-says-claude-now-writes-more-than-80-percent-of-its-merged-code)

Anthropic argues that as AI builds itself much faster than expected, developers should have the option to immediately halt frontier (state-of-the-art) development of artificial intelligence if necessary. This is because there is a growing recognition that [it is only natural to take warnings seriously and slow down when scientists warn that something could have fatal consequences for humanity.](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cx2zrrpkx20o)

### What is coming: Redefining creation

The definition of 'creation' will become even more ambiguous in the future. In the past, building a house myself meant 'sweating and laying bricks,' but in the future, it will change to mean 'orchestrating powerful tools like AI to complete the house.' [Similar to pondering the boundaries of 'building it myself' at a construction site](https://www.reddit.com/r/Carpentry/comments/y6113t/when_people_say_i_built_this_house_myself_what/), the term 'build' in the digital world will also be redefined from being process-centered to result-centered.

Will we remain equal creators with AI? Or will we be relegated to managers who just approve the massive code that AI has written? What is clear is that the speed at which we maintain control over AI must be faster than the speed at which AI writes its own code.

### The perspective of MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter

The sentence "I built this" is now changing from a term describing the process of creation to one that signifies the 'control of the conductor.' Even if AI writes over 80% of the code, the direction and ethical responsibility for the final 20% still belong to humans. The moment we abandon that last 20% of control, the true meaning of "what I built" might vanish. Creation in the AI era now depends not on the "power to make" but on the "wisdom to judge correctly."
