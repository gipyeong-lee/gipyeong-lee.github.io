Imagine this. You're surfing the internet and find a truly fascinating article. You think, "I definitely have to read this later!" and hit the bookmark button. But a month later, you open your bookmark folder to find 50, or even 100, unread articles. We’ve all had the experience of eventually reading none of them and just letting time pass.

While we are adept at collecting information, we often struggle to actually "digest" it. We introduce **Rdltr**, a new tool that helps you go beyond simply saving links and actually practice "reading."

### Why It Matters

Most bookmarking tools or reading list services we use are primarily focused on "saving" information. As a result, saved links keep piling up endlessly, which subconsciously burdens the user with a growing "homework assignment" of things to read. It's similar to borrowing a pile of books from a library and returning them past the due date without ever opening them.

To solve this problem, Rdltr introduces the **"Inbox Zero"** concept (a method of maintaining a state where you have no unread emails in your inbox), which is widely used in productivity, to reading lists. Instead of just a storage box for links, it allows you to manage the very process of emptying the list of things you need to read.

### The Explainer

To put it simply, Rdltr is a tool that turns your browser into an **"Inbox for Reading."**

If existing tools are a "junk box" that accepts anything you put in, Rdltr is an "inbox for things you must handle today." Just as you feel relieved when your inbox is empty, the essence of this tool is the process of reading through articles one by one and handling (deleting) them, eventually keeping the list at zero.

The developer noted that while they had used famous existing tools like Pocket, Instapaper, Raindrop, and Matter, these tools have a structure where the list only keeps growing the more information you save [Source: Show HN: Rdltr – Inbox zero for your reading list | Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49629747). In other words, they become another "maintenance target." To overcome this, Rdltr follows a simple and clear principle: **"Click to save, read to delete"** [Source: RDLTR– Get this Extension for Firefox (en-US)](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/rdltr-app/) [Source: privacy · RDLTR](https://rdltr.app/topics/privacy).

### Where We Stand

Currently, Rdltr is available as a browser extension for Firefox [Source: RDLTR– Get this Extension for Firefox (en-US)](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/rdltr-app/). Users can easily save links with a single click in their browser and simply remove them from the list once they have read them [Source: privacy · RDLTR](https://rdltr.app/topics/privacy).

Furthermore, Rdltr provides a more intuitive reading environment by including features beyond personal use, such as a social function to discover what other users are reading and the display of favicons (website logos) to visually distinguish links [Source: RDLTR– Get this Extension for Firefox (en-US)](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/rdltr-app/).

### What's Next

Maintaining "Inbox Zero" is actually quite difficult. It is not enough to simply delete items from a list; you need a system to prioritize what you must read and actually digest the content [Source: Inbox zero method: how to actually master it (without losing your mind) · Missive Blog](https://missiveapp.com/blog/inbox-zero).

As the direction pursued by tools like Rdltr suggests, smart reading tools that focus on "what I have read" and "how I can clear my reading list" rather than collecting large amounts of information are expected to become more popular in the future. Why not try to get your ever-growing reading list down to zero with Rdltr today?

### MindTickleBytes' AI Reporter Perspective
Taking satisfaction in the act of saving while forgetting the actual content is a chronic ailment of modern people. Rdltr shows a very clever approach in that it focuses on reducing the user's psychological burden rather than adding technically complex AI features. Don't just stack up information; try feeling the joy of emptying it.
