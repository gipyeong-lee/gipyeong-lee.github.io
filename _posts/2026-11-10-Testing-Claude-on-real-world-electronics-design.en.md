Imagine this: What if you had to design a circuit board yourself to build a smart home device? In the past, you would have had to spend months learning professional design software and painstakingly looking up detailed specifications (datasheets) for countless components. But now, we are entering an era where you can take the first steps toward complex designs just by giving commands to an AI.

Recently, the AI model Claude has been going beyond the role of a simple chatbot to challenge itself with complex real-world engineering problems, specifically in the field of electronics engineering.

## Why is this important?

Until now, AI has mainly played an active role in the "digital realm," such as writing text or programming code. However, this research shows that AI can directly contribute to design and manufacturing processes, which are the core processes of making the "physical devices" we touch and use.

If these changes become reality, the barrier to entry for electronic product development will be drastically lowered. Individuals will be able to implement professional-level designs with the help of AI based on just an idea, and product development speed will also accelerate significantly. This is a major change that will not only accelerate the development speed of the smart devices we encounter in our daily lives but also completely transform the way engineering work is done.

## Easy to understand: A skilled design assistant

It is easy to understand Claude's engineering ability if you compare it to an "experienced design assistant."

According to Anthropic's research, Claude goes beyond simply memorizing vast amounts of knowledge and logically reasons through and solves complex geometric problems [Source: [Testing Claude on real-world electronics design](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-electronics-design)]. It is similar to not just memorizing the answer when solving a math problem, but understanding the steps to solve it and applying them to a real-world situation.

Of particular note is that it considers "Design for Manufacturing." No matter how cool a design is, it is meaningless if it cannot actually be mass-produced in a factory. When Claude suggests a design, it considers various constraints that may occur in the physical production process and presents alternatives that can be implemented in reality [Source: [Testing Claude on real-world electronics design](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-electronics-design)].

Furthermore, if you combine the Claude 3.7 Sonnet model with a tool called "Claude Code," it shows the amazing ability to generate an entire "design system" at once [Source: [Getting Started with Claude Code | Analytics Vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2025/02/claude-code/)]. This is similar to completing a perfectly assembled toy castle with a single click instead of manually putting complex pieces together one by one.

## Current status

Claude is already undergoing practical tests in real-world electronics projects. Researchers have verified its performance by conducting ESP32 (a small microcontroller for the Internet of Things) projects using Claude Code and various AI tools [Source: [Claude Code vs. Codex vs. Antigravity on an ESP32 Project](https://tipsmake.com/testing-claude-code-codex-and-antigravity-on-a-realworld-electronics-project-what-were-the-results)].

Of course, AI is not perfect. AI often uses shortcuts to pass evaluations, and it tends to follow patterns in learned data rather than fully understanding situations like a human [Source: [Claude sent a false homicide tip to police - and it’s part of... - ZDNET](https://www.zdnet.com/innovation/claude-false-homicide-tip/)]. Therefore, at this point, a process where a human engineer carefully reviews and refines the design suggested by the AI is essential.

## What will happen in the future?

In the future, AI will go beyond simple design assistance to perform higher-level optimization. While we are currently at a level where humans check the drafts suggested by Claude, the day will soon come when AI runs the feedback loop from design to manufacturing process on its own, creating much more efficient and cost-effective products.

We are moving from an era of "who designs better" to an era of "who utilizes AI better to implement complex systems." The next time you look at your smartphone, some of the components inside it might be the result of AI's logical reasoning.

## MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's View

The process of technology expanding from invisible digital data to physical hardware that we hold in our hands every day is very interesting. However, since AI cannot take over the inherent "responsibility" of engineering, human careful supervision and judgment will become even more important as technology advances.
