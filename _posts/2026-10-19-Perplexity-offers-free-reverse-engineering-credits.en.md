Imagine you are using an app you love and have wondered, "How on earth was this feature implemented?" Typically, only the developers would know its internal structure, but we are entering an era where AI can help us peek behind the curtain.

Recently, the AI search service Perplexity added a very intriguing tool to its Computer platform: a technology named "REA (Reverse Engineer Anything)." Today, we will break down what this tool is and what it means for us.

### Why does this matter?

"Reverse engineering"—the process of deconstructing a finished product to understand its operating principles—has traditionally been a complex task requiring skilled engineers to spend significant time. The fact that this is now being offered as an AI agent service means the barrier to entry for this technology has been significantly lowered.

Crucially, this tool can analyze how an app works even without the source code (the program's blueprint) and can even help reconstruct a functional version [Source: Perplexity Computer Adds REA to Inspect and Rebuild Apps Without Source Code](https://www.tao.media/perplexity-computer-adds-rea-to-inspect-and-rebuild-apps-without-source-code/)[Source: Computer on X](https://x.com/AskPerplexity/status/2111120664543596957). This could lead to major changes in how developers experiment with ideas or learn app structures.

### Understanding it simply: Enter the AI detective

Setting aside complex technical jargon, let's use an analogy. Reverse engineering is like eating a finished dish and trying to deduce the ingredients and the order in which they were cooked.

Until now, we usually followed the recipe (source code) written by the chef, but Perplexity's REA tool is like hiring a detective with "superhuman taste buds" who can infer the recipe just by tasting the final dish. When we ask, "Tell me how this app works," the AI detective carefully observes (inspects) the app's appearance and responses, then draws up a blueprint for how to make it work the same way [Source: Computer on X](https://x.com/AskPerplexity/status/2111120664543596957).

While this sounds very convenient, there are precautions. Perplexity makes it clear that this tool must only be used on "authorized applications" [Source: AGTP on X](https://x.com/AGTPinsights/status/2111198187059081318). Security researcher Dino Dai Zovi commented, "It's surprising and cool that they've implemented reverse engineering as an agent service" [Source: AGTP on X](https://x.com/AGTPinsights/status/2111198187059081318).

### Current status: A chance to try it for free

Perplexity is currently running a promotion so that more users can experience this new feature.

*   **Who can use it?**: Any Perplexity Pro or Max subscriber [Source: Perplexity Computer /reverse-engineer free until Oct 31 ...](https://www.aipromonow.com/en/promos/perplexity-computer-reverse-engineer-free-10000-credits-oct2026).
*   **Benefits**: It provides free credits that can be used when performing `/reverse-engineer` tasks [Source: Perplexity integrates viral reverse engineering tool REA ...](https://lookonchain.com/feeds/77437).
*   **Scale**: Up to 10,000 credits (worth approximately $100) are provided per person [Source: Perplexity Computer /reverse-engineer free until Oct 31 ...](https://www.aipromonow.com/en/promos/perplexity-computer-reverse-engineer-free-10000-credits-oct2026).
*   **Duration**: These free credits are valid until **October 31, 2026** [Source: Computer on X](https://x.com/AskPerplexity/status/2111120664543596957).

### What lies ahead?

This case demonstrates that AI is penetrating deep into professional domains like software design and analysis, beyond just searching for information. In the future, AI agents might move beyond assisting with development tasks to taking full charge of analysis and reconstruction. If that happens, the range of services individual developers can create using their imagination will expand exponentially. However, as it is a powerful technology, responsible behavior and correct ethical usage guidelines must accompany these tools to ensure they are not misused.
