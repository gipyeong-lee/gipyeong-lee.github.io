Imagine this: How would you feel if a story about a disease in a distant country, which you usually only hear about in the news, suddenly started closing in on you at an uncontrollable speed? Recently, the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has faced a major crisis as the Ebola virus spreads at an unprecedented rate in history. In a situation that feels like being lost in a complex maze, scientists and health organizations have grasped an unexpectedly powerful tool—artificial intelligence—to confront and break through this crisis.

### Why is this important?

Ebola is a deadly disease that evokes fear just by its name. The virus currently circulating is a bit different from the Ebola we have known until now. Unfortunately, the 'Bundibugyo' species currently spreading in the Congo does not yet have an officially approved vaccine or specific cure ([Source: Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/ebola-response), [Source: WHO](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON614)). This means an extremely difficult battle for medical staff and brings immense anxiety to the general public.

The reason this news is important is not just because of the spread of the disease itself. This situation is considered the deadliest and fastest-spreading outbreak in Congo's history ([Source: BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cz72p75zg4qo)), and it is now threatening neighboring Uganda across the border ([Source: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ebola_epidemic)). It is a time to pay attention to how humanity's way of fighting this invisible enemy is fundamentally changing with the introduction of AI.

### Easy to understand: What role does AI play?

How can AI fight a virus? Utilizing 'Claude' here is, simply put, like having a **'high-speed assistant that processes an enormous amount of data'** by your side.

To use an analogy, imagine thousands of patient records, complex infection routes, and urgent reports from the field pouring in every day like a huge mountain. If a person were to read and analyze this information one by one, it might take days. However, AI scans this vast data in just a few seconds, identifying in real-time where the greatest danger lies and through which paths the infection is spreading rapidly. It is no different from having a smart librarian by our side who instantly reads tens of thousands of books in a library and creates a core summary ([Source: Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/features/ebola-response)).

Because the Bundibugyo species does not yet have an approved vaccine, unlike the existing Ebola Zaire species, sharing information from the field quickly and preparing efficient countermeasures is more important than anything else ([Source: WHO](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON614)). AI is playing a key shield role in securing the so-called 'golden time.'

### What is the current situation?

Since the initial case was reported in Ituri Province, Congo, on May 14, 2026 ([Source: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ebola_epidemic)), the situation has become increasingly serious. This outbreak is the 17th Ebola outbreak the Congo has experienced and has reached the deadliest level among all records to date ([Source: BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cz72p75zg4qo), [Source: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ebola_epidemic)).

Health authorities are moving quickly, but the lack of a vaccine is the biggest barrier ([Source: WHO](https://www.who.int/emergencies/situations/ebola-outbreak---2026)). Research to test promising vaccine candidates is ongoing, but as the means that can be applied in the field right now are limited, data-driven response using AI is more desperate than ever ([Source: WHO](https://www.who.int/emergencies/situations/ebola-outbreak---2026)).

### What will happen in the future?

Now, the task remaining for us is to watch how closely AI and health organizations can cooperate to effectively suppress the spread of this virus. Experts emphasize that how quickly this situation stabilizes depends on rapid data analysis and swift, accurate responses in the medical field ([Source: BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cz72p75zg4qo)). This case, which proves how efficiently AI technology can operate in disaster situations, will be an important opportunity to change the standards of future disease crisis management.
