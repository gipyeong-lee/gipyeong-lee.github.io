Imagine you are a young person with a very promising future. You are achieving incredible results at a major corporation and are held in high regard by everyone around you. However, your bank account is constantly in the red due to massive monthly rent and self-development expenses. You look like a brilliant talent on the surface, but if you went to a bank for a loan right now, you would struggle to get a high credit rating.

The situation facing Anthropic, the "giant" of the AI industry (the company behind the AI model 'Claude'), is remarkably similar. News is circulating that this company, which is preparing for an initial public offering (IPO), is expected to receive a high "junk" (investment-grade ineligible) rating from credit agencies. [Source: Financial Times, Anthropic expected to receive high 'junk' rating from credit agencies following IPO report says](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/anthropic-expected-to-receive-high-junk-rating-from-credit-agencies-following-ipo-report-says/)

### Why does this matter?

For us, a credit rating is an important yardstick that determines our ability to get credit cards or the interest rates on our loans. It is the same for companies. If you receive an "investment-grade" rating, you can borrow money easily at low interest rates, but a "junk rating" makes that difficult.

Anthropic has long been expected by the market to naturally receive an investment-grade rating. [Source: Anthropic IPO Expected to Receive Junk Credit Rating](https://emergent.sh/news/anthropic-ipo-expected-receive-junk-credit) However, this new outlook signifies that those expectations might be off the mark. It means Anthropic might have to pay higher interest rates when raising funds in the market, and it serves as a signal for investors to rethink the company's profitability and debt management. [Source: Anthropic may not secure investment-grade rating after IPO, Blackstone tells investors report](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-may-not-secure-investment-grade-rating-after-ipo-blackstone-tells-investors-report/articleshow/135371047.cms)

### In simple terms, why 'junk'?

Why is a company that builds such intelligent AI being called "junk"?

Metaphorically, Anthropic is like running a massive "supercomputer factory." To keep this factory running, it constantly needs high-performance chips (semiconductors), enormous data centers to cool the heat those chips emit, and a staggering amount of electricity.

Looking at Anthropic’s data, revenue is growing "explosively." They have reached a point where all of the Fortune 10 companies are their customers. [Source: Anthropic Eyes $40T Market as Revenue Surges Ahead of IPO](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/anthropic-eyes-40-trillion-market-as-revenue-surges-ahead-of-ipo/) However, costs that far exceed that revenue are being siphoned off into chip purchases, data center operations, and electricity bills. [Source: Is AI a Bubble? What Anthropic’s IPO Filing Admits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GfI7mOss8Sk) In other words, because they are in a state where **"they are spending far more money than they are earning,"** credit agencies classify them as a high-risk company rather than a stable investment at the moment.

### Current Situation

Anthropic plans to raise up to $100 billion through this IPO, and the company is valued at up to $2 trillion. [Source: Anthropic points to AI dangers, soaring sales in IPO filing](https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/technology/anthropic-points-to-ai-dangers-soaring-sales-in-ipo-filing/article71629700.ece)

Apart from this massive valuation, experts, including anchor investor Blackstone, expect the credit rating this company will receive to be a high junk rating at the "double-B" level. [Source: Anthropic после IPO может получить «мусорный» рейтинг](https://dzen.ru/a/auUz0QcaDHThyNB9) This is a slightly more cold-eyed realization of reality, quite different from the market's previous view that expected a high credit rating. [Source: Anthropic may not secure investment-grade rating after IPO, Blackstone tells investors report](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-may-not-secure-investment-grade-rating-after-ipo-blackstone-tells-investors-report/articleshow/135371047.cms)

### What will happen in the future?

Anthropic views the size of their target market as over $40 trillion. This is because the value created by knowledge workers worldwide using AI is that vast. [Source: Anthropic Eyes $40T Market as Revenue Surges Ahead of IPO](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/anthropic-repos-40-trillion-market-as-revenue-surges-ahead-of-ipo/)

Ultimately, the key question is, **"Can this massive wave called AI settle into a model that truly makes money?"** Right now, they have the "junk rating" label attached to them, but if AI truly becomes a core engine that dramatically changes how companies work and generates profit, this credit rating will naturally rise to investment grade.

For now, the report card of "electricity bills and chip purchase costs" that the company is showing looks too heavy, but the process of proving that this report card is an "investment cost" for the future is the path Anthropic must walk.
