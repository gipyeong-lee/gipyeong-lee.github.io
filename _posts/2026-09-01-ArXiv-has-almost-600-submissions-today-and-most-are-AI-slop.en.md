Imagine you are reading a groundbreaking research paper that could cure a difficult disease, only to discover that dozens of the references cited in the paper are non-existent "fakes." Or perhaps the research data itself is just plausible-sounding gibberish fabricated by AI (Artificial Intelligence). This is not just a story from your imagination; it is happening on the front lines of scientific research at ArXiv, the world's largest repository for sharing pre-print research papers.

ArXiv is currently facing a major threat to its credibility as it is being flooded with low-quality, AI-generated content known as "AI slop." To address this issue, ArXiv has now launched a powerful "expulsion operation."

## Why is this important?

Scientific research is the foundation of the knowledge humanity has accumulated. However, if research platforms are filled with unverified data and papers, finding genuinely valuable research becomes as difficult as "finding a needle in a haystack." In particular, if AI-generated misinformation is mixed into academic data, there is a high risk of "knowledge contamination," where subsequent research is conducted based on this faulty information.

ArXiv is currently facing a situation where the volume of submitted papers is exploding. According to [Source 12](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1rzp5ph/n_arxiv_the_pioneering_preprint_server_declares/), while submissions were around 10,000 per month before 2020, they exceeded 20,000 per month as of March 2026. According to [Source 11](https://theconversation.com/a-key-science-publishing-platform-is-cracking-down-on-ai-slop-283136) and [Source 10](https://www.sciencealert.com/ai-slop-is-flooding-scientific-publishing-and-one-major-site-is-fighting-back), ArXiv introduced policies starting last year to restrict submissions of certain types of computer science papers to prevent this rapid quantitative expansion and the overflow of AI content.

## Put simply

"AI slop" is, simply put, "AI trash with no nutritional value." To use an analogy: it is like going to a restaurant where, instead of a decent meal, you are served "fake dishes" that look fancy on the outside but are actually just spices sprinkled over nothing, with no real ingredients inside.

AI is proficient at connecting sentences in a very plausible way, but it lacks the function to verify whether information is true. Therefore, when writing papers, it sometimes fabricates non-existent books or papers as references—a phenomenon known as "hallucination," where AI confidently presents false information as if it were true. According to [Source 4](https://longlanedigest.substack.com/p/ai-slop-is-flooding-scientific-publishing), ArXiv is intensively cracking down on these unique AI-driven errors within papers, specifically clear evidence such as non-existent "fake citations."

## Current Situation

ArXiv is no longer just standing by. According to [Source 3](https://www.techspot.com/news/112442-arxiv-moves-combat-ai-slop-submissions-one-year.html), [Source 4](https://longlanedigest.substack.com/p/ai-slop-is-flooding-scientific-publishing), and [Source 7](https://www.machinebrief.com/news/arxiv-takes-a-stand-against-ai-generated-slop-in-research-pa-vd14), if it is confirmed that a paper contains unverified AI-generated material, a severe sanction of a **"one-year submission ban to ArXiv"** is imposed on the researcher who submitted the paper.

This is a grave measure that goes beyond simply rejecting the paper, impacting the researcher's activities themselves. Authors who have utilized AI in their papers must now thoroughly verify for themselves whether the results are accurate. According to [Source 7](https://www.machinebrief.com/news/arxiv-takes-a-stand-against-ai-generated-slop-in-research-pa-vd14), researchers are being required to undergo more demanding verification stages, such as having their research recognized for its value by reputable academic journals or conferences.

## What will happen in the future?

The academic community is expected to continue its war against "AI slop" for the time being. It will not just be ArXiv; other academic platforms will also establish stricter standards for AI-generated material. In the future, researchers will use AI as a powerful tool to increase the speed of their research, but they will also realize that they are entirely responsible for the results. This is because the papers of the future that we read should not be written by AI, but be the results of deeper and broader verification by humans, aided by AI.
