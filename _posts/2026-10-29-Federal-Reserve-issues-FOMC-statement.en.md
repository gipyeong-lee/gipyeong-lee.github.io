## Lead: The Movements of a Giant Engine Room
Imagine this: just like the microscopic semiconductor chips that dictate the performance of the smartphones we use, there is a giant "engine room" that controls the flow of our economy from behind the scenes. That engine room is the U.S. Federal Reserve (Fed), the central bank of the United States.

Whenever news breaks that "the Fed has released an FOMC statement," financial markets around the world tense up. Why? Because a single number written in that statement can shake up your salary, your loan interest rates, and even the price of your groceries today. Today, we will learn about the FOMC—the operating principle of this giant engine room—in an easy and clear way.

## Why Is This Important?
"The U.S. central bank raised interest rates." When people hear news like this, many think, "What does that have to do with my wallet?" In reality, however, the connection is profound.

The U.S. is the "central axis" of the global economy. When the Fed makes a decision on interest rates, the speed and direction of money moving around the world change instantly. Simply put, when the Fed raises interest rates, the cost of borrowing money from banks becomes more expensive. This leads people to cut back on spending and companies to become cautious with investments. [Source: Latest news and headlines related to the Federal Reserve](https://www.cnbc.com/federal-reserve/) In other words, the FOMC statement is an invisible guideline that forces us to think about whether we should buy that extra cup of coffee today or when we should pay off our loans.

## Understanding It Simply: What Is the FOMC?
FOMC stands for the "Federal Open Market Committee." Simply put, it is the highest decision-making body within the Fed that determines whether to "tighten or loosen the money supply" of the U.S. economy. [Source: Federal Reserve Board - Federal Reserve issues FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)

There are two principles they consider most important when meeting. These are called the "Dual Mandate." [Source: (FED) Federal Reserve Issues FOMC Statement - Action Forex](https://www.actionforex.com/central-banks/federal-reserve/412848-fed-federal-reserve-issues-fomc-statement-29/)

1. **Maximum Employment**: Maintaining a state where people can work actively without losing their jobs.
2. **Price Stability**: Controlling inflation so that prices do not skyrocket.

These two are like a seesaw. To balance this seesaw, the Fed uses a regulator called the "federal funds rate." If prices are too high, they raise interest rates to suppress the flow of money; if the economy is so poor that jobs are at risk, they lower interest rates to ensure money circulates well in the market. It is much like adjusting the thermostat in your home. [Source: Federal Reserve issues FOMC statement (0.25bp cut)](https://contents.premium.naver.com/habono/habono2/contents/241108041844348bw)

*   **2025 Case**: Conversely, they have lowered interest rates depending on the economic situation. In October 2025, they cut rates by 0.25 percentage points to stimulate the economy.
