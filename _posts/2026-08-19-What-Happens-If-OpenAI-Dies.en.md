Imagine this: Every morning, you turn on your AI to plan your work, summarize complex meeting minutes in one go, and handle difficult research. But then, one day, the company providing the service suddenly announces, "We are shutting down." What would happen? It would be similar to turning on the smartphone you use every day, only to find the operating system itself has vanished. Not only would your daily routine be paralyzed, but your business would quite literally grind to a halt.

Recently, voices in the AI industry have begun to speculate about what would happen if the "AI giants" were to fall. This concern stems from the risks associated with over-reliance on a single company.

### Why is this important?

It is because our lives and businesses are tightly interwoven with AI. Many companies are currently building their services on top of the technology (AI models) of just a few firms, such as OpenAI or Anthropic. Investors are even asking AI startups, "What will you do if OpenAI cuts off your access?" when evaluating them. [Source: How to Raise Money for an AI Startup in 2026 (Playbook) | Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/how-to-raise-money-for-ai-startup/)

These massive AI companies are often called the industry's "Achilles' heel." Investor Eisman warned that "the future of the AI enterprise space is a bet on the expectation that these companies will succeed," and that any negative event occurring at these firms represents a critical vulnerability for the entire industry. [Source: Eisman's AI bombshell: \"IfOpenAIand Anthropic collapse, we have...&q...](https://www.bankingnews.gr/analyseis-ektheseis/articles/893652/eisman-s-ai-bombshell-if-openai-and-anthropic-collapse-we-have-a-problem-price-war-coming-from-china)

### Simply put: The trap of 'Platform Lock-in'

Let’s compare this to a photo editing app on your computer. Once you start using a specific filter, you become accustomed to the color tone and atmosphere it provides, making it difficult to achieve the same result with other filters. In the AI world, this is called "platform lock-in." If you have developed an app optimized for the tone and data processing method of a specific AI model, and that model disappears, you are faced with the awkward situation of having to redesign your entire app from scratch.

Smart startups are already taking out "insurance." Rather than using a single model, they are pre-testing systems that allow them to switch models instantly as needed (routing across multiple LLMs). [Source: How to Raise Money for an AI Startup in 2026 (Playbook) | Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/how-to-raise-money-for-ai-startup/) It’s like always carrying a spare tire in case your car breaks down.

### Where are we now?

OpenAI itself is caught in the flow of change. In March, OpenAI’s technology adoption rate fell by approximately 1.5%, marking its largest monthly decline. [Source: WhatHappensIfOpenAIor Anthropic Fail The AI Risk?](https://www.aifire.co/p/what-happens-if-openai-or-anthropic-fail-the-ai-risk) Furthermore, the 'gpt-3.5-turbo' model, which has served as the foundation for many services, is officially slated for retirement on October 23, 2026. [Source: GPT-3.5 Alternatives: What to Migrate To Before Shutdown | Markaicode](https://markaicode.com/alternatives/gpt-35-alternatives/)

This means that AI technology is not static but constantly evolving, and in that process, existing services can change at any time. Businesses are scrambling to find a stable footing amidst an ever-changing environment, such as dealing with OpenAI, which has modified its advertising policies four times in just five months. [Source: OpenAIAllows Some Health & Finance Ads In ChatGPT via...](https://netzender.com/openai-allows-some-health-finance-ads-in-chatgpt-via-sejournal-mattgsouthern)

### Future Outlook

The AI ecosystem will move from "monopoly" to "diversification." Companies developing AI models will no longer try to be the "only choice," and users will seek more flexible alternatives.

If you are planning an AI-based business, it is crucial not to be buried in one specific technology. The real survival strategy in the AI era will be "technical flexibility"—the ability to easily migrate to another model even if the AI service you use today disappears tomorrow.
