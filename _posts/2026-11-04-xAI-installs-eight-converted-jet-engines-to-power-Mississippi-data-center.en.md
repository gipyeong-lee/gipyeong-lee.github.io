Imagine this: every time we ask an AI a question or task it with complex work, a massive 'brain'—the data center—runs tirelessly in the background. Keeping this brain awake requires a staggering amount of electricity. Recently, Elon Musk's AI company, xAI, chose a very unique method to secure this 'fuel' (electricity). They brought jet engines, designed for the sky, down to earth and converted them into generators.

### Why It Matters

As AI technology advances brilliantly, the amount of power consumed by data centers increases exponentially. Typically, this power is drawn from a nation's massive grid (the electricity supply system that delivers power generated at plants to homes and factories). However, when a data center's scale exceeds the capacity of the grid, as is the case with xAI, they must find independent power sources.

Yet, the resulting environmental pollution and conflict with local communities are challenges we must all ponder together. This Mississippi case poses critical questions about the realistic sacrifices that can accompany state-of-the-art technological advancement, and how massive corporations must face local communities and environmental regulations.

### The Explainer

In simple terms, this move by xAI is similar to **"installing an airplane engine next to your house to run a self-sufficient generator."**

Originally, xAI converted jet engines into gas turbines for power generation through a company called 'ProEnergy' in Missouri to supply power to its 'Colossus 2' data center located in Southaven, Mississippi[[Ref 2](https://www.siliconreport.com/xai-uses-eight-converted-jet-engine-turbines-in-mississippi), [Ref 3](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/xai-installs-eight-converted-jet-engines-to-power-mississippi-data-center-amid-emissions-challenge)].

These converted engines convert the massive force an airplane exerts during takeoff into energy to produce electricity. Each engine has a generating capacity of up to 50 megawatts (MW), a force large enough to sustain a small city[[Ref 3](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/xai-installs-eight-converted-jet-engines-to-power-mississippi-data-center-amid-emissions-challenge)].

To train high-performance AI models like Transformers (the core structure of AI that grasps relationships between words in a sentence), tens of thousands of Graphics Processing Units (GPUs, semiconductors specialized for large-scale parallel computing) must run 24 hours a day without rest. To achieve this, xAI chose self-sufficient power production that pushes the limits of the existing grid[[Ref 3](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/xai-installs-eight-converted-jet-engines-to-power-mississippi-data-center-amid-emissions-challenge), [Ref 16](https://aidatacenterindex.com/datacenters/xai-gigascale-data-center-tennessee-mississippi.html)].

### Where We Stand

However, reality is not so smooth. These gas turbines operated by xAI are currently at the center of serious environmental controversy and legal battles.

1. **Unpermitted Operation Controversy**: xAI is suspected of operating some gas turbines without proper environmental permits[[Ref 7](https://airisk.mit.edu/ai-incidents/1677-xai-reportedly-operated-unpermitted-gas-turbines)].
2. **Backlash from the Local Community**: Residents of the area where the data center is located, the NAACP, and environmental groups have filed lawsuits, claiming that air pollution, including noise, threatens the health of residents[[Ref 7](https://airisk.mit.edu/ai-incidents/1677-xai-reportedly-operated-unpermitted-gas-turbines), [Ref 12](https://www.wired.com/story/xai-adds-19-new-gas-turbines-despite-ongoing-lawsuit/)].
3. **Unstoppable Expansion**: Amidst the controversy, it is reported that xAI recently installed 19 additional gas turbines in the last two months, further exacerbating the situation[[Ref 12](https://www.wired.com/story/xai-adds-19-new-gas-turbines-despite-ongoing-lawsuit/)].

Currently, xAI is building a vast AI campus spanning Tennessee and Mississippi; the Colossus center near Memphis alone is already running 35 gas turbines to produce 420MW of power[[Ref 14](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/elon-musk-xai-power-plant-overseas-to-power-1-million-gpus), [Ref 16](https://aidatacenterindex.com/datacenters/xai-gigascale-data-center-tennessee-mississippi.html)].

### What's Next

Once completed, xAI's Southaven project is expected to supply a staggering 2 gigawatts (GW) of computing power[[Ref 16](https://aidatacenterindex.com/datacenters/xai-gigascale-data-center-tennessee-mississippi.html)]. This is a massive scale, equivalent to approximately two nuclear power plants.

There are two things to watch for going forward. First, what warning will the outcome of this lawsuit sound for the data center power permitting practices of other IT companies in the future? Second, will xAI accept environmental criticism and transition to more eco-friendly power generation methods, or will they maintain their current aggressive approach to secure massive power? Facing the premise that more energy is needed for smarter AI, we are at a point where deep deliberation is required on how to coordinate the 'environmental costs' we must pay.
