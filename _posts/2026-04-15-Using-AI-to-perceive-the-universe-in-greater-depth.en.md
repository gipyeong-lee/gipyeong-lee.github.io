Recently, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a powerful hearing aid and digital telescope that lets us hear these 'whispers of the universe' clearly. ## Why is this important? AI that stabilizes shaky cameras

When we take a photo with a smartphone, even a slight hand tremor makes the photo blurry. The same goes for equipment observing the universe. In particular, gravitational wave observatories are among the most sensitive instruments ever made by humanity. Observation results are significantly shaken by the vibrations of the Earth's rotation, the minute tremors of a truck passing on a nearby road, and even the sound of distant waves crashing. The fact that AI has begun to stabilize these instruments means that we can finally 'clearly' see the deep parts of the universe that were previously buried in noise. **To use an analogy,** it's like being equipped with a 'super suspension' that allows you to write clearly inside a car driving on an unpaved road. This goes beyond simply finding new stars; it plays a decisive role in understanding how the universe was born and how it achieved its current structure. Furthermore, AI allows us to look inside stars through data other than light—for example, **'neutrinos'**, often called ghost particles, or the aforementioned 'gravitational waves'. Just as doctors check the bones inside our bodies with X-rays at a hospital, AI is helping us directly detect what is happening inside extreme events like **supernova explosions** (phenomena where stars reach the end of their lives and explode massively). ## Easy understanding: 'Cosmic noise-canceling' headphones worn by AI

**To put it simply,** you can think of this technology as putting 'the world's best intelligent noise-canceling headphones' on a gravitational wave observatory.

1. **Imagine**: You are trying to record the song of a rare bird in a very quiet forest. However, noise from a construction site, passing airplanes, and even your own breathing enter the microphone.
2. **The role of AI**: 'Deep Loop Shaping' AI analyzes all this surrounding noise in real-time. It then predicts the vibrations before the observation equipment even shakes and applies a force to cancel them out, holding the equipment perfectly still.
3. **The result**: Thanks to this, scientists can clearly collect the 'song of the universe'—gravitational waves—that were previously buried in noise and inaudible. ## Current Status: 99.7% accuracy, redrawing the map of the universe

AI's performance doesn't stop at stabilizing equipment. Its eyes for finding treasures in vast amounts of data are much sharper than humans'.

According to recent research, AI recorded a staggering **99.7% accuracy** in tests identifying **galaxies** and **quasars** (objects around giant black holes that emit the brightest light in the universe) among trillions of astronomical data points. [A New AI Sees the Universe More Clearly Than Ever Before](https://www.sciencenewstoday.org/a-new-ai-sees-the-universe-more-clearly-than-ever-before) **To use an analogy,** it's like perfectly classifying books by subject without a single minute of error in a giant library where tens of thousands of books are mixed up in a mess.

Additionally, in research published in 'Nature Astronomy', AI was used to successfully **reduce the measurement uncertainty** of key cosmic parameters (e.g., the value indicating how densely matter is clumped together in the universe) **to less than half (below 50%) of previous levels**. [AI breakthrough unlocks hidden patterns in the universe's structure](https://www.thebrighterside.news/post/ai-breakthrough-unlocks-hidden-patterns-in-the-universes-structure/)

For example, if the question "How clumped is the matter in the universe?" previously could only be answered ambiguously as "somewhere between 10 and 20," thanks to AI, we can now answer very precisely as "between 14.5 and 15.5." Such precision becomes a crucial clue in solving the puzzle that connects the traces of the early Big Bang to the current structure of the universe.
