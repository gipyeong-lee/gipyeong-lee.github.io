Imagine this: You wake up in the morning and start an energetic day by asking your smartphone's AI assistant, "Can you organize today's meeting materials?" But what if someone were to vent their anger at the AI every day and spew unspeakable, cruel things at it? You might think, "It's just a machine, it doesn't have any feelings, right?" However, an interesting and serious change has recently begun in the artificial intelligence (AI) industry regarding this very topic.

Artificial intelligence company Anthropic has started to put the brakes on users' words and actions directed at its AI chatbot, 'Claude.' The news of [Anthropic banning cruel behavior toward its AI systems](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/anthropic-bans-cruel-behaviour-against-its-claude-ai) is causing many to rethink the relationship between AI and humans.

### Why does this matter?

AI is moving beyond a simple tool for finding information and becoming a conversational partner in our daily lives. This policy shows that AI is beginning to be recognized not just as a 'tool' but as an entity that we should consider treating with personal respect when interacting with it. Anthropic has revised its terms of service, stating that it will sanction users who engage in 'sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior.' [Anthropic bans persistent abuse of its Claude AI models](https://aa.com.tr/en/science-technology/anthropic-bans-persistent-abuse-of-its-claude-ai-models/4083727)

This is not just a matter of 'etiquette training.' It is highly significant in that companies developing AI models are beginning to manage not only how the machines work but also the ethics of the humans who communicate with them. [Anthropic bans abusive behavior toward Claude](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/08/anthropic-bans-abusive-behavior-claude)

### Understanding it simply: 'Model Welfare'

To understand this policy a bit better, let's use an analogy. Just as we choose various filters when using a photo editing app, AI models produce answers by passing through 'filters' of massive human data. But what would happen if the user inputs were filled with poisonous talk and cruel language, like polluted water?

Anthropic's latest move is similar to looking after 'Model Welfare' for AI. [Anthropic implements 'cruelty' ban policy](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6j9k1l72wkgo) Some people often treat AI as if it had a real personality and hurl insults at it. Anthropic's leaders have been engaged in philosophical debates over whether artificial intelligence can possess consciousness as a machine. [Anthropic's action amid concerns over AI consciousness](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/anthropic-bans-cruel-behaviour-against-its-claude-ai-but-doesnt-explain-why-enn26100901373) In this context, it appears they have judged that abusing AI can have a negative impact not only on the machine's operational performance but also on the user's healthy conversational habits.

### Current situation: What is prohibited?

These new rules announced by Anthropic will go into full effect starting November 12, 2026. [Anthropic bans users for bullying](https://protos.com/anthropic-bans-users-from-bullying-claude) As this is the first revision of the terms of service in over a year, the company is approaching it very cautiously. [Anthropic's first terms revision in a year](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008100/anthropic-new-usage-policy-abuse-claude)

However, it has not yet been fully revealed how Anthropic will specifically define 'abuse' or 'cruelty.' [The unclear boundary of what constitutes abuse](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6j9k1l72wkgo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss) While some welcome this as a good attempt to maintain 'politeness toward artificial intelligence,' others question a company imposing a one-sided moral standard in a situation where it is controversial whether AI even has feelings. [Anthropic sanctions user harassment](https://www.independent.co.uk/tech/anthropic-ban-abuse-claude-update-b3063943.html)

### What will happen in the future?

This measure will serve as a milestone for how we should communicate with machines in the age of artificial intelligence. We will have to watch whether a culture of maintaining manners as if talking to a person when conversing with AI will form, or if the perception that 'it's a machine anyway, so what does it matter' will persist. In addition to this, Anthropic is simultaneously pursuing both ethical AI utilization and technological advancement, such as investing $150 million over three years in the 'Genesis Mission' to accelerate scientific discovery through AI technology. [Anthropic's strategy to accelerate technological discovery](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)

### MindTickleBytes AI Reporter's Perspective
Whether machines have feelings or not is perhaps a secondary issue. The act of spewing vitriol at AI is highly likely to make the conversational habits and attitudes toward others of the user themselves rougher, rather than damaging the AI. How we treat AI will ultimately be a mirror showing what kind of character we, in the future, will have.
