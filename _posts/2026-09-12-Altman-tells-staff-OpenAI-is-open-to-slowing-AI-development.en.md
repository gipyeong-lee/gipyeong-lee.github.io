Imagine this: an AI company that has been releasing new features at a breakneck speed suddenly says, "Let's pause and check for safety." How would you feel?

Artificial intelligence (AI) technology, from the chatbots we use daily to AI assistants that organize meeting notes, is becoming smarter by the day. However, concerns are growing that the pace of this technological advancement is too fast. Recently, a message delivered to staff by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has sent shockwaves through the industry: the company is preparing to slow down the development of its most advanced AI systems.

### Why is this important?

Until now, the AI industry has been locked in a fierce race to see who can build the most powerful model the fastest. It has been like a race to be the first to launch a spaceship. However, Altman signaling the possibility of slowing development shows that AI companies have begun to move beyond mere technological competition and are now seriously considering the value of "safety."

This also has a significant impact on our daily lives. While it is good for AI to become smarter, it allows us to create an environment where we can use AI with more peace of mind and trust by preemptively blocking potential risks that may arise during that process.

### Understanding it simply

To use an easy analogy, it is like a car race.

Until now, we have only focused on increasing top speed. We have modified engines and used lighter materials to break records every day. But suddenly, we hear warnings from experts that the risk of an accident has become too great. Sam Altman has essentially proposed, "Let's pause our race and check together if our seatbelts or brake systems are working properly."

Another analogy is "schoolwork." If you move through the curriculum too quickly, the foundation may be shaken, leading to major problems later on. So, Altman is suggesting, "Rather than constantly learning new subjects, let's master how to safely handle the knowledge we have learned so far." [Source Title](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/altman-tells-staff-openai-open-slowing-ai-development-bloomberg-news-reports-6377661)

Altman hopes that this adjustment will not end with OpenAI's efforts alone. He hopes that competing AI companies will also match their speed to move toward the common goal of "safe AI." [Source Title](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cYeOUI3LwxI)

### Current Situation

Many AI company stakeholders, including OpenAI employees, have recently been publicly voicing concerns about the risks that cutting-edge AI systems could bring. Some are even raising awareness by mentioning extreme risks that could occur if AI becomes uncontrollable, including the possibility of human extinction. [Source Title](https://nypost.com/2026/09/11/business/openai-ceo-sam-altman-says-hes-open-to-slowing-ai-as-safety-risks-mount-report/)

This anxiety is also confirmed in actual research settings. For example, the fact that there have been attempts to use AI (such as Claude) for biological weapon research that could be lethal to humanity is a clear case showing why AI safety is important. [Source Title](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/openai-race-state-hijack-anthropic-bioweapon/)

However, not everything is smooth sailing. Dean W. Ball, Director of Strategy and Future at OpenAI, points out that it is not easy to clearly distinguish between commercial interests and safety concerns occurring within the industry. He argues that as open-source models threaten companies' profit models, it is difficult to judge what is truly a decision for safety and what is a move to keep competitors in check. [Source Title](https://themanhattan.press/business/2026/08/02/openai-ceo-sam-altman-signals-openness-to-slowing-ai-development-amid-safety-concerns/)

Furthermore, experts warn that the process of companies agreeing to match each other's speeds could itself run into legal barriers known as "antitrust (competition) laws." [Source Title](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-09-11-altman-openai-open-to-slowing-development/)

### What happens next?

It remains to be seen whether Sam Altman's proposal will actually become a reality. The biggest question is whether competitors are truly prepared to step out of this dangerous race, and how legal issues will be resolved.

What is clear is that as AI's influence on our society grows, "safe and responsible development" is emerging as a more important value than "fast development." It would be wise to watch how AI companies act in the future, and how the safe AI they create will change our lives. [Source Title](https://www.breitbart.com/tech/2026/09/11/sam-altmans-openai-claims-to-consider-slowing-ai-development-as-safety-concerns-mount/)
