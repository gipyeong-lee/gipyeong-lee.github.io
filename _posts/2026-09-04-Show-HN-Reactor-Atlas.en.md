Imagine this: You open a map app, and you can instantly see when a nuclear power plant in your neighborhood—or on the other side of the world—was built and exactly what it is used for. 'Reactor Atlas', a new service, has emerged that allows you to easily explore the professional and complex realm of nuclear power, just like the map apps we use every day. [ShowHN: Reactor Atlas | Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49549148)

## Why It Matters

Nuclear power is one of the key energy sources supporting modern society. However, for ordinary citizens, the perception remains that nuclear facilities are "difficult to access and closed-off places." Reactor Atlas has greatly increased accessibility to information about nuclear infrastructure by visualizing this data and making it available to the public. Transparent information sharing is the first step toward resolving vague fears. Only when we objectively grasp where the energy for the world we live in comes from can we finally make more rational and cautious judgments about energy policy or local safety issues.

## The Explainer

Simply put, you can think of 'Reactor Atlas' as a specialized map created just for nuclear facilities. [Reactor Atlas · World map of nuclear reactors](https://reactoratlas.com/)

Metaphorically speaking, just as our AI reads context within vast amounts of data, this service is the result of collecting complex data that was scattered and fragmented across the globe and adding an "intelligent layer." [ShowHN: Reactor Atlas | Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49549148) It doesn't just stop at marking dots on a map to show locations. It concisely organizes and shows by country what type of reactor (a device that generates energy using nuclear fission) each facility is, what its history has been from the past to the present, and how it will be used in the future. [ShowHN: Reactor Atlas | Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49549148)

## Note: The name is the same, but it's a completely different 'Atlas'

Did you search the internet, see the name 'Atlas Reactor', and think, "Wait, isn't that a game?" If you did, you are quite correct. It is an entirely different entity from the 'Reactor Atlas' we are introducing now. 'Atlas Reactor', released by the game developer Trion Worlds, was a popular turn-based strategy game that was serviced from 2016 to 2019. [Atlas Reactor - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Reactor) Although the official service has ended, there are recent moves to revive it as a new cooperative narrative game utilizing the game's intellectual property (IP), which is exciting news for game fans. ## What's Next

Attempts like Reactor Atlas will become even more important in the future. As digital transformation accelerates, how transparently and efficiently we can monitor the core infrastructure of the physical world we live in will become a measure of social trust. As data technology becomes more sophisticated, such maps will become even more precise, and it is expected that the scope of services will expand beyond nuclear power to various core areas that support modern civilization, such as renewable energy facilities.
