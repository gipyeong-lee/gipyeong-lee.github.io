Imagine this: You wake up in the morning, turn on your smartphone, and say to your AI assistant, "Buy me a suitable gift on Amazon for the birthday party I'm going to this afternoon." Until now, this has been a complex process where we had to connect to the site, search, add to the cart, and complete the payment ourselves. But what if an AI could do all of this for us?

Recently, Meta introduced an AI agent called 'Muse' with dreams of exactly this kind of convenience. However, this future we expected hit an unexpectedly high wall as soon as it began. Amazon, the world's largest online retailer, has completely blocked Muse's shopping functionality.

### Why does this matter?

This incident goes beyond a mere squabble between two tech giants; it serves as a significant preview of how our way of enjoying shopping with AI will change in the future. Online shopping to date has been premised on 'humans' accessing websites. But now, we are in an era where 'AI agents' can explore platforms and make purchasing decisions on behalf of people.

Amazon's move signifies that companies have begun to establish criteria for how to manage platform access for AI agents. From the consumer's perspective, we entrusted purchasing authority to AI for convenience, but the AI is being turned away at the entrance of the shopping mall. This shows that the utility of the AI services we use in the future may vary greatly depending on whether they are partnered with or blocked by specific shopping malls or platforms.

### Making it easy to understand

Think of an 'AI Agent' (AI that judges for itself and explores websites to perform user commands) not just as an AI that provides information, but as a 'proxy'. It's similar to hiring an errand runner to do your grocery shopping when you are busy.

Simply put, Muse is a 'digital shopping errand runner' living inside your smartphone. Amazon has essentially said to this runner: "I cannot allow you to enter my store, browse, and put items in your cart without identifying who you are and where you are from."

In fact, Amazon doesn't unconditionally block all other AIs. However, they are firm in their stance that **"AI agents must identify themselves and receive official authorization from us (Amazon) in advance."** [Reference 3](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pauldoforno_amazon-blocks-metas-muse-ai-agent-from-activity-7509967502089838592-xrcq), [Reference 5](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/amazon-just-blocked-metas-ai-192537732.html)

### Current Situation

On September 8, 2026, Meta unveiled 'Muse', an AI agent that handles various tasks for users. [Reference 2](https://worldef.com/2026/09/21/amazon-blocks-metas-ai-agent-muse/) However, less than two weeks after its launch, on September 21, Amazon implemented immediate blocking measures. [Reference 6](https://www.nikeshoebot.com/amazon-blocked-meta-muse/), [Reference 13](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/amazon-blocks-metas-ai-141950908.html?fr=sycsrp_catchall)

Amazon requested that Meta exclude the Amazon shopping mall from Muse's service scope, but Meta refused. Ultimately, instead of complex legal procedures like a federal lawsuit, Amazon chose to directly block access for customers using the service based on violations of its 'Conditions of Use'. [Reference 16](https://runtimewire.com/article/amazon-blocks-meta-muse-agentic-shopping), [Reference 20](https://www.businessworld.in/article/amazon-blocks-meta-s-muse-ai-agent-from-its-retail-site-report-624863)

Currently, if a user attempts to shop on Amazon through Muse, the access is deemed unauthorized AI agent activity, resulting in a warning message or a state where shopping functionality is unavailable. [Reference 16](https://runtimewire.com/article/amazon-blocks-meta-muse-agentic-shopping) Amazon believes such strict control is essential because, if an AI agent makes a wrong order or an issue occurs, it is ultimately Amazon that must take responsibility and soothe both customers and sellers. [Reference 14](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/metas-ai-agent-has-been-blocked-from-using-amazon-com/)

### Where We Stand

This incident symbolizes that AI agents have moved beyond the level of simply searching for information and have begun to open our wallets and directly intervene in actual commercial transactions. However, platform companies are reinforcing a kind of 'digital border' to protect their service environments and maintain data sovereignty. Whether this barrier becomes a stumbling block that hinders the growth of AI agents or an essential safety device to create a safer commercial order is being decided right before our eyes.

### What's Next

It is highly likely that this situation will not end as a one-time event. Experts point out that this is a kind of 'growing pain' that occurs as AI agents emerge as protagonists in the commercial ecosystem. [Reference 9](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/meta-ai/articles/amazon-blocks-meta-muse-ai-190958361.html)

In the future, all online shopping malls will need to establish rules to decide 'who are the authorized AI agents allowed to use our platform'. From a consumer perspective, a time may come when we need to carefully check whether the AI we use supports Amazon shopping or only supports other competing online malls. Whether this case leads to discussions on technical standardization between companies or creates 'AI walls' strictly divided by platform will be a point to watch.

### AI's Take

Companies are demanding clear rules of 'identification' and 'authorization' for AI accessing platforms on behalf of users. This incident can be seen as the first major growing pain in the process of AI agents establishing themselves as new entities within the commercial ecosystem. Ultimately, a process where technical convenience and platform safety find a compromise is absolutely necessary.
