Imagine this. One day, suddenly notified of unfair dismissal or troubled by failure to recover your deposit, you turn on your smartphone and ask an AI chatbot: "What should I do in this situation?" Within seconds, the chatbot fluently mixes legal terminology and presents a seemingly clear solution. Because the answer is so confident and logical, you might even mistake it for consulting an experienced lawyer face-to-face.

However, shocking survey results have been released showing that acting blindly on such AI answers could lead to unexpected disaster. According to a recently released expert evaluation, even today's most advanced AI models miss critical core details in real-world legal consultation settings [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical)].

---

## Why is this important?

In daily life, the majority of people experiencing legal disputes or unfair treatment feel deeply helpless, burdened by high lawyer retention costs or not knowing where to seek help. Because of this, many first turn to internet searches or AI chatbots. If AI could provide reliable legal advice, it could become a strong savior for countless people in legal blind spots.

However, this evaluation clearly showed that a massive gap still exists between AI's simple "ability to speak fluently" and its "ability to accurately solve actual complex problems" [[Source Title](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/austinchang3115_legalai-accesstojustice-aigovernance-activity-7526154590501838848-Nq32)]. Flawed legal advice can permanently forfeit an individual's precious opportunity for rights remedy or lead directly to financial damage. Therefore, accurately grasping the limitations hidden behind the convenience of AI technology is crucial for protecting our lives.

---

## Making It Easy: Why Isn't AI Legal Consultation Perfect?

Large Language Models (LLMs) that we commonly use are systems trained on vast text data from around the world to write naturally like humans. Simply put, AI can be compared to an exemplary student who has read tens of thousands of legal textbooks and precedents and brilliantly imitates "how to speak fluently like a legal expert."

However, actual legal consultation is on a completely different level from simply memorizing and reciting statutes written in books. This can be compared to a photo editing program filter. While it wonderfully applies a basic filter that adjusts the overall photo tone (general legal knowledge), it repeatedly fails at precision retouching work that delicately catches minute dust or shakes hidden in every corner of the photo (region-specific complex special regulations, subtle exception clauses arising from an individual client's specific situation).

The newly released **Access to Justice Benchmark** sharply targeted this critical weakness of AI [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical)].

---

## Current Status: What is the Report Card of the 23 Models?

On November 10, 2026, **Vals AI** and **NextLadder Ventures** jointly released the **Access to Justice Benchmark** [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical), [Source Title](https://www.unite.ai/vals-ai-legal-aid-benchmark-finds-models-fall-short-on-local-help/)]. This evaluation was fairly conducted based on 252 vivid, real-world legal aid scenarios directly written by frontline legal aid experts [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical), [Source Title](https://www.nextladder.com/news/new-test-of-leading-ai-models-legal-aid-advice-finds-key-gaps-still-to-be-closed-across-issues)]. 

The test subjects included **23 leading AI models** currently rated best in the global market [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical), [Source Title](https://www.nextladder.com/news/new-test-of-leading-ai-models-legal-aid-advice-finds-key-gaps-still-to-be-closed-across-issues)]. The organizers set a **strict 80% threshold** to verify whether AI could properly fulfill the role of a "trusted first point of contact" for ordinary citizens [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical), [Source Title](https://www.unite.ai/vals-ai-legal-aid-benchmark-finds-models-fall-short-on-local-help/)].

What were the evaluation results? **Surprisingly, not a single one of the 23 models tested managed to clear the 80% threshold [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical), [Source Title](https://www.unite.ai/vals-ai-legal-aid-benchmark-finds-models-fall-short-on-local-help/)].** 

Across 252 meticulously crafted scenarios by legal aid experts from various U.S. states, the AIs repeatedly missed core details that hold the key to problem-solving, such as slightly differing local legal procedures or specific evidence-gathering tips [[Source Title](https://epyhia.com/new-access-to-justice-benchmark-finds-leading-ai-models-miss-critical), [Source Title](https://www.nextladder.com/news/new-test-of-leading-ai-models-legal-aid-advice-finds-key-gaps-still-to-be-closed-across-issues)]. Although legal AI technology has advanced at a rapid pace, this vividly demonstrates that it still has a long way to go before safely and reliably assisting humans with real-world problems [[Source Title](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/austinchang3115_legalai-accesstojustice-aigovernance-activity-7526154590501838848-Nq32)].

## What's Next?

This benchmark result simultaneously delivers a critical milestone and homework to both the AI industry and the legal profession as a whole. 

First, AI developers should not be satisfied with merely fostering the ability to generate fluent text; they must acquire advanced verification capabilities that grasp region-specific legal contexts and concrete facts without a single error. Second, institutions and individuals seeking to adopt or utilize AI in legal services have learned the bitter lesson that they must approach it strictly as an auxiliary tool rather than through blind trust.

In the near future, it will become even more common for the general public to turn to AI to lower the barriers of the law; however, as this survey result warns, until the limitation of "missing critical details" is completely overcome, the touch of human experts meticulously verifying and supplementing AI answers must always accompany it.
