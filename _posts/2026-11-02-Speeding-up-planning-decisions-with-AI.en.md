Imagine this: You have applied for a permit from the local government to build a new home or renovate your existing one. Your excitement is short-lived, however, when you are informed that document review alone will take several months. If the permit is delayed, moving dates get pushed back, and construction costs snowball. In fact, such delays are often not because of slow work performance, but because of the countless "repetitive document reviews" that officials have to handle.

Recently, the UK government embarked on an amazing attempt to solve this problem: the full-scale introduction of artificial intelligence (AI) into the administrative system. The plan is to have AI analyze planning documents previously handled by humans, thereby dramatically increasing the speed of the process.

## Why does this matter?

Housing is not just a building, but a foundation that supports our lives. Even if the government promises to "supply 1.5 million homes," it is difficult to realize this if the "administrative bottleneck" that approves them is not resolved. [Source 19](https://thepropertyai.co.uk/property-news/ai-tools-speed-up-planning-decisions-england)

The reason the UK government is attempting to automate the planning process through digital innovation is clear. It is to increase the speed of housing construction so that citizens can enjoy comfortable living environments faster and to ensure the government's large-scale housing supply plan proceeds without setbacks. [Source 18](https://cedrec.com/r/news/0625-gov-launches-ai-tool-to-speed-up-planning-decisions) In particular, drastically reducing review times gives officials the leeway to step away from routine administrative tasks and focus on more complex and important issues.

## Simplified: AI administrative assistant, 'PlanAssist'

The AI tool 'PlanAssist' (also known as Extract), developed jointly by the UK government, Google, and the tech firm Faculty, is a kind of "smart document assistant." [Source 15](https://deepmind.google/blog/speeding-up-planning-decisions-with-ai/), [Source 20](https://mhclgdigital.blog.gov.uk/2026/06/19/using-ai-to-support-planning-decisions-what-it-means-for-planners-and-residents/)

**Here is an easy analogy:**
Let us assume an official who reviews a large volume of documents is a "professional chef." When a planning application arrives, the chef must check if the ingredients (documents) are fresh, prepare them, and set the cooking order. This task is very important, but it is a time-consuming, repetitive chore.

The AI 'PlanAssist' is like a "skilled kitchen assistant" who washes and preps ingredients next to the chef. When an application is filed, the AI immediately classifies the necessary information and writes an initial assessment, searching for evidence that it meets legal standards. Now, the chef (the official) no longer needs to dig through piles of complex paperwork; they just need to review the neatly organized documents provided by the AI and make a final decision.

Routine householder planning applications alone account for 70% of the total workload for planning officials. [Source 16](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/faster-planning-decisions-as-new-rules-and-ai-tackle-delays) By having the AI assist with this task, the overall review speed has become up to 58% faster. [Source 15](https://deepmind.google/blog/speeding-up-planning-decisions-with-ai/)

## Current Situation

Currently, this AI tool is being piloted in councils across the UK, including Barnet, Camden, and Dorset. [Source 19](https://thepropertyai.co.uk/property-news/ai-tools-speed-up-planning-decisions-england), [Source 20](https://mhclgdigital.blog.gov.uk/2026/06/19/using-ai-to-support-planning-decisions-what-it-means-for-planners-and-residents/) The UK government has shown strong commitment, having signed a contract worth approximately 6.9 million pounds (about 12 billion KRW) with Google Cloud to develop this technology. [Source 2](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/bluestone-planning_uk-government-hires-google-to-develop-ai-activity-7437773757475151872-qwKK)

Technically, the core is "data extraction and matching" technology that reads complex administrative documents and compares them against legal requirements. This is not merely a copy-paste level of information handling, but a form of outputting results in line with policy guidelines. [Source 18](https://cedrec.com/r/news/0625-gov-launches-ai-tool-to-speed-up-planning-decisions)

## What lies ahead?

The UK aims to supply 1.5 million homes over the next parliamentary term. [Source 19](https://thepropertyai.co.uk/property-news/ai-tools-speed-up-planning-decisions-england) The successful establishment of this AI technology appears set to play a pivotal role in achieving this ambitious goal.

In the future, it is highly likely that more advanced AI will go beyond mere document review to support overall city planning simulations or environmental impact assessments. Of course, final judgment will still be made by human officials, but AI will provide the necessary grounds for those decisions dozens of times faster than before. Observing how the cities we live in change and how technology accelerates that change will be a very interesting experience.
